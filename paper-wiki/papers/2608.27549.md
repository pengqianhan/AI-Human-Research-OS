# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** multimodal evidence about a physical scenario — either a natural-language description or a real-world monocular video.
* **Output (Y):** an executable world representation (EWR) `p = (C, E, A)` — physical composition `C`, dynamic evolution `E`, visual appearance `A` — expressed as code that a physics simulator can instantiate, execute, and render; an EWR is accepted only once it is verified against the input evidence. Downstream, accepted EWRs are converted into exact-label `(V, q, y)` quantitative-physical-reasoning VQA examples used to train a family of vision-language models, Code-as-World-VL.
* **Core problem:** modern vision-language models can recognize and narrate physical events but lack an explicit representation of the underlying mechanism — object states, physical parameters, governing dynamics — needed to reliably reason about how the world evolves or responds to interventions. The paper frames this as a "phenomenon-mechanism dichotomy": describing what happens is not the same as representing why it happens.
* **Scope:** the current implementation targets rigid-body dynamics only (the paper argues the paradigm generalizes further but does not implement it); the main discovery-loop evaluation uses a fixed budget of `K = 5` iterations; the downstream application is restricted to quantitative physical reasoning (size, displacement, velocity, acceleration) rather than open-ended physical question answering.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Section 2 surveys three existing representation paradigms and argues each is individually insufficient: **pixels** (video generative models predict future frames without needing to distinguish, e.g., camera motion from object motion — a visually plausible future is not necessarily a physically correct one); **3D/reconstruction** (recovers geometry and viewpoint but reconstructability does not imply interpretability — a latent representation can reproduce an observation while entangling persistent structure, dynamic variables, and appearance); and **natural language** (compact and compositional, but cannot precisely encode continuous quantities like trajectories, contact relationships, or physical parameters). Code-as-World's claimed novelty is treating executable code as a representation that is simultaneously semantic (like language), structured (like 3D reconstruction), and capable of modeling temporal evolution (like generative video models), while explicitly exposing states, parameters, and dynamics rather than only reproducing appearance.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If physical worlds are represented as executable code (composition + dynamics + appearance) recovered through an agentic, abduction-inspired discovery loop — propose, instantiate, execute, render, verify, and iteratively refine a world hypothesis against multimodal evidence — then (a) the resulting executable worlds will reconstruct real videos' physical states more accurately, at a matched evaluation budget, than one-shot generation or Best-of-N independent sampling, and (b) using these verified worlds' exact simulator state trajectories as physical supervision will improve a vision-language model's quantitative physical reasoning (size, displacement, velocity, acceleration from monocular video) beyond training on pixel-level (image-space) supervision alone, and beyond substantially larger general-purpose VLMs.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Physical understanding and reasoning** (Sec. 6): prior work spans spatial reasoning, physical question answering, intuitive physics, and interactive physical environments, but per the authors "existing approaches primarily evaluate physical understanding through task-specific outputs, while the underlying representations of physical entities, states, and mechanisms remain largely implicit" — the gap Code-as-World targets directly.
* **Code as world representations** (Sec. 6): code-based world models for interactive virtual-environment agents, and code-based static content creation at the object level (procedural 3D assets) and scene level (editable indoor scenes); the paper's explicit differentiation from the closest prior work — code-driven physical dynamics and physics-aware reasoning — is that those approaches "do not address the more fundamental problem of abstracting physical mechanisms from real-world observations into executable representations," i.e. they specify dynamics but do not *discover* them from evidence.
* **Agentic optimization and discovery** (Sec. 6): the paper positions its discovery loop as one instance of a broader trend of agents that autonomously propose, evaluate, and refine artifacts — citing evolutionary algorithm discovery by coding agents, automated academic experimentation, and discovering reusable robot skills as sibling applications of the same propose-evaluate-refine pattern, here applied to physical world representations specifically.
* Within this wiki, the closest siblings are in [3D generation agents](../topics/3d-generation-agents.md): VibeWorlding (verifier-gated open-3D-world construction trained with RL) and Procedura (procedural CSG assembly gated by deterministic compile/mate/connectivity checks plus a decoupled vision critic). Code-as-World differs from both by targeting continuous, *simulatable* physical dynamics recovered from real observations (text or video) rather than generating novel static/discrete 3D content from a specification, and by verifying against a physics simulator's executed state trajectory rather than only a rendered image or geometric/connectivity check.
* Authors: a 17-person team led by Hanyang Wang (marked project lead), spanning MirroS, Tsinghua University, Peking University, and Nanyang Technological University; submitted 2026-08-27 as a MirroS Technical Report; notable co-authors include Ziwei Liu and Mingsheng Long.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Executable World Representation (EWR) as code, `p = (C, E, A)`.** `C` (physical composition) specifies what exists — objects, geometry, metric dimensions, and physical properties (mass, friction, gravity) — including static structures like floors, tables, and walls as physical entities that participate in contact and collision. `E` (dynamic evolution) specifies initial states, temporal changes, key events, and simulation duration; expanding `E` through execution produces a complete state trajectory (contacts, collisions, velocity changes, termination). `A` (visual appearance) specifies camera parameters, backgrounds, materials, lighting, and rendering configuration, and does not itself alter the physical process. Because the three components are independently inspectable and editable, a user or agent can change one physical quantity, initial condition, or camera setting while preserving the rest — the basis for the paper's controllable-resimulation demonstrations (e.g., changing a bowling ball's initial velocity direction, or re-rendering the same collision from a different camera).
2. **Agentic discovery loop** (Algorithm 1): given evidence `ε`, an LLM agent proposes or updates an EWR `p`; `p` is compiled into simulator-ready parameters `φ`; the simulator executes `φ` into a state trajectory `τ`; `τ` is rendered into predicted observations (and, for video input, projected into depth maps, instance masks, and image-plane tracks); a verifier compares the prediction against the input evidence at selected key frames and aggregates frame-level discrepancies into a diagnosis that guides the next iteration's local revision. The loop accepts the EWR once it explains the evidence "sufficiently well and parsimoniously," and otherwise continues until an iteration budget `K` is exhausted, at which point the hypothesis is rejected.
3. **Modality-specific evidence construction.** For text input, the agent extracts entities, spatial relations, physical events, and intended outcomes as semantic evidence, filling in unspecified geometry/parameters/camera settings with physical priors and defaults that are then refined by the loop; for the downstream application, a separate video-generation model is applied *after* the EWR is finalized to add realistic objects, materials, and backgrounds for sim-to-real visual transfer. For video input, SAM3 supplies instance masks and tracks, VGGT-Omega supplies depth and camera geometry, and SAM3D generates per-object 3D meshes; the candidate EWR's rollout is projected back into the input camera view and compared against this visual evidence for iterative refinement.
4. **Physical supervision for VLM training.** Because each accepted EWR carries an exact, synchronized state trajectory, it can be queried directly for ground-truth answers: object size is read from scene geometry, and displacement/velocity/acceleration are computed from the recorded trajectory — producing exact-label `(V, q, y)` VQA triples without needing real-world physical-quantity annotations. Code-as-World-VL is trained in two phases: (i) supervised fine-tuning on pixel-level (image-space) measurement data converted from bounding boxes, masks, and tracks in existing visual datasets, establishing localization/measurement/tracking ability; (ii) GRPO reinforcement learning on world-level VQA drawn from both text-driven and video-driven executable worlds, with a reward combining scale-normalized numerical accuracy, a unit-correctness term, and a format term.

# Q6 Experiment Design

Two separate empirical studies. **(A) Agentic-discovery-loop evaluation** (Sec. 4.3, video-driven reconstruction only): the loop is run for up to `K = 5` rounds and compared against one-shot generation and Best-of-5 independent sampling at a matched five-evaluation budget, on candidate videos filtered from WISA-80K by a motion-focused pipeline (retaining clips with salient rigid-body motion or collisions, rejecting heavy camera motion or incomplete physical events) plus manual review. Metrics are Visual Alignment, Object IoU, Traj-ADE, Velocity-ADE, and Accuracy@2%D (distances normalized by the frame diagonal `D`). Reported result: at the matched budget, the agentic loop outperforms Best-of-5 on most metrics (Fig. 5), which the authors read as more effective use of compute than independent resampling — this claim is presented as trend curves across rounds rather than a single reported number per metric. **(B) Downstream VLM evaluation** (Sec. 5.4): Code-as-World-VL is trained at 4B and 9B (direct-answer) and 27B (reasoning, emitting a chain-of-thought before its final answer) on 8×H100 GPUs. It is evaluated on (i) image-space measurement across five held-out datasets (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, RefCLEF, GOT-10K) via Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA), and (ii) world-space quantitative physical reasoning on the QuantiPhy-validation set (2S/2D/3S/3D subsets plus a macro-average), against roughly twenty proprietary and open-weight baselines from 0.26B parameters to undisclosed size (including Gemini-3.1 Flash, ChatGPT-5.1, Gemini-2.5 Pro/Flash, Grok 4.1, the Qwen3-VL and InternVL-3.5 families, and several smaller open models). Headline numbers (Table 1): Code-as-World-VL-9B reaches 55.4 average MRA, beating every listed open-weight baseline and the larger Gemini-3.1 Flash (48.4); Code-as-World-VL-27B (Reasoning) reaches 58.6, the highest score in the table; the 4B variant (50.6) already exceeds most larger open-weight baselines. The authors explicitly caveat the 27B result: because model scale and response protocol (reasoning trace) change simultaneously, it should be read as evidence the framework extends to larger reasoning models, not as an isolated, controlled estimate of reasoning's own effect.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

No new physical-simulation benchmark is introduced for the discovery loop itself: video evidence is filtered from the external WISA-80K dataset plus manual review, and text specifications are LLM-generated and human-reviewed. Downstream training and evaluation reuse five existing image-space datasets (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, RefCLEF, GOT-10K) and evaluate world-space reasoning on the pre-existing, externally released QuantiPhy-validation set (introduced in a separate cited paper not itself in this wiki) — Code-as-World contributes new *training* supervision (the executable-world-derived text-driven and video-driven VQA sets), not a new evaluation benchmark. Code and a project page are public: `github.com/mirros-lab/code-as-world` (280 GitHub stars per HF metadata at note time) and `mirros-lab.github.io/code-as-world`; a blog post is also linked. # Q9 Contributions

1. Code-as-World: an executable world representation (EWR) paradigm expressing physical composition, dynamic evolution, and visual appearance as independently inspectable and editable code, explicitly positioned against pixel-only, 3D-reconstruction-only, and language-only representations of the physical world.
2. An agentic discovery loop (propose-instantiate-execute-render-verify, Algorithm 1) that recovers an EWR from text or video evidence through abduction-style iterative hypothesis refinement, reported to use a fixed evaluation budget more effectively than Best-of-5 independent sampling on video-driven reconstruction.
3. A physical-supervision pipeline that converts verified EWRs' exact simulator state trajectories into world-space quantitative-reasoning VQA data, used to train Code-as-World-VL, reported to reach state-of-the-art performance on QuantiPhy — including a 9B model outperforming the larger Gemini-3.1 Flash and other larger baselines.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Simulator fidelity gap** (Sec. 4.4): current simulators cannot faithfully capture the full range of real-world physical conditions (terrain, contact geometry, material properties), so the discovery loop can converge to a locally plausible EWR that is not mechanistically accurate.
* **Narrow evaluation scope** (Sec. 5.5): QuantiPhy covers only monocular scale calibration under fairly constrained motion; camera motion, rotation, deformation, occlusion, fluids, and long-horizon multi-object dynamics remain untested.
* **Discovery process is not internalized** (Sec. 5.5): Code-as-World-VL learns only from the *outcomes* of verified world discovery — hypothesis construction, simulation, diagnosis, and revision remain external to the model, which the authors flag as an explicit direction (turning the discovery loop into a native model capability).
* **Regime coverage** (Sec. 7.1): the current implementation covers rigid-body dynamics only; extending the executable interface and its evidence/verification mechanisms to fluids, deformables, combustion, fracture, elasticity/plasticity, and gas dynamics is named as future work requiring agents that can invoke and compose specialized simulators.
* **Research-OS fit:** this wiki's [3D generation agents](../topics/3d-generation-agents.md) topic already tracks VibeWorlding and Procedura as verifier-gated 3D-construction agents whose verifiers check a static rendered image or discrete geometric/connectivity constraints. Code-as-World is a natural third entry whose verifier instead checks a *simulated dynamic trajectory* against evidence, which sharpens that topic's open question about verifier design: whether a physics-simulator execution trace is a categorically stronger verification signal than a rubric judge or compile/mate/connectivity gates, or simply a different one suited to dynamics rather than static geometry or assembly.
