# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a user's interaction history $\mathcal{M}_u = \{r_i\}$, where each record $r_i = (g_i, p_i, c_i, \tau_i, \xi_i)$ holds a goal, application, scenario, timestamp, and a trajectory of screen-action pairs. Two settings: *personalized execution* adds an ambiguous instruction $q$ ("order my usual coffee"), the current screen $s_t$, and the preceding actions; *proactive suggestion* gives only a scenario and a timestamp $(c_x, \tau_x)$.
* **Output (Y):** for execution, the next GUI action; for suggestion, a proposed routine or an abstention.
* **Constraints:** the GUI policy and all encoders stay frozen; nothing is trained. History is the chronological first 80% of each user's records.
* **Core problem:** which part of the history should reach the policy at each decision. Retrieval answers at the task level (which records relate to this instruction); the paper argues the useful answer depends on the situation at decision time (which past action fits this screen; whether a routine is due at this time and place).

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Personalized and proactive mobile agents are an existing line (Fingertip 20K, KnowU-Bench, PersonalAlign with the AndroidIntent benchmark and HIM-Agent), and all of them use history by retrieving or summarizing records into the prompt. The new part is the diagnosis: scoring an episode's first step separately from the rest shows that retrieved history is used up by the first decision. The paper names this the gap between relevance and applicability and recasts personalization as selection at decision time.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

1. **Task-level retrieval pays only at the opening.** If retrieved history is appended to the prompt, step success improves on an episode's first action (which commits to an application) and stays at the no-history level on the remaining steps.
2. **Step-level activation pays within the trajectory.** If each new screen is matched against recorded screens of task-relevant trajectories and the single best-matching past action is supplied as a reference, recomputed at every observation, within-trajectory step success rises, with fewer history tokens.
3. **Explicit gating makes proactivity selective.** If a routine is suggested only when its past occurrences give enough support at the current time of day and in the current scenario, the agent separates states that call for a suggestion from those that do not, which prompt-level use of history fails to do.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Personalized GUI agents.** Fingertip 20K, KnowU-Bench, and PersonalAlign (Lyu et al., 2026), which introduced AndroidIntent and HIM-Agent, a hierarchical intent memory that aggregates long-term records into preference and routine prototypes. HIM-Agent is the closest precedent and the strongest baseline.
* **Memory and experience reuse in GUI agents.** Agent Workflow Memory and other workflow or structured memories; MementoGUI, which learns memory selection and compression around a frozen backbone; ATMem, which tracks the role and status of task information. ExpActivator differs by selecting one concrete past action per step without training a memory controller.
* **Proactive assistance.** Benchmarks of intent inference from history, screen activity, and device context, and interactive evaluations of intervention timing.
* **Key people.** Nanyang Technological University and Alibaba (the affiliation line reads "NTU & Alibaba"; Wei Yang Bryan Lim is the last author). Likely critics: the PersonalAlign authors, since two of the four baselines are their benchmark's own mechanisms and one (LLM-UM) had to be regenerated, and GUI-agent researchers who would ask for live execution rather than step-level agreement with recorded actions.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

Both mechanisms instantiate one rule: from a candidate set, pick the element most compatible with the current input and return its action or intent.

**Step-level activation (during execution).**

* *Narrow by task.* A frozen multilingual MiniLM encoder embeds the instruction and each historical goal (with the leading open-the-app phrase removed); the top $K_\xi = 8$ records with readable trajectories form the candidate set, fixed for the task.
* *Opening reference.* Before the first observation, a rank-weighted vote over the ten most similar records picks an application and supplies `open_app(p*)`.
* *Match the screen.* The GUI backbone's own visual tower gives pooled token blocks for the current screenshot (four quadrants of the final token grid, plus a global block for MAI-UI), each centered by its mean over calibration images and normalized. The compatibility score is the mean block-wise cosine similarity with each recorded screen in the candidate trajectories.
* *Supply one action.* The action recorded at the best-matching screen is serialized (clicks in a 0-999 normalized frame) and appended to the prompt as a candidate the policy may adopt, adapt, or reject after looking at the current screen.
* *Refresh every step.* The match is recomputed after each observation, so the reference can move between steps and between trajectories.

**Task-level activation (before execution).**

* *Form routines.* Each historical intent is a center; its neighborhood is every historical intent with embedding similarity at least $\eta = 0.94$, leaving out records at the query timestamp. Neighborhoods may overlap.
* *Score support in context.* $M_j(x)$ sums, over neighbors recorded in the same scenario as now, a Gaussian weight on circular time-of-day distance ($\sigma = 1$ hour).
* *Gate, then rank.* A routine is eligible only with at least $n_{\min} = 12$ occurrences, at least $n_{\mathrm{ctx}} = 4$ in the current scenario, and $M_j \geq \delta = 0.25$. If none is eligible, the agent abstains. Otherwise the eligible routine with the highest $M_j / n_j$ is chosen, and its most central same-scenario intent is passed to the frozen backbone to verbalize as a suggestion.

The decision whether to speak is therefore made by an explicit rule before the language model is called; the model only words the suggestion.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Data.** AndroidIntent: long-term mobile records of 82 users over 135 applications and 13 scenario categories. History is 15,966 records (39 to 414 per user, median 196.5). Execution has 775 episodes and 7,915 steps with deliberately ambiguous instructions; proactive suggestion has 315 states, 215 positive and 100 negative.
* **Protocol.** Offline and step-wise: the policy receives the recorded current screen and the ground-truth preceding actions and its predicted action is compared with the recorded one. Step success needs matching type and arguments (a click within 0.14 normalized distance, same scroll direction, typed text at character similarity 0.7, the right application). CSR is the benchmark's episode metric that weights early steps exponentially.
* **Backbones.** Qwen3-VL-8B, [MAI-UI-8B](2512.22047.md), Qwen3.5-9B, and GUI-Owl-1.5-8B, all frozen, with greedy decoding; QwenVL-Max is an external reference.
* **Baselines.** No history; Recent (latest three records); Retrieve (three most similar records); LLM-UM (an offline LLM-written profile, regenerated by the authors with Qwen3.5-9B because the original profiles are not released); HIM-Agent with its released pipeline. All read the same history.
* **Proactive metrics.** Balanced accuracy and Matthews correlation coefficient, chosen because 68% of states are positive and a rule that always suggests already reaches 81.1 F1.
* **Statistics.** Paired bootstrap over users (20,000 draws, all of a user's steps kept together).
* **Analyses.** Opening against within-trajectory success, history-token cost, user quartiles by history size, selecting against appending with the same trajectory, stale-screen controls, how often the reference changes trajectory, three task-level ablations, and threshold sweeps.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported.**
  * *The diagnosis.* On Qwen3-VL-8B, opening-step success goes from 28.26% without history to 62.06-62.32% with the three retrieval or profile baselines, while within-trajectory success over the other 7,140 actions stays at 17.63-18.36% against 18.43% without history; every interval for the difference includes zero. Across all sixteen baseline-backbone pairs the within-trajectory gain never exceeds 2.4 points and is indistinguishable from zero in twelve.
  * *Step-level activation helps where retrieval does not.* Within-trajectory gains over no history are +8.24, +5.67, +3.74, and +2.38 points on the four backbones, all intervals excluding zero. Overall SSR is the best in every block (30.9, 26.6, 24.3, 28.9) and CSR likewise (47.9, 42.7, 40.9, 45.8).
  * *Selection, not more history, is the cause.* With only the top-ranked trajectory available, supplying it whole gives 18.47% SSR and selecting one action from it gives 23.84%. Matching against the previous screen costs 4.03 points and refreshing every fourth step costs 3.50, both with intervals excluding zero.
  * *It is cheaper in prompt tokens.* 144.6 history tokens per step against 417.8 for HIM-Agent and 730.6-843.7 for the other three.
  * *It does not need a long history.* The gain over the strongest baseline is at least 6.4 points in every user quartile, including users with 39 to 111 records.
  * *Gating gives the only selective proactive behavior in the table.* MCC is 42.7, 45.2, 38.9, and 44.6 against 20.7, 14.1, 22.0, and 19.0 for HIM-Agent; the record and profile baselines sit near zero, and three of them on MAI-UI suggest on every state. False alarms are 21-37% for ExpActivator against 70-100% for most baselines.
* **Weaker or narrower than the headline.**
  * *Evaluation is agreement with recorded actions, not task completion.* The policy is fed the recorded screen and the true action prefix at every step, so no episode is actually executed and errors never compound. The numbers show better next-action prediction on a user's own data, which is the benchmark's protocol, and say nothing direct about live success.
  * *The reference can be close to the answer.* The method retrieves what the same user did on a near-duplicate screen, and the metric is whether the model reproduces what that user did next. For repeated routines this is strong prior information by construction, which is the paper's point, but it also means the gain measures how repetitive these users are in these applications (reader observation). In calibration the matched screen's action agrees with the recorded one only 35.1-36.6% of the time (29.6% for pixel-level matching), so the reference is a hint, not a lookup.
  * *The gain is uneven across backbones.* On GUI-Owl-1.5-8B the within-trajectory gain is +2.38 [+0.53, +4.41] against +1.92 for HIM-Agent, and overall SSR beats Retrieve by 0.7 points (28.9 against 28.2). The "28% on average" in the abstract is a mean of relative gains from about 11% to 45% (reader calculation from the paper's Table 12).
  * *Most of the proactive gain may come from having any explicit gate.* The baselines leave the decision to an 8-9B model reading raw records, and mostly always suggest. The paper's own ablations of its rule, with time weighting or the scenario condition removed, reach 69.33 and 64.58 balanced accuracy (scored before suggestion generation), above every prompted baseline (best 61.2). The comparison therefore shows a rule beating a prompt more than this rule beating other rules (reader inference from the paper's Tables 1 and 10); no simple frequency-and-time baseline is included.
  * *How the gate's constants were chosen is not described.* The step-level settings are calibrated on pseudo-held-out history, with counts given. For the six task-level constants the appendix lists values and says they were fixed before evaluation; the sweep over the 315 test states shows the declared setting within 0.33 AUROC points of the grid maximum and at the largest usable $n_{\min}$. No separate validation split for them is mentioned in the sections read.
  * *Suggestion quality is not uniformly best.* The semantic score is lower than HIM-Agent's on MAI-UI (49.6 against 50.5) and lower than Retrieve's on Qwen3.5-9B (51.0 against 52.1). Recall falls to 62.8-82.3% from near 100%, and F1 is about equal to always suggesting. At the declared thresholds 27.6% of states have no eligible routine at all.
  * *One dataset, 82 users.* AndroidIntent comes from the HIM-Agent authors; one baseline was re-implemented. The token count leaves out the extra visual encoding per step and the stored features of every historical screenshot, which the paper describes but does not price.

# Q9 Contributions

* The opening-versus-within-trajectory split as an analysis: a simple way to see where memory helps, applicable to any agent benchmark with step-level labels.
* Evidence that the same record helps when one applicable action is selected from it and does nothing when appended whole.
* A training-free step-level mechanism that reuses the policy's own visual features for state matching, and a rule-based proactive gate that separates eligibility (absolute support) from ranking (support per occurrence).
* The case against F1 for proactive evaluation on imbalanced state sets, with balanced accuracy and MCC reported alongside the full confusion-matrix rates.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Run it live (reader suggestion).** Execute episodes on devices or emulators so that a followed reference changes the next screen, and measure completion.
* **Add rule baselines for proactivity (reader suggestion).** Frequency-only and time-only triggers passed through the same backbone prompt, and a validation split for the gate's constants.
* **Test outside repetition (reader suggestion).** Report gains separately for episodes whose application or goal has few prior occurrences, to see what activation does when history is only loosely related.
* **Ask the policy to judge applicability (reader suggestion).** The prompt already tells the model it may reject the candidate; measuring how often it does, and whether rejections are right, would show whether the screen match or the policy carries the result.
* **Project fit (reader note).** The split between "relevant to the task" and "applicable to this step" applies to this repository's memory layers. Loading a related handoff or memory entry at session start is task-level retrieval and, by this paper's measurement, mostly helps choose where to begin. Guidance that should affect a specific later decision needs to be fetched at that decision, keyed on the state actually observed there.
