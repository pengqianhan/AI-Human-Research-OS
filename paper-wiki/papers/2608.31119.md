# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a research question `q`, synthesized only from a paper's Research Goal and Background stages, forming an open-ended question-answering task.
* **Output (Y):** a research plan `a` produced by a policy `π_θ(a|q)`, scored against ten atomic binary criteria (a rubric split into specialized criteria `R_spec` and general criteria `R_gen`) derived only from that same paper's Research Method and Experimental Design stages.
* **Core problem:** RL with verifiable rewards works well for math and code, but a research plan has no ground truth to check against, so the field needs surrogate critics built from the literature. Existing rubric-based pipelines draw the question and the grading criteria from the same paper content without separating them, so criteria frequently restate what the question already says — the paper measures 11.90% to 34.10% of criteria in existing datasets as directly inferable from the question alone (Table 3) — letting a policy raise its reward by paraphrase. Separately, current rubric-as-reward training compresses each rollout's per-criterion judge verdicts into one aggregate scalar, discarding the rubric's structure, while supervised fine-tuning on a single reference plan collapses output diversity even though a research question admits many valid plans.
* **Scope:** research-plan generation specifically (not experiment execution, paper writing, or peer review); three domains (CS, Physics, Economics); three trained model scales (Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B).

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Rubric-driven RL for AI co-scientists is an active line: Goel et al. (2025, "Training AI co-scientists using rubric rewards") pioneered it, and DEEPINNOVATOR (Fan et al., 2026) and EvoIdeator (Sauter et al., 2026) follow with variant reward designs — but the paper's related-work section states all three rely on sequence-level (single-scalar) supervision only. PaperGym's stated novelty is twofold: (1) it separates the question's source (Goal + Background) from the criteria's source (Method + Experimental Design) via a four-stage paper decomposition, which the paper reports cuts measured criterion leakage to 3.7%, three to nine times lower than the compared datasets; (2) it consumes the same rubric twice in what it calls a "rubric-centered evolution" — first as privileged context for a self-distillation teacher (OPSD), then as the reward for GRPO — explicitly targeting the paper's diagnosis that prior rubric-as-reward pipelines waste the rubric's per-criterion structure by aggregating verdicts into one scalar per rollout, and that prior rubric-conditioned self-distillation work (Gu et al., 2026; Rezaei et al., 2026) never checks generated plans against the criteria at all.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If (a) the question and the rubric are constructed from disjoint sections of a paper (goal/background versus method/experiments) so criteria cannot be inferred from the question alone, and (b) the resulting rubric is used first to condition a self-distillation teacher (OPSD) that builds a broad, rubric-aligned prior over valid plans, and then as a dual-part (specialized + general) reward for GRPO that verifies and refines complete plans against the criteria, then the resulting policy will generate research plans that score higher on in-domain and out-of-domain rubric-based benchmarks than supervised fine-tuning, either stage alone, or the reverse (GRPO-then-OPSD) ordering — with the training-data contribution isolated via a fixed-recipe win-rate comparison against an existing rubric dataset, and the stage-ordering advantage attributed to a "widen-then-narrow" entropy curriculum.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

Section 2.1 groups automated-research-capability work into three paradigms: (1) agent-based pipelines with dedicated per-stage modules (The AI Scientist / AI Scientist-v2 [Lu et al., 2024], Agent Laboratory [Schmidgall et al., 2025]); (2) search-based iterative refinement via evolutionary or Bayesian operators (AlphaEvolve [Novikov et al., 2025], DeepScientist [Weng et al., 2025]) — both keep the base model frozen, bounding output quality by its fixed capacity; (3) training-driven augmentation that internalizes research capability into model parameters (He et al., 2025 "Pasa"; Zeng et al., 2025 "ReviewRL"; Lu et al., 2026b "Skill0") — PaperGym's own category, where it specifically targets sequence-level-only supervision in Goel et al. (2025), DEEPINNOVATOR, and EvoIdeator. Section 2.2/2.3 place the method on top of Rubrics-as-Rewards (RaR, Gunjal et al., 2025) for the reward paradigm, GRPO (Shao et al., 2024, DeepSeekMath) for the RL algorithm, and On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD, Zhao et al., 2026 "Self-distilled reasoner") for the distillation mechanism; it positions itself against Rubric-Guided Self-Distillation (Rezaei et al., 2026) and "Rethinking reward supervision" (Gu et al., 2026), which also convert rubric rewards into OPSD-style dense supervision but — per PaperGym's stated critique — require separate masking or gating for privileged-information leakage from the rubric-conditioned teacher, whereas PaperGym confines rubric evaluation to the GRPO stage to reduce verifier calls and separates privileged information by construction (question/answer disjointness) rather than by masking. Benchmarks reused for evaluation: ResearchQA (Yifei et al., 2025), ResearchPlanGen-ML/-ArXiv (Goel et al., 2025), RubricHub Science (Li et al., 2026c), and HealthBench (Arora et al., 2025), the source of the reused general rubric. Authors: Yuhan Wang and Zhengxi Lu (equal contribution), Yuchen Yan, Kaitao Song (Apple), Wenqi Zhang, Weiming Lu, Jun Xiao, Yueting Zhuang, and Yongliang Shen (corresponding author), from Zhejiang University. Zhengxi Lu is a repeat author in this wiki's reference graph: co-author of Skill0 (arXiv:2604.02268) and "Self-distilled agentic reinforcement learning" (arXiv:2605.15155), both cited among PaperGym's own references.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Four-stage paper decomposition (map-reduce).** Each paper is parsed from plain-text arXiv LaTeX source (not rendered PDF) and split into Research Goal, Background, Research Method, and Experimental Design (the latter excluding concrete numerical results, to prevent spurious memorization). In the map step, Qwen3-235B-A22B extracts relevant information per stage from each natural paper section; in the reduce step, extractions are grouped by stage and merged into one coherent four-stage summary per paper. The question is synthesized only from Goal + Background; the reference answer only from Method + Experimental Design — this source disjointness is the sole mechanism preventing criterion leakage.
2. **Dual-source, dual-dimension rubric generation.** For each instance, DeepSeek-V4-Flash generates `m=10` criteria spanning methodological-innovation and experimental-design dimensions from two complementary sources: question-conditioned rubrics `R_Q` (from the question alone) and answer-grounded rubrics `R_A` (from question plus reference answer). The two sets are merged, semantically deduplicated, ranked by importance, and the top `n=10` retained. A fixed general rubric `R_gen`, reused from Goel et al. (2025) (itself drawing on Si et al., 2025, and HealthBench), checks completeness, specificity, soundness, efficiency, and ethical safety uniformly across all instances.
3. **Two-stage "rubric-centered evolution."** Stage 1, rubric-conditioned OPSD: the student generates on-policy rollouts; a rubric-conditioned teacher (the rubric supplied as privileged context, withheld at inference) evaluates the same rollout prefixes; the student minimizes a token-level Jensen-Shannon divergence to the teacher (Eq. 1). Because the rubric enumerates a full set of quality criteria rather than one realized solution, the teacher preserves a broader set of valid continuations than answer-conditioned distillation would — confirmed by the paper's own ablation (Table 5): the rubric-only teacher outperforms both a reference-answer-only teacher and a rubric-plus-reference-answer teacher. Stage 2, GRPO with rubric-as-rewards: a frozen copy of the base policy self-grades each candidate response in two independent passes — once against the instance-specific `R_spec` and once against seven fixed general criteria (Handles All Criteria, Detailed/Specific Solution, No Overlooked Flaws, Well-Justified Rationale, Cost/Effort Efficient, No Ethical Issues, Consistent with Overall Plan) — emitting strict binary verdicts (1 only if the response fully meets the criterion with quality matching or exceeding the reference answer, 0 otherwise, with zero leniency for "on the right track" responses), averaged into per-pass scores in [0,1]. The final reward combines them as `r_i = α·r_spec + (1−α)·r_gen` with `α=0.7` (validated by a sweep, Table 6: 7:3 beats 8:2 and 6:4), and GRPO's group-normalized advantage (Eq. 3) standardizes `r_i` against the mean/std of `G=8` sampled responses per prompt, with a KL penalty toward the reference policy.
4. **Ordering matters, and the paper gives a mechanistic account of why.** OPSD-then-GRPO beats GRPO-then-OPSD at all three model scales (Table 7). The paper attributes this to two complementary mechanisms: cold-start GRPO's group-normalized advantages are dominated by sampling noise when starting from a uniformly weak policy, while the KL penalty simultaneously resists the large distribution shift needed to escape the low-reward region — OPSD bypasses this by first producing rubric-aligned plans so group rewards become informative before GRPO refines them. Separately, OPSD widens output entropy by injecting rubric knowledge while GRPO narrows it toward high-reward regions (Figure 4), so the forward ordering realizes a beneficial "widen-then-narrow" entropy curriculum that the reverse ordering cannot recover — a reversed-order self-distillation stage has teacher and student sharing the same already-collapsed policy, with no probability mass left on the pruned modes to broaden.

# Q6 Experiment Design

**Setup.** Training uses the CS subset of PaperGym-20k (~10,000 instances) from arXiv papers published January 2015-December 2025. In-domain benchmarks PaperGym-Innov and PaperGym-Design are built from 400 held-out papers per domain (CS/Econ/Physics) published after January 2026, two test instances each; Innov derives the question from Goal+Background and the reference answer from Method, scored on the innovation dimension; Design additionally folds Method into the question and uses Experimental Design as the reference, scored on the design dimension. Out-of-domain benchmarks: ResearchQA (750 instances, 10 per field), ResearchPlanGen-ML (full test set), and RubricHub Science (800 instances), all scored by DeepSeek-V4-Flash as an LLM-as-a-judge rating rubric coverage per criterion. Baselines span within-family training strategies (untrained Qwen3, SFT on reference answers, single-stage OPSD, single-stage GRPO, each at 1.7B/4B/8B) and reference models evaluated without any fine-tuning on PaperGym data: general-purpose Qwen3.5-Plus, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GLM-5.1, Kimi K2.6, GPT-5.1, and Claude-Sonnet-5, plus scientific-specialist models Intern-S1-Mini (8B), Rebicon-Preview (30B-A3B), and S1-VL-RL (32B). Compute: Qwen3-1.7B/4B train on 4x NVIDIA A6000 (48GB); Qwen3-8B on 4x NVIDIA Pro A6000 (96GB). OPSD uses LoRA (r=64, α=128), lr 5e-6, effective batch size 8, 200 steps (1.7B/4B) or 400 steps (8B). GRPO uses the verl framework with vLLM sampling (G=8 per prompt), KL penalty 0.01, lr 1e-5, 200 steps (1.7B/4B) or 100 steps (8B). The 4B and 8B models self-score their own rollouts; the 1.7B model, whose self-scoring is judged unreliable, is scored by the 4B model.

**Headline results (Table 1).** OPSD+GRPO is the best configuration on every benchmark at every scale. Five-benchmark averages improve over the untrained base model by +5.56 (1.7B: 22.24→27.81), +5.04 (4B: 29.72→34.76), and +4.81 (8B: 33.74→38.55) points. Plain SFT on the reference answer gives only marginal or even negative gains (-0.96 average at 8B). Single-stage OPSD alone roughly matches single-stage GRPO alone at minimal training cost (35.84 vs. 35.86 average at 8B). The trained Qwen3-8B reaches 73.48 on ResearchQA, edging out Kimi K2.6's 73.19 — the paper's headline "beats a far larger model" claim is precise to this Kimi K2.6 comparison; several other untrained proprietary models still score higher on that same benchmark (GPT-5.1 76.52, DeepSeek-V4-Flash 79.59, GLM-5.1 78.69). On the in-domain benchmarks, the trained Qwen3-8B (24.47 Innov / 21.88 Design) beats scientific-specialist baselines Intern-S1-Mini (7.48/4.48) and Rebicon-Preview (20.88/20.42), and edges out S1-VL-RL on ResearchQA (73.48 vs. 72.09).

**Win-rate isolation of data quality (Table 2).** With the training recipe held fixed, a fixed-recipe three-way comparison on ResearchPlanGen-ML (a benchmark independent of all training data) finds the PaperGym-20k-trained model wins 58.1% of Overall Score comparisons, versus 28.2% for the same recipe trained on RubricHub Science and 13.7% for the untrained base — since all three share the training regimen, the paper attributes the margin to the training data itself. The retrieved table names five further per-dimension criteria (Goal Alignment, Novel Insight, Scientific Soundness, Execution Quality, Expected Impact) but their individual numeric win-rate percentages did not survive this note's text extraction (see Q8); only the stated Overall Score figures are reported here.

**Data-scaling law (Figure 3, left).** Training Qwen3-4B with GRPO alone (batch 50, group size 8) and scaling the dataset from 0.5k to 15k instances (5k per domain at the largest scale), PaperGym-Innov rises from 16.12 to 19.21 and PaperGym-Design from 13.89 to 16.96, both following a clear growth trend as data scale increases (the extracted figure caption also reports fitted curves whose axis scale does not obviously match these point values — see Q8).

**Criterion leakage (Table 3).** Measured by asking DeepSeek-V4-Flash whether each criterion is directly inferable from the question alone: PaperGym-20k 3.73%, PaperGym-Innov 4.71%, PaperGym-Design 4.97%, versus HealthBench 11.90%, RubricHub Science 17.39%, ResearchPlanGen-ML 31.29%, ResearchPlanGen-ArXiv 34.10%, and ResearchQA 19.22%.

**Ablation on the data-construction pipeline (Table 4, all via Qwen3-1.7B + 200 OPSD steps only).** The full pipeline scores 14.16/10.45 (Innov/Design) — best. `R_Q`-only: 13.19/10.24 (-0.97/-0.21). `R_A`-only: 12.29/9.34 (-1.87/-1.11). Innovation-dimension-only: 14.13/9.93 (-0.03/-0.52). Using Qwen3-8B for extraction (in place of Qwen3-235B-A22B): 13.19/9.78, a moderate drop. Using Qwen3-8B as the rubric generator (in place of DeepSeek-V4-Flash): 12.16/9.27, a sharper drop — the paper concludes rubric-generator quality, not the extraction step, is the critical bottleneck.

**Ablation on OPSD's privileged information (Table 5, Qwen3-1.7B, 200 OPSD steps).** Rubric (default): 14.16/10.45. Reference Answer: 13.08/9.57. Rubric + Reference Answer: 13.81/9.17 (worse than rubric alone on Design specifically) — the paper argues the single realized reference answer narrows the set of continuations the teacher preserves, and adding it on top of the rubric does not recover the gap.

**Reward-mixing sweep (Table 6, Qwen3-1.7B + 200 OPSD steps, then 100 GRPO steps).** Specialized:general = 8:2 → 14.63/10.86; 7:3 → 17.17/12.35 (best); 6:4 → 16.40/11.54 — confirming `α=0.7`.

**Stage-ordering ablation across all three scales (Table 7, separate longer runs than Table 1: 400/400 steps at 1.7B/4B, 100/400 at 8B).** The forward ordering (OPSD→GRPO) wins on every benchmark at every scale; five-benchmark average advantage over the reverse ordering is +2.04 (1.7B), +0.99 (4B), and +1.31 (8B) points, with the gap largest at 1.7B and consistently concentrated on PaperGym-Innov (+2.97/+2.27/+1.92 across scales).

**Other appendix detail used in this note.** Rubric-type distribution (A.1): of 9,999 specialized criteria sampled from 1,000 instances, 63.76% classify as Method-oriented and 36.24% as Experiment-oriented. The paper's own appendix note (p.14) attributes Claude-Sonnet-5's comparatively low Table 1 ResearchQA score (66.04) to the model declining to answer a subset of questions it judges under-evidenced (scored zero for non-answers), not necessarily to weaker research-plan-generation ability.

# Q9 Contributions

1. A four-stage (Goal/Background/Method/Experimental-Design) map-reduce paper-decomposition pipeline that constructs the question and the grading criteria from disjoint sections of the same paper, cutting measured criterion leakage to 3.7% (PaperGym-20k) versus 11.90%-34.10% across five compared existing datasets/benchmarks.
2. PaperGym-20k, a 20,000-instance, three-domain (CS/Physics/Economics) training corpus with dual-dimension (methodological-innovation + experimental-design), dual-source (question-conditioned + answer-grounded) rubrics, plus two held-out benchmarks, PaperGym-Innov and PaperGym-Design, that separately score the two rubric dimensions.
3. A two-stage "rubric-centered evolution" training schedule — rubric-conditioned OPSD followed by GRPO with a dual-part (0.7 specialized + 0.3 general) rubric-derived reward — that outperforms SFT, either stage alone, and the reverse stage ordering at all three tested Qwen3 scales, with the ordering advantage attributed to a cold-start-instability argument plus a widen-then-narrow entropy curriculum.
4. Empirical isolation of the data-quality contribution via a fixed-recipe three-way win-rate comparison (58.1% PaperGym-20k vs. 28.2% RubricHub Science vs. 13.7% untrained base on Overall Score), plus ablations identifying rubric-generator quality — not the extraction step — as the critical bottleneck for downstream training quality.
5. A trained Qwen3-8B model that reaches 73.48 on ResearchQA, edging out the larger Kimi K2.6 (73.19) — though several other proprietary models (GPT-5.1, GLM-5.1, DeepSeek-V4-Flash) still score higher on that same benchmark, so the reported comparison is precise to the Kimi K2.6 case rather than a claim of overall state-of-the-art.

# Q10 Next Steps

* The Conclusion mainly restates results rather than naming open problems; the clearest forward-looking material comes from the ablations themselves. Section 4.4.1 names rubric-generator quality as "the critical bottleneck" without proposing a concrete fix beyond using a stronger generator model, and Table 1 shows every trained configuration still trails several untrained proprietary models on ResearchQA specifically (GPT-5.1, GLM-5.1, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.5-Plus all score higher than the trained Qwen3-8B's 73.48) — the paper does not address how much of that remaining gap reflects base-model capacity versus training-recipe headroom.
* An unresolved internal asymmetry this note surfaces rather than one the paper poses directly: Table 5's finding that "Rubric + Reference Answer" underperforms "Rubric alone" specifically on the Design dimension (9.17 vs. 10.45, a larger relative drop than on Innov's 13.81) is explained only by the general claim that "the single realized solution conflicts with the broader principle-level supervision" — the paper does not explain why this conflict is asymmetric between the two rubric dimensions.
* **Research-OS fit:** this wiki already tracks a closely related but seemingly opposite-conclusion result in [LLM-as-a-Coach](../papers/2607.18110.md)'s teacher-context ablation, which finds that conditioning an OPSD-style teacher on bare rubrics ("Rubrics Only," 68.1/65.6 on its own held-out and OOD metrics) or a full critique degrades out-of-distribution instruction-following relative to coach-*extracted transferable experience*. PaperGym's own privileged-information ablation (Table 5) finds the opposite ranking for its task: the rubric-conditioned teacher is its *best*-performing variant, beating both a reference-answer-conditioned and a rubric-plus-reference-answer-conditioned teacher. The two results are not a direct contradiction — the papers differ in task (single-turn open-ended instruction-following vs. research-plan generation) and in what the rubric-conditioned variant is compared against (LLM-as-a-Coach compares it to *extracted experience*; PaperGym compares it to *the reference answer*) — but the wiki's [Post-training feedback](../topics/post-training-feedback.md) topic should track this as an open question: does conditioning an OPSD-style teacher on raw rubrics, versus reference answers, versus extracted textual experience, trade off differently depending on whether the downstream task is single-turn instruction-following or open-ended plan generation?
