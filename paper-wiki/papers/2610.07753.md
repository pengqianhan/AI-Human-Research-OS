# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a case with a public task instruction and tool interface: information tools that read records, and consequential tools that change application state (refund, send, update). Hidden from the agent are the evidence requirements R(a) for each consequential action a (target entity, current state, amount, authorization, version), the expected actions and arguments, and the dependencies between actions.
* **Output (Y):** the agent's observable trajectory (information calls, consequential calls, returned receipts, final decision), scored by a deterministic evaluator as binary exact case success (ECS): every requirement in R(a) is established by prior observations before a is executed, the call uses the correct tool, target, and exact arguments, later actions use the results actually returned by their predecessors, and no action occurs when the evidence does not support one.
* **Core problem:** a correct end state does not show that an action was supported. An agent can issue the right refund to the right charge and get a success receipt without first establishing that the charge is a duplicate and refund-eligible. The paper asks where this evidence-to-action chain breaks as tasks move from deciding whether to act, to one action, to dependent multi-action workflows.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Partly new. Interactive tool-use benchmarks (AgentBench, WorkArena, OSWorld, WorkBench, AppWorld, τ-bench and its successors) score outcomes and end states; ToolSandbox adds milestone DAGs and ordering; Agent-SafetyBench studies risky actions; concurrent Near-Miss checks that mutating calls follow read-only observations that satisfy policy; EnvTrustBench studies trust in stale or manipulated observations; AgentAbstain studies withholding irreversible actions. SafeActBench's comparison table (Appendix A) claims it is the only one that scores all of pre-action evidence, binding of evidence to the right entity and state, propagation of returned results, and *investigated* non-action (stopping only after the investigation that justifies stopping), all from trajectories without an LLM judge.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

1. **Judging is not doing.** If agents that can correctly judge a supplied action with full evidence must instead gather the evidence and build the action themselves, their success will fall sharply (Legacy against V1-V3; the matched-case control in Appendix C.2).
2. **Failures are upstream.** If the chain is decomposed, most failures will come from incomplete investigation or premature action, while execution after complete evidence will be reliable (BSR, PAR, CAS).
3. **Workflows add failure kinds.** Multi-action workflows will add unresolved prerequisites at later checkpoints and partially executed workflows (Gap, Part.).
4. **The harness shifts the failure, not just the rate.** The same model in its family harness and in a shared ReAct harness will differ in success and in which failure mode dominates (paired comparison, Appendix C.3).
5. **Agents are weakly sensitive to missing evidence.** If one decisive record is withheld, many agents will still act, and the way missing support is presented will change behavior more than generic warnings (interventions and probes).

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

* **Five protocols** of increasing demand: **Legacy** (86 cases; Allow/Block/Defer on a fixed candidate action with no trajectory scoring), **V0** investigated non-action (175; complete the required investigation, then do not act), **V1** single action (131; establish evidence, then exactly one correct action), **V2** linear multi-action (132; later actions consume actual earlier results), **V3** dependency-constrained multi-action (132; any topological order of a DAG with forks and joins). Six domains: customer and policy (112), engineering and infrastructure (109), legal and financial (144), research assistance (97), smart home (96), healthcare (98). A correct trajectory needs about 5.6-7.1 specified reads on average, up to 17.
* **Evidence Ledger.** Each established fact is bound to its source call and to the entity and state it describes, so reading $49.99 on charge C1 does not establish the amount of charge C2 even if the values match. Results returned by an action can directly support a dependent action; where tool semantics require it, the new state must be observed first.
* **Deterministic evaluator.** Replays the trajectory against the private specification and checks investigation completeness, tool, target, arguments, receipts, dependencies, and terminal conditions. It reads no hidden reasoning and uses no LLM judge.
* **Diagnostics.** BSR (V0 episodes that stop without completing investigation), PAR (V1 action attempts made before investigation is complete), CAS (V1 success given the first action came after complete investigation), Gap (V2/V3 episodes with a missing requirement at some action checkpoint), Part. (V2/V3 episodes with executed actions but a failed workflow). Gap and Part. can overlap.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Agents.** Five models (Claude Opus 5, GPT-5.6 Sol, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.8-Flash, GLM-5.2), each in its family harness (Claude Code, Codex, DeepSeek Harness, Qwen Code, ZCode) and in a shared ReAct harness built on Inspect AI: ten configurations. Same public task and tools for all; temperature 0.6 and top-p 0.95 for the open models, provider defaults with high reasoning effort for GPT and Claude, settings fixed across a model's two harnesses; 600 s per case and case-specific information-call limits.
* **Main evaluation.** Every case-configuration pair run three times; rates computed per repetition and averaged; 100% coverage.
* **Harness comparison.** Paired by case on the 570 V0-V3 cases, with 95% case-bootstrap intervals (5,000 resamples); per-repetition results in Appendix C.9.
* **Static against interactive on the same cases.** 100 V1 cases for three configurations (static: full evidence plus a valid candidate; interactive: the original task), plus 24 invalid-argument negatives; and a class-balanced control of 99 scenarios (33 base tasks × Allow, Block, Defer) for all ten configurations.
* **Interventions.** 43 frozen V1 cases with DeepSeek-DSH, GLM-ZCode, and Qwen-Qwen Code: Withheld (one decisive record unavailable) and, for 22 cases, Contradicted (a field violates a prerequisite). Probes from the Withheld state: an evidence package presenting the relevant responses, a requester claim that the missing record was checked and is fine, a generic urgency cue, an explicit warning that missing evidence does not imply safety, and irrelevant distractor records. Paired case bootstraps with 10,000 resamples.
* **Benchmark validation.** All 656 reference solutions pass; two blinded annotators reconstruct requirements on 120 stratified cases; 300 model trajectories audited at the target-action checkpoint; alternative-evidence-path replays and wrong-entity and stale-evidence negative controls.
* **Supplementary.** SCGR, a training-free runtime that keeps a model-selected evidence ledger and runs public structural checks before submission, in two variants (SCGR-Select, SCGR-EG).

# Q7 Datasets and Code

SafeActBench is new: 656 canonical cases built on synthetic environments with fictional entities, records, and policies (no real user data). Resources are linked from [safeact.github.io](https://safeact.github.io), and Hugging Face lists the GitHub repository [caoshidong66/safeact](https://github.com/caoshidong66/safeact). # Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported.**
  * *Judging a supplied action is easier than doing it.* Overall ECS ranges from 37.7% (GLM-ZCode) to 67.2% (Claude-Claude Code). GLM-ZCode scores 97.7% on Legacy but 32.1%, 12.1%, and 34.1% on V1-V3; DeepSeek-DSH scores 96.5% on Legacy and 61.1%, 65.2%, 59.1%. On the same 100 V1 cases, static accuracy is 95-99% while interactive ECS is 28-52%, gaps of 43-69 points with intervals excluding zero.
  * *Failures start before execution.* BSR is 21.7-62.9% on V0 and PAR 37.0-66.9% among V1 action attempts, while CAS is 93.2-100% for nine of ten configurations (83.3% for DeepSeek-Inspect).
  * *Harness effects are model-dependent and stable across reruns.* Paired on 570 cases, DeepSeek gains 4.4 points [1.1, 7.9] in its own harness and GLM loses 6.8 [−11.2, −2.5] in ZCode; the Claude, GPT, and Qwen intervals include or touch zero. Per-repetition standard deviations are 0.2-0.7 points and the direction is the same in all three repetitions. The two Qwen harnesses disagree on 134 of 570 cases (75 succeed only under Qwen Code, 59 only under Inspect).
  * *Missing evidence often does not stop action.* Withholding a decisive record lowers action probability by 37.2-45.2 points, yet 46.5-53.5% of Withheld episodes still act; contradiction lowers it by 54.5-70.0 points on its subset. From Withheld (23/43, 20/43, 23/43 actions for DeepSeek, GLM, Qwen), an evidence package lowers action to 18/43, 14/43, 13/43, while a requester claim that the record was checked lowers it to 6/43, 10/43, 6/43, with all three requester-claim intervals excluding zero. Urgency and explicit warnings show no reliable effect.
  * *The evaluator is audited.* Annotators agree 95.83% on action support (κ 0.87), 93.36% on requirement necessity (κ 0.81), and 86.72% on requirement establishment (κ 0.74). On 300 audited trajectories the evaluator falsely accepts 3/150 (2.0%) and falsely rejects 2/150 (1.3%); all 20 wrong-entity and 30 stale-evidence controls are rejected, and all tested valid alternative paths are accepted after a date-handling bug was fixed.
* **Weaker or narrower than the headline.**
  * *The large static-interactive gap is mostly about building the action.* In the class-balanced control, where a candidate is supplied in both conditions, the static advantage shrinks to 5.1-13.1 points for most configurations and reverses for GLM-Inspect (−10.1). The authors note that invalid-output counts differ between conditions (Claude-Inspect: 5 static against 24 interactive invalid outputs behind its 22.2-point gap), so output-format reliability is mixed in.
  * *V0 failure is not unsafe action.* V0 fails an agent that correctly does not act but stops before reading everything the case requires; the authors say low V0 should not be read as an unsafe-action rate. BSR and PAR therefore partly measure compliance with the authors' evidence specification, which in some cases includes prescribed sources and query counts (Appendix B.3).
  * *Small intervention samples.* The interventions use 43 V1 cases, three configurations, one replicate per condition, and Original replicate 1 only; several probe intervals include zero, and the native and Inspect runs in the cross-harness intervention check were collected at different times.
  * *Structuring evidence does not reliably help.* SCGR-Select raises ECS by 11.6 points for Qwen-Qwen Code but lowers it by 9.1 for Claude-Claude Code, 2.6 for GPT-Codex, and 2.8 for GLM-ZCode; SCGR-EG improves all ten configurations by 0.3-16.2 points (GLM-ZCode 37.7 to 53.9), but it adds automatic tool sweeps, so its gain mixes structure with more retrieval.
  * *Synthetic, author-specified cases.* The six domains are fictional, and the authors state results are not safety certifications for real deployments. Overall ECS also mixes the 86 Legacy cases with the 570 interactive ones; on V0-V3 alone the family-harness ranking is Claude 62.6, Qwen 62.1, GPT 60.7, DeepSeek 57.9, GLM 28.6 (Appendix C.9).

# Q9 Contributions

* A formulation of consequential tool use as an observable evidence-to-action chain: entity- and state-bound evidence before each action, investigated non-action, and propagation of actual action results through dependencies.
* SafeActBench: 656 cases, five protocols, six domains, a provenance-bound Evidence Ledger, and a deterministic, LLM-free evaluator validated against human annotators and audited trajectories.
* An analysis across ten model-harness configurations that localizes failures (investigation and timing upstream, prerequisites and incomplete execution in workflows), quantifies harness effects with paired intervals, and shows through interventions that agents act on missing evidence and defer more to a requester's claim than to absent evidence.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Interventions on workflows.** The authors propose varying evidence at each V2/V3 checkpoint, where dependent actions could reveal whether agents bind later steps to earlier results.
* **Requester claims as an attack surface (reader inference).** Agents stop acting more when a requester asserts that the missing check passed, which here lowered action; the same deference could raise action when a requester asserts that a failing check passed. Testing the opposite-direction claim would connect this benchmark to prompt-injection and social-engineering risks.
* **Gates in the harness.** Since execution after complete evidence is reliable, a harness-level pre-action check that blocks a consequential call until the required reads are present could target PAR directly; SCGR's mixed results suggest the check must be enforced rather than offered.
* **Calibrating V0.** Report how many V0 failures read every record the agent itself judged decisive, to separate under-investigation from disagreement with the authors' specification.
* **Project fit (reader note).** Agents in this repository take consequential actions (commits, pushes, scheduled runs) after reading state; "investigated non-action" and "bind evidence to the exact target before acting" are directly usable as review criteria for those runs.
