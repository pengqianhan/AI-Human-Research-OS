# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a frozen [decision model](../concepts/decision-models.md), meaning a model that answers a question about a context with a typed output (a choice among options with a probability for each, or a score in [0,1]) instead of generated text; a goal; and either the current state with its valid actions (acting) or a recorded trajectory of observations and actions (judging).
* **Output (Y):** as Actor, the next action; as Judge, a score for one executed action; as Teacher, the same scores on a training policy's rollouts, used as step-level supervision in RL.
* **Constraints:** the judge sees observations and actions only, with success labels and rewards masked; the teacher stays fixed during training and is not called at test time; environments are text-based with episodes of at most 30 actions.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Decision models are new (the paper dates the first public one, JEV, to September 2026) and earlier studies evaluated them as actors, where they do badly on ordered subgoals. The question here is different: whether a model that acts poorly can still supply useful step-level credit. That matters because outcome rewards give every action in a trajectory the same credit, and the alternatives are step labels per environment or an LLM judge queried at every action. The problem of step-level credit assignment is old; asking it of a cheap typed-output model, and separating what the model sees from what it is asked, is the new part.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If a decision model is shown the whole trajectory (Hindsight) and asked whether each action helped achieve the goal (Progress), then its scores separate useful from useless actions better than under a prefix-only context or a will-the-task-succeed question (Value), and adding those scores to the RL advantage trains a better policy, including on tasks the same model fails as an actor. The stated reason: judging after the episode shows the consequences that acting has to anticipate.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Decision models.** JEV (TypeSafe AI), GPT Decisions (OpenAI), and Decider v1.2 (Perplexity AI, fine-tuned from Qwen3.8-27B). Prior studies use them to act: Yang et al. on JEV's surface shortcuts, JevAgentHarness on sharing online control with an LLM, and two diagnostic papers (arXiv 2610.18579, 2610.13092) on decision failures in agent loops.
* **LLM judges and process supervision.** [LLM-as-a-judge](../concepts/llm-as-a-judge.md) is mostly used to compare final responses; process reward models (Lightman et al.) score steps but need step labels per domain.
* **Credit assignment for LLM agents.** [GRPO](../concepts/grpo.md) gives every step the trajectory's advantage; [GiGPO](../concepts/gigpo.md) compares actions taken from the same state; hindsight credit assignment (Harutyunyan et al.; Tan et al. for LLM agents) uses what happens after an action; milestone-guided policy learning (Wang et al.) supplies the milestone definition used here. The paper also points to weak-to-strong generalization (Burns et al.).
* **Key people.** Haochen Liu, Qianhao Zhu, and Zixuan Wang (equal contribution), with Dingming Li, Tongbo Chen, Peng Qiu, Yingqi Qu, Weiming Lu, Jun Xiao, and Yongliang Shen, at Zhejiang University and Baidu Inc. The group (ZJU-REAL) also wrote the cited Agent-G2 and milestone-guided papers and [Agent-R2](2611.08219.md). Likely critics: the GiGPO authors, whose reported GRPO numbers serve as the baseline, and authors of LLM-value-function credit assignment (Zhu et al.), which is cited but not compared.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

The paper is a controlled study with one small training change.

1. **Three roles, one model.** Actor: a multiple-choice question over the valid actions. Judge: a yes/no question about one executed action in a recorded trajectory. Teacher: the Judge applied to the training policy's own rollouts.
2. **Two judge factors.** Context is Prefix (trajectory up to the action, without the observation it produced) or Hindsight (the whole trajectory, outcome labels masked). Question is Value (probability the task is completed within the remaining budget if this action is executed first) or Progress ("Did the target action make a useful contribution toward completing the goal?", with the instruction that later failure does not erase useful progress and later success does not make a detour useful).
3. **Teacher scores in GRPO.** For each task, all scored actions in the rollout group are mean-centered and added to the outcome advantage: A = A_out + λ(s − s̄), with λ = 0.5. Scores are not divided by their standard deviation, so a group the teacher scores uniformly gets almost no adjustment. One teacher request per executed action.

Six models are compared: the three dedicated decision models, plus Qwen3.8-27B prompted for JSON probabilities, Qwen3.8-27B read out from first-token probabilities, and GPT-6 Luna prompted, all with reasoning disabled.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Environments.** [ALFWorld](../concepts/alfworld.md), split into tasks without dependency (Pick, Look, Pick2) and with dependency (Clean, Heat, Cool, where the state change must precede placement), and [WebShop](../concepts/webshop.md). Splits follow GiGPO.
* **Actor.** Success per task type, one run per task; a goal-masking test on 116 states from 66 tasks where one action would complete the easy subgoal while the prerequisite is unmet (full goal against the goal with the placement subgoal removed); and a hint test on the same states (original prompt, a dependency hint, hint plus a worked example).
* **Judge.** Prefix against Hindsight on the first action from those states in successful continuations (167 trajectories); Value against Progress on 462 trajectories that each contain a milestone and a no-op action, with mean scores, Recall@R of milestones, and within-trajectory gaps; a context-expansion test over 2,849 actions that reveals feedback and then 25/50/75/100% of the remaining trajectory; miss rate against API cost for all six judges.
* **Teacher.** JEV scores rollouts of Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct under all four context-question combinations, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct under Hindsight + Progress; 150 iterations, 16 tasks by 8 rollouts; each policy evaluated with three inference seeds. The GRPO baseline is not rerun: its numbers are those reported in the GiGPO paper.

Each analysis changes one factor and holds the rest fixed, which is the design's strength. Only one teacher (JEV) is trained with, and only two environments are used.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

No new dataset. ALFWorld and WebShop with GiGPO's training and evaluation splits, plus the L1 split of RLVMR for a broader actor check. All six models are queried through OpenRouter (JEV 1.13, Decider v1.2, GPT-6 Luna Decisions, Qwen3.8-27B, GPT-6 Luna). Training uses veRL's PPO-style update with settings from GiGPO. Code is linked at https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/decision-agents with a project page at https://zjureal.com/decision-agents/; this note did not inspect either. Prompts and the training algorithm are in the appendix.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

**Actor (ALFWorld success, percent).**

| Actor | Pick2 | Clean | Heat | Cool | All |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| JEV | 70.8 | 11.1 | 18.8 | 16.0 | 42.9 |
| Decider v1.2 | 83.3 | 55.6 | 18.8 | 40.0 | 63.6 |
| GPT Decisions | 62.5 | 29.6 | 25.0 | 24.0 | 52.9 |
| Qwen prompted | 79.2 | 22.2 | 31.2 | 20.0 | 49.3 |
| Qwen constrained | 75.0 | 33.3 | 6.2 | 8.0 | 45.0 |
| GPT Luna | 37.5 | 18.5 | 0.0 | 4.0 | 34.3 |

At the 116 diagnostic states, JEV puts 63.8% of its probability on the easy placement and 14.6% on the prerequisite; with the placement subgoal removed these become 2.5% and 77.6%, and all six models shift the same way. The hint alone moves success by −2.8% to +5.2%; adding the example helps some models (Qwen constrained 41.7% to 58.3%) and not others (Decider v1.2 89.7% to 85.3%).

**Judge.** With Hindsight, JEV's mean score for the prerequisite-directed first action rises from 0.41 to 0.59. Under Hindsight, Value gives milestone, other, and no-op actions 0.632, 0.604, and 0.577; Progress gives 0.659, 0.440, and 0.298. Within a trajectory, Progress scores milestones 0.36 above no-ops and Value 0.05; Progress finds 8.0% to 16.0% more milestones at Recall@R. Action rankings move 1.95 positions from Prefix when immediate feedback is added and 3.30 with the full trajectory. Decider v1.2 misses fewer milestones than its prompted base (36.5% against 49.6%) at about 43% lower cost per score; GPT Decisions against GPT Luna is 50.2% against 54.1% at about 28% lower cost.

**Teacher (success, percent, mean ± std over three evaluation seeds).**

| Policy, method | ALFWorld All | ALFWorld with dependency | WebShop success |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5B, GRPO (from GiGPO paper) | 72.8 ± 3.6 | n/a | 56.8 ± 3.8 |
| 1.5B, Prefix + Value | 79.0 ± 3.6 | n/a | 67.7 ± 0.5 |
| 1.5B, Prefix + Progress | 78.3 ± 0.4 | n/a | 66.6 ± 0.2 |
| 1.5B, Hindsight + Value | 69.3 ± 0.7 | n/a | 68.3 ± 1.5 |
| 1.5B, Hindsight + Progress | 82.9 ± 2.9 | 86.8 | 71.4 ± 0.7 |
| 7B, GRPO (from GiGPO paper) | 77.6 ± 5.2 | n/a | 66.1 ± 3.7 |
| 7B, Hindsight + Progress | 83.8 ± 1.6 | n/a | 73.6 ± 0.3 |

The paper gives the with-dependency aggregate only for Hindsight + Progress (86.8%, 6.9% to 16.2% above the other three teacher settings). Teacher API cost per training run is 22.21 to 30.07 USD at 0.042 USD per million input tokens.

**Limits on the evidence (reader observations).**

* The GRPO rows come from another paper's runs. Training follows GiGPO's settings, but a same-codebase GRPO run is absent, so part of the gap may be implementation. GiGPO itself is not in the table, although its abstract (as recorded on this wiki's GiGPO page) reports gains over GRPO of more than 12% on ALFWorld and 9% on WebShop, the same size as the gains here. The paper therefore shows the teacher beats outcome-only GRPO as published, not that it beats existing step-level credit methods.
* Every teacher variant beats the cited GRPO on WebShop by at least 9.8 points, including Hindsight + Value, which the judge analysis says carries almost no within-trajectory signal and which falls below GRPO on ALFWorld (69.3). The WebShop gain therefore does not track the judge-quality story as cleanly as the ALFWorld one.
* On ALFWorld overall, Hindsight + Progress (82.9 ± 2.9) and Prefix + Value (79.0 ± 3.6) overlap within one standard deviation. The ± is over three inference seeds of one trained policy; no training-seed variation is reported.
* Actor scores fit whole-task counts on 35, 13, 24, 27, 16, and 25 tasks per type (140 total; JEV's 42.9% is 60 of 140, and its 14.7% on dependency tasks is 10 of 68), each run once. One Heat task is 6.25 points, so per-type differences between actors are a few tasks. The with-dependency 86.8% equals the count-weighted mean of the Clean, Heat, and Cool cells.
* Actor ordering is not stable across splits: on the broader L1 split Qwen constrained goes from 45.0% to 58.2% and GPT Luna from 34.3% to 55.2%, while JEV stays lowest among dedicated models (38.8%). The dependency gap holds for all six on both splits.
* Under full hindsight JEV still scores the premature placement in the worked example at 0.60, above its 0.50 for walking to the microwave, despite the instruction that later success does not make a detour useful.
* Only JEV is used as teacher. The judge analysis ranks Decider v1.2 as the better judge on milestones, but no policy is trained with it, so "judge quality predicts teaching quality" is tested across questions and contexts for one model, not across models.

The actor and judge findings are consistent across six models and are the best-supported part. The teaching claim is supported in direction on two environments; its size relative to a rerun baseline and to GiGPO is not established.

# Q9 Contributions

1. A three-role protocol that holds the model fixed and varies role, context, and question, with a goal-masking test showing actors know the prerequisite step but are drawn away by an easier subgoal.
2. Evidence that the question sets the granularity of a judge's signal: an outcome question gives near-uniform scores within a trajectory, a contribution question separates them, for all six models.
3. A simple way to use a frozen decision model as step-level supervision in GRPO (mean-centered scores added to the advantage) at tens of dollars per run.
4. A practical recommendation: screen candidate judges and questions on recorded trajectories before paying for an RL run, and choose by judging quality instead of acting ability.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Limits the paper states.** Two text environments with at most 30 actions; judging happens after the episode, so online filtering or reranking (prefix only) is not covered; JEV and GPT Decisions are closed, so what training makes a good judge is unknown.
* **Rerun the baselines.** GRPO and GiGPO in the same codebase with training seeds, so the teacher's gain is measured against step-level credit that needs no API (reader suggestion).
* **Second teacher.** Train with Decider v1.2, whose weights are open and which judges milestones better than JEV here; if judge quality drives teaching, its policy should be better (reader suggestion).
* **Combine with state grouping.** GiGPO's same-state comparison and a hindsight judge use different information; the sum is untested (reader suggestion).
* **Judge-pleasing.** The policy is optimized against a fixed judge's scores. Whether it learns actions that look useful to JEV without being useful is not examined; the outcome term in the advantage limits this but λ is not ablated (reader observation).
* **Longer tasks.** Hindsight requests grow with trajectory length, since each action is scored with the whole trajectory in context. Cost and judge accuracy on trajectories of hundreds of steps are open.
