# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** an environment e = (σ, S): a task statement σ and a scoring function S that maps a candidate solution to a scalar (a validation metric on a held-out split, or the fraction of hidden tests passed), plus a budget of T rounds. In each round the agent reads, edits, runs, and searches, then submits a solution y_t and receives feedback f_t (score, logs, errors, timings).
* **Output (Y):** a sequence of solutions whose best-so-far score s_t = max over k ≤ t of S(y_k) should keep rising as the budget grows.
* **Core problem:** in most systems the try-measure-revise loop lives in the scaffold, which decides when to retry and what to keep, and the model only produces one attempt at a time. The authors want to move the loop inside the model. Existing agent training data does not show how to do this, because it is built one attempt at a time: only passing solutions are kept, and the intermediate attempts, their feedback, and the revisions are discarded.
* **Formal framing:** with r_t the expected gain of round t, the total improvement over T rounds is the sum of the r_t. If T* rounds stay productive (r_t above a small ε) with mean gain r̄, the improvement is about r̄·T*. **Reflection** sets r̄; **long-horizon execution** sets T*. A larger budget helps only up to T*.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Partly new. Iterating on a solution against feedback is the basis of AIDE, AlphaEvolve, The AI Scientist, and Frontis-MA1, but in those the loop is scaffold-driven. Training on one's own successful outputs (STaR, ReST, rejection-sampling fine-tuning) and on single-attempt agent trajectories (SWE-Gym, SWE-smith, R2E-Gym) is also established. The new part is treating the *multi-round improvement process itself*, including failed rounds, as the supervised object, choosing training domains for how well they supervise that process rather than for resemblance to the target, and testing whether it transfers to deep research.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

Long-horizon reflection (judging where a solution falls short, choosing what to change, and continuing after a failed round) is a domain-agnostic meta-skill. If a model imitates whole trajectories of sustained improvement in domains with clear, graded feedback (ML engineering and algorithmic programming), it should (a) do well in those domains, (b) keep improving as its round budget grows, and (c) transfer to deep research, a domain with no new training data.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Scaffold-driven iterative systems:** AIDE, AlphaEvolve, The AI Scientist, Frontis-MA1 (an AI4AI model for ML engineering, whose OpenMLE protocol is used for evaluation).
* **Agent training data from single attempts:** SWE-Gym, SWE-smith, R2E-Gym; self-training on successful outputs: STaR, ReST, rejection-sampling scaling.
* **Benchmarks:** MLE-bench, FrontierCS, BrowseComp, Humanity's Last Exam, GAIA, DeepSearchQA.
* **Predecessor:** AREX (Lu et al., 2026, arXiv:2607.21461), whose deep-research data and evaluation protocol are reused unchanged.
* **Key people:** the AREX Team at the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence (BAAI). Core contributors Hongjin Qian, Chaofan Li, Kun Luo, Wenqing Wei, and Jianlyu Chen; project leader Zheng Liu; advisors Zhicheng Dou and Defu Lian. Several of the same authors wrote [Repo-To-Skill](2609.02749.md), which frames "operational knowledge" as a third component of a research agent and builds the AREX-Skill Library from ML repositories. AREX-2 does not cite it, but its operational-knowledge framing and repository-derived general skills match (reader note).

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

* **Environments compiled from sources.** A teacher model turns GitHub repositories into ML tasks (improve the metric the repository measures, scored on a held-out split the agent cannot access, in a sandbox with the repository installed) and online-judge problems into programming tasks (the statement, a compiler and sample cases, hidden-test scoring that is graded where the judge allows). An environment is admitted only if the source's reference solution runs and scores, and a simple baseline scores well below the reference, so there is room to improve.
* **Trajectories that show sustained improvement.** A teacher agent works each task with a budget of hours and hundreds of tool calls, and is told the budget, so it can measure first and revise later. It acquires operational knowledge (APIs, data formats, permissible optimizations) by reading code and documentation, searching for the papers and libraries the code depends on, and running small experiments. Compact skill documents are placed in its context: general skills prepared from ML repositories and common practice, and task-specific skills built by searching for related material, with material about the task itself excluded. Rounds that lower the score stay in the trajectory, followed by the agent's response.
* **Whole-trajectory selection.** A trajectory is kept if its best submission reaches a threshold relative to the reference and its process is valid: correct user-assistant-tool format, every tool call answered, a well-formed end state, and no rule violations such as reconstructing answers from the held-out split or exposing credentials. Individual rounds may fail.
* **Loss on progress-making decisions.** The whole trajectory stays in context. The loss applies to decisions that move the solution forward (diagnosing a failure, repairing it, changing strategy, running the next experiment, submitting an improvement). Repeated polling, calls with no new observation, near-duplicate turns, system messages, environment reports, and retrieved documents receive no loss.
* **Training.** Supervised imitation on the new trajectories mixed with the unchanged AREX deep-research data, fine-tuning Qwen3.8-27B.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Benchmarks:** Frontier-CS Agent Track (188 open-ended algorithmic problems with graded scores, 5 hours per task), MLE-bench Lite under the OpenMLE protocol (12 hours per task, Any Medal averaged over three seeds, AREX-2 run with skills in its context), and BrowseComp, Humanity's Last Exam (text-only subset), GAIA, and DeepSearchQA (F1) under the AREX protocol (at most 300 inner turns and 1,500 turns overall).
* **Comparisons:** closed and open models from 4B to 2.8T parameters. Some Frontier-CS rows are the authors' reproductions; MLE-bench Lite rows are reproduced and reported by OpenMLE; HLE rows mix the full set and the text-only subset.
* **Round scaling with feedback:** mean best-so-far Frontier-CS score over the 5-hour budget for AREX-2 (with the authors' optimized harness), DeepSeek-V4-Pro, and DeepSeek-V4-Flash.
* **Round scaling without feedback:** BrowseComp accuracy against the average number of turns, under a two-loop recursive research process (an inner loop gathers evidence; an outer loop accepts, refines, or restarts by the model's own confidence), compared with AREX (122B) under the same process.
* **Stage-wise ablation on MLE-bench Lite:** M0 base Qwen3.8-27B without skills; M1 with general skills; M2 with general and task-specific skills and more rounds; M3 AREX-2 with M2's skills and the base number of rounds; M4 AREX-2 with M2's skills and rounds.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported:**
  * *In-domain results.* MLE-bench Lite Any Medal 81.8, the highest in its table: 8.1 points above Naive-N0.5-Flash (73.7) and 9.1 above GPT-5.6 Sol (72.7); Kimi-K3 scores 72.7, Frontis-MA1-35B 71.2, and Claude Opus 4.8 63.6. Frontier-CS 70.7: 16.0 points above the strongest open-weight row (Kimi-K2.7-Code, 54.7, reproduced) and 5.7 below GPT-5.6 Sol (76.4), below Claude Opus 4.8 (74.5) and GPT-5.5 (72.1), above Gemini-3.1-Pro (68.9).
  * *Deep research.* BrowseComp 84.0, HLE text-only 52.6, GAIA 92.2, DeepSearchQA 93.8, above both earlier AREX models on all four (AREX 4B: 70.7/40.6/81.6/78.5; AREX 122B: 82.5/52.4/85.4/89.9). Among models of at most 40B, it leads on BrowseComp, HLE, and DeepSearchQA (for example Iris-mini: 82.2/52.3/86.9 on BrowseComp/HLE/DeepSearchQA) and is third on GAIA behind XYZ-Aquila-mini (97.1) and Agents-A1 (96.0).
  * *Longer budgets stay productive.* On Frontier-CS, AREX-2 reaches 54.4 after one hour, 65.9 after two, and 70.7 after five, gaining 2.2 points in the last hour, while DeepSeek-V4-Pro stops at 44.7 after two hours and V4-Flash at 39.1 after three. On BrowseComp without correctness feedback, accuracy rises from 64.8 at 47 turns to 84.0 at 143 turns; at about 140 turns it is more than 12 points ahead of AREX (122B), and its gain per turn is about three times larger.
  * *Ablation.* MLE-bench Lite rises from 28.8 (M0) to 41.2 with general skills (M1) and 68.2 with task-specific skills and more rounds (M2). With the same skills and rounds, AREX-2 reaches 81.8 (M4, +13.6 over M2); with fewer rounds it still reaches 75.8 (M3, +7.6 over M2); more rounds add 6.0 (M3→M4).
* **Weaker or bounded:**
  * *The transfer claim changes the base model.* The paper argues that deep-research gains over AREX (4B) and AREX (122B) come from the new trajectories, because the deep-research data is unchanged. But AREX-2 is fine-tuned from Qwen3.8-27B, a different and newer base than either earlier model. No Qwen3.8-27B trained on the old AREX recipe alone is reported, so the transfer effect cannot be separated from the base-model change (reader note). The BrowseComp turn-scaling comparison has the same confound.
  * *MLE-bench Lite headline includes skills the baselines lack.* AREX-2 is evaluated with general and task-specific skills in context. The ablation shows skills and more rounds alone take the base model from 28.8 to 68.2, about three-quarters of the total gain, and 68.2 already equals GPT-5.5's row. The baseline rows, reproduced by OpenMLE, are not stated to have the same skills, so the 8-9-point lead over them mixes training, skills, and protocol. The training contribution is the M2→M4 difference (13.6 points).
  * *Harness and protocol differences.* The Frontier-CS scaling run uses "our optimized harness" for AREX-2; the baselines' harness is not described in the retrieved text. Several baseline rows are copied from other reports, and HLE mixes the full set with the text-only subset (AREX-2's 52.6 is text-only).
  * *Data and contamination details are missing.* The number of environments, the teacher, the selection threshold, and the size of the training mixture are not reported. Training environments come from GitHub ML repositories, and MLE-bench is built from Kaggle competitions; the retrieved text excludes task material from task-specific skills but reports no overlap check between training repositories and benchmark competitions (reader note).
  * *Variance.* Only MLE-bench Lite reports a seed average (three seeds); the other results and the scaling curves have no reported variance or significance tests.
  * *A related reference point (reader note).* In [Repo-To-Skill](2609.02749.md), from overlapping authors, a fixed GPT-5.5 Codex agent with the AREX-Skill Library scores 77.14 on FrontierCS under the same 188-task, 5-hour Agent Track, above AREX-2's 70.7. The two papers use different agents and harnesses, so this is context, not a controlled comparison.

# Q9 Contributions

* A definition of agent self-improvement as within-task test-time scaling, with a simple decomposition (r̄ for reflection, T* for long-horizon execution) that turns "keeps improving with more rounds" into something measurable.
* A data recipe: environments compiled from repositories and online judges with admission checks for working references and headroom, long-budget teacher trajectories with operational-knowledge acquisition and skills, whole-trajectory selection that keeps failures, and a loss restricted to progress-making decisions.
* A 27B open model with strong MLE-bench Lite and Frontier-CS results, released weights, and budget-scaling curves with and without external feedback.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **The authors' plan:** widen the training domains, lengthen the horizons, and close the loop by training the model on its own trajectories.
* **The missing control (reader note):** fine-tune Qwen3.8-27B on the old AREX recipe alone and compare it with AREX-2 on the four deep-research benchmarks; that difference, not the comparison with AREX (4B/122B), measures transfer from the new trajectories.
* **Measure r̄ and T* directly (reader note):** the decomposition is defined but not estimated. Reporting per-round gain distributions and the round at which r_t falls below ε would show whether training raised r̄, T*, or both, and whether skills affect the same quantity.
* **Which failures matter (reader note):** ablate the "keep failed rounds" choice by training on the same trajectories with failed rounds removed from context, to test the paper's claim that recovery from setbacks is what transfers.
* **Relevance to this repository (reader note):** the formalization gives a measurable meaning to "self-improving" for the Research OS's long-running agent loops (for example an autoresearch-style campaign): log best-so-far score per round and check whether added rounds stay productive, rather than reporting only the final score.
