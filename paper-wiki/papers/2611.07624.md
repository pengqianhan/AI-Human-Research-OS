# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a Seed agent made of a frozen model (GPT-5.4) and a harness (MiniSWE 2.4.5, whose source can be edited, or Codex 0.143.0, where only prompts, skills, configuration, and outer orchestration can change), a task set split into Train, Validation, and Test, and a harness-evolution method run for 20 generations.
* **Output (Y):** not a new agent but a diagnosis of what evolution produced: for each final Champion, reliability (Avg@8), coverage (Pass@8), tasks gained and lost against the Seed, deployment and evolution cost, transfer to another benchmark, and how the method's select, analyze, mutate, and gate operators contributed. A constructive follow-up turns the diagnosis into operator revisions (AGE).
* **Constraints:** model weights never change; Test tasks never feed search or admission; every comparison uses eight complete passes over the same tasks.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Partly new. Harness evolution is established ([Agentic Harness Engineering](../concepts/agentic-harness-engineering.md), [HyperAgents](../concepts/hyperagents.md), A-Evolve, [GEPA](../concepts/gepa.md)), and two earlier papers already questioned its evaluation: Wang et al. (arXiv:2607.12227) report inconsistent advantages over test-time scaling at matched budgets and weak held-out generalization, and EvoAgentBench (arXiv:2607.05202) benchmarks self-evolution through ability transfer. What is new here is a task-level accounting across four methods, two harnesses, and two domains (coverage gains and losses per task, cost per trial against attempts needed), plus controlled experiments on single operators: expanding a fixed pool of Parents with one shared mutation process, isolating components of a Champion, and replaying admission rules on frozen Candidate pools.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If evolved agents are judged by their task-level behavior instead of one aggregate score, then higher scores will turn out to mix newly solved tasks with lost ones, to cost more to deploy, and to transfer only partly; and the operators that drive evolution (choosing a Parent by its current score, generic diagnoses, combining changes, admitting by score) will each be shown to misjudge which changes are useful. Revising those operators with the diagnostics should then give better and more preserving evolution than the reference method.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Agent improvement without weight updates.** [Reflexion](../concepts/reflexion.md), Self-Refine, ReflectEvo, [DSPy](../concepts/dspy.md), TextGrad, Promptbreeder, ACE, and CoEvoSkills; the four methods studied: AHE (arXiv:2604.25850, debugger reports over traces and one testable change per round), HyperAgents (arXiv:2603.19461, an editable improvement procedure), A-Evolve (arXiv:2602.00359, framed as deployment-time optimization over persistent state), and GEPA (trajectory reflection with Pareto selection).
* **Program and harness evolution.** GPTSwarm, AgentSquare, ADAS, STOP, the [Darwin Gödel Machine](../concepts/darwin-godel-machine.md), [AlphaEvolve](../concepts/alphaevolve.md), optimizers that build their own pipelines (arXiv:2608.09629), and RSIHub (arXiv:2610.32253), a framework from overlapping authors that composes evolution methods from reusable operators and is the released code here.
* **Capability boundaries and evaluation.** The small-k versus large-k distinction from RL-for-reasoning studies, which motivates reporting Avg@k and Pass@k separately ([Pass@k](../concepts/pass-at-k.md)); "AI agents that matter" on cost and reproducibility; the τ-bench family and [Terminal-Bench](../concepts/terminal-bench.md).
* **Key people.** Equal-contribution authors Xiaobo Wang, Zimu Wang, Siyu Ye, and Jiaqi Li; corresponding authors Xiaodong Gu (Shanghai Jiao Tong University), Qi Liu (University of Science and Technology of China), and Zilong Zheng (Beijing Institute for General Artificial Intelligence), with co-authors at UC Berkeley and Simple Agent Lab. Interested critics: the authors of AHE, HyperAgents, A-Evolve, and GEPA, whose methods are run here as reimplementations inside one operator framework.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

**A common operator view.** Each method is written as seven operators: select (choose a Parent), rollout (collect evidence), analyze (diagnose it), mutate (propose a Candidate), an optional validate, gate (admit or reject), and record. Table 11 gives each method's initial policy: AHE picks the newest eligible Parent and admits every valid evaluation; HyperAgents samples by score with a child-count penalty and can edit its own operators; A-Evolve picks the highest-scoring Parent and admits only strict improvements; GEPA samples from a per-task Pareto front and filters proposals in validate. AHE and HyperAgents are called implementation-centric, A-Evolve and GEPA reflection-centric.

**Three analyses.** *Phenomenology* compares each Champion with its Seed over eight passes: Avg@8, Pass@8, a 9×9 task-transition heatmap of Seed against Champion success counts, and coverage against cost at k = 1, 2, 4, 8. *Generalization* splits the gain into Train, Validation, and Test contributions and tests frozen Champions on [τ²-bench](../concepts/tau2-bench.md) (278 tasks) and [DeepSWE](../concepts/deepswe.md) v1.1 (113 tasks). *Mechanisms* tests one operator at a time: select by giving four frozen Champions per setting three Candidates each from the same A-Evolve process; analyze and mutate by adding each of a Champion's two functional Components to the Seed alone, against a generic reminder; gate by replaying original and constrained admission (strict improvement plus a 0.05 cap on mean per-task regression) over frozen pools of four Candidates.

**AGE.** EvoAudit, an agent-assisted workflow over the archived runs, proposes four revisions to A-Evolve: break exact score ties in select by fewest Seed-relative regressions; diagnose from behavior-only trajectory evidence that separates observations from inferences; require mutations to name the mechanism, preserve useful behavior, and close over their dependencies; and in gate admit an equal-score Candidate only if it is cheaper.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Benchmarks and splits.** τ³-Banking (97 tasks: 50 Train, 20 Validation, 27 Test) for general tool-agent-user tasks and Terminal-Bench 2 (89 tasks: 50, 19, 20) for coding tasks.
* **Evolution runs.** 4 methods × 2 harnesses × 2 benchmarks = 16 lineages of 20 generations each, one run per lineage, with GPT-5.4 as the target model; AGE uses Codex 0.146.0 with GPT-5.4 at xhigh effort for diagnosis and mutation, one Candidate per generation, and search seed 0.
* **Reporting.** Every Seed and Champion gets eight passes over all tasks (7,760 τ³-Banking and 7,120 Terminal-Bench 2 executions in the main comparison). Costs are recorded in USD per call; per-task deployment cost at k passes is k times the mean cost per trial.
* **Behavior audit.** 22 task-lineage pairs with the largest success-count changes are compared trace by trace and given one behavior category each (Table 2).
* **Human review of AGE's revisions.** Eight experts choose a direction before seeing each proposal, then rate its evidence, whether it addresses the problem, and whether it should be validated.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

No new dataset. The paper links [simple-agent-lab/RSIHub](https://github.com/simple-agent-lab/RSIHub) as its code (not opened for this note). It gives the operator policies (Table 11), full Component definitions with prompt and code excerpts (Appendix D.5), the transfer adapters' contract, and the evolution trees of all sixteen runs. The model is a proprietary API model, and each 20-generation run cost about $700 to $2,400 in evolution calls (Table 10).

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported.**
  * *Gains and losses come together.* Every one of the sixteen Champions gains some previously uncovered tasks and loses some covered ones (gains 1 to 15, losses 2 to 10). HyperAgents with MiniSWE on τ³-Banking raises Avg@8 by 5.2 points while losing 10 covered tasks; eleven lineages improve reliability and ten improve coverage, and four move the two in opposite directions.
  * *Gains usually cost more.* Eight of eleven reliability-improving Champions cost more per trial than their Seed; the three cheaper ones are all MiniSWE on Terminal-Bench 2. A pricier Champion can still be cheaper to reach a coverage level: AHE with MiniSWE on τ³-Banking reaches 56.7% coverage in four passes, above the Seed's 54.6% in eight.
  * *Specific changes beat generic advice.* Fifteen of sixteen isolated Components beat a generic reminder on reliability, and the reminders leave reliability near the Seed or lower it.
  * *Changes interact.* Both HyperAgents Champions on τ³-Banking show negative interaction between their two Components (−6.3 and −6.6 points, task-paired 95% intervals excluding zero), and in two of the eight cases one Component alone beats the full Champion.
  * *Current score is a poor guide to improvement potential.* In all four Parent pools the highest-scoring Parent is not the one whose Candidates gain most; the methods' select rules pick the best Parent below the 25% random rate on τ³-Banking (17.5% and 8.8%) and above it on Terminal-Bench 2 (59.5% and 55.7%).
  * *Strict admission loses alternatives.* The constrained gate keeps the reporting-panel best Candidate in 6 of 16 pools, against 15 for the original gates.
  * *AGE.* It has the best Avg@8 in all four settings (34.0, 38.1, 71.8, 73.5) and beats A-Evolve, its reference, on both reliability and coverage in all four, while losing fewer covered tasks in three and tying in the fourth.
* **Weaker or narrower than the headline.**
  * *Headline numbers include the training tasks.* Table 1 and Table 10 are computed over all tasks, more than half of them Train tasks that drove evolution. Test contributions are small and are divided by the full task count; AHE with MiniSWE on τ³-Banking adds 1.2 points of Avg@8 from Test, about 4.3 points on the 27 Test tasks alone (reader arithmetic). AGE's split-level results are not reported.
  * *One evolution run per lineage.* Eight passes measure evaluation noise (pass-to-pass Std 1.7 to 4.8 points), but the variance of evolution itself is not measured, and the paper notes that some aggregate directions differ between passes 1-4 and 5-8 on τ³-Banking. AGE's margins over the best existing method are 0.9 to 1.6 points of Avg@8 (reader arithmetic).
  * *AGE is designed from the same benchmarks it is evaluated on.* EvoAudit derives the revisions from archived runs on τ³-Banking and Terminal-Bench 2, and AGE is then compared on those benchmarks and harnesses, with no transfer test of AGE itself (reader observation).
  * *The τ²-bench coverage drop is near a ceiling.* Seeds already reach 96.2% and 97.5% Pass@8 there, so "coverage falls for seven of eight" means drops of at most 1.0 point (reader observation from Table 14).
  * *Methods are reimplementations.* All four run inside a shared operator framework from overlapping authors, with choices such as GEPA's per-benchmark reflection unit (Appendix A.4); differences between methods may partly reflect these implementations.
  * *Expert review measures plausibility, not effect.* Mean endorsement of 6.75 of 8 experts says the revisions look justified, not that each one helps; there is no per-revision ablation of AGE.

# Q9 Contributions

* A shared seven-operator description of four harness-evolution methods and a matched evaluation of sixteen lineages across two harness types and two task domains.
* Evidence that evolution redistributes task success instead of only adding to it, with paired gain and loss counts, per-trial and per-coverage cost, and split- and transfer-level accounting.
* Operator-level diagnostics: improvement-potential tests for select, Component and reminder controls for analyze and mutate, interaction estimates, and admission replays for gate.
* AGE, four evidence-linked operator revisions that improve on A-Evolve in all four settings.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Report gains and losses separately (from the paper).** Task turnover and cost per coverage level are cheap to compute from the passes a method already runs; harness-evolution papers could adopt them as standard columns.
* **Repeat evolution runs (reader suggestion).** Three seeds per lineage would show whether the differences between methods, and AGE's 1-point margins, exceed run-to-run variation.
* **Test-only and transfer results for AGE (reader suggestion).** Reporting AGE on the Test split and on τ²-bench and DeepSWE would separate a better search procedure from one tuned to these benchmarks.
* **Select by expected gain (reader suggestion).** Since current score misranks Parents, a select rule that estimates improvement potential, for example from the gains of a Parent's earlier children, is a natural test; AGE's tie-breaking rule leaves the score objective unchanged.
* **Preservation-aware gates.** The replay suggests admitting by score plus regression on a held-out panel while keeping a few alternatives, rather than one incumbent.
