# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a research question, initial evidence (literature, earlier results, expert input), a reusable Cognitive Repertoire of methods, a stopping rule, and a budget.
* **Output (Y):** a set of reviewed research ideas, each a complete proposal with a mechanism or design, supporting references, constraints, testable consequences, and open uncertainties; or executable plans whose experimental or simulation results return as evidence. Outputs come with their supporting evidence and unresolved questions.
* **Constraints:** in the benchmark comparison every harness uses GPT-5.6-Sol at high reasoning effort and must return K = 10 ideas per task. In the real-world cases, domain experts choose which idea to pursue and validate its implementation.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Not a new problem. Iterative AI-scientist workflows already exist: The AI Scientist and [AI Scientist-v2](../concepts/ai-scientist-v2.md), Google's Co-Scientist, Robin, and [EvoScientist](2603.08127.md). The paper's argument is that iteration driven by the latest round's feedback does not keep findings from different branches together with their evidence and conditions, so earlier failures, unresolved conflicts, and premises shared by several ideas get overlooked, and search over-reacts to recent outcomes. The claimed difference is an explicit, revisable record of what the search currently understands, used to pick both targets and reasoning methods.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If a scientific agent keeps an explicit meta-level model of its own search (what is supported, what conflicts, which assumptions several ideas share) and uses it, through monitoring and control, to choose the next target and a reasoning method from a reusable repertoire, then it will recover more of the decisions that real studies made and propose more novel and more varied ideas than iterative harnesses on the same base model, and its proposals will connect to practical validation.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **AI scientists.** The AI Scientist and AI Scientist-v2, Co-Scientist, Robin, and Kosmos; systems that refine hypotheses through critique, including [ARIS](2605.03042.md); systems with persistent memory or shared representations, including EvoScientist; and AlphaEvolve-style use of execution results to guide search.
* **Scientific ideation.** Systems that ground ideas in prior research and check them against it, including [ResearchStudio-Idea](2607.04439.md); search methods that track idea lineages; evaluations of breadth, of expert judgments before and after implementation, and of how proposals build on prior work (IG-Arena).
* **Cognitive science.** Problem solving as search over representations and operators (Newell and Simon), Nelson and Narens' object level and meta level joined by monitoring and control, strategy selection as metareasoning, analogical mapping, schema induction, and case-based learning.
* **Cognitive architectures for agents.** CoALA, Generative Agents, [Reflexion](../concepts/reflexion.md), an analysis of cognitive elements in LLM reasoning, and Chain of Mindset, which selects a reasoning mode from the evolving state.
* **Key people.** The byline is "Sage-Taiyi Team, Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory"; arXiv lists 90 authors. Appendix A names ten lead authors (Weida Wang, Benteng Chen, Yigeng Jiang, Shuchen Pu, Weiping Shen, Zichao Liu, Yulin Su, Zitong Wang, Siyu Zhou, Zifu Wang), project leaders Bo Zhang and Shufei Zhang, and advisors Li Cao, Lei Chen, Dahua Lin, Xing Xie, and Bowen Zhou. Interested critics: the AI Scientist-v2, Co-Scientist, and Robin teams.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

**Object level: the search itself.**

* *Target Tree.* The research question is the root; targets and subtargets record requirements and completion criteria, each subtarget has local strategies, and strategies yield subideas (partial solutions). The tree is revised as ideas expose requirements no target covers.
* *Idea Graph.* Ideas are nodes and transformations are edges, across all target branches. Five transformations create new ideas: Merge (consolidate similar approaches), Fuse (combine complementary mechanisms after checking that assumptions and interfaces fit), Diverge (open a different branch), Repair (fix a defective dependency or missing interface), and Evolve (improve while keeping the core mechanism). Each idea links to its parents and to the evaluation that motivated it.
* *Evidence State.* Experimental and computational results, literature, and expert input, each linked to its source, the ideas it bears on, and its conditions.

**Meta level: how the search is going.**

* *Cognitive State.* A selective, source-linked summary of current understanding (key findings, competing explanations, necessary assumptions, applicability conditions, open questions) plus monitoring assessments: how well key judgments are supported, which conflicts remain, and which dependencies ideas share. The paper's example: when several ideas rest on the same untested premise, their agreement is recorded as not independent support.
* *Cognitive Repertoire.* Ten methodological heuristics that organize a whole research path (evidence-driven discovery, model-driven discovery, analogical transfer, representation-driven reformulation, compositional synthesis, inverse design, constrained search and rule induction, active hypothesis testing, bottleneck-driven redesign, capability-driven frontier expansion) and Cognitive Operators, local reasoning steps grouped as Framing, Constructing, Evaluating, and Learning. General entries are specialized per domain from classic papers and refined from task feedback.

**The loop (Algorithm 1).** Monitoring updates the Cognitive State from the current search state; control picks the next task and focus; for ideation it selects repertoire entries and applies them to update the tree and graph; otherwise the harness turns the task into an execution or validation plan with tools and skills, and results enter the Evidence State. Subagents explore branches in parallel.

**Real-world use.** Sage-Taiyi proposes candidate routes and defines the variables, objectives, and constraints; experts select one; Taiyi-Optimizer (Bayesian optimization in the chemistry cases) searches the condition or design space; measurements or simulations return as evidence.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Taiyi-Bench (the authors' benchmark).** Key decisions of 50 studies first disclosed on or after 1 March 2026 (after GPT-5.6-Sol's reported cutoff) are reconstructed as DAGs, and 265 tasks disclose them progressively from level L0 (find the root problem) upward, across ten disciplines. Rediscovery metrics: Finding, Exact, Coverage, Lookahead. Open-ended metrics: Novelty, Effectiveness, Feasibility, Diversity. A literature-grounded verifier scores them with decision-level rubrics; Overall is the mean of the two group averages; Elo comes from a Bradley-Terry model of pairwise task outcomes.
* **Baselines.** Direct generation (GPT-5.6-Sol, Claude-Opus-5, Gemini-3.7-Flash); general harnesses (Codex with GPT-5.6-Sol at high and ultra effort, Claude Code with GPT-5.6-Sol or Claude-Opus-5); scientific harnesses (EvoScientist, AI Scientist-v2, Robin), reduced to their ideation stages.
* **External benchmarks.** AgentIdeaBench (40 subfields, weighted score over five rated dimensions) against AI Scientist-v2, Codex, and Claude Code run locally; IG-Arena (30 frontier questions, Population-Evolution Score) against four published reference scores.
* **Case studies.** One L2 communication-engineering trajectory (a 200 GHz receiver) and one IG-Arena quantum-computing question, used to show the tree, graph, and state updates.
* **Five collaborations.** Cobalt-catalysed carbonylation, photocatalytic allylic amination, a metamaterial band-stop filter, a cast magnesium alloy, and a centrifugal compressor.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported.**
  * *First on all eight Taiyi-Bench metrics with the same base model.* Overall 53.95 against 47.85 for AI Scientist-v2, 44.14 for Claude Code (GPT-5.6-Sol), and 39.56 for direct GPT-5.6-Sol. Rediscovery average 44.62 (next: Claude Code 40.29); Open-ended average 63.28 (next: AI Scientist-v2 57.39). Elo 1,322.5, with a 66.3% win rate against AI Scientist-v2.
  * *Largest margin on Novelty.* 70.54 against 55.20 for AI Scientist-v2; Diversity 42.33 against 39.40.
  * *Shorter generation time than the two closest systems.* 29.9 minutes per task against 39.3 for AI Scientist-v2 and 44.8 for Codex (ultra); direct generation and several other harnesses are faster and score lower.
  * *Consistent across fields and levels.* Highest Overall in all ten disciplines (leads of 1.9 to 9.4 points) and at every level L0 to L8, among the four systems plotted.
  * *AgentIdeaBench.* Weighted mean 7.195 against 6.852 for AI Scientist-v2, highest on all five dimensions.
* **Weaker or narrower than the headline.**
  * *No ablation.* Nothing is removed or swapped: there is no run without the Cognitive State, without monitoring and control, or without the repertoire. The paper says the results "are consistent with" the structured search; they cannot show that the metacognitive loop, as opposed to the ten heuristics, the idea transformations, or parallel subagents, produces the gain (reader observation).
  * *The authors' own benchmark and an LLM verifier.* Taiyi-Bench is built by the same team, and its scores come from a "literature-grounded verifier" whose agreement with human experts is described as good without a number. Novelty, the metric with the largest margin, is the most judge-dependent of the eight (reader observation).
  * *Where the Overall margin comes from.* The 6.1-point lead over AI Scientist-v2 combines +6.3 on Rediscovery and +5.9 on Open-ended, and within Open-ended, Novelty contributes +15.3 while Effectiveness is nearly tied (87.81 against 87.23). On Rediscovery the strongest baseline is not a science harness but Claude Code, 4.3 points behind (reader calculation from Table 3).
  * *Exact recovery in counts.* Most Exact scores sit on a grid of 1/215, which fits the 265 tasks minus one L0 task per paper: Sage-Taiyi 19.53% is 42 tasks, AI Scientist-v2 16.74% is 36, Claude Code (GPT-5.6-Sol) 13.95% is 30 (reader inference; two baseline values do not fit the grid). No repeated runs or intervals are reported.
  * *Baselines were cut down.* The science harnesses keep only their ideation stages, so AI Scientist-v2, Robin, and EvoScientist are not run as designed.
  * *IG-Arena is thinly reported.* The text gives only Sage-Taiyi's mean PES of 87.634 and says a figure shows four published reference scores; that figure's caption says all systems use GPT-5.5, while the rest of the paper uses GPT-5.6-Sol, and the reference scores were not rerun by the authors.
  * *The collaborations show integration, not attribution.* In each case experts chose the route and Taiyi-Optimizer searched the conditions: the carbonylation yield rose from trace to 84% over 35 evaluations in ten rounds, and the amination yield from 13% to 69% (68.7% measured) over 61 evaluations in five rounds, both by Bayesian optimization inside a space Sage-Taiyi defined. There is no control arm, such as experts alone or the optimizer on an expert-defined space, so the share due to the metacognitive architecture is unknown (reader observation). The figures state that the mechanistic tests proposed in the workflows "remain prospective".
  * *Uneven strength of the five outcomes.* Only the metamaterial case has a comparator: an expert-designed fishbone structure at 9.5% stopband and 87.4% passband transmission against 6.2% and 94.5%, in simulation. The alloy's 342 MPa mean tensile strength and the compressor's 91.5% CFD impeller efficiency (up from about 90.0% over three optimization rounds) are reported without an external baseline. The metamaterial case is the clearest example of the loop itself: optimization feedback led the system to name the fixed substrate thickness as the bottleneck, and relaxing it raised the reward from 0.253 to 0.822.
  * *Cost is not reported.* Only wall-clock time is given; token use and API cost for ten ideas per task are absent.

# Q9 Contributions

* An architecture that separates scientific search (Target Tree, Idea Graph, Evidence State) from its regulation (Cognitive State, Cognitive Repertoire) and joins them by monitoring and control.
* A catalogue of ten methodological heuristics and a set of reasoning operators with applicability conditions, specialized per domain.
* Taiyi-Bench: ideation tasks built from the decision structure of recent papers, with rediscovery at several disclosure levels and open-ended scoring.
* Benchmark results on three ideation benchmarks and five documented collaborations that connect proposals to laboratory, simulation, or CFD outcomes.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Ablate the meta level (reader suggestion).** Run the same Target Tree and Idea Graph with the Cognitive State frozen, or with random method selection, to measure what monitoring and control add.
* **Human-scored subsets.** Report the verifier's agreement with experts per metric, especially Novelty, and release the tasks so others can score their systems.
* **Controls in the wet-lab cases (reader suggestion).** Give the optimizer an expert-defined space, or compare routes proposed by experts and by the system at equal experimental budget.
* **Authors' plans.** Refine the repertoire from accumulated research experience, test its reuse across domains, and scale multi-agent collaboration under limited time and iteration budgets.
* **Reader connection.** ResearchStudio-Idea induces ideation patterns from conference outcomes and stops before experiments; Sage-Taiyi's repertoire is a hand-built counterpart drawn from cognitive science. Whether induced or theory-derived method libraries generalize better is an open comparison.
