# Summary

This paper introduces AutoScientists, a decentralized team of AI agents for long-running computational scientific experimentation. The system is designed to sustain multiple research directions, adapt to new experimental evidence, and preserve knowledge about failed attempts.

The paper frames scientific research as an iterative loop of hypothesis generation, experiment design, execution, and revision. Its central claim is that self-organizing teams can explore this loop more effectively than a single trajectory or a centrally planned agent.

# Key Ideas

* Agents interpret a shared experimental state and organize into teams around promising hypotheses.
* Proposals are critiqued before consuming experimental compute.
* Successes and failures are shared to reduce redundant exploration.
* The system targets long-running experiments where evidence changes over time.

# Experiments

* BioML-Bench: reports a mean leaderboard percentile of 74.4 across 24 tasks, improving over the strongest AI agent by 8.33 percentage points.
* GPT training optimization: reports reaching target validation bits-per-byte 1.9x faster than Autoresearch.
* ProteinGym ACE2-Spike binding: reports a 12.5 percent improvement in Spearman correlation over the prior state of the art.
* ProteinGym full sweep: reports a 6.5 percent Spearman correlation improvement across 217 assays.
