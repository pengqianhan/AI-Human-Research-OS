# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a 1280 by 720 screenshot of a 3D open-world scene and a task goal, given as text (GTAVBench) or only as an in-game objective drawn in the first screenshot (GTAVBench-AGI), followed by the screenshot after each action.
* **Output (Y):** keyboard and mouse actions as a JSON object: keys with individual hold durations of at most one second, relative mouse movement, scroll, and button events. The episode outcome is binary success, decided by the environment.
* **Constraints:** at most 300 interaction rounds for the online 3D tasks and 20 for the image-based aiming tasks; no partial credit; by default the simulation pauses while the model is thinking and advances only while an action executes.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Game benchmarks for foundation-model agents exist in three forms that the paper wants to combine: verifiable tasks in sandbox or purpose-built games (MineDojo, GameWorld, OmniGameArena), agents acting in commercial 3D worlds with OCR or human judgment of outcomes (Cradle, SIMA, Lumine), and benchmarks that score gameplay from video or offline questions (VideoGameBench, GameVerse, [GameHorizon Suite](2609.25001.md)). The claimed gap is an environment that keeps objectives from real AAA missions, verifies outcomes automatically from engine state, runs in parallel through a web server, and can generate new training instances. The comparison table marks GTAGym as the only entry with all five features.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

Two claims. First, if mission-derived tasks are rebuilt in an environment with state-based checks, the results will show which control abilities current models lack, beyond what offline or human-judged game evaluations show. Second, if a small model is trained with RL on procedurally generated instances of one task family, it will improve on held-out instances of that family and carry some gain to other families and to the native game.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Foundation-model agents in commercial games.** Cradle (screenshots and keyboard-mouse control in Red Dead Redemption 2), SIMA and SIMA 2, Lumine (end-to-end policy with human evaluation), and OctoGTA, which instruments GTA V through scripting plugins and programmatic action functions.
* **Game benchmarks.** Procgen and MineDojo for RL generalization and configurable tasks; lmgame-Bench, Orak, and VideoGameBench across many games; GameWorld (state-verifiable browser tasks), OmniGameArena (custom UE5 games), GameVerse (VLM milestone scoring on AAA games), GameHorizon-Bench (offline AAA evaluation with a verifiable online track in Minecraft).
* **Protocol lineage.** Screenshot-based computer-use evaluation in the style of OSWorld, and the Codex harness for the second branch.
* **Key people.** Jiajun Chen, Shicheng Fang, Jiahao Lu, Yuxin Wang, and Xipeng Qiu (the last two corresponding), at Shanghai Innovation Institute and Fudan University; the Hugging Face page lists the OpenMOSS organization. The 3D environment builds on the open GTA_SZ project, which the acknowledgements credit. Likely critics: authors of native-game agents such as Cradle and Lumine, who would point out that the benchmark's online track is a reconstruction and not the commercial game.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

Rebuild the missions' objectives, not the game.

1. **Task extraction.** 75 GTA V main-story missions are decomposed by hand, from walkthrough videos, into sub-tasks with a goal, a required interaction order, completion constraints, and entity behaviors. These yield 150 tasks in eleven categories.
2. **Reconstruction.** The online scenes are built in Babylon.js with task controllers that expose state. Routes are traced from the source minimap and matched to routes with comparable turn counts in the available city geometry; NPC behaviors and mechanics such as collision slowdown are predefined. The paper says the scenarios keep goals, interaction dependencies, and movement constraints while adapting spatial layout. Shooting and sniping tasks are instead built on GTA V imagery with annotated hit regions.
3. **State-based scoring.** Each task has executable success and failure predicates over engine state: destination regions with movement-mode requirements, height and depth tolerances, a following distance that must never be exceeded, an interception that requires holding the target at or below 3 m/s for 1.5 s, an evasion that requires sixty consecutive undetected seconds. An agent's own claim of completion does not count.
4. **Two branches.** GTAVBench gives a text instruction and keeps a ten-pair screenshot history. GTAVBench-AGI shows the instruction only inside the first screenshot and runs the model in the Codex harness, which keeps longer history and compacts it automatically.
5. **RL instance generation.** A template fixes scene, assets, interaction logic, evaluator, and budget; the user sets numeric ranges or candidate sets (route length, turn count, actor speed, pursuer count), and a rule-based generator samples feasible instances with a seed, sparse outcome reward, and termination signals.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Models.** GPT-6-Astra and GPT-6.1-Sol (high reasoning effort), DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017, GLM-5.3-Flash, and Kimi-K3 (max), Qwen3.8-27B (xhigh); temperature 0.7, three random seeds per configuration, under both branches.
* **Human baseline.** Three experienced GTA V players, each attempting all 150 tasks once through the task interface.
* **Analyses.** Four failure mechanisms from trajectory inspection; manual first-error annotation (Perception, Planning, Calibration, Regulation) with a ten-decision recovery criterion; harness trade-offs; action-duration cap (0.5 s, 1 s, 2 s) and evaluation without pausing, both with GPT-6.1-Sol and Codex on the 103 online tasks.
* **RL.** 50 generated on-foot instances matching the benchmark category's route-length and turn-count distribution and spatially separated from benchmark routes. Qwen3.8-27B is trained with [GRPO](../concepts/grpo.md) inside the Codex harness, LoRA rank 32 on attention query and value projections, four tasks by eight rollouts per update, terminal reward of success plus a 0.3-weighted distance-progress term for failures and a 0.1 deduction beyond 100 rounds. Evaluation covers held-out on-foot tasks, the other ten categories, and 100 native GTA V tasks from 49 source missions.

The evaluation design matches the benchmark claims. The RL design tests one task family, the easiest one for the base model to make progress on, and the native-game check is the only place the commercial game is run.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

GTAVBench: 103 online 3D tasks (Road 20, On-Foot 16, Aquatic 11, Vertical 5, Aerial 8, Indoor 8, Following 6, Interception 13, Evasion 16) and 47 image-based tasks (Shooting 24, Sniping 23). Code is linked at https://github.com/ljbro/GTAGym and the dataset at https://huggingface.co/datasets/OpenMOSS-Team/GTAVBench; this note inspected neither. The appendix reproduces the full system prompt, the AGENTS.md environment instructions, and the single `game_step` tool description used with Codex CLI 0.163.0.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

**Main results (accuracy %, mean and population standard deviation over three seeds).**

| Model | GTAVBench | GTAVBench-AGI (Codex) |
|---|---|---|
| GPT-6-Astra | 66.4 ± 3.1 | 70.9 ± 4.4 |
| GPT-6.1-Sol | 66.0 ± 0.9 | 66.9 ± 1.3 |
| GLM-5.3-Flash | 38.9 ± 1.3 | 41.8 ± 1.3 |
| DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 | 33.8 ± 4.1 | 38.2 ± 2.5 |
| Kimi-K3 | 28.4 ± 0.3 | 33.1 ± 2.2 |
| Qwen3.8-27B | 24.2 ± 0.8 | 34.2 ± 3.1 |
| Human (three players) | 89.8 ± 1.4 | |

The category profile is the main finding. Both GPT models score above 90% on on-foot, aquatic, vertical, and indoor navigation in both branches, yet aerial navigation is 0% for every model in the text branch and at most 8.3% with Codex (humans 45.8%); interception is 5.1% for both GPT models in the text branch (humans 82.1%); evasion peaks at 25.0% (humans 81.3%). The four non-GPT models are at 0% on interception in both branches and between 1.7% and 23.3% on road driving.

**First errors and recovery.** From one text-branch run per model (900 trajectories), 565 have an identified first error: 112 Perception, 96 Planning, 157 Calibration, 200 Regulation, so Calibration and Regulation make up 63.2%. Recovery within ten decisions is 65.7% for Astra and 49.3% for Sol against 21.6 to 25.6% for the other four; pooled, 179 of 565.

**Evaluation settings (GPT-6.1-Sol with Codex, 103 online tasks, 62.1% at the default).** Capping key holds at 0.5 s raises accuracy to 66.3% and roughly doubles rounds (62.6 to 121.2) and input tokens (4.32M to 11.20M per task); a 2 s cap lowers it to 60.5%, and 66.6% of actions then exceed one second. Letting the simulation run during inference drops accuracy to 39.8%, with following, interception, and evasion at zero while on-foot and indoor navigation stay at 100%.

**Reinforcement learning (Qwen3.8-27B, Codex harness, mean over three seeds).**

| Split | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| On-foot navigation (held out, 16 tasks) | 54.2 | 72.9 |
| Other ten categories | 31.8 | 34.8 |
| Overall (150 tasks) | 34.2 | 38.9 |
| Vertical / Aquatic | 46.7 / 57.6 | 73.3 / 69.7 |
| Road / Aerial / Interception | 3.3 / 0 / 0 | 3.3 / 0 / 0 |
| Shooting | 73.6 | 68.1 |
| Native GTA V (100 tasks) | 15 | 23 |

**Limits on the evidence (reader observations).**

* The online track is a Babylon.js reconstruction with adapted layouts and scripted NPCs, not GTA V. "Real AAA game tasks" describes where the objectives come from. The commercial game appears only in the 100-task transfer check, which is scored by people reviewing recordings and has no reported repeat count or per-category breakdown.
* The headline "best configuration" (Astra with Codex, 70.9 ± 4.4) is about one seed standard deviation above the other three GPT rows (66.0 to 66.9). The paper's reading that the Codex branch helps all six models is right in sign, but the gains range from 0.9 to 10.0 points and only the larger ones clear the seed spread.
* GTAVBench-AGI changes two things at once: where the instruction appears and how history is managed (Codex's long history with compaction against a ten-pair window). The harness analysis is qualitative, so the gain cannot be assigned to either change. It also records that compaction can lose the goal: Kimi-K3 replaces a rooftop objective with a driving objective after a compaction.
* All headline scores are for paused, turn-based play. The no-pause ablation shows that the dynamic categories collapse under real-time latency, so the 18.9-point gap to humans understates the gap in the game as played.
* In task counts (reader calculation from the percentages), the RL gain is about 3 more of 16 on-foot tasks and about 4 more of 134 tasks elsewhere, with about 1.3 tasks gained in each of vertical and aquatic and 1.3 lost in shooting. "Transfers to out-of-domain categories" rests on a handful of tasks in two movement categories closest to walking. The base numbers equal the main-table row, so the three seeds are evaluation seeds; the number of training runs is not stated.
* The first-error labels are manual and come from one run per model; no agreement statistic between annotators is reported.
* The human baseline is three players with one attempt per task, and humans score 45.8% on aerial tasks, so that category may be hard to control through this interface for anyone.

The benchmark result is solid in its qualitative shape: models that navigate on foot perfectly cannot fly, intercept, or evade, and the bottleneck is timing and continuous adjustment more than perception or planning. The RL and native-transfer claims are proofs of concept.

# Q9 Contributions

1. GTAGym, a web-served environment with a shared screenshot-and-action interface, parallel execution, and engine-state outcome checks for tasks derived from GTA V missions.
2. GTAVBench (150 tasks, eleven categories) and its GTAVBench-AGI branch, with results for six models and a human baseline.
3. A template-based generator of RL instances with automatic reward and termination, demonstrated with LoRA GRPO on on-foot navigation.
4. An error taxonomy and recovery analysis, and measurements of how action-duration caps and inference-time pausing change results.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Real-time evaluation as the default.** The pause ablation suggests reporting both paused and unpaused scores for dynamic categories, or scoring against a latency budget (reader suggestion).
* **Separate the harness from the instruction format.** Run text instructions inside Codex and in-image instructions with the ten-pair window to split the two changes (reader suggestion).
* **RL on the hard categories.** Train on generated interception, evasion, or driving instances, where the base model is near zero and sparse outcome reward gives little signal; the on-foot reward already adds a distance-progress term for failed rollouts.
* **Fidelity of the reconstruction.** Report per-category agreement between results in the rebuilt environment and in native GTA V for the same model, using the 100-task native set.
* **Action-duration control.** Models pick long holds when allowed (66.6% of actions above one second at the 2 s cap) and do better when forced to act in shorter steps, at more than twice the token cost. Whether a model can be trained or prompted to choose its own step length is open.
* **Project fit.** The first-error and recovery-within-ten-decisions protocol is a reusable way to compare agents on long tasks where final success alone hides whether a model can correct itself (reader connection).
