# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a high-level brief (`task.md`, sometimes with a proposal) resembling a principal investigator's instructions, supporting data and files where needed, and a sandbox with the agent's tools. No methodology or solution path is given.
* **Output (Y):** deterministic artifacts (computed quantities, simulation outputs, processed data) and a manuscript, `paper.pdf`. The benchmark's own output is a pass/fail decision and a quality score for each rollout.
* **Constraints:** a 10-hour cap per rollout, and a pass requires every verifier test and every blocker rubric. Non-blocker criteria score 0, 1, or 2 with weights from 1 to 10, and the task score is their weighted percentage if the gates pass and 0 otherwise.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Partly new. The authors' Table 1 lays out the field: reasoning benchmarks (CritPt, [FrontierScience](../concepts/frontierscience.md)-Research), workflow sub-tasks (Terminal-Bench-Science), paper reproduction with the paper supplied (PRBench, ReplicationBench, QMP-Bench), and end-to-end tasks with the solution hidden (NatureBench, PRL-Bench, ResearchClawBench, PhySciBench). They claim that FrontierPhysics alone combines contributors' own research, hidden references, graded literature and write-up, expert rubrics, deterministic verifiers, and failure analysis. The comparison is the authors' own coding, and benchmarks outside it, such as [Into the Unknown](2610.19159.md), also grade open-ended research (reader note; not cited by this paper).

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If tasks come from researchers' own projects, are curated for open-endedness and against false negatives, and are graded on both correctness and manuscript quality, then frontier agents should score far from saturation, and expert reading of failures should reveal what actually limits them.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Science benchmarks.** Textbook-level sets (OlympicArena, PHYBench, UGPhysics), [Humanity's Last Exam](../concepts/humanitys-last-exam.md) for expert-level questions, and [Agents' Last Exam](../concepts/agents-last-exam.md) for research-level problems, plus the Table 1 benchmarks above.
* **Science agents.** Tool-augmented chemistry models (Bran et al.), AI Scientist, Co-Scientist, [AlphaEvolve](../concepts/alphaevolve.md), Agent Laboratory, Virtual Lab, and CASCADE.
* **Grading.** Rubrics in [PaperBench](../concepts/paperbench.md), HealthBench, and SedarEval; FrontierCode's split into blockers and non-blockers; Ansari et al. on expert re-grading that finds broken physics evaluations.
* **Key people.** Bingran You, Wenjun Ke, Ria Rosenauer, and Bo Liu (equal contribution), and senior authors including Xiangyi Li (BenchFlow) and Gerbrand Ceder and Hartmut Haeffner (UC Berkeley), across 37 listed affiliations. Likely critics: Ansari et al. on grading validity, and authors of the end-to-end benchmarks in Table 1.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

**Tasks only their authors could grade.** A contributor must show personal authorship of a project involving at least two weeks of work, then write the brief and rubric. Criteria are meant to encode insight only someone who did the research would have. Fully AI-generated submissions are rejected, and 56 of 182 submissions were accepted.

**Curation aimed at the benchmark's own errors.** Domain reviewers (mostly PhD students and PhD holders, 47% and 37%) check that the brief does not over-specify, that the rubric has real physics content, and that at least three runs with state-of-the-art agents do not reveal false positives or negatives. A six-person admin team approves the merge.

**Gates plus a quality score.** Blockers are binary requirements for scientific validity. High non-blocker scores cannot rescue a failed gate, and a correct but badly written paper passes with a low score.

**An agent as judge.** A reviewer agent grades in a separate sandbox with access to the trajectory, code, and outputs, because checks for reward hacking, hallucinated results, and literature search need more than `paper.pdf`. All rollouts use one reviewer: GPT-5.6 Sol (xhigh) in Codex.

**Failure reading as part of the benchmark.** Each contributor writes a failure report on three GPT-6 Astra rollouts, and the admin team re-checks them and derives a 14-tag taxonomy over the 168 rollouts.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Runs.** 22 models in 26 configurations, three trials on each of 56 tasks, giving 4,364 rollouts, at highest available effort (four GPT models also at xhigh).
* **Harnesses.** Codex for GPT, Claude Code for Claude, Grok Build for Grok, and Antigravity for Gemini. DeepSeek, GLM, Kimi, MiMo, and Mistral all run in Claude Code.
* **Infrastructure.** BenchFlow CLI, Daytona sandboxes, or Docker on Azure VMs with Lambda GPU access for heavier tasks.
* **Handling failures.** Endings caused by the model count as failures, and infrastructure failures are re-run. Missing results count as failures (one slot for GPT-5.6 Luna xhigh, four for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash).
* **Statistics.** Pass@1, Pass@3, score, binomial intervals, a bootstrap over tasks, and paired sign-flip tests against the top configuration.
* **Reliability checks.** Expert false-negative audit, regrading with Claude Opus 5.5 as reviewer, and grading the authors' own manuscripts on 33 tasks.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

Results are for FrontierPhysics v0.1. The tasks are listed by name with domains (nine primary, 15 tasks with a secondary domain), and Appendix A shows one complete example. The retrieved text gives no public URL for the tasks or for the Hugging Face datasets holding trajectories, and `hf papers info` lists no repository. The runner, [BenchFlow](https://github.com/benchflow-ai/benchflow), is public; it was not opened for this note.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported.**
  * *Far from saturation.* Opus 5.5 leads at 27.4% Pass@1 (95% CI 17.9 to 36.9), passing 8 tasks three times and 25 at least once. GPT-6 Astra at max (23.2), GPT-6 Astra at xhigh (19.6), and GPT-6.1 Sol (19.6) are not statistically separable from it, and Gemini 3.8 Flash and Mistral Large 4 pass nothing.
  * *Failure modes are mostly not physics knowledge.* In the GPT-6 Astra audit: rigor 48%, intuition 34%, literature 27%, and instruction following 26% of failed rollouts, with tags overlapping. Circular self-verification is the leading example. No rollout in the audit table carries the reward-hacking tag, and two carry hallucination (reader count of Table 5).
  * *Spending does not always buy passes.* Pass rate generally rises with cost, but among these Claude models it falls as cost rises: Opus 5.5 ($26.95, 27.4%), Fable 5.1 ($32.63, 11.3%), Opus 5 ($56.77, 9.5%). GPT-6.1 Sol reaches 19.6% at $5.33, about $27 per pass against about $98 for Opus 5.5 (reader calculation).
  * *Auditing changes the number.* Nine of 168 GPT-6 Astra rollouts failed incorrectly, moving that configuration from 14.9% to 20.2%. After task fixes and re-runs its Pass@1 is 19.6%, close to the corrected value.
  * *Rubrics fit human work.* Authors' own materials score 96.2% on average on manuscript-level criteria, which recomputes from Table 11 (reader check).
* **Weaker or narrower than the headline.**
  * *The diagnosis is from one configuration.* The abstract speaks of "frontier agents", but the audit covers GPT-6 Astra (xhigh), which is not the top configuration. The authors say other models may fail differently and that subagent trajectories were too hard to read.
  * *Correction is asymmetric.* False negatives can be tagged accurately only for GPT-6 Astra. Task fixes reached all configurations, but other models' rollout-level grader errors stay in their pass rates, which matters when the top four are statistically close (reader observation). No false-positive rate is reported.
  * *One grader family.* Regrading with Opus 5.5 gives the same decision for almost all GPT-6 Astra rollouts, but that reviewer is more lenient on its own family (Table 6, whose values are not in the converted text). Every main result is graded by a GPT reviewer, and whether Sol favors GPT models is not tested in the same way (reader observation).
  * *Model and harness are confounded.* Each lab's models run in that lab's harness, and five other labs' models share Claude Code, so a gap between lab families is not purely a model gap (reader observation).
  * *"Publishable" is a threshold the authors define.* Passing means clearing gates they designed, and no manuscript was peer reviewed. The reference check covers only manuscript-level criteria, and three reference entries fail a blocker (`cpt-frustrated-chain-spectra`, `cvqkd-calibrated-detector-noise`, `phase-controlled-optical-tweezer-holography`), while one original paper scores 50% (reader reading of Table 11).
  * *Selection and incentives.* Tasks that survive curation are chosen partly by how agents fail on them, and task authors are paper authors. The paper names adversarial rubric engineering as a defect source and audits against it, but only for one configuration (reader observation).
  * *Table 5 cannot be fully re-tallied.* Tags on its 129 rollouts without a PASS label give 47%, 34%, 27%, and 26% over 143, nearly the stated shares, but the table has 39 PASS labels against 25 passes before correction, because PASS coexists with failure tags. The meaning of PASS in that table is not defined in the text (reader check).
  * *Domains are thin.* With few tasks per domain, the paper itself treats domain differences as uncertain.

# Q9 Contributions

* A 56-task physics benchmark built from contributors' own research, with deterministic verifiers, blockers, weighted rubrics, and graded manuscripts.
* Measurement of 26 configurations with cost, time, tokens, and task-clustered uncertainty.
* A 14-tag failure taxonomy from expert reading of 168 rollouts.
* Direct measurement of grading error: false negatives corrected, a second reviewer's disagreement, and scoring of human reference work.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **More open-ended evaluation (from the paper).** Capable agents should go beyond specified tasks, which needs evaluation that handles exploratory reasoning, partial progress, and unexpected findings.
* **Audit a second configuration (reader suggestion).** Applying the same reading to Opus 5.5 would test whether rigor leads there too, and would make the top comparison symmetric. It needs tooling for reading subagent trajectories.
* **Cross-family grading (reader suggestion).** Grading a shared sample with a matrix of reviewer and worker families would measure leniency in both directions.
* **Same model across harnesses (reader suggestion).** One model in Codex and Claude Code on all 56 tasks would separate the harness share of the gap.
* **Rigor as an intervention (reader question).** Would a verifier independent of the agent's own assumptions reduce circular self-verification? [Into the Unknown](2610.19159.md) finds lenient self-verification in 58 of 64 audited trajectories, so the same weakness appears under a different benchmark.
