# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a base policy P₀ (an LLM), a seed harness H₀ (the code and prompts that decide what the model sees, how its actions run, what feedback it gets, and when an episode ends), a pool D of software-engineering training tasks, each with an executable verifier giving a binary resolve signal, and a harness search budget K.
* **Output (Y):** an improved harness-policy pair (H_t, P_t) that resolves more issues on held-out benchmarks.
* **Objective:** the expected resolve rate J(H, P) over tasks in D and trajectories sampled from the pair.
* **Constraints:** in the self-improving setting no stronger model may propose harness changes, so the policy under training must also serve as the proposer; harness search and RL share the same training tasks; the backbone's sampling settings are fixed across candidate harnesses.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Both halves exist. Harness optimization holds the model fixed and searches over prompts, workflows, or agent code (Meta-Harness, Self-Harness, Task-CoEvolve, Evo2), and policy optimization holds the harness fixed and trains the model from verifiable rewards (RLVR in the Orchard recipe). The paper's argument is that each limits the other: a harness tuned for one checkpoint stops targeting the failures of the next, and RL can only learn from trajectories its fixed harness elicits. Joint methods also exist, and the paper separates itself from them on two axes (its Table 5): WHALE keeps a frozen external proposer with SFT policy updates, Evo2 trains a separate proposer for a frozen solver, and HASE puts harness edits into a joint RL action space with a self-proposing policy. Self-HPO's specific claims are that the policy just trained becomes the next harness proposer, and that the next search set is drawn from tasks the policy failed during RL, rather than from a fixed set.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If harness search and policy training alternate, with the current policy proposing harness revisions that target its own failures and the next search set drawn from tasks it could not solve during RL, then each component compensates for the other's limits. The resolve rate should then rise at every step, beat harness-only and policy-only optimization at matched per-component budgets, and give a joint gain larger than the sum of the two components' separate gains. The paper also expects the gains to carry over to languages and repositories outside its Python training set, and to show up both as reliability (pass@1) and as a wider capability frontier (pass@k).

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Harness optimization.** Prompt and program optimizers (automatic prompt optimization by Pryzant et al., DSPy, Promptbreeder, backpropagated language-model feedback by Yuksekgonul et al., GEPA), workflow optimizers (Automated Design of Agentic Systems, AFlow), and systems that revise harnesses from execution evidence: Meta-Harness, Agentic Harness Engineering, Self-Harness, [AutoSaddler](2608.23041.md), and others; continual adaptation ([Harness²](2610.14224.md)) and generalization through search controls and modular edits ([RRSI](2609.24972.md)) are cited in the same group.
* **Policy learning and self-improvement.** STaR-style filtered reasoning, iterative preference training, self-play curricula, evolving environments ([EnvHarness](2608.19880.md)), executable SWE training environments (SWE-Gym, R2E-Gym, SWE-smith), SWE-RL, and verifiable-reward RL (GRPO, DeepSeek-R1, and Orchard, whose RLVR recipe and Balanced Adaptive Rollout the P-step reuses).
* **Joint harness-policy evolution.** WHALE, HarnessForge, Evo2, HASE (harness-aware self-evolving), MetaRSI (data generation, harness editing, and training in one loop), and concurrent studies of harness transfer and multi-harness training.
* **Benchmarks.** SWE-bench Verified (500 tasks), SWE-bench Multilingual (300 tasks, nine non-Python languages), and the public set of SWE-bench Pro (642 tasks).
* **Key people.** Zhepei Wei (University of Virginia, internship at Microsoft Research), with Wenlin Yao, Baolin Peng, Qianhui Wu, Hao Cheng, and Jianfeng Gao (Microsoft Research), Danqing Wang (CMU), and Yu Meng (UVA). Peng, Yao, Wu, Cheng, and Gao are also authors of Orchard. Likely critics: the Meta-Harness and Self-Harness authors, whose methods are the harness-only baselines, and the WHALE, Evo2, and HASE authors, whose joint methods are compared only conceptually.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

**Alternate, and let the policy propose.** Round t has two steps. In the **H-step** the policy P_t is frozen and plays two roles: solver and proposer. The current pair runs on the search set S_t; the proposer, run as a coding agent with shell access to the parent harness, its trajectories, reference fixes for the search-set tasks, earlier candidates' outputs, and its own running notes, writes a revised harness. K = 6 candidates are proposed one after another, each revising the best harness so far using all evaluated harnesses and trajectories, and the best scorer on S_t becomes H_{t+1}. In the **P-step** H_{t+1} is frozen and P_t is trained with RL on all of D under it, giving P_{t+1}.

**The search set follows the policy.** S₀ is 300 random training tasks. Afterwards, S_{t+1} is the set of tasks never resolved in any rollout during the P-step, so the next H-step looks at fresh failures of the updated policy on those tasks rather than at the tasks the first harness was tuned for. The loop stops when the hard-task set stabilizes between rounds; the paper ran two rounds.

**What a harness is here.** A candidate is a Python subclass of the seed mini-SWE-agent class plus its prompt templates. It may override the agent's step to inspect state, return feedback before accepting a submission, replace the submitted patch, or intervene at any step. The harness found in the second H-step of the external-proposer run keeps the query-and-execute loop and adds four controls: a warning near the step or time limit that asks for a best-effort submission, a one-time guard that rejects an empty patch and asks the model to stage new files with `git add -N`, a one-time hold on the first non-empty submission that asks the model to check API names and signatures against the repository and its tests, and a fallback that submits the sandbox's git diff when the submitted patch does not apply.

**RL details.** GRPO with Orchard's Balanced Adaptive Rollout (BAR): eight rollouts per task, of which four are kept to form a group with a success fraction between 0.25 and 0.75, until 32 groups give a 128-trajectory batch and one optimizer update. Clipping thresholds 0.2/0.28, Adam at a constant learning rate of 10⁻⁶, no KL penalty, 50 updates per P-step.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Setup.** Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as base policy, mini-SWE-agent as seed harness, 2,484 training tasks from SWE-rebench, SWE-rebench V2, and Scale-SWE (Python only), two rounds with K = 6 candidates per H-step and 50 RL updates per P-step. RL and SFT run on one node with eight B200 GPUs.
* **Evaluation.** Resolve rate on SWE-bench Verified (in-distribution) and on SWE-bench Multilingual and SWE-bench Pro (out of distribution), averaged over three runs with standard deviations; step limit 250, temperature and top-p 0.95.
* **Baselines.** Self-improving setting: RLVR (policy only, seed harness, same RL configuration and 50 updates per P-step) and Self-Harness (harness only, base policy as proposer, same search budget). External setting with Opus 4.8: SFT on Opus 4.8's resolved trajectories (policy only), Meta-Harness with Opus 4.8 as proposer (harness only), and HPO (Opus 4.8 proposes, RL updates the policy).
* **Ablations.** On SWE-bench Verified from HPO: SFT or rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RFT) instead of RL in the P-step, a fixed search set, and the policy as proposer (which is Self-HPO).
* **Analyses.** A 3×3 cross-evaluation of H₀-H₂ against P₀-P₂; pass@k for k = 1-8 from eight attempts per task; pass@1 by task difficulty bins defined by the base model's success rate; the fraction of zero-success rollout groups during the last P-step against RLVR; a frontier-model comparison against SWE-bench Verified leaderboard numbers under mini-SWE-agent.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

No new dataset; training tasks come from public SWE-rebench, SWE-rebench V2, and Scale-SWE, following Orchard. Code is at [MSR-Orchard/Self-HPO](https://github.com/MSR-Orchard/Self-HPO) (5 stars per `hf papers info`), with a Hugging Face organization page and a project blog; none was opened for this note. The appendix gives the proposer's system prompt, a diff of the optimized harness against the seed, and a worked SWE-bench case where the evolved harness changes the outcome for the same policy.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported.**
  * *Joint beats one-sided in both settings.* Self-HPO reaches 69.5 / 59.7 / 65.2 on Verified / Multilingual / Pro (average 64.8), against 67.5 / 56.3 / 61.8 (61.9) for RLVR, 65.2 / 54.3 / 60.7 (60.1) for Self-Harness, and 61.0 / 54.2 / 58.3 (57.8) for the base. With Opus 4.8 proposing, HPO reaches 71.6 / 61.4 / 66.0 (66.3) against Meta-Harness's 69.1 / 56.5 / 64.8 (63.5).
  * *Out-of-distribution gains.* Training uses Python tasks only; on Multilingual, Self-Harness adds 0.1 points over the base and Self-HPO 5.5.
  * *Each step helps.* Along the Base-H₁-P₁-H₂-P₂ schedule the average rises at every step, while a second harness-only step lowers Self-Harness on Multilingual (55.0 to 54.3). The candidate-level curve on Verified goes 64.3 (first H-step), 67.8 (P-step), 68.6 (second H-step), 69.5 (second P-step), while continued harness-only search reaches 65.2.
  * *Ablations.* SFT in the P-step gives 67.2 and RFT 70.5 against RL's 71.6; a fixed search set gives 70.1; the self-proposer gives 69.5.
  * *Reliability and frontier.* pass@8 rises from 80.0% to 84.8%; pass@1 gains are 17.2 points on Medium, 12.9 on Easy, and 6.1 on Hard tasks, with a 2.2-point loss on tasks the base always solved. Zero-success groups fall to 6.5% over the last ten updates of the final P-step, against 11-17% over RLVR's last 50 updates.
  * *External-proposer knowledge transfers better through the harness.* Meta-Harness beats SFT on Opus 4.8 trajectories by 4.1 points on Verified and 8.9 on average, and SFT falls below the base on both out-of-distribution benchmarks.
* **Weaker or narrower than the headline.**
  * *Superadditivity mixes in co-adaptation.* The cross-evaluation (H₂ with P₀ 64.5, H₀ with P₂ 64.4, H₂ with P₂ 69.5) gives a joint gain of 8.5 points against 3.5 + 3.4 = 6.9. But P₂ was trained under H₁ and H₂ was selected for P₁, so the "individual" gains measure each component with a partner it was not tuned with, and the 1.6-point excess is close to the reported standard deviations (0.9-1.5) (reader observation).
  * *Compute is not matched across methods.* Self-HPO spends both the harness search budget and the RL budget, while each one-sided baseline spends one of them, so "joint beats either alone" is also "more total optimization beats less" (reader observation). Harness search cost is not reported.
  * *The proposer sees reference fixes.* The H-step proposer has the gold patches of its search-set tasks; these are training tasks, so this is not test leakage, but "analyzes its own failures" understates the information it uses (reader observation from Appendix D).
  * *Selection noise.* Each candidate runs once per search task and the best of six is kept; the held-out benchmarks are averaged over three runs, which guards the final numbers but not which harness was chosen.
  * *One base model, two rounds.* All results use Qwen3.5-35B-A3B; whether the self-proposer works for a smaller policy, and whether a third round still helps, is untested.
  * *Frontier comparison is cross-source.* The leaderboard numbers in its Table 4 come from the SWE-bench site under mini-SWE-agent, not from reruns, while Self-HPO's 69.5 uses an evolved harness.

# Q9 Contributions

* A harness-policy co-evolution loop in which the policy being trained is also the harness proposer, so no stronger external model is needed.
* A failure-driven search set that moves with the policy: the tasks it never solves during RL become the next harness search set.
* Evidence on three SWE-bench variants that joint optimization beats RL-only and harness-only optimization in both a self-improving and an external-proposer setting, with out-of-distribution transfer from Python-only training and gains at both pass@1 and pass@8.
* A concrete picture of what a learned harness change looks like for coding agents: runtime checks at budget exhaustion and submission (empty-patch guard, API check, patch fallback), not only prompt edits.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Measure the proposer, not only the pair (reader suggestion).** The loop's recursive claim is that a better policy is also a better proposer. Running the H-step with P₀ and with P₂ as proposer for the same solver and search set would show whether proposal quality itself improves across rounds, which the current ablation (Opus 4.8 versus the self-proposer) does not test.
* **Matched-compute baselines (reader suggestion).** Give RLVR extra updates, or Self-Harness extra candidates, equal to Self-HPO's combined budget, to separate the value of alternation from the value of more optimization.
* **Fresh validation for harness selection (reader suggestion).** [RRSI](2609.24972.md) and the [Self-evolving Agent Harness](2610.18813.md) survey both warn that harnesses selected on the tasks they were searched on can overfit. Selecting H_{t+1} on a held-out slice of hard tasks, rather than on S_t itself, would test whether Self-HPO's search set needs that guard.
* **Beyond SWE (from the paper and reader).** The setup relies on a binary executable verifier for both RL rewards and harness scoring; whether the alternation works where rewards are graded by rubrics or judges, as on the professional benchmarks [Harness²](2610.14224.md) uses, is open.
