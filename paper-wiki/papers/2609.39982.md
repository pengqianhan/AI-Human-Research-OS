# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a terminal task, a fixed action-generating model π, and a fixed harness. At step t the agent has an interaction history h_t of earlier actions and terminal observations.
* **Output (Y):** the action executed at each step, and the resulting trajectory's success (exact pass of the task's tests).
* **Core problem:** a terminal agent commits to a long sequence of stochastically sampled commands, and each one changes the environment. A poor command (a wrong package install or code edit) can block later progress even when the same model could have produced a better alternative. The authors ask when spending test-time compute *before* each action is executed improves trajectory success, and what makes it effective.
* **Constraint:** the generator's weights and the harness loop stay unchanged; the intervention sits only in the model-call wrapper, and it needs one environment instance rather than parallel copies.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Partly new. Test-time scaling by sampling and verification is established for reasoning (verifiers for math, process reward models, pairwise tournaments such as V1) and for complete agent trajectories (Best-of-N with [LLM-as-a-Verifier](2607.05391.md), sequential refinement). Per-step verification of candidate actions also has precedents: a trained critic for SWE agents (Zainullina et al., 2025) and per-step candidate verification on Terminal-Bench in LLM-as-a-Verifier. The contribution here is a controlled study across terminal tasks of *which* verification mechanism works at the action level, how much a generator's own candidates are worth under a strong verifier, whether that verifier can be distilled into the generator's own backbone, and how action scaling combines with trajectory scaling.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If the generator's samples at a step already contain useful alternatives, a verifier that can recognize them should raise trajectory success without changing the generator. The gain should therefore depend more on verifier quality and comparison format than on the number of candidates, and should add to, not replace, trajectory-level scaling.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Terminal-agent models and harnesses:** SWE-agent and OpenHands (agent interfaces), context management, automated harness optimization ([Meta-Harness](../concepts/meta-harness.md)), and model-side scaling with synthetic tasks, including TMAX (arXiv:2606.23321), the generator family used throughout.
* **Test-time scaling and reward models:** compute-optimal test-time scaling, verifiers for math word problems, process reward models, generative verifiers, and V1's pairwise tournaments.
* **Agent test-time scaling:** parallel trajectory verification (LLM-as-a-Verifier, whose probabilistic pivot tournament Mid-Harness reuses), sequential refinement ("Rethinking thinking tokens", "Scaling test-time compute for agentic coding"), and guided search with a trained critic for SWE tasks.
* **Key people:** NVIDIA. Minki Kang (internship work) is first author; Byung-Kwan Lee is project lead; Yu-Chiang Frank Wang is a co-author. Likely critics: authors of TMAX and LLM-as-a-Verifier, and anyone wanting a comparison at matched inference cost with simply raising one candidate's reasoning effort, which the paper leaves untested.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

* **Scale at the model-harness boundary.** At each step, sample N candidate actions from the same history (reasoning in `<think>` tags is generated but not shown to the verifier), deduplicate identical tool calls, drop unparsable ones, and let a verifier return one candidate; the harness executes it and the others are discarded. The integration is a few lines in the model-call wrapper.
* **Three verification mechanisms.** Listwise: one call sees all candidates and picks one. Pointwise: each candidate gets an independent score, like a process reward model. Pairwise: two candidates are compared under the same history with a rationale, two 1-10 scores, and an A/B/TIE preference. At N = 4 all six pairs are compared; at N = 8 a ring pass followed by a four-pivot stage (22-25 comparisons, LLM-as-a-Verifier's pivot tournament) feeds margin-weighted win rates.
* **Verifier distillation.** A LoRA adapter on the generator's own backbone, active only when verifying, is trained to reproduce GPT-5.6 Sol's pairwise rationale, scores, and preference: 117,631 inputs from 244 difficult TMAX-15k tasks (three TMAX-9B trajectories each), A/B-balanced, with 21 further tasks held out for analysis.
* **Decision-only variant.** The verifier emits a single A/B token, and candidates are ranked by the mean normalized A/B probability, cutting verifier decoding.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Main setting:** TerminalBench-Lite (OpenThoughts-TBLite: 100 tasks, two excluded for sandbox errors, leaving 98), TMAX-9B generator (trained from Qwen3.5-9B with RL on synthetic terminal tasks) in the Vanillux2 harness. Generator temperature 0.8, at most 64 steps, 65,536-token context; verifier thinking off, temperature 0. Three runs per task; Pass@1 is the mean success rate and Pass@3 the fraction of tasks solved at least once. An agent-timeout multiplier of three is used throughout.
* **Mechanism and width:** listwise, pointwise, and pairwise at N = 4 and 8; a first-runnable control (execute the first parsable sample, no verification); GPT-5.6 Sol as a frontier listwise verifier.
* **Cost:** parallelized output tokens (an idealized latency proxy), total verifier output tokens, and reference-priced token cost at Qwen3.5-9B OpenRouter rates.
* **Offline analysis:** agreement with the GPT-5.6 Sol teacher on 1,355 states from the 21 held-out tasks; teacher disagreements reviewed by GPT-5.6 Terra and classified into a predefined failure taxonomy.
* **Composition:** Best-of-T trajectories (T = 3, 5, 7, chosen by a TMAX trajectory verifier) and Sequential Refine (R = 1-3), with and without Mid-Harness, at 4B, 9B, and 27B.
* **Transfer:** Terminal-Bench 2.1 (89 tasks), SWE-bench Verified Mini (50 tasks), and FeatureBench-Mini (23 CPU tasks) with TMAX models; Qwen3.5-9B, Nemotron3.5 Lightning, and Nemotron3 Ultra in the Terminus-2 harness.
* **Uncertainty:** paired task-level bootstrap (100,000 resamples) 95% intervals for six base-versus-Mid-Harness comparisons; task-group breakdowns by domain, difficulty, and base-agent execution length.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported:**
  * *The generator's candidates contain useful alternatives.* With GPT-5.6 Sol as listwise verifier, Pass@1 rises from 50.00% to 64.63% (N = 4) and 68.03% (N = 8), Pass@3 from 69.39% to 80.61%. Sampling without verification does not help: the first-runnable control scores 49.66%.
  * *Verification format matters more than width under self-verification.* Zero-shot listwise moves only from 49.32% (N = 4) to 51.02% (N = 8); pointwise gives 52.72% and 52.38%; pairwise gives 54.42% and 54.76% (Pass@3 71.43%).
  * *Distillation helps without touching the generator.* The distilled pairwise verifier reaches 55.44% (N = 4) and 57.14% (N = 8), Pass@3 75.51%. Offline, it cuts the score error against the teacher from 2.59 to 1.05 and raises pairwise agreement from 59.01% to 74.58% and top-candidate agreement from 38.52% to 57.79%. Reviewed clear failures fall from 3,328 to 1,810; candidate semantics and execution feasibility account for 67.4% of the remaining ones.
  * *Action and trajectory scaling combine.* At 9B, Best-of-3 alone reaches 55.10%, with zero-shot Mid-Harness 61.22%, with distilled Mid-Harness 66.33%, using the same three environment runs. Sequential Refine plateaus (55.10%, 56.46%, 55.78% for R = 1-3) and reaches 60.20% with distilled Mid-Harness. Distilled Mid-Harness alone (57.14%) matches Best-of-5 at about one-third of its reference-priced cost, and both compositions beat Best-of-7 (59.18%) at lower reference-priced cost.
  * *Scale and transfer.* Pass@1 rises at 4B (38.78% → 41.50% zero-shot → 43.88% distilled) and 27B (71.09% → 73.13% → 76.19%). On Terminal-Bench 2.1, TMAX-9B rises from 21.72% to 27.34% (zero-shot) and Nemotron3 Ultra from 50.94% to 56.18%. FeatureBench-Mini rises from 1.45% to 7.25% (TMAX-9B) and from 17.39% to 23.19% (TMAX-27B, distilled). Zero-shot Mid-Harness improves Pass@3 and matches or improves Pass@1 in all seven transfer settings.
* **Weaker or bounded:**
  * *Small samples, wide intervals.* The main benchmark has 98 tasks with three runs each. In the authors' paired bootstrap, all six base-versus-Mid-Harness Pass@1 differences are positive, but only distilled 9B (+7.14, [+1.36, +12.93]) and distilled 27B (+5.10, [+0.34, +9.86]) exclude zero; zero-shot 9B is +4.76 with [−0.68, +10.20]. The 2-3-point differences between verification mechanisms carry no intervals, and the transfer benchmarks are smaller still (50 and 23 tasks).
  * *The frontier "upper bound" uses a different mechanism.* GPT-5.6 Sol is run only listwise for cost reasons, while the best self-verifier is pairwise, so the 68.03% result is neither a ceiling nor a like-for-like comparison. Without gold action labels, candidate coverage is inferred from trajectory success, as the authors state.
  * *Costs are proxies.* Token prices are reference rates for locally served models, and parallelized output tokens assume ideal parallelism. Pairwise verification at N = 8 makes 22-25 verifier calls per step (125.7k total verifier output tokens per run against 0.6k for listwise). Wall-clock latency under the threefold timeout is not reported.
  * *Untested alternative.* The study does not compare action scaling with giving a single candidate more reasoning effort at matched cost, which the authors list as a limitation.
  * *Cheaper variants are inconsistent.* Decision-only pairwise verification raises 9B Pass@1 (to 56.12% zero-shot, 59.18% distilled) but lowers it at 4B and 27B and at N = 4. Sampling five verifier responses per pair lowers Pass@1 from 57.14% to 53.40% at five times the verifier output.
  * *Gains are uneven across tasks.* Distilled verification improves software-engineering tasks at all sizes (+20.5 points at 27B) but lowers 27B Pass@1 on scientific-computing tasks by 10.0 points. The failure taxonomy comes from GPT-5.6 Terra's review against a teacher reference, not from executed outcomes. The retrieved text does not state an overlap check between TMAX-15k distillation tasks and TerminalBench-Lite (reader note).

# Q9 Contributions

* Mid-Harness, a drop-in model-call wrapper that turns per-step action sampling and verification into a controllable test-time scaling axis, leaving the generator and harness untouched.
* A controlled comparison of listwise, pointwise, and pairwise action verification, showing that comparison format and verifier quality, not candidate width, decide whether extra samples pay off.
* Evidence that a generator-backbone verifier distilled from a frontier verifier recovers part of the gap, with an offline analysis locating the remaining errors in judging command semantics and execution feasibility.
* Evidence that action scaling adds to Best-of-T and Sequential Refine and improves their cost-success trade-off, with cost accounting in tokens and reference prices.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **The authors' list:** train verifiers with reinforcement learning or jointly with the generator (as in V1); use world models to predict command effects; adapt candidate width and verification effort per step instead of verifying every step; test computer-use and robotics agents; compare against raising reasoning effort for one candidate at matched cost; build branching evaluations from intermediate states once environments can be cloned.
* **Ask when to verify (reader note):** the verifier errs most on later, state-dependent steps (agreement 68.13% at turns 1-4 against 54.07% at turns 17-32), where a wrong action is also most costly. A gate that spends verification only on irreversible or environment-changing commands (installs, deletions, service launches) could keep most of the gain at a fraction of the 22-25 calls per step.
* **Relevance to this repository (reader note):** the Research OS's agents act in a shell, and its `project-dispatch` flow already hands work to Claude Code or Codex sessions. Mid-Harness is a concrete pattern for a pre-execution check at the model-call boundary, complementary to permission prompts: the paper's ethics statement says action verification should complement restricted permissions, isolation, and human approval, not replace them.
