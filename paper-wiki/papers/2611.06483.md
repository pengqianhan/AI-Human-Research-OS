# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a base LLM, an RL algorithm (GRPO, GiGPO, or PPO), and interactive tasks whose verifier gives a terminal reward within a budget of at most T turns.
* **Output (Y):** a description of what training changed, measured at three levels: trajectory success across sampling breadth K and interaction depth T, action quality at matched states, and likelihood of actions and trajectories under the base and RL policies. Also a training variant, BudgetMix RL, meant to hold up across budgets.
* **Constraints:** step-level quality needs a trustworthy per-step score, so that analysis is limited to FrozenLake (exact Q* by dynamic programming) and DeepCoder-MT (unit-test pass rate of each submission).

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Partly new. Asking what RL adds beyond the base model is established for single-turn reasoning: Yue et al. use Pass@K to argue that RLVR mainly reweights solutions the base model can already reach, while ProRL reports boundary expansion. Zhai et al. introduced Pass@(K,T) for agents and showed RL can pass the base model's capability boundary. The new parts are the budget dependence at tight T, the matched-state comparison of single actions, the separate result for states just after an error, and a training fix.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If multi-turn RL mainly strengthens long-horizon execution and error recovery, not each decision, then its advantage should need a large turn budget, should not show up in matched-state action quality, and should appear after errors and later in a trajectory. If so, training across varied budgets should raise tight-budget performance and protect step quality without losing full-budget results.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Multi-turn agentic RL.** AgentGym-RL, Search-R1, ReTool, ToolRL, WebRL, and ZeroSearch extend outcome-based RL to tools and the web.
* **Credit assignment and process signals.** [GiGPO](../concepts/gigpo.md), SPA-RL, ARPO, TRACE, hindsight credit assignment, AgentPRM, and ToolPRM.
* **Understanding post-training.** Yue et al. (Pass@K), ProRL, entropy dynamics (Cui et al.), RAGEN-2, Hao et al. on collapse in multi-step tool RL, and Zhai et al. on Pass@(K,T).
* **Test-time scaling.** Shen et al. (thinking versus doing), Li et al. (test-time scaling of agents), and Timely Machine (awareness of time).
* **Key people.** Kejian Zhu (first author), Zhuoran Jin, Tianyi Men, Yupu Hao, Jiachun Li, Kang Liu, and Jun Zhao at the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences, with Shangqing Tu at Tsinghua. Likely critics: Zhai et al., whose budget sweep this extends, and credit-assignment authors, who would ask whether better process signals remove the budget dependence on realistic tasks.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

**Two axes.** Pass@(K,T) is the chance that at least one of K trajectories succeeds within T turns. Fixing K and sweeping T gives Pass@T (the paper fixes k = 8). The Pass@K estimator is 1 − C(n−c, k)/C(n, k), with k swept up to 128.

**Matched-state comparison.** Intermediate states are drawn equally from base and RL trajectories to reduce visitation bias. From each state both policies sample 16 next actions, whose quality scores are averaged. Error states are made by injecting a bad step: a random FrozenLake move that lengthens the shortest path, or a Qwen3-1.7B code edit that passes fewer unit tests. The authors call these proxies.

**Likelihood analysis.** Per-token action NLL under the base policy tests whether RL actions are locally unusual. Cumulative log-likelihood ratio (LLR) between the RL and base policies tests how much whole trajectories differ.

**BudgetMix RL.** For each task, candidate budgets are ⌈(k/10)·T_max⌉ for k = 1 to 10, and one is sampled uniformly per task group, so a GRPO group shares a budget. The total and remaining budget appear in the prompt, and a trajectory that runs out without success gets zero reward. Rewards and hyperparameters otherwise match full-budget RL. GiGPO anchors also include remaining budget, and PPO's actor and critic get the same budget conditioning.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Tasks.** FrozenLake (T_max 70), DeepCoder-MT (10), BFCL-v3 (24), and InterCode-SQL (10) in the main text, plus Terminal-Bench 1.0 (50) in the appendix, with Tmax training tasks for it and EnvFactory data for BFCL. Train and test splits are separate.
* **Models and algorithms.** Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B with GRPO, GiGPO, and PPO, at learning rate 1e-6, group size 16, and KL regularization 0.001, on VeRL. Appendix checks use InternVL3.5-8B on FrozenLake and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B on DeepCoder-MT.
* **Evaluation.** Temperature 0.6 and top-p 0.9 on identical instances, with the budget stated in the prompt for all models. Table 1 reports Pass@8 at 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of T_max. Structural upper bounds (shortest paths, annotated tool-call paths) check that small T is not simply infeasible.
* **Process signals.** Oracle Q-values on FrozenLake and a GPT-5.6-Sol judge scoring each action from 0 to 10, on FrozenLake and BFCL.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported.**
  * *Tight budgets erase or reverse the gain.* At 25% of T_max, GRPO Full is below the base model on seven of the eight model-task pairs in Table 1 and level on the eighth (reader count), for example 29.2 against 34.0 on DeepCoder-MT at 4B and 31.5 against 42.8 at 8B. At 100% it is ahead on all eight (59.0 against 48.3 and 66.5 against 56.8 on the same task). Figures show this is not just infeasibility, since structural upper bounds leave headroom.
  * *Not better per step.* RL picks the better action on 46.8% of DeepCoder-MT states and 42.8% of FrozenLake states, with mean quality no better than the base model. On DeepCoder-MT, trajectory reward rises 0.331 during training while step reward falls 0.097.
  * *Better after errors.* RL wins on 67.3% of DeepCoder-MT and 65.7% of FrozenLake error states. Continuing from the same error states it succeeds more often: 38.5% against 22.9%, and 51.3% against 38.6%. On BFCL it corrects a failed tool call on its next action 41.4% of the time against 27.2%, across 191 prefixes.
  * *Exact process signals help, but are rarely available.* Oracle Q-values and GiGPO stabilize FrozenLake across budgets. On BFCL, GiGPO and PPO behave like GRPO under tight budgets, and the appendix says the same for DeepCoder-MT and InterCode-SQL.
  * *BudgetMix gains at tight budgets.* It adds 7.5, 5.3, and 4.0 points on average at 25%, 50%, and 75%, with the largest cell +13.2 (8B, DeepCoder-MT, 25%). The averages recompute from Table 1 (reader check). Step reward stays stable or rises slightly under it.
* **Weaker or narrower than the headline.**
  * *The likelihood result may be close to automatic.* The expected log ratio of a policy against any other policy, under its own samples, is a KL divergence and cannot be negative, and it adds up over turns. So a large positive LLR for RL rollouts (about 228, against 26 for base rollouts, or 185.9 and 805.8 nats after length matching) confirms the policy moved. It does not by itself show better composition of actions. Small per-step differences multiply over a long trajectory in any case (reader observation).
  * *Step quality may undersell multi-turn strategy.* On DeepCoder-MT, quality is the unit-test pass rate of this one submission. A policy that learns to use early submissions as cheap probes under a 10-turn budget would look worse here without being worse at the task (reader inference). This would also fit BudgetMix protecting step quality, because tight budgets make probes costly. FrozenLake's Q* measure does not have this problem, and it also shows RL behind.
  * *Part of the gain could be learning to read the budget.* Full-budget models train with one T_max in the prompt and then see shorter limits for the first time at test. Table 1 cannot separate budget awareness from a change in long-horizon skill, and no arm trains on varied budgets without showing them, or the reverse (reader observation).
  * *"Parity" at full budget rests on no spreads.* BudgetMix is higher in 22 of the 24 full-budget cells, by small margins, and lower only for GiGPO on InterCode-SQL at 4B and on BFCL at 8B (reader count). Table 1 has no spreads and the text does not state a run count, so neither parity nor a small gain can be claimed firmly.
  * *Small models and easy settings.* Results come from 4B and 8B models, and step-level evidence from two tasks, one a grid world. Terminal-Bench is limited to Pass@K, Pass@T, and likelihood. Frontier-scale models are not tested, and budgets are modest (10 turns on DeepCoder-MT and InterCode-SQL, 50 on Terminal-Bench).
  * *Pass@8 is not deployment.* Table 1 takes the best of eight trajectories, which hides what a single run under a tight budget would do. The deployment risk discussed for irreversible actions is argued, not measured (reader observation).

# Q9 Contributions

* A two-axis evaluation showing that agentic RL's advantage depends on turn budget and can reverse at tight budgets.
* Matched-state evidence that RL's gains are concentrated in recovery and in later turns, not in every decision.
* A training-dynamics result in which trajectory reward rises while step reward falls.
* BudgetMix RL, a one-line change to the budget distribution that works with GRPO, GiGPO, and PPO and helps at tight budgets.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Better process signals (from the paper).** Exact step scores work on FrozenLake, and the open problem is getting comparable supervision on realistic tasks, where even a GPT-5.6-Sol judge did not provide it consistently.
* **Budget-prompt ablation (reader suggestion).** Crossing budget sampling with budget visibility would show how much of BudgetMix's gain is awareness of the limit.
* **Report Pass@(1,T) (reader suggestion).** Single-trajectory results at tight budgets are closer to deployment than Pass@8.
* **Frontier scale (reader suggestion).** Repeating the sweep on large models and on Terminal-Bench 2.x-sized budgets would show whether the reversal is a small-model effect.
* **Irreversible-action tests (reader suggestion).** The paper's main risk argument could be tested directly on a consequential tool-use benchmark such as [SafeActBench](2610.07753.md), comparing base, full-budget, and BudgetMix models.
