# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** an LLM agent's full parameter set θ, plus a distribution of long-horizon, multi-turn agentic tasks (e.g., a multi-turn Sudoku environment, ReAct-style tool-use math/DocVQA, WebArena-Lite web tasks, or constructive-optimization heuristic design) that yield only a sparse, trajectory-level scalar reward.
* **Output (Y):** updated parameters θ (and, optionally, jointly evolved external context c such as skills or prompts) that improve expected trajectory-level reward on held-out tasks from the same distribution.
* **Core problem:** reinforcement learning works for single-turn LLM fine-tuning, but extending it to long-horizon agentic reasoning exposes two compounding failures — its backpropagation-based training stack needs GPU memory far beyond inference (making full-parameter fine-tuning of larger models impractical), and sparse trajectory-level rewards become harder to assign credit for as the interaction horizon grows.
* **Scope:** proposes a training method (Agentic ESOpt) and its theoretical justification (variance analysis), evaluated across controlled and semi-realistic agentic benchmarks; it does not claim evolution strategies (ES) are a cheaper RL substitute so much as a differently-scaling paradigm that becomes preferable specifically as horizon length and model size grow.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Evolution strategies as a black-box alternative to policy-gradient RL are not new in general optimization, but the paper's stated novelty is applying full-parameter ES specifically to long-horizon multi-turn LLM agent fine-tuning, arguing this regime is where ES's properties (inference-level memory footprint, trajectory-level rather than per-step credit assignment, easy composition with prompt-space methods) become decisive rather than incidental. The paper frames this as a horizon-dependent crossover rather than a blanket claim that ES beats RL: its own controlled experiments show RL variants (GRPO, PPO) still win at short horizons, with Agentic ESOpt's advantage emerging and growing as the minimum successful horizon increases.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If a long-horizon LLM agent is fine-tuned by sampling full-parameter perturbations, evaluating the resulting agents' trajectory rewards, and applying a reward-weighted parameter update (rather than backpropagating a per-step policy gradient), then because ES attributes an entire trajectory's outcome to a single coherent parameter perturbation instead of summing action-level score terms across the horizon, its estimator variance should scale independently of horizon length H — whereas policy-gradient variance scales with H — so ES should increasingly outperform RL as H grows, become more memory-scalable to larger models, and compose more easily with prompt-space (skill/context) evolution.

# Q4 Key Solution Idea

**Core algorithm.** Agentic ESOpt samples full-parameter perturbations ε ~ N(0, I) around the current parameters θ, evaluates the resulting perturbed agents to obtain scalar trajectory rewards, and applies a reward-weighted update θ_{t+1} = θ_t + (α/G) Σ R̂ᵢεᵢ over a population of size G — storing only random noise seeds and using in-place arithmetic, which keeps memory at inference level rather than requiring backpropagation's activation/gradient storage.

**Cosine-decayed perturbation radius.** The perturbation scale σ is decayed on a cosine schedule across training steps: larger σ early gives broader exploration (and doubles as regularization), while σ shrinks later for finer exploitation; for train-time optimization a nonzero floor σ_T preserves regularization, while for test-time compute σ is decayed fully to zero for an unbiased final output.

**Prompt-parameter co-evolution.** Because the ES update is a lightweight, black-box feedback interface, it composes with prompt-space methods: the paper alternates optimizing parameters θ and external context c (skills, prompts), rather than treating the policy's parametric and non-parametric parts as separately optimized.

**Theoretical justification.** A variance analysis argues policy-gradient methods accumulate per-step action-score terms across H timesteps, so estimator variance scales with H, whereas Agentic ESOpt assigns each full trajectory's reward to one parameter perturbation ε/σ without horizon-dependent summation — predicting an increasing relative ES advantage as H grows, which the paper says is directly validated by a controlled Sudoku experiment.

# Q5 Experiment Design

Four evaluation settings, escalating in realism and scale:

* **Controlled long-horizon Sudoku:** multi-turn Sudoku with a tunable minimum successful horizon H*, comparing Agentic ESOpt against GRPO (best-of-sweep) and PPO at H*=5, 10, and 15, plus GPU memory measurement (Agentic ESOpt vs. GRPO).
* **ReAct-style tool-use (Qwen3.5-4B):** math reasoning (DAPO, Mean@4) and DocVQA (Mean@4 accuracy), comparing Agentic ESOpt against a frozen base model and Agentic GRPO.
* **WebArena-Lite (Qwen3.5-27B):** full-parameter optimization of a 27B model — reported as previously infeasible for RL on 4× H100 80GB GPUs — with and without a "Trace2Skill" skill-composition baseline, plus a per-category (GitLab/OSS/Map) breakdown.
* **Test-time automatic heuristic design:** constructive optimization problems (TSP, KP, ASP) and ACO-style heuristics, combining Agentic ESOpt with Sample/EoH baselines across 36 (task, setting) combinations.
* **Population-scaling probe:** doubling the ES population from G=8 to G=16 on 15-turn Sudoku, compared between Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.5-9B.

# Q6 Experiments and Evidence

* **Sudoku crossover:** at H*=5, PPO leads (90.63% vs. Agentic ESOpt 89.58%, GRPO 85.42%); at H*=10 GRPO leads (67.71% vs. ESOpt 62.50%, PPO 56.25%); at H*=15 Agentic ESOpt leads (53.13% vs. GRPO 40.63%, PPO collapsing to 0.00%, attributed to critic failure under sparse terminal rewards) — a 12.5-point ESOpt advantage over GRPO at the longest horizon, read as validating the horizon-dependent variance prediction. GPU memory: 8.41GB for Agentic ESOpt vs. 58.88GB for GRPO (an 85.7% reduction).
* **Math/DocVQA (Qwen3.5-4B):** DAPO math accuracy rises from a 63.0% base to 76.8% under Agentic ESOpt (+13.8 points) vs. 68.8% under Agentic GRPO; DocVQA rises from 40.3% base to 52.5% under Agentic ESOpt (+12.3 points) vs. 48.0% under Agentic GRPO. Average improvement over base: 13.7 points for ESOpt vs. 8.3 for GRPO, with Pass@K reportedly preserved across benchmarks.
* **WebArena-Lite (Qwen3.5-27B):** frozen-model baseline 29.47% → Agentic ESOpt + No Skill 36.16% (+6.69 points); Trace2Skill baseline 33.94% → Agentic ESOpt + Trace2Skill 36.36% (+2.42 points); category gains of +8.33 (GitLab), +9.42 (OSS), +5.96 (Map) points.
* **Automatic heuristic design:** 21/24 constructive-task comparisons improved (87.5%); 28/36 settings improved overall including ACO-style tasks (77.8%); one worked example (TSP, N=20, budget 1000) shows EoH alone at an optimality gap of 4.2481 vs. 4.2170 with Agentic ESOpt added (a 7.83% relative gap reduction).
* **Population scaling:** doubling population from G=8 to 16 on 15-turn Sudoku improved 4B final success by +677%, but produced 0% change for the 9B model — read as preliminary evidence that stronger models may need smaller populations, though this is explicitly a small, single-comparison probe.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

The reported numbers span four increasingly realistic settings and are directionally consistent with the paper's central claim — a widening ESOpt advantage as horizon length and model scale grow, anchored by the clean Sudoku crossover (RL wins short-horizon, ESOpt wins long-horizon, PPO collapses under sparse rewards) and a large, concrete GPU-memory reduction (85.7%). The WebArena-Lite result is the strongest scalability evidence (enabling full-parameter tuning of a 27B model where the paper says RL was infeasible on the stated hardware), though the retrieved material does not specify whether that RL-infeasibility claim was independently tested by the authors or is a stated assumption. The 28/36 heuristic-design figure (77.8% success rate) is reported at the level of "improved vs. not" per setting rather than with per-setting effect sizes, so it is unclear from the retrieved summary how large the losing 8/36 gaps were. The population-scaling comparison (G=8 vs. 16, one horizon, two model sizes) is explicitly a small probe and should not be read as an established scaling law. Most importantly, this note's Q4/Q5/Q6 experimental detail was retrieved through an AI-summarized secondary fetch of the paper's HTML rendering rather than the primary source text (the HF CLI's direct paper-read command failed for this ID, returning an unrelated figure fragment), so numeric values here carry a materially higher risk of transcription or summarization error than in this wiki's other notes and warrant a primary-source check before being relied on for citation.

# Q9 Contributions

1. Agentic ESOpt: a full-parameter evolution-strategies fine-tuning method for long-horizon LLM agents that runs at inference-level GPU memory (no backpropagation), with a cosine-decayed perturbation-radius schedule balancing exploration and exploitation across training.
2. A variance-based theoretical argument for why ES's advantage over policy-gradient RL should grow with task horizon length, because ES attributes trajectory reward to one coherent parameter perturbation rather than summing per-step terms.
3. Empirical validation across four settings of increasing realism (controlled Sudoku, ReAct math/DocVQA tool use, WebArena-Lite web agents, and automatic heuristic design), including a demonstrated full-parameter fine-tune of a 27B model and a lightweight mechanism for composing parameter-level ES updates with prompt-space skill/context evolution.

# Q10 Next Steps

* The paper's own stated limitations: new hyperparameters introduced by the cosine schedule (σ₀, σ_T); degraded practicality in settings with slow/expensive environment evaluation (since ES needs many rollouts per update); unclear behavior under continual learning; and limited experiments on frontier-scale LLMs.
* Open questions the retrieved material leaves unresolved: how the population-size vs. model-size relationship generalizes beyond the single 4B-vs-9B, G=8-vs-16 probe; whether the reported RL-infeasibility claim on WebArena-Lite (27B on 4×H100) reflects a hard memory wall or an untuned RL baseline; and how Agentic ESOpt's variance-reduction argument interacts with reward shaping or intermediate-step supervision, which the paper does not evaluate.
* **Research-OS fit:** this is the wiki's first paper specifically about the *weight-level* optimization algorithm used to fine-tune long-horizon agents (as opposed to prompt/skill/memory-level adaptation tracked under this wiki's Agent self-evolution topic), motivating the new [Agent fine-tuning](../topics/agent-fine-tuning.md) topic; worth revisiting once a primary-source read confirms the numbers above.
