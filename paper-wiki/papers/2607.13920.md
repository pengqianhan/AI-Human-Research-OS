# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a search agent (a model plus its harness), a question with dataset-provided supporting evidence, and a scenario that sets the probability that each returned document is trustworthy-looking (T), relevant to the query (R), and factually correct (F).
* **Output (Y):** the agent's final-answer outcome in one of five classes (correct, incorrect, abstention, tool budget exceeded, missing answer tag), plus aggregate scores that account for how reliable the evidence was and what the run cost.
* **Constraints:** each search call returns one generated document; one dimension is degraded at a time while the others stay at 100%; at most 100 tool calls per question.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Robustness of retrieval-augmented models to bad evidence is a known topic, and recent work perturbs tool outputs or poisons search results as attacks. The paper's position is that such failures are observed after the fact in fixed corpora and are hard to reproduce, because different agents issue different queries. Generating the document at query time, under labels the experimenter controls, makes the degradation rate an independent variable for an active search agent. That, and scoring abstention by evidence reliability, are the new parts. The framework is offered as a laboratory, not a replacement benchmark.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If evidence quality is degraded along one controlled axis, then agents that look similar on final-answer accuracy under clean retrieval will separate by how they fail (confident wrong answers, abstention, endless search, broken output format), and these differences will track how the agent was trained. A secondary hypothesis is about datasets: a benchmark answerable from parametric knowledge will stay partly correct when all evidence is false.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Search agents.** ReAct and IRCoT (prompted); [Search-R1](../concepts/search-r1.md), ZeroSearch (which already uses a fine-tuned model to generate documents, for training), and DR-Tulu (trained).
* **Search benchmarks.** HotpotQA, BrowseComp, and [BrowseComp-Plus](../concepts/browsecomp-plus.md), whose fixed corpus separates retriever from model.
* **Adversarial environments.** AgentNoiseBench, SafeSearch, SearchGEO, and WARP (poisoned user-generated pages).
* **Beyond accuracy.** Failure taxonomies, trajectory analysis (TRACE), and the Tokens Per Correctness metric of Xie et al.
* **Knowledge conflict.** DRAGged into Conflicts and TRACK, on static RAG or in-context reasoning.
* **Key people.** Ismaël Rousseau and Géraldine Damnati (Orange Research, Lannion) and Frédéric Béchet (Aix-Marseille University, CNRS, LIS); Rousseau is listed under both. SpiqaDR, one of the evaluated agents, is their own model. Likely critics: the Search-R1, AutoRefine, ASearcher, and DR-Tulu authors, whose agents are run outside their native retrieval setting.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Intercept the search call.** For each call the simulator samples one binary label per dimension from the scenario's probabilities and has an LLM (Gemma-4-31B) write one document of about a Wikipedia paragraph, with title and URL, addressed to the agent's actual query.
2. **Three controls.** Factual documents are grounded in the dataset's gold evidence; non-factual ones are written with severe errors. Untrustworthy documents get a domain sampled from the OpenSources list of misleading sites and a matching tabloid style; trustworthiness is about appearance and is independent of factuality. Irrelevant documents are grounded documents written for a different question from the same dataset.
3. **Five outcomes.** Budget exhaustion and missing answer tags are detected mechanically; GPT-4.1 classifies the rest as correct, incorrect, or abstention.
4. **RAS.** Reliability ρ is the product of the realized shares of trustworthy, relevant, and factual documents in the run. A correct answer scores 1, an incorrect or malformed one 0, and abstention or budget exhaustion 1 − ρ. An agent that always abstains therefore scores 1 at ρ = 0 and 0 under fully reliable evidence.
5. **CoPRAS.** Total cost (generated tokens + 0.25 × input tokens + 500 × search calls, from Xie et al.) divided by total RAS, the reliability-aware counterpart of Tokens Per Correctness.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Data.** 200 random questions each from HotpotQA and BrowseComp-Plus.
* **Scenarios.** Each axis at 0%, 50%, and 100% with the other two at 100%, seven scenarios in all.
* **Systems (12).** gpt-5-nano and gpt-5-mini at low, medium, and high reasoning effort; SpiqaDR (3B), Search-R1 (7B), AutoRefine (3B), ASearcher (7B, local-RAG variant), DR-Tulu (8B), and DR-Tulu with its reasoning blocks closed immediately. Documents are formatted to each harness's conventions.
* **Scale.** 12 × 2 × 200 × 7 = 33,600 traces.
* **Checks.** Human annotation of 50 generated documents by three annotators; a direct-inference baseline with no search; recomputation of all metrics under smaller tool budgets; exploratory analysis of query operators and of whether reasoning steps voice concerns about document quality (100 sampled steps per system, scenario, and dataset, LLM-judged).

One factor at a time keeps the results readable but means combined degradations, the realistic case, are not measured.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

HotpotQA and BrowseComp-Plus supply questions and the gold evidence that grounds factual documents. Generated documents average 102.7 and 106.4 words. The environment's code is promised "after peer-review" and no link is given; SpiqaDR's URL is likewise withheld. Prompts for generation and judging are in the appendix. The authors state the generated documents are not meant to be distributed.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

**Per-axis behavior.**

* *Trustworthiness.* Search-R1 stays between 78% and 80% correct at every level; SpiqaDR, AutoRefine, and ASearcher also barely change. DR-Tulu's correct rate falls from 59.8% to 40.9% as its no-answer rate rises from 10.8% to 34.6%. GPT agents avoid answering more often: gpt-5-mini (low) abstains 22.5% of the time against 14.0%, and gpt-5-mini (high) exhausts the budget in 62.7% of runs against 32.0%.
* *Relevance.* Correctness falls for all systems. Search-R1's incorrect rate goes from 19.5% to 79.2%; AutoRefine's missing-tag rate from 18.2% to 61.9%.
* *Factuality.* SpiqaDR abstains in 15.2% of runs at 0% factuality against 38.0% at 0% relevance; DR-Tulu is incorrect in 59.1% against 33.8%. The paper's reading is that relevant-but-false documents are harder to reject than irrelevant ones.

**Datasets.** At 0% factuality, average correctness is 33.75% on HotpotQA and 0.33% on BrowseComp-Plus; gpt-5-mini (medium) reaches 60% on HotpotQA. With no search tool at all, the same systems score 0.19 to 0.69 on HotpotQA and 0.00 to 0.02 on BrowseComp-Plus. The paper's recommendation is not to evaluate search agents on HotpotQA without a direct-inference baseline.

**Generator check.** Human labels agree with intended labels at 96.4% for trustworthiness and 85.5% for factuality (inter-annotator κ 0.86 and 0.64).

**Budget.** Correctness rankings stabilize only after about 50 tool calls; RAS rankings are already informative at about 10.

**Limits on the evidence (reader observations).**

* RAS gives budget exhaustion the same credit as explicit abstention. gpt-5-mini (high) has the highest RAS at 0% (0.96) with the lowest GPT correctness (0.27) and the highest cost (55.6k), largely by searching until the limit. The appendix acknowledges the choice and suggests a lower weight as an alternative; CoPRAS penalizes it through cost.
* The trustworthiness term treats declining a tabloid-styled but correct document as acceptable. That is a stated epistemic position, and it means RAS at 0% trustworthiness rewards not answering questions whose evidence was in fact correct.
* The factuality confusion matrix shows 13 of 69 annotations of documents labeled factual were judged not factual, so the "100% factual" condition contains some errors in annotators' eyes. Its cells give 86 of 100 agreements, while the table reports 85.5%.
* The dataset-level table (Table 11) gives three different values for the fully reliable setting (HotpotQA correct 55.45, 63.25, and 65.48 under the Trust, Relevance, and Factuality rows), though by design 100% on any axis is the same scenario, and its Trust row has higher correctness at 0% than at 100%, opposite to the per-system text. The paper does not explain this; the Trust columns may be swapped or the rows may come from different run sets. Per-system claims above come from the main text.
* The main text gives average RAS at 0% as 0.61 and CoPRAS as 36.8k where its table has 0.60 and 36.9k.
* ASearcher scores 0.17 with fully reliable evidence, so its robustness numbers mostly describe a harness mismatch (local-RAG variant, frequent missing tags), not a reaction to degradation.
* The link from training method to brittleness ("narrow RL-based rewards tend to produce brittle models") compares 3B to 8B RL-trained open models with GPT-5 variants, so training method, model scale, and vendor post-training are confounded. The appendix's Qwen2.5 base models (0.5B to 32B, no RL) show RAS at 0% rising with size to 0.66 or 0.67 at 32B, which fits a scale explanation at least as well.
* No per-cell intervals are shown in the main table; outcome intervals are in an appendix this note did not check cell by cell. With 200 questions per dataset a rate near 50% has a 95% half-width of about 7 points (reader calculation).
* One document per call removes the within-result-page conflict that real search produces, as the limitations section says.

The qualitative finding, that agents with similar clean accuracy fail in different ways under degraded evidence, is clear in the outcome distributions. RAS rankings depend on scoring choices the authors label as choices. The causal reading about RL training is not established.

# Q9 Contributions

1. A simulation environment that makes evidence trustworthiness, relevance, and factuality controllable per search call, for any agent with a single search tool.
2. A five-way outcome classification and two aggregate metrics (RAS, CoPRAS) that credit not answering in proportion to how unreliable the evidence was.
3. A 12-system comparison showing distinct failure styles: context-following in RL-trained open agents, abstention or prolonged search in GPT-5 agents, format collapse in DR-Tulu and AutoRefine.
4. A measurement that HotpotQA is substantially answerable without evidence by current models, with a direct-inference baseline to quantify it.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Limits the paper states.** One document per call; single-loop agents with one tool; independent axes; synthetic documents cleaner than the web; a need for gold evidence to ground factual documents.
* **Combined degradation.** Low trust with high factuality against high trust with low factuality would test whether agents use credibility cues at all or only content consistency (reader suggestion; the pipeline already samples labels independently).
* **Separate the confound.** Run the same base model with and without search RL under the simulator. The paper has Qwen2.5-7B and the Qwen2.5-7B-based Search-R1 in different tables; putting them side by side would test the RL-brittleness claim directly (reader suggestion).
* **Training signal.** The authors propose RAS and the document labels as a reliability-aware reward. The always-abstain score of 1 at ρ = 0 means such a reward needs mixed scenarios to avoid teaching refusal.
* **Process measures.** The query-operator and verbalization analyses are exploratory; verbalizing a concern did not clearly improve answers, and GPT agents expose only reasoning summaries.
* **Harder harnesses.** Agents with reranking, summarization, or sub-agents may filter bad documents before the policy sees them.
