# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a small student agent (Qwen3-4B or Qwen3-8B); a teacher agent that can only be sampled from, with no token probabilities and possibly a different tokenizer (Qwen3.7-Flash or DeepSeek-V4-Flash; Qwen3-32B in the τ² comparisons); executable multi-turn tool-use training tasks ([Agent World Model](../concepts/agent-world-model.md) and [EnvScaler](../concepts/envscaler.md)); and a semantic matcher that decides whether two responses take the same action.
* **Output (Y):** a student whose next tool call or user-facing message agrees with what the teacher would do *at the states the student itself reaches*, evaluated alone (greedy decoding, no teacher) on benchmarks it never trained on.
* **Unit of supervision:** a semantic action, meaning a tool name with its arguments or the intent and factual content of a message. The same action may be worded or reasoned about differently; the student's own reasoning tokens are trained only through the action's reward, never toward the teacher's text.
* **Constraints:** generation-only teacher access; no outcome reward in the loss; one immediate action per teacher sample.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Agent distillation is an established problem; the contribution is the unit and the interface. The paper places itself against three lines:

* **Token-level distillation.** Offline sequence distillation imitates teacher sequences at the teacher's states and can suffer state-distribution mismatch (the DAgger argument). Token-level [on-policy distillation](../concepts/on-policy-distillation.md) (GKD, MiniLLM) fixes the mismatch but needs teacher log-probabilities and compatible tokenization; cross-tokenizer OPD needs an explicit alignment. Privileged self-distillation (OPSD, SDPO, privileged-information distillation) adds context to a same-weights teacher but stays token-level.
* **Black-box and semantic distillation.** A discriminator reward (GAD), rubrics from teacher-student contrasts (ROPD), verbal teacher scores (OVD), reasoning chunks compared against teacher continuations (OmniOPD), trajectory-wide tool-set agreement plus correctness (MENTOR), and Action Semantics Learning, which derives a semantic-agreement policy gradient for app agents and compares UI transitions with a world model. ActionOPD's stated difference: it matches the *immediate* tool call or message against teacher samples at the same state, and needs no reasoning-text matching, complete reference trajectories, or terminal rewards. Its matcher is told to judge agreement, not correctness.
* **Interactive agent learning.** DAgger, SCoRe (local teacher corrections), Guided-OPD and ATOD (annealed teacher turns or OPD-RL mixing), and GiGPO (same-state grouping across trajectories with return-based advantages). ActionOPD scores same-state candidates directly against a shared teacher sample set, without estimating returns.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If a student is rewarded, at states it reaches itself, for producing an action that means the same as an action sampled from a black-box teacher, then (1) it will improve on tool-use benchmarks outside its training environments, (2) gains will grow with student size and teacher strength, including across model families, and (3) it will match token-level OPD and offline distillation without needing teacher log-probabilities.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

**Objective.** Maximize the expected probability that the student produces the teacher's action at student-visited states: E over states s from the student's rollouts and teacher actions a^T of π_θ(a^T | s). Writing that probability as the total mass of student responses that realize a^T and exchanging expectations turns it into an expected-reward objective whose gradient is REINFORCE with semantic agreement as the reward. A single action-level reward therefore weights every token of the student's response, reasoning included. The appendix notes two properties: the objective has no inverse-likelihood factor (unlike expected log-likelihood), and it need not preserve teacher modes; with teacher probabilities 0.7 and 0.3 on two actions, a student putting all its mass on the first maximizes it.

**Reward.** At each state the teacher is asked for K = 3 responses (two retries each for parser- or schema-invalid output). The matcher m(y, y^T) compares action content, not reasoning: tools must have the same name; arguments are canonicalized (key order ignored, integral floats normalized, booleans kept distinct from numbers) and remaining differences go to a frozen LLM with the tool schema, both argument objects, public context, and tool-implementation evidence; messages pass on a normalized exact match or else an LLM compares intent and factual content. Ambiguous judgments count as no match; matcher failures mean no supervision rather than a negative label. With c the number of teacher samples a candidate matches, the reward is −1 for an invalid action, 0 for a valid non-match, and 1 + λ(c − 1)/(K − 1) otherwise; with λ = 0.5 and teacher samples [A, A, B], a response matching A earns 1.25 and one matching B earns 1.

**State-group rollout.** At each visited state the frozen old policy samples N = 4 candidates, all scored against the same teacher set. Only the highest-reward candidate is executed (ties broken uniformly), so each task produces one teacher-guided trajectory; every candidate in a retained group is trained on. The authors note this introduces some off-policy state visitation.

**Update.** Groups without supervision or with equal rewards are dropped. Within a group, rewards are centered on the group mean and divided by one standard deviation shared across the batch; the advantage is broadcast to every generated token and optimized with a PPO clipped surrogate (clip 0.2, dual clip 3), learning rate 10⁻⁶, one PPO epoch, minibatch 32, no KL penalty, no entropy bonus, no outcome reward. The authors state these updates are not unbiased estimates of the ideal gradient after normalization, masking, and clipping.

**Self-ActionOPD.** Teacher and student share the current Qwen3-4B weights and are sampled independently. The teacher additionally sees action guidance and privileged task information: reviewed summaries of the simulated user's private goals and constraints, reference tool names and arguments, target states, and required messages taken from the evaluation criteria. The student sees only public context and is evaluated that way.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Transfer study (Table 1).** A 2 × 2 design: Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B students, Qwen3.7-Flash and DeepSeek-V4-Flash teachers. 100 steps of 64 task starts (59 AWM, 5 EnvScaler) drawn from a 6,697-task pool: 5,942 AWM tasks from 794 environments and 755 EnvScaler tasks from 49, kept from 10,000 and 2,550 source tasks by a context-length cut (AWM only), deterministic integrity audits, and static feasibility screening with DeepSeek-V4-Flash (no expert rollout needed). DeepSeek-V4-Flash also serves as matcher and EnvScaler user simulator.
* **Evaluation.** [BFCL](../concepts/bfcl.md) multi-turn (800 tasks, one trial), [ToolSandbox](../concepts/toolsandbox.md) (490 local scenarios, four trials, similarity score), [AppWorld](../concepts/appworld.md) Normal (168) and Challenge (417) task and scenario goal completion, and [τ²-Bench](../concepts/tau2-bench.md) Airline, Retail, and Telecom (50, 114, 114 tasks) as pass¹ and pass⁴ over four trials, with a Qwen3.5-9B user and natural-language assertions disabled. The headline average weights the four benchmarks equally (AppWorld by mean TGC, τ² by mean pass¹). Teachers are evaluated directly for reference.
* **Distillation comparison (Table 2).** Qwen3-4B student, Qwen3-32B teacher, the official 178 τ² training tasks and 100 test tasks, 24 task starts per step, evaluated at step 50. KD fine-tunes on all tokens of successful teacher trajectories (four per task start); OPD supervises the student's own trajectories with sampled-token reverse KL using teacher log-probabilities.
* **Ablations (Table 3).** Validity reward (+1 valid, −1 invalid; semantic scores kept only for choosing which candidate to execute), binary reward (λ = 0), and random commit (execute a uniformly chosen candidate).
* **Other.** Three selected before/after cases (one per τ² domain) and Self-ActionOPD at step 50 (Table 5). Each configuration is trained once; repeated trials measure interaction variance, not training variance.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported.**
  * *Every configuration improves the four-benchmark average.* Qwen3-4B: 30.8 to 35.9 (+5.1, Qwen3.7-Flash teacher) and 37.7 (+6.9, DeepSeek-V4-Flash). Qwen3-8B: 35.6 to 39.0 (+3.3) and 40.0 (+4.4). BFCL (4B: 34.3 to 42.3 and 44.1), ToolSandbox (55.5 to 61.8 and 63.3), and AppWorld Normal TGC (9.5 to 14.3 and 13.1) rise in all four configurations.
  * *Cross-family distillation works through the action interface.* A DeepSeek teacher trains Qwen students with no tokenizer alignment, and it gives the higher student averages at both sizes.
  * *Semantic reward beats validity reward.* On τ² test pass¹, ActionOPD averages 40.0 against 30.8 for the validity reward, 36.9 for the binary reward, and 37.5 for random commit (base 29.2).
  * *Privileged self-distillation helps without the privileged input at test time.* Self-ActionOPD raises τ² test pass¹/pass⁴ from 29.2/7.5 to 34.0/10.0, with pass¹ higher in all three domains.
* **Weaker or narrower than the headline.**
  * *"Scales with student size" is two sizes, and the gain shrinks.* The 8B student ends higher because it starts higher (35.6 against 30.8); its gains (+3.3, +4.4) are smaller than the 4B student's (+5.1, +6.9) (reader observation from Table 1).
  * *"Scales with teacher capability" is two teachers, and the students stay far below both.* The teachers' direct averages differ by 5.6 points (71.2 against 65.6), and all of that difference comes from AppWorld (TGC 84.5 against 70.2 on Normal, 74.8 against 36.5 on Challenge); Qwen3.7-Flash is ahead on BFCL, ToolSandbox, and τ² Airline and Retail (reader arithmetic from Table 1). The students' averages differ by 1.8 (4B) and 1.0 (8B). The best student averages 40.0 against its teacher's 71.2.
  * *τ² transfer is mixed.* Some cells fall below the base: 4B with Qwen3.7-Flash on Airline pass¹ (33.5 against 34.5), 8B with Qwen3.7-Flash on Retail (37.7 against 38.2 pass¹, 10.5 against 14.0 pass⁴), 8B with DeepSeek on Telecom pass⁴ (12.3 against 14.9).
  * *"Competitive with OPD and KD" is level on pass¹ and behind on pass⁴.* Test pass¹ is 40.0 against 39.6 (OPD) and 37.5 (KD); test pass⁴ is 14.2 against 17.5 and 19.2, and on Telecom test pass⁴ 15.0 against 22.5 and 25.0. The authors say the results "do not establish uniform superiority". Collection budgets are not equalized (Appendix B.4: equal task starts do not equalize examples, tokens, teacher queries, or compute), each method is trained once, and the 0.4-point pass¹ gap is small for 100 test tasks with four trials (reader note). The teacher here is same-family, so the comparison does not exercise the tokenizer-free advantage.
  * *Ablations are not uniform.* Random commit beats full ActionOPD on Retail test pass¹ (40.6 against 39.4) and Telecom test pass¹ (44.4 against 40.6), and the binary reward ties it on test pass⁴ (14.2). The validity-reward variant also masks all-valid groups, so it changes how many groups are trainable as well as the reward.
  * *The matcher is unaudited.* No human check of the LLM matcher's judgments is reported; the authors list false positives and negatives as a limitation. In the transfer runs one model (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) is a teacher, the matcher, the task screener, and the user simulator.
  * *τ² numbers are protocol-specific.* A Qwen3.5-9B user and disabled natural-language assertions make them not comparable with leaderboard or other papers' τ² numbers.
  * *Selected cases and in-domain splits.* The three case studies were chosen as base-failure, trained-success pairs. Train-split τ² scores measure in-domain fit, and Self-ActionOPD's teacher sees reference actions and target states derived from the evaluation criteria of the training tasks.

# Q9 Contributions

* An action-level on-policy distillation objective with its REINFORCE derivation, which separates what the teacher decides from how the student words it.
* A generation-only teacher interface: no log-probabilities, tokenizer alignment, or shared model family.
* State-group rollout: score several student candidates at the same state against one teacher sample set, execute the best, train on all.
* A shaped agreement reward (invalid penalty, first-match unit reward, smaller bonus for further matches) with its induced objective worked out, and a privileged self-distillation variant at the action level.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **From the authors.** Agreement is not correctness, so teacher errors become targets; matcher errors misassign reward; with fixed K and N few candidates may match when plausible actions are dispersed over long horizons, and larger K and N are untested.
* **Repeat with seeds and matched compute (reader suggestion).** Several training seeds per configuration, and KD and OPD baselines matched on teacher queries or tokens, would show whether the τ² ranking holds.
* **Audit the matcher (reader suggestion).** Human labels on a sample of tool-argument and message judgments, split by tool and domain, would bound how much reward is misassigned.
* **Add outcome reward (reader suggestion).** The loss deliberately has none; combining agreement with environment success would test whether agreement mainly helps when the teacher is right, given the mode-seeking property shown in Appendix A.1.
* **Cross-tokenizer baseline (reader suggestion).** Compare against cross-tokenizer OPD on the DeepSeek-to-Qwen pair, the setting where ActionOPD's interface advantage is claimed.
