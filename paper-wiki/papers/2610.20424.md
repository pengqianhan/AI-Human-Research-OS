# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** one customer request in a reconstructed payments environment. The task fixes a customer objective, an initial database state, scheduled events, and a customer scenario. The agent has nine always-available tools through which it searches 789 knowledge documents, queries records, and discovers and invokes 601 business capabilities; an LLM user simulator holds the customer's private facts and materials and performs customer-side actions such as approvals.
* **Output (Y):** a full execution history τ = (s0, e1, s1, ..., eT, sT) of tool executions, environment events, and business states, ending in a final state.
* **What is measured:** strict success, Success = G ∧ P ∧ F. G checks the requested outcomes and permitted terminal conditions, P checks requirements that govern execution (approval that covers the executed terms and comes before the operation, continuous constraints such as a minimum reserve), and F checks protected objects and prohibited side effects. All checks are deterministic code over states, ledgers, receipts, and event histories. No LLM judge is used and executions are not matched to a reference trace.
* **Constraints:** rules are spread over documents that include outdated guidance and guidance for related products, so the agent must decide which rule applies by scope, version, and effective period. Workflows share funds, authorization limits, and deadlines, and some information arrives only after a delay, so waiting can make a deadline unreachable.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

The authors argue that existing long-horizon agent benchmarks mostly score final task success, which cannot show an intermediate violation that is later repaired, such as an unauthorized payment whose funds come back. They place BizBench against three lines. Long-horizon technical benchmarks (SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench, SWE-Milestone, Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench) check software progress on final states. The τ family (τ-bench, [τ²-Bench](../concepts/tau2-bench.md), τ-Knowledge) and enterprise suites (WorkArena, TheAgentCompany, CRMArena, CRMArena-Pro) study policy-governed tool use and organizational context; [ToolSandbox](../concepts/toolsandbox.md) is cited for stateful, policy-governed tool use with knowledge access. [AutomationBench](../concepts/automationbench.md) scores final application states without prescribing tool-call sequences. The claimed new combination is: workflows linked by shared resources, deadlines, and earlier commitments; guidance whose applicability depends on scope, version, and effective period; and deterministic checks of process and protection requirements over execution histories that still accept different valid strategies.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If an evaluator checks how a task was executed as well as what it achieved, it will reject runs that outcome-only grading would count as successes, and agents that look capable on goals will prove less reliable once approvals, continuous constraints, and the choice of applicable rule are enforced. The paper tests this by reporting goal, process, protection, and strict pass rates separately for ten models, counting runs that reach the goal but fail P or F, and using information ablations to locate where agents lose success (finding the applicable documents versus eliciting the customer's own facts).

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

**Score execution, not only outcomes.** The evaluator separates an executed action from a proposal, an approval, or a rejected call with no side effect. Approval checks require that the approval covers the actual operation and its terms, comes from an authorized approver, and precedes execution; continuous checks test a constraint at every relevant state change. In the paper's example an account holds $60 and must keep $20: paying a $60 retainer is accepted by the tool but breaches the floor at once, and later refunds that restore the closing balance do not repair the breach. Different valid action orders can satisfy the same criteria, because nothing is compared with a reference trace.

**Rules with scope, version, and effective period.** Source business documents were turned into SOPs (Algorithm 1: extract workflow candidates with evidence links, group them into plans, generate SOPs, review each against its evidence, drop content with unresolved conflicts, and rewrite sensitive details for simulation). Differences that scope, version, or effective period can resolve are kept, so current and historical guidance coexist in the environment's documents.

**Linked workflows.** Tasks combine workflows that share funds or authorization limits, or whose outputs are prerequisites for others, and scheduled events link timing across them. In the delivery case (Q34) the priority-booking window closes before the warehouse confirmation arrives, so an agent that waits for confirmation cannot meet the first deadline. In Q24, correct final payments still fail because return charge, guarantee fee, intermediary deduction, and top-up fee total $80 against a $75 fee budget.

**Validated tasks.** Every released task has a reference (Golden) trace that passes when replayed from the initial state. The evaluator is also tested on alternative valid traces, targeted violation traces (a temporary reserve breach must fail P), changed facts that alter what is valid, and idempotent retries that must not change the outcome. Five researchers and a team of business-domain experts reviewed tasks and checks, and only tasks passing both validation and review were kept.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Models.** Claude Opus 5, GPT-6 Sol, GLM-5.3-Flash, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Muse Spark 1.3, Qwen3.8-27B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, and Qwen3.5-9B on all 50 tasks. Claude Opus 5 and Muse Spark 1.3 have one run per task; the others have three, and single-run success is averaged.
* **Runner.** Each run uses its own copy of the database. There is no cumulative limit on tokens, tool calls, or scheduler steps, only a wall-clock timeout and a tool-error threshold. On a provider's context overflow, the oldest quarter of eligible knowledge-search and document-read responses is replaced in the model-facing context while the full record is kept. The default customer simulator is DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. Infrastructure and simulator-protocol failures are tracked apart from business outcomes.
* **Metrics.** G, P, F, intermediate failure (¬(P ∧ F)), strict success, and pass^k (the fraction of tasks that succeed in every one of k runs, averaged over k-run subsets).
* **Ablations and diagnostics (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash).** Gold-doc (applicable documents supplied) and full customer disclosure (all customer-known facts in the opening request, approvals still required); retrospective tool-call caps at 1.2, 1.5, and 2.0 times each task's Golden call count; trace statistics (repeated identical database reads, tool errors, distinct documents).
* **Simulator checks.** Protocol violations under the original and a revised simulator prompt with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash in both roles, and four simulator models with Qwen3.8-27B as the agent.
* **Not included.** A human baseline, confidence intervals on the main table, and the ablations for any model other than DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

The frozen release has 50 tasks, 789 knowledge documents (598,827 document-body tokens, cl100k_base), 9 always-available agent tools, 601 discoverable business capabilities, 10 simulator tools, 398 queryable database collections (227 non-empty), and 2,994 records. Golden traces average 111.8 assistant tool calls (median 100.5, range 51-282), 28.6 simulator calls, 5.4 distinct documents, 29.9 distinct records, and 14.2 executed business requests per task. Sensitive business details were rewritten for confidentiality and the records are synthetic, so the environment reconstructs the company's decision logic rather than exposing real data. Code is at [ant-intl/BizAgentBench](https://github.com/ant-intl/BizAgentBench) (1 star per `hf papers info`); the title page also links a Hugging Face release. Neither was opened for this note.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported.**
  * *Frontier models leave a large gap.* Strict success is 64.0% for Claude Opus 5 and 61.3% for GPT-6 Sol, 43.3% for GLM-5.3-Flash and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, 39.3% for DeepSeek-V4-Pro, 38.0% for Muse Spark 1.3, and 32.7% for Qwen3.8-27B; DeepSeek-V4-Flash reaches 5.3%, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B 0.7%, and Qwen3.5-9B none of its 150 runs (Table 1).
  * *Process checks catch what goal checks miss.* Every model has intermediate failures, from 2 of 50 runs (4.0%) for Claude Opus 5 to 125 of 150 (83.3%) for Qwen3.5-9B, and P pass rates sit below F for every model (18.7-96.0% against 87.3-100.0%). Runs that reach the goal but fail P or F number 5 of 150 for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (four crossed the cash-reserve floor before restoring it, one tried to execute before approval and was rejected) and 14 for Qwen3.8-27B (eight rejected pre-approval attempts), so Qwen3.8-27B's goal rate of 42.0% falls to 32.7% strict.
  * *Runs are not repeatable.* pass^3 is 40.0% for GPT-6 Sol, 26.0% for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, 22.0% for GLM-5.3-Flash, 10.0% for Qwen3.8-27B, and 0% for DeepSeek-V4-Flash and both Qwen3.5 models.
  * *Customer facts matter more than documents.* For DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, supplying the applicable documents lifts strict success from 43.3% to 50.0%, while supplying all customer facts lifts it to 70.0%; 30% of tasks stay unsolved even then (Table 2, which the text calls Table 3).
  * *Long traces are not progress.* DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash averages 191.3 assistant calls against 111.8 in the Golden traces; the excess is in information access (111.1 database queries against 59.2, 43.3 searches or document reads against 11.5), while business calls and executions are similar (26.9 against 28.7). It opens 20.1 distinct documents against 5.4. GLM-5.3-Flash matches its strict success with 118.3 calls and 9.4 documents, and Qwen3.5-397B-A17B makes 216.4 calls for one success in 150 runs. Qwen3.5-9B receives 101.6 tool-error replies per run, 71% of them for non-public resources.
  * *Budgets would bite.* Retrospectively capping DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at 1.2, 1.5, and 2.0 times the Golden call count leaves 0.7%, 6.7%, and 26.7% strict success against 43.3% uncapped.
* **Weaker or narrower than the headline.**
  * *The top ranking rests on one run.* Claude Opus 5 (64.0%) and Muse Spark 1.3 have one run per task, while GPT-6 Sol's 61.3% is a three-run mean whose pass^3 is 40.0%, and no confidence intervals are given. The 2.7-point lead is one or two tasks (reader observation).
  * *One model carries the analysis, and it also plays the customer.* The ablations, caps, and trace studies use DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, which is also the default simulator, so its agent runs talk to a copy of itself. The simulator-model check (strict success 30.7-36.0% across four simulators, at most 3.3 points from the default 32.7%) uses Qwen3.8-27B as the agent only (reader observation).
  * *Caps are retrospective.* Truncating finished runs at a call count shows how long successful runs were, not how an agent would act if told its budget, so the cap table is an upper bound on lost runs rather than a budgeted evaluation (reader inference).
  * *Reconstructed, not real.* The business logic is real but the records are synthetic and sensitive details are rewritten; how closely the 50 tasks track the distribution of real customer requests is not reported.
  * *Simulator prompt sensitivity.* The original simulator prompt produced protocol violations on 4 of 50 tasks and the revised one on none (paired McNemar p = 0.125), so the revision is motivated by a small, non-significant difference; the paper adopts it as the default without reporting whether agent scores moved.

# Q9 Contributions

* A benchmark that makes business requirements testable on execution histories, with success defined as goal, process, and protection checks jointly, so a repaired intermediate violation still fails.
* An environment construction recipe: SOPs extracted from business documents with evidence links, versioned guidance with explicit scopes and effective periods, workflows linked by shared resources and timing, and an LLM customer whose approvals bind to specific terms.
* An evaluator validation procedure beyond reference replay: alternative valid traces, targeted violation traces, fact changes, idempotent retries, and expert review.
* Measurements that separate failure sources: goal versus process versus protection, run-to-run repeatability, document retrieval versus customer elicitation, and redundant versus productive tool use.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **From the authors.** Agents that coordinate information gathering with action selection: deciding when evidence suffices to act and which obligations must stay satisfied as execution continues.
* **Budget-aware evaluation (reader suggestion).** Run agents with a stated tool-call budget instead of truncating finished runs, and report strict success against calls used, since GLM-5.3-Flash already reaches DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's score with 62% of its calls.
* **Separate the simulator from the agent (reader suggestion).** Repeat the main table with a simulator from a family not under test, and report the simulator-model spread for the strongest agents, not only for Qwen3.8-27B.
* **Elicitation as a trainable skill (reader suggestion).** The 43.3% to 70.0% jump from full disclosure isolates asking the customer the right questions; a clarification policy trained or prompted for business elicitation could be measured directly against that ceiling.
* **Link to evidence-before-action benchmarks (reader connection).** [SafeActBench](2610.07753.md) scores whether each consequential action was preceded by evidence bound to the right entity and state. Scoring BizBench's pre-approval execution attempts with that lens would show whether they come from missing evidence or from ignoring evidence the agent already had.
