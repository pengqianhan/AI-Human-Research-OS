# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** an academic-scale compute budget and open data sources. The pipeline consists of general pre-training (≈0.99T tokens in Stage 1, 3.20T in Stage 2); mid-training over a 16K → 64K → 256K context curriculum that includes reasoning, instruction, and agentic traces; SFT on 4,921,933 examples (96.46% general, 3.54% agentic); and mixed GRPO RL on math, code, and general tasks.
* **Output (Y):** ZGCM-1-7B, a 7.39B-parameter dense decoder with a 256K context, plus stage-wise weights, intermediate checkpoints, training code, per-stage data and recipes, W&B logs, and evaluation harnesses. At inference, a thinking-enabled policy that either answers directly or runs a ReAct tool loop (web search/read, Ghidra binary analysis, bash).
* **Core problem:** two barriers the authors name. The *Scale Barrier* is the assumption that frontier reasoning and deep agentic search need hundred-billion-parameter models. The *Opacity Barrier* is that competitive models are released open-weight but not open-source, so filtering recipes, mid-training curricula, long-context schedules, and agent traces cannot be studied.
* **Scope:** one 7B dense model; the reported evaluations use the released 256K *SFT* checkpoint in think mode (§5.1). The mixed-RL stage is described but is not the checkpoint behind the headline tables. Agentic claims center on web deep research and binary analysis; software-engineering and terminal agency are reported only as appendix diagnostics.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

The goal is not new: fully open small models (e.g., Olmo-3-7B-Think, OpenThinker3-7B, MiMo-7B-RL, MiniCPM4.1-8B) and small search agents already exist. The claimed contribution is a *combination*: a from-scratch, fully open 7B model that targets both competition math and multi-step agentic search in one set of weights, with an efficiency-first recipe and released intermediate artifacts. The "compact model + deliberate thinking + active tool use" thesis matters to this wiki because it asks whether search-agent capability can be moved into a model small enough for academic labs to train and study end-to-end, rather than only prompted at the frontier.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If a compact model cannot memorize the open web, then training it to couple long chain-of-thought with external tool use — via mid-training that turns interaction traces into dense state-conditioned next-action supervision, and SFT whose tool schemas exactly match the inference environment — should let a 7B model approach much larger models on tasks where evidence can be *fetched* (web deep research, binary analysis), while it still lags on closed-book, recall-heavy knowledge. The evaluation supports this pattern:

* **Where evidence can be fetched:** ZGCM-1-7B is competitive or better on the search-heavy benchmarks — WebWalkerQA 63.09 vs. Kimi-K2 63.00, and BrowseComp 19.43 vs. Claude 4 Sonnet 12.20 as reported.
* **Closed-book knowledge:** it trails 7–8B peers on MMLU (73.88 vs. up to 85.40) and GPQA-Diamond (47.87 vs. up to 60.10).

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Architecture and optimizer:** gated sliding-window attention (Qiu et al., 2025) at the Gemma 3 5:1 local-to-global ratio; GQA, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, partial RoPE, QK-norm; Muon (Jordan et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025) with hybrid FP8 and TWEO outlier regularization (Liang et al., 2025). The architecture study compares against MLA and a GDN linear-attention hybrid.
* **Agentic mid-training:** follows *agentic continual pre-training* (Su et al., 2025) in reformulating traces as MDP-style state-conditioned next-action prediction — the same MDP framing [Agent Lightning](2508.03680.md) uses on the RL side.
* **Post-training:** [GRPO](../concepts/grpo.md) with DAPO-style dynamic sampling and overlong handling (Yu et al., 2025); SFT tool schemas follow the GLM-5.1 structured-tool format.
* **Baselines:** 7–8B reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B, MiniCPM4.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, Olmo-3-7B-Think, MiMo-7B-RL, OpenThinker3-7B); for web research, WebDancer-32B, Qwen3-235B-A22B, Kimi-K2, DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B, GPT-4o, GPT-5, Claude 4 Sonnet; for binary search, DeepSeek-V4 Flash, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, GLM-5.1, Kimi-K2, DeepSeek-R1, GPT-4o, Qwen3-8B.
* **Key people.** Zhongguancun Academy and Zhongguancun Institute of Artificial Intelligence (HF organization ZGCAGI). Jiyan He is Project Lead; Tie-Yan Liu, Shuxin Zheng, and Bin Shao are among the contributors. Likely critics: open-model groups whose 7–8B models are compared (Olmo, MiMo, MiniCPM, Qwen), and search-agent groups, given that most web baselines are copied from other reports.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

An efficiency-first, fully open full-stack recipe organized around "think + seek":

* **Hybrid attention:** 27 gated SWA layers (128-token window) and 5 global layers (indices 6, 12, 18, 24, 30) cut per-token KV cache from 128 KiB to 20 KiB. At 256K, that is 32.0 GiB → 5.0 GiB (6.4×), with a throughput gain growing from 1.13× at 4K to 3.94× at 256K. In a 10B-token, 4K-sequence sweep, SWA 5:1 matched full attention's tail loss (1.93) at the highest throughput (9,566 tokens/s/GPU).
* **Systems co-design:** FP8 + Muon + TWEO for a ~4.2× pre-training time-to-loss speedup over AdamW/BF16 at 16K.
* **Curriculum mid-training:** context scaled 16K → 64K → 256K. The introduction summarizes the curriculum as spanning 600B tokens. Mid-training keeps full-sequence causal LM while adding reasoning, instruction, and agentic data. Agentic traces become MDP-style next-action examples, and software-engineering traces are execution-filtered. A staged 10B@64K + 20B@256K route beat a direct 30B@256K route on final loss.
* **Schema-aligned agentic SFT:**
  * 20,217 deep-research trajectories (search/visit tools), 30,014 execution-grounded plus 30,000 execution-free software-engineering trajectories, and terminal trajectories.
  * Tool calls are validated for argument structure, call/observation pairing, and unique IDs.
  * Joint interleaving of general and agentic data beats a sequential schedule.
  * Assistant-only loss; aligned think and no-think forms.
  * About 50% of raw general SFT candidates are pruned by tiered filtering, which beat the uncurated corpus in aggregate.
* **AI-native R&D:** researcher-directed agents run data cleaning, cluster operations (via a co-developed skill package), auto-research on I/O performance, and evaluation. ACE, a 2,503-probe, 183-capability diagnostic suite, completes in about 2–3 minutes. The meeting agent ZGent turns discussions into dispatched tasks.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Non-agentic:** 20 benchmarks (math and abstract reasoning, code, knowledge, instruction following), mean pass@1 over 32 runs at temperature 1.0 with a 262,144-token budget; some baseline cells use 4-run means or single runs (§5.1).
* **Web deep research:** thinking-enabled ReAct, at most 64 search-and-read steps, Serper/Jina search, and Jina Reader. Pages are summarized by ZGCM-1 itself in no-think mode, and answers are judged by Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 (Appendix 11.1). *Baselines other than WebDancer are taken from public reports, not rerun in this harness.*
* **Binary Function Search (new):** given a behavior description, find the exact entry address of a target function in a stripped ELF binary through a Ghidra-backed tool interface. It uses 50 tasks (5 from each of 10 held-out projects — tmux, tree, GNU Wget2, XZ Utils, YAJL, zlib, libuv, libgit2, nm, Lua) and is checked against a hidden oracle built from the unstripped binary. All baselines are rerun in the shared harness.
* **Appendix diagnostics:** SWE-bench Verified Mini50 (mini-SWE-agent v2, 250 steps) and Terminal-Bench 2.0 (89 tasks, 64 steps).
* **Recipe ablations:** attention schedules, context routes, SFT filtering strength, joint vs. sequential agentic SFT, and think/no-think transfer (Section 10.4, not read in detail here).
* **AI4AI autonomy:** nine core contributors rate 11 R&D task categories on an L1–L5 rubric (99 ratings).

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Well supported:**
  * Math at the 7–8B scale is run under one protocol with 32-run means: AIME 2026 75.00 (best in its table), HMMT 2025 70.42 and HMMT 2026 59.48 (both best), and MATH-500 97.13.
  * Binary Function Search: all systems use the same harness and an exact-address oracle over held-out projects. ZGCM-1-7B scores 31/50 = 62%, vs. Qwen3-8B at 12%, GLM-5.1 at 66%, and DeepSeek-V4 Flash / Qwen3.5-397B at 76%.
  * The attention and KV-cache efficiency numbers are direct measurements.
* **Weaker than the framing:**
  * *Web comparisons are not like-for-like.* Apart from WebDancer, the baseline cells come from other reports with different harnesses, search providers, dates, and judges. ZGCM-1 is judged by a Qwen3-30B-A3B judge in its own harness (Appendix 11.1 itself says results "depend on search date and availability, provider behavior, page extraction, summarization, judge settings, and retry policy"). The BrowseComp 19.43 vs. Claude 4 Sonnet 12.20 and Qwen3-235B 2.30 gaps therefore mix model and harness effects.
  * *"Competitive with GLM-5.1" rests on Binary Function Search only.* GLM-5.1 does not appear in the web table.
  * *Checkpoint mismatch.* The RL stage is described, but the reported numbers come from the 256K SFT checkpoint, so the paper does not show RL's contribution to the headline results.
  * *Mixed breadth.* ZGCM-1 trails peers on MMLU, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench v6, IFEval (75.42 vs. 88.20 best), and MBPP+. The appendix diagnostics resolve 2/50 SWE-bench Verified Mini50 tasks (4.0%) and 2/89 Terminal-Bench 2.0 tasks (2.25%), which the Limitations section acknowledges as "modest".
  * *AI4AI autonomy* is self-rated by the team's own contributors on an ordinal rubric. The paper labels these "contributor judgments, not standardized measurements."
* **Unverified here:** the "eight actionable empirical findings" are announced in the abstract, but the retrieved HTML text did not surface them as a labeled list. This note does not enumerate them.

# Q9 Contributions

* A genuinely open 7B reasoning-plus-agent model with stage-wise artifacts, which enables the study of how agentic capability emerges across pre-training, mid-training, and SFT — something open-weight releases do not permit.
* Evidence that a 7B model trained with schema-aligned agentic data can be competitive on structured tool tasks (web research as reported, and binary function localization under a controlled harness) while remaining weak on open-ended repository and terminal work. This is a useful map of where small-model agency currently works.
* Reusable engineering results: the 5:1 gated-SWA KV/throughput trade-off, the staged long-context route, joint rather than sequential agentic SFT, and schema-mismatch sensitivity.
* A new, harder-to-contaminate agentic task (Binary Function Search) and an early, candid per-task autonomy profile for AI-assisted model development: operational tasks rated L4, architecture and algorithm design rated L2.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Controlled web evaluation:** rerun all web baselines in the same harness, date window, and judge so that "7B vs. frontier" claims isolate the model.
* **Report the RL checkpoint** and ablate agentic MDP mid-training directly (with vs. without) on web and binary tasks. Neither contribution to the agentic results is isolated in the main text.
* **Interactive agentic RL:** the authors' own future-work item is moving from SFT and outcome-reward RL to multi-turn RL inside terminal, web, and compiler sandboxes, which is the obvious lever for the 2–4% SWE-bench/Terminal-Bench numbers.
* **MoE extension** of the same recipe, as the authors propose.
* **Link to this wiki (reader-constructed):** the L1–L5 AI4AI rubric invites direct comparison with [The Last AI Built by Humans](2609.11873.md)'s five-level autonomy taxonomy and [Atria Dawn](2609.15818.md)'s decision-attributed development study. All three agree that design decisions remain the least automated part of model development.
