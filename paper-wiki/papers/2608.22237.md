# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** an agent step that accesses an external artifact (web page, PDF, long document, table or JSON, a set of workspace files, a script library), together with what the agent needs from it for its next decision.
* **Output (Y):** the content admitted to the model context for that step: a bounded evidence slice with source anchors and a record of which needs are covered, or the full view when that is cheaper or required.
* **Constraints:** no change to model weights and no separate generative compressor; the layer must work across artifact types and agent frameworks; cumulative raw exposure per reading episode must stay bounded relative to simply reading the artifact; whether the evidence is sufficient is the agent's decision, not the layer's.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

The cost of tool observations is a known problem, addressed after the fact by observation compression and masking and before the fact by retrieval. The paper names the specific failure *over-reading*: artifacts are accessed at object granularity although a decision needs a small slice, and what is admitted early is replayed in later calls. Its measurements motivate this. Annotated supporting evidence is a median 3.17% of source tokens per LooGLE question and about 5.8% for the Qasper questions analyzed, and in paired high-sparsity runs unmodified agents spend up to 17.3 times more tokens without a higher task score. The new part is treating reading as a stateful contract (intent in, evidence plus coverage out) with an explicit rule for when to stop reading sparsely.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If an agent must state what it needs before an artifact enters its context, and the reading layer tracks what has been inspected and bounds how much raw content sparse reads may expose, then the agent uses far fewer tokens and less time at equal or better task quality, including for frontier models. A companion hypothesis is that sparse reading is not always economical, so a gate with full-view fallback is needed to avoid losses on small artifacts and whole-object tasks.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Prompt and KV compression.** Selective Context, LLMLingua, Gist Tokens, AutoCompressors, ICAE; KV-cache methods. These lower the cost of context but do not choose what the model sees.
* **Agent observations and histories.** SWE-Pruner, Squeez, CoACT, AgentDiet, TACO, [ACON](../concepts/acon.md), AgentFold, and observation masking (the "Complexity Trap" paper). SparseRead positions itself above individual compressors, which could serve as Reader backends.
* **Active reading and retrieval.** ReadAgent, IRCoT, Self-RAG, Adaptive-RAG. Retrieval is one acquisition method under the evidence contract, next to structured projections and exact source operations.
* **Budgeting.** The gate adapts classical ski-rental budgeting (Borodin and El-Yaniv) to cumulative exposure.
* **Key people.** Zedong Liu, Jiaan Wu, Xinyang Ma, Le Xu, Kai Wang, Yuanchao Hu, Dingwen Tao, and Guangming Tan. The HTML version carries no affiliations; the Hugging Face page lists the Institute of Computing Technology. Likely critics: authors of agentic-RAG systems, since the RAG Agent baseline is the paper's own configuration, and benchmark authors, since tasks were adapted and selected by "relevance".

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

Separate reading control from evidence acquisition, and keep three quantities apart: the *scope* a Reader processes, the *exposure* that enters the model context, and the *coverage* of the current intent.

1. **Read Intent Protocol.** At each access the agent supplies a structured Read Intent for its next decision; a session intent from the opening request fills in unspecified details. The layer returns an EvidencePack with anchored evidence, the scope examined, the amount exposed, and per-slot coverage (which needs have support, which are unresolved). Vague intents get a bounded Object Preview. A SHA-256 fingerprint invalidates recorded evidence when the artifact changes. The agent has five actions (scout, focus, collect, refine, verify), and adding Readers does not add actions.
2. **Adaptive Reader Backends.** A Reader is defined by the artifact types it handles (text and PDF, tables and JSON, multi-file collections, script libraries). A configuration combines a Reader, a location or processing method, and an exposure form (anchored snippets, a lossless compact view, or the result of an exact operation with provenance). The layer picks the cheapest configuration whose calibrated reliability is at least τ = 0.6, where reliability is the lower end of a 95% Wilson interval over manually designed calibration requests; if none qualifies it falls back to a full view. A table Reader can scan every row to compute a sum and expose only the result, row count, and source.
3. **Budgeted Read Gate.** Let L be the length of the cheapest faithful full view. Small artifacts and requests that need the whole content go straight to the full view. Otherwise sparse reads proceed while their cumulative payload E stays within L; a batch that would not fit intact is withheld and the full view is delivered once, with earlier sparse observations retired from later prompts. Total raw exposure per episode is therefore at most 2L, and the constant is tight.

The bound is in characters of raw artifact exposure. The paper states that it says nothing about replay-weighted context, billed tokens, latency, or sessions with several episodes, and that fallback may lose prefix-cache reuse from the edited point.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Scenarios.** Long-context reading (LooGLE, [PinchBench](../concepts/pinchbench.md) document tasks, annual-report fact extraction adapted from Workspace-Bench-Lite); multi-file audit and diagnosis (QwenClawBench); structured analysis (QwenClawBench plus tasks adapted from [SpreadsheetBench](../concepts/spreadsheetbench.md) Verified); Web deep search (FRAMES and [BrowseComp-Plus](../concepts/browsecomp-plus.md) on its fixed corpus); AI for Science (LAMMPS script generation adapted from MDGYM).
* **Models.** Qwen3.6-Plus, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, GLM-5.1, Kimi-K2.5, Claude Opus 5.
* **Frameworks.** NanoBot v0.2.0 for the main matrix; [OpenCode](../concepts/opencode.md) v1.17.14 and OpenClaw v2026.6.11 for portability, through thin adapters.
* **Baselines.** Naive (the unmodified framework, including its own search and partial reads), RAG Static (one retrieve-and-rerank pass, 8 chunks, 4,096 tokens, no further source access), RAG Agent (iterative retrieval with native tools), ACON (observation and history compression), and observation masking (keep the latest ten observations).
* **Metrics.** Total-token reduction, wall-time saving, and task-score change against Naive, measured end to end including retrieval, reranking, and compressor calls.
* **Ablations.** Intent handling (none, session, access, both), four text location methods on LooGLE, and gate checks before versus after delivery.

Each design has an ablation, but with different instruments: the intent ablation uses protocol diagnostics, the Reader ablation is an offline evidence-hit study, and the gate ablation measures exposure ratios. None of the three reports end-task score with the component removed.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

All tasks come from existing benchmarks, with adaptation to the agent interface and checker. The appendix says every "scenario-relevant" task from each source is included, with relevance decided from input artifacts and required operations; it gives no task counts per scenario. Reader calibration uses separate, manually designed requests grouped by artifact type, size band (up to 8K, 8K to 32K, above 32K characters), and intent type; groups with fewer than 20 requests use a default configuration. No code repository is linked on the arXiv or Hugging Face pages.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

**Main matrix (NanoBot, 6 models by 5 scenarios, against Naive).**

| Scenario | Token reduction | Time saving | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-context reading | 76.5 to 92.9% | 67.1 to 89.0% | preserved or improved |
| Multi-file diagnosis | 26.8 to 74.5% | not broken out | preserved or improved for every model |
| Structured analysis | 15.7 to 64.1% | not broken out | preserved or improved for every model |
| Web deep search | 31.0 to 43.0% | 23.0 to 37.0% | +5 to +14 points for all six models |
| AI for Science | 82.7 to 89.1% | 28.3 to 63.0% | preserved or improved for five models; GLM-5.1 regresses |

Across the 30 settings the median reductions are 61.4% (tokens) and 53.7% (time), and the abstract's "97%" is 29 of 30. Claude Opus 5 saves 41.0 to 89.0% of tokens across scenarios, so the effect is not limited to weaker models.

**Against other methods (15 settings, three scenarios by five models).** SparseRead preserves or improves score in all 15 and uses the fewest tokens in six and the least time in nine. RAG Static is faster on all five Web settings but scores below Naive in nine of 15; RAG Agent loses score in eight; ACON loses score in five and raises both costs in six; observation masking loses score in five.

**Portability (18 framework-model settings).** Tokens and time fall in all 18, and score is preserved or improved in 15. Median token reductions are 65.0% (NanoBot), 65.2% (OpenCode), and 30.6% (OpenClaw). The three medians match the table (reader recomputation). Which tasks this table aggregates is not stated.

**Ablations.** Using both intents cuts repeated reads from 4.8 to 1.5 per task and untraceable evidence from 28% to 8%, raises the share of verification checks that resolve a gap from 37% to 89%, and lowers answers failing for missing evidence from 25% to 5%. On 47 LooGLE documents and 741 questions, hybrid location reaches a 65.3% evidence-hit rate at 1,490 characters per collect against BM25's 60.1% at 1,497 and lexical matching's 39.5% at 780. Checking the budget before delivery keeps exposure ratios at 1.62 to 1.93 of the full view; checking after allows 2.10 to 2.33. On three boundary tasks where native processing can be preferable (DiD regression, SPARQL generation, discount calculation), forced sparse reading raises tokens by 49.0%, 309.0%, and 210.5%, while the gate saves 45.0%, 22.5%, and 54.0%.

**Limits on the evidence (reader observations).**

* No number in the main results has a run count or an interval; the gate ablation (five tasks, three repeats) is the only place repeats are mentioned. Task counts per scenario are missing, so the weight behind each percentage is unknown.
* Tasks were selected as "relevant" and adapted by the authors. Web deep search uses BrowseComp-Plus's fixed corpus, not live browsing, and the AI for Science scenario scores script generation without running the simulations, as the appendix says.
* The appendix shows the main matrix was changed: an earlier five-scenario matrix that included the native-fit controls preserved or improved score in 26 of 30 cells, and those controls were then moved out and treated separately. The appendix also reports 22 of 24 for the four retained scenarios, while the main text's 29 of 30 with all six Web settings improving implies 23 of 24.
* Other internal mismatches in the v2 text: the gate-economics figure caption names DeepSeek-V4-Pro where the text says DeepSeek-V4-Flash; the gate ablation reports six exposure ratios but upgrades in five of 15 runs; the main text attributes the 309% increase to "global statistics" where the appendix's task order assigns it to SPARQL generation.
* The Reader ablation compares location methods and, as the paper says, does not measure what the configuration selector adds over a fixed configuration. The selector and its calibration are therefore untested as a component.
* The native-fit comparison uses "the earlier Gate policy", so the token economics of the current gate on unsuitable tasks are not measured directly.
* The largest score gain in the portability table (Kimi-K2.5 on NanoBot, +0.598 with 92.9% fewer tokens) suggests the Naive agent was failing those tasks outright, perhaps from context overflow or timeouts; the paper does not break this down.

The direction of the result is well supported: across models, frameworks, and artifact types, asking for evidence before admitting content saves a large share of tokens without an obvious quality cost. The exact magnitudes and the "preserved or improved" counts rest on unreported run counts and on a task set the authors assembled.

# Q9 Contributions

1. A named problem, over-reading, with measurements of evidence sparsity and of the cost of broad reads replayed across calls.
2. A reading contract (Read Intent to EvidencePack) that records intent, source anchors, and per-slot coverage across accesses to an artifact version.
3. A Reader interface that separates how evidence is located from how much is exposed, including exact operations that process everything and expose only a result.
4. A gate with a proved 2L bound on raw exposure per episode and an explicit full-view fallback.
5. An evaluation across six models, five scenarios, and three agent frameworks.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Report runs and task counts.** The headline percentages need the number of tasks and repeats behind them (reader suggestion).
* **Test the selector.** Compare adaptive configuration selection with the best fixed configuration per artifact type on end-task score; the paper names this gap.
* **Replay-weighted accounting.** The bound is on raw characters per episode. A bound, or at least a measurement, in replay-weighted tokens with prefix caching priced in would match what users pay; the paper notes fallback can break cache reuse.
* **Coverage is not sufficiency.** Coverage records that support was found for a slot, not that it is correct or complete. A study of cases where an agent stops on covered but wrong evidence would test the "sufficiency is the agent's decision" design (reader suggestion).
* **Compliance.** Guidance to the model (a SKILL.md) is advisory while the budget is enforced in the core. How often models bypass the layer through native tools, and what that does to savings, is not reported.
* **Project fit.** For a file-system research workspace read by agents, the three-way split of scope, exposure, and coverage is a useful way to design index and preview files: an agent can be handed a compact view or a computed result with provenance instead of whole documents (reader connection).
