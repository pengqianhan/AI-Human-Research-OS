# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a language instruction ℓ plus, at each control step t, a multi-view visual observation and the robot's proprioceptive state (gripper height, contact, gripper open/closed).
* **Output (Y):** at each step, one discrete semantic action unit from a compact, embodiment-agnostic vocabulary — MV_FWD/BACK/LEFT/RIGHT/UP/DOWN (view-relative end-effector translation), ROTATE_CW/CCW about a specified axis, GRASP/RELEASE, and DONE — deterministically grounded into a small bounded robot motion by an embodiment-specific interpreter, plus GUMI, a GUI exposing the same action units so humans and agents can collect demonstrations without teleoperation hardware.
* **Core problem:** foundation VLMs already encode much of what manipulation requires (object/spatial recognition, long-horizon decomposition) but existing routes to turn that into robot behavior each lose something — VLA models collapse semantics into an opaque low-level regression target requiring embodiment-specific retraining, while hierarchical/programmatic systems keep the VLM semantic but hand physical realization to a downstream controller or hand-designed API, weakening the model's direct responsibility for fine-grained physical decisions.
* **Scope:** two real-robot platforms (7-DoF Franka, bimanual 6-DoF AgileX), ten object×receptacle pick-and-place tasks plus targeted probes of physical and semantic adaptability, evaluated zero-shot with frontier VLMs and via LoRA fine-tuning of a 2B open VLM; the paper does not evaluate dexterous or humanoid embodiments.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

The paper does not claim to originate VLA models, hierarchical/programmatic robot agents, or discrete action interfaces for digital agents — it positions itself against RT-1/RT-2, π0/π0.5, GR00T-style VLAs (semantics collapsed into continuous or tokenized low-level control), and against code-as-policy and skill-library agentic systems (semantics preserved but physical grounding handed to an opaque, system-specific executor). Its stated novelty is a middle interface: a discrete action space that is simultaneously semantic enough for a VLM to reason over natively and fine-grained enough that the VLM remains "directly responsible for fine-grained physical decisions," with embodiment-specific detail isolated entirely inside a deterministic interpreter rather than a learned or hand-engineered controller. It explicitly draws the analogy to discrete, human-operable interfaces already standard in digital/simulated agents (mouse-keyboard computer-use agents, discrete-action game and navigation agents) and argues real-world manipulation has lacked a comparably simple, broadly operable interface because control is normally embodiment-specific, high-dimensional, and precision-sensitive — closing that gap is the paper's central claim.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If robot control is reformulated as a small, view-relative, incremental discrete action vocabulary that a VLM selects from directly (rather than regressing continuous or embodiment-specific control), and if an embodiment-specific interpreter deterministically and bijectively grounds each semantic unit into bounded physical motion — then (1) frontier VLMs should become capable zero-shot robot agents purely through prompting, without any fine-tuning or embodiment-specific pretraining, and (2) small open-source VLMs should be adaptable to the same interface with only a few GPU-hours of LoRA fine-tuning on a modest number of demonstrations, with both modes generalizing more robustly across tasks, environments, and embodiments than representative VLA and agentic baselines, because separating "what to do" (semantic, VLM-native) from "how to execute it" (deterministic, interpreter-native) lets precision and embodiment transfer be handled without touching the model.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Foundation models as low-level policies (VLAs).** π0/π0.5, GR00T, RT-1/RT-2, and diffusion/flow-matching or tokenized-action variants attach action generation to a VLM backbone, trading semantic breadth for an opaque sensorimotor mapping that typically needs fresh trajectories per embodiment or task family.
* **Foundation models as intermediate decision makers.** PaLM-E-style subgoal/keypoint/affordance/value-map generation keeps the VLM semantic but hands physical realization to a system-specific downstream grounding pipeline, which the paper argues surrenders the model's responsibility for how intent becomes motion.
* **Agentic robot systems and interface design.** Code-as-policy (RoboScript, CaP-family), skill-library selection (SayCan/AutoRT lineage), and VLA-steering agents (Harness VLA, Goal-VLA) close a perception-decision-execution loop but expose entire skills/controllers/VLAs as opaque callable primitives ("the agent decides what, an opaque executor decides how"); Show-Harness instead exposes the how itself as fine-grained, transparent semantic units. The paper cites Anthropic's "Claude plays robotics" research note as a contemporaneous example probing frontier-VLM levels of control abstraction.
* **Digital-interface precedent.** Computer-use/game agents (mouse/keyboard/controller) and discrete-action navigation agents/simulators are cited as the source of the paper's "compact, interpretable, human-and-model-operable action space" design principle, which real-world manipulation interfaces have not previously matched.
* **Key people and affiliations.** Yanzhe Chen, Zechen Bai, Zhijun Cao, and Wenzheng Zeng are co-first authors; Mike Zheng Shou is the corresponding author. Submitted 9 Sep 2026 (arXiv v1); institutional affiliation is not stated in the retrieved text.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Nine-unit view-relative semantic action space.** Six translation units (forward/back, left/right, up/down) defined relative to a per-step reference view, two rotation units (clockwise/counter-clockwise about a chosen axis), GRASP/RELEASE, and DONE — designed to be incremental (small, correctable, locally observable), embodiment-agnostic (symbolic, not numeric), and visually grounded (directions map onto observable views).
2. **Deterministic embodiment grounding.** A per-embodiment interpreter g_E updates a 6-DoF Cartesian setpoint via a fixed geometric rule (Eq. 4: calibrated translation/rotation increments mapped through the embodiment's motion frame, with workspace/limit projection); Franka tracks the setpoint with impedance control, AgileX uses inverse kinematics with streamed joint targets — adding a new embodiment requires only a new interpreter, not retraining.
3. **Perceive-reason-act harness with nine plugins.** Perception (Multi-View Guidance, Proprioception-to-text), Reasoning (Subtask Planning with model-checked completion criteria, Situated Planning that defers uncertain branches until evidence appears, Action Chunking, Adaptive Step size, Visual Prompt for ambiguous targets), and Action (Action History to prevent oscillation, Failure Recovery that detects empty grasps and rolls back) — each independently ablated in Sec. 5.4.3.
4. **Two modes on one interface.** Zero-shot (ZS): a frontier VLM (default Gemini-3.1 Pro) acts through the harness with no fine-tuning. Fine-tuned (FT): a small open VLM (default Qwen3.5-2B) is LoRA-adapted (rank 64, ~3% of parameters, vision encoder and projector frozen) via token-level cross-entropy on the semantic-unit vocabulary itself — no dedicated action head or special tokens — using a minimal context (instruction, multi-view observation, short action history) for controlled comparison.
5. **GUMI (GUI Manipulation Interface).** Exposes the same semantic units as labeled on-screen controls/keystrokes so humans can "play" the robot from a keyboard, computer-use agents can operate the same GUI, and general VLM agents can predict units directly; records (observation, action) pairs pre-execution, supports step-wise or chunked control, single/dual-arm operation, and mixed human-agent collection with human correction — reusable across embodiments sharing the same interpreter contract and requiring no specialized teleoperation hardware.

# Q6 Experiment Design

Two real-robot rigs: 7-DoF Franka Research 3 (exocentric + wrist RealSense views) and bimanual 6-DoF AgileX (egocentric + two wrist Orbbec views); FT models served on one RTX 5090, ZS models via frontier APIs. Ten pick-and-place tasks pair five objects (block, banana, tennis ball, teddy bear, chess piece — spanning rigid, irregular, rolling, deformable, and precision-manipulation properties) with two receptacles (plate, bowl); teddy bear and chess piece are held out of FT training data for OOD evaluation. Metrics are success rate and steps/episode over 10 randomized trials per task (50-step timeout = failure). Demonstration data: 164 real-robot episodes (7.8K decision steps: 101 Franka, 63 AgileX) collected via GUMI (human keyboard control plus frontier-VLM browser rollouts) at a shared 2 cm translation step, jointly training the FT policy and compared VLA baselines; sim-to-real uses 230 simulated episodes (13.5K steps) across ManiSkill and RoboLab. Baselines span three families under controlled comparison (same demonstrations where trainable): VLA (π0.5, GR00T), VLA-centric agents (Harness VLA, Goal-VLA), and code-as-policy agents (CaP-X, RATS).

**Main results (Sec. 5.2).** Across cross-task, cross-environment (background/lighting/viewpoint/distractor/sim-to-real shift), and cross-embodiment (Franka↔AgileX) generalization, both ZS and FT consistently outperform all baselines; FT succeeds sim-to-real using only simulated GUMI demonstrations where trainable VLA baselines fail, and the same semantic interface transfers between the two embodiments (ZS via interpreter swap alone; FT via co-training).

**Physical adaptability (Sec. 5.3.1).** Fine control: shrinking the interpreter step from 2cm to 1cm (no retraining) lifts ZS 60%→82% and FT 40%→65%, while π0.5 reaches only 18% with the same FT demonstrations (62% only after additional fine-grained-specific training). Action composition: diagonal-move composition cuts steps with no success drop. Rotation extrapolation: on a carrot-grasping task trained only at 0°/45°, ZS stays robust to an unseen 90° orientation while FT reaches 70% (π0.5 20%). Workspace shift: Show-Harness degrades only mildly from core (25%) to boundary (90%) workspace regions versus a sharp π0.5 drop. Multi-arm coordination: a joint two-arm policy beats two independent single-arm agents and eliminates collisions on tidy-table/pass-banana tasks.

**Semantic adaptability (Sec. 5.3.2).** Reasoning-intensive tasks (hidden-cup search, letter rearrangement): ZS+Situated Planning reaches 85% vs. FT/π0.5 at 10%/0%; with the same Gemini-generated subtask instructions FT rises to 70% (π0.5 stays at 5%). In-context learning from a human/robot video demonstration: ZS reaches 20/20 when order is shown vs. 20% with instruction alone; FT reaches 95% conditioned on the extracted task outline.

**Ablations (Sec. 5.4).** Zero-shot performance broadly tracks frontier-VLM capability; higher "thinking effort" mainly cuts redundant steps rather than raising success (up to 3.4× wall-clock cost for GPT-5.6-sol) and >98% of responses are valid action units regardless. Errors concentrate in fine-grained grasping/placement rather than planning; adding bounding-box cues helps. FT backbone scaling: 2B is already strong; larger backbones mainly help fine-grained tasks (stacking, peg insertion), 1B models over-adjust near targets. Plugin leave-one-out on real Franka: Subtask Planning drops success to 60% when removed; forcing Action Chunking throughout drops success to 74% (fewer model calls) vs. 96% with selective chunking; Visual Prompt raises handle-aware grasping 40%→85% only when enabled (little effect otherwise, supporting default-off); Situated Planning raises hidden-object search 35%→85% only when enabled; removing Failure Recovery drops success to 72%. Action-space representation ablation (2×2: semantic-name vs. arbitrary-symbol × with/without written conventions): arbitrary symbols with explicit conventions nearly match the semantic-name default; semantic names without conventions remain usable but less efficient; arbitrary symbols with no conventions succeed in only 1/20 episodes (23.3% of self-inferred action mappings correct) — the paper concludes explicit conventions, not semantic priors alone, do most of the grounding work.

# Q9 Contributions

1. Show-Harness: a model-agnostic Embodied Harness exposing robot control through a nine-unit, view-relative, incrementally-grounded discrete semantic action space, with embodiment-specific detail isolated entirely inside a deterministic per-embodiment interpreter.
2. Demonstrated feasibility of two complementary control modes on the same interface — zero-shot frontier-VLM control with no fine-tuning, and LoRA fine-tuning of a small (2B) open VLM directly on the semantic-unit vocabulary with no dedicated action head — both outperforming representative VLA, VLA-centric-agent, and code-as-policy baselines across task, environment, and embodiment generalization on real hardware.
3. A nine-plugin perceive-reason-act harness (multi-view guidance, proprioception, subtask/situated planning, action chunking, adaptive step, visual prompting, action history, failure recovery) with a leave-one-out ablation isolating each plugin's contribution on real robots.
4. Evidence, via the action-representation and precision/workspace/rotation-extrapolation probes, that separating semantic decision from metric execution lets the same VLM (without retraining) adapt to new physical precision, workspace regions, and unseen rotation angles purely through interpreter changes or unit composition.
5. GUMI, a keyboard/GUI-based manipulation interface that lets humans, agents, and mixed human-agent collaboration collect embodiment-transferable demonstrations in the same semantic action space without specialized teleoperation hardware, yielding policy-ready (observation, action) pairs alongside the grounded low-level trajectory.

# Q10 Next Steps

The paper's own stated limitations: evaluation covers only single- and dual-arm manipulation with parallel-jaw grippers, and it names extending to more complex embodiments (humanoids, dexterous hands) as a valuable future direction; it also flags enriching perception with additional embodied modalities (tactile, force feedback) for more contact-rich, fine-grained physical interaction as future work it has not attempted.

Beyond the paper's own framing, three points are worth flagging for a reader assessing this note's evidence. First, the ablations and generalization claims are single-lab, single-hardware-rig results (one Franka, one AgileX) with no cross-lab replication found in the retrieved text — whether the interpreter-grounding approach transfers as cleanly to other robot platforms (different DoF, gripper types, or non-parallel-jaw end effectors) is untested here. Second, the paper does not report a systematic failure-mode taxonomy beyond "errors concentrate in fine-grained grasping and placement" (Sec. 5.4.1) — it is not established how often DONE is declared prematurely or how the Failure Recovery plugin's empty-grasp detector performs on failure modes other than empty grasps. Third, the action-representation ablation (Sec. 5.4.4) is evaluated only on two tasks (Block/Banana→Plate) with a frontier ZS model; whether "explicit conventions do most of the grounding work" holds for the FT mode, where the model must learn the mapping from demonstrations rather than read it from a prompt, is not tested and would be a natural extension.
