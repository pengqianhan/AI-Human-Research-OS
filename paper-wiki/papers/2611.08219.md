# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a user instruction, a set of attachment files (PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint) placed in a persistent sandbox, and four tools: `bash`, `web_search`, `web_fetch`, and `view_image` (answered by an auxiliary vision model when the solver is text-only).
* **Output (Y):** deliverable files that meet the instruction, judged against a task-specific rubric and, when available, reference deliverables.
* **Constraints:** the work is long-horizon and file-grounded; the training data must come with real work intent, authentic files, and deliverable-level scoring; the target model is small (4B).

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Evaluating agents on economically valuable office work is established ([GDPval](../concepts/gdpval.md) and several successors). The gap the paper names is training data: existing sets cover instruction following, document question answering, or one deliverable type at a time, and synthetic files lack the layouts, gaps, and cross-file references of real ones. Hand-written workflows do not scale. Synthesizing end-to-end productivity tasks from real sources is the new part; SFT followed by RL on synthesized tasks is not.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If every synthesis stage is grounded in a real source (occupational activity seeds, workflow evidence in web text, public files), and tasks carry fine-grained rubrics, then a small model trained on a few thousand such tasks improves on realistic productivity benchmarks and on file-grounded reasoning it was not trained for. Two sub-claims are tested separately: real files train better than generated files, and rubric rewards train better than rewards without rubrics.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Productivity benchmarks.** GDPval, $1M-Bench, Agents' Last Exam, Finance Agent Benchmark, APEX-Agents, AgentSkillOS, ClawBench, EdgeBench, Workspace-Bench, EnterpriseClawBench.
* **Data synthesis for agent post-training.** Tool-use trajectories and executable environments scaled from corpora, programs, or templates; web tutorials replayed as GUI trajectories; terminal-agent environment generators. The paper's objection is that these target predefined tool interfaces or structured text files, not office files.
* **Office-file work.** Document-grounded question answering and single-type file generation (presentations, spreadsheets).
* **Key people.** Teng Pan, Zixuan Wang, Yuchen Yan, Qifei Wu, Yao Chen, Yingqi Qu, Ruiqing Zhang, and Yongliang Shen, at Zhejiang University and Baidu Inc. (ZJU-REAL, the group behind [Actor, Judge, or Teacher?](2611.17167.md)). Likely critics: authors of other GDPval training pipelines seeded from O*NET, such as [GraphForge](2609.38923.md), who report GDPval on a different scale.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

A three-stage pipeline run by Qwen3.6-Plus, then two training stages.

1. **Workflow extraction.** Web-corpus segments (DLR-Web, filtered from FineFineWeb) are matched by discipline to O*NET occupational activity seeds. A segment that passes a workflow filter and a file-oriented filter becomes a blueprint: steps, information dependencies, required file types, expected outputs.
2. **Attachment searching.** A multi-turn loop writes search queries (with file-type filters), screens titles and snippets, downloads promising files, and inspects cover pages and tables of contents. Files over 50 pages or 2 MB are dropped. Only workflows whose retained files jointly cover the needed information go forward.
3. **Task synthesis.** In the sandbox, the model inspects the files, picks the attachment subset, and writes the instruction and rubric together. Claude-Opus-4.5 then solves the task; its trajectory becomes SFT data and its output the reference deliverable. A verifier checks the reference against the rubric: an unmet item that is unsupported or unreasonable is revised, a reasonable one sends the expert back once.
4. **Training.** Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 is fine-tuned on about 3k expert trajectories, then trained with [GRPO](../concepts/grpo.md) on about 1k disjoint tasks. The reward is a judge's rubric score divided by the maximum, zero if no deliverable is produced. The training judge is Qwen3.6-Plus reading rendered page images (and recalculated values and formulas for spreadsheets), not an agent with tools. No penalty is added for tool-call errors.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Benchmarks.** GDPval (220 tasks), split by deliverable type into office and non-office; OfficeQA in its Markdown version as an out-of-domain test; GTA-Workflow (132 workflows) in the appendix.
* **Scoring.** GDPval deliverables are scored against GDPval rubrics by an agent-as-a-judge with Gemini-3.1-Pro. Pass@k "scores the first attempt that produces a deliverable" among up to k attempts. OfficeQA combines rule matching and model assessment. Maximum 100 steps.
* **Baselines.** GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-Opus-4.5, DeepSeek-v3.1, GLM-4.7, and Qwen3 30B and 235B (text and VL), all rerun in the same sandbox and tool framework.
* **Analyses.** Completion rate, tool-call steps and errors per variant; rule-based shortcut metrics on trajectories and deliverables; real against generated files (1k matched SFT pairs); with and without rubric rewards; judge repeats with two judge models; SFT data scaling; a 4k-SFT control for the RL stage; a contamination audit; a 100-task manual audit.

The controls are well chosen: the 4k-SFT control separates RL from extra data, and the matched pairs isolate the files. Each is a single training run.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

About 4k tasks from 10k candidates (about 2.2k dropped at workflow extraction, about 3.8k at attachment search and verification). Attachments: 5,010 PDF, 3,149 DOCX, 1,258 XLSX, 390 PPTX; 2.51 per task on average. Rubrics average 15.3 items. Expert trajectories average 18.0 steps and 41.8k tokens. Manual audit of 100 tasks: workflow-question alignment 86%, rubric-item validity 99.8%, visual presentation 97%, cross-file consistency 95%, feasibility from instruction and attachments 85%. Of the specific numbers in rubrics, 41.9% appear in the instruction, 39.0% only in the attachments, and 19.1% in neither. The agent loop runs on verl v0.6.x in AIO Sandbox, with search through Serper. Code is linked at https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/Agent-R2; this note did not inspect it, and the text read does not say whether the 4k tasks and their files are released.

| Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking | 48.4 | 54.7 | 49.7 | 52.4 |
| Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 | 46.9 | 52.0 | 48.0 | 54.1 |
| Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 (base) | 31.1 | 37.8 | 32.4 | 37.0 |

| Agent-R2-4B | 51.5 | 48.9 | 51.0 | 54.8 |

**OfficeQA (Pass@1 / Pass@3).** Base 2.2 / 4.5; Qwen3-235B 13.8 / 25.6; SFT 11.4 / 21.7; Agent-R2-4B 14.8 / 26.8.

**Behavior on GDPval.** Tool-call steps per task go from 6.7 (base) to 39.0 (SFT) to 33.1 (RL), errors from 2.88 to 16.3 to 12.9, and completion rate from 86.8% to 78.2% to 90.9%. Re-checking the deliverable after writing rises from 0.5% to 91.7% to 95.8%; writing despite an unfixed error falls from 55.0% to 16.0% to 11.0%; placeholder text from 12.5% to 9.6% to 5.7%.

**Ablations and controls (GDPval Pass@1).** SFT on 1k tasks with real files 43.3 against 41.4 with generated files (expert trajectories use 20.4 against 8.0 tool calls). RL with rubric rewards 51.0 against 49.5 without. SFT on all 4k trajectories 45.5, against 45.3 for 3k SFT and 51.0 for 3k SFT plus 1k RL. SFT scaling: 43.3, 44.9, 45.5 at 1k, 2k, 4k, while the held-out synthesized score keeps rising (0.486, 0.507, 0.617).

**Judge stability.** Three repeats each: Agent-R2-4B 50.8 to 51.1 and Qwen3-235B 47.7 to 48.2 under Gemini-3.1-Pro; 51.7 to 52.3 and 49.2 to 49.9 under DeepSeek-v4-Flash; per-task Pearson correlation between judges 0.93.

**Limits on the evidence (reader observations).**

* "Pass@1" here is a mean rubric score, not a pass rate. The stability appendix reports per-task scores differing by a median of 3.2 points between repeats, which a binary outcome could not do. These numbers are not comparable with GDPval win rates or Elo ratings in other papers.
* The two component claims rest on 1.9 points (real files, SFT only, 1k tasks) and 1.5 points (rubric rewards), each from one training run. Judge repeats bound judge noise at a few tenths of a point but say nothing about training variance. The main text does not describe what the reward is in the no-rubric variant.
* The largest measured effect is the RL stage (5.7 points over 3k SFT, 5.5 over the 4k-SFT control). The data's distinguishing features, real files and rubrics, account for much less than the pipeline as a whole.
* The headline comparison to Qwen3-235B is 3.0 points at Pass@1 and 0.7 at Pass@3 (54.8 against 54.1). Claude-Opus-4.5, which wrote the SFT trajectories and references, scores 72.6, so the student recovers under half of the base-to-teacher gap (reader subtraction: 18.6 of 40.2 points).
* SFT lowers the completion rate (78.2% from 86.8%) while raising the score, and the SFT-only model is below Qwen3-235B on both benchmarks; the RL stage is what produces the headline.
* GDPval saturates under SFT scaling, which the paper reads as not overfitting the benchmark. It also means more of this data, used for imitation, has stopped helping there.
* Contamination audit: no shared file hashes or URLs; nearest-instruction cosine similarity to GDPval prompts averages 0.43, close to the 0.46 between a GDPval prompt and its nearest other GDPval prompt. Both use O*NET occupations, so the training set is near GDPval's task distribution by construction; OfficeQA and GTA-Workflow are the cleaner transfer tests.
* The manual audit finds 15% of tasks not completable from instruction and attachments, and 14% only partly aligned with their workflow. These stay in the training set.
* The paper links effective steps to GDPval score across models, and its own appendix reports that within a model longer trajectories score lower (Spearman −0.15 for SFT, −0.41 for RL). Reader subtraction from the reported means gives effective steps of about 3.8 (base), 22.7 (SFT), and 20.2 (RL).

The end-to-end result, a 4B model at 51.0 from 32.4 with all baselines rerun in one harness, is well supported for one training run. Attribution to real files and rubrics is weak.

# Q9 Contributions

1. A real-source synthesis pipeline for file-grounded productivity tasks, with structured filtering decisions at each stage and an attrition count.
2. About 4k tasks with authentic multi-format attachments, 15-item rubrics, expert trajectories, and reference deliverables, with a manual quality audit.
3. A two-stage training recipe whose RL stage uses a rendered-deliverable judge against rubrics, with a control showing the RL gain is not from extra data.
4. Rule-based shortcut metrics (re-checking, writing despite an unfixed error, placeholder text, traceable numbers) that describe how training changes behavior.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Training variance.** Repeat the real-file and rubric ablations over seeds; at 1.5 to 1.9 points they are the claims most in need of it (reader suggestion).
* **Judge coupling.** Qwen3.6-Plus writes the rubrics and scores the RL reward. A different training judge would test whether the policy learns the rubric author's preferences (reader suggestion).
* **RL share.** RL gives most of the gain from a quarter of the data. Shifting the 3k/1k split toward RL is an obvious experiment the paper does not run (reader suggestion).
* **Infeasible tasks.** Filtering the 15% infeasible tasks, or using them to train the agent to say what is missing, is untested.
* **Non-office deliverables.** Audio, video, and other media are outside the pipeline; RL helped more than SFT on them, which suggests general habits transfer where formats do not.
* **Larger students.** Everything is at 4B; whether the data still helps a model that already explores and re-checks is open.
