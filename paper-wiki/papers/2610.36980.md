# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** an LLM agent with 18 MCP tools (remote operations, physical execution, and a persistent notebook), a fixed system prompt, and a daily report on cash, net assets, stock, holidays, and each machine's inventory, sales, and expiry status. The world is a simulator of 20 vending machines (six worksite, two campus, four residential, six commercial, two office) that share one warehouse and a catalog of 1,372 products.
* **Output (Y):** 180 simulated days of decisions on assortment, per-machine prices, procurement, restocking visits, and disposal. The score is terminal net assets: cash plus unexpired inventory at cost on shelves, in the warehouse, and in transit, starting from 40,000 CNY.
* **Constraints:** orders are paid at placement and arrive after three days; shelf stock changes only through an on-site visit that costs 35 CNY per machine per day; batches expire 30 or 90 days after arrival and selling an expired unit costs a 1,000 CNY fine; warehouse rent is 0.03 CNY per unit per day and machine rent 4 CNY per day; each machine holds at most 200 units. Demand parameters and unmet demand are hidden from the agent.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Partly new. Long-horizon business benchmarks exist: Vending-Bench runs one vending machine, RetailBench one supermarket, MerchantBench and StoreBench one online store, and E-Commerce Bench up to four online stores with pooled inventory. The paper's Table 1 places AutoVend as the only one of these with 20 operating units of different types, real commercial data, and physical operations together. The new element is the combination: one agent must divide shared stock among heterogeneous sites while every physical action costs money and time.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If an agent must turn decisions made through a Web-style interface into physical retail outcomes across many sites, then terminal profit will depend on three abilities that single-site benchmarks do not separate: coordinating shared resources across sites, planning paid physical execution, and improving the operating policy from accumulated feedback. The paper expects that current LLM agents are weak enough on these that a classical inventory rule remains a hard baseline, and that operational metrics will explain where the money is lost.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

**A data-grounded demand model.** Each machine's daily purchase attempts are Poisson with a rate set by the total attraction of its shelf against a no-purchase weight, and each attempt picks a product in proportion to its attraction (a multinomial logit model). A product's attraction is a base value times a shelf-fullness factor, a penalty when fewer than five units are displayed, a price-elasticity term, and a weather and holiday multiplier. A day-to-day correlated shock makes demand more variable than Poisson. Base attractions are calibrated from six months of order-level records of 99 real machines (220,128 units sold): product popularity with empirical-Bayes shrinkage, site-type preferences, subcategory coefficients, and a per-machine scale.

**Assumed, not estimated.** Margins (30-40% for drinks, 50-60% for snacks), shelf lives, price elasticities (2.4 to 3.5 by site type), weather and holiday responses, and all operating costs are benchmark settings.

**The interface.** Tools mirror a networked-vending operator's platform over the [Model Context Protocol](../concepts/model-context-protocol.md): procurement, pricing, disposal, queries on products, stock, sales, and finances, read-only SQL, and calendar and weather lookups; two physical tools move stock between warehouse and shelves and trigger the visit fee; memory tools read and overwrite a notebook that survives [context compaction](../concepts/context-compaction.md) while the conversation history continues across days.

**Diagnostics beyond profit.** Assortment quality against a heuristic reference mix, waste rate, pricing regret against a price-search reference, shelf utilization, share of empty site-days, capital efficiency, tool calls, and net-asset growth per USD of estimated API cost; plus a reassignment gap (how much better the existing assortments would do if redistributed across sites) and returns per restocking cycle.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Agents.** Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Kimi K3, and GLM-5.3-Flash under the Pi harness at each model's highest reasoning effort; three runs per model, all in one frozen world with the same random seed.
* **Baseline.** A deterministic rule: newsvendor critical ratio with periodic order-up-to replenishment from rolling 30-day demand estimates, weekly site visits, assortments from observed sales, and prices from local gross-profit trials. It scores 208.73 ± 2.26 thousand CNY over seeds 1-3; seed 1 (210.97) is reported.
* **Simulator validation.** Parameters re-estimated before an eight-week held-out window, real shelves of 67 machines replayed at reference prices under neutral weather, 100 seeds, against a naive and a persistence reference.
* **Research questions.** RQ1 sustained operation (net-asset trajectories), RQ2 cross-site coordination (reassignment gap at six 30-day checkpoints), RQ3 physical execution (availability and visit-cycle returns over days 16-180), RQ4 improvement from feedback (days 16-45 against days 136-165), and four inspected failure cases.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

The simulator is built from sales records provided by a commercial smart vending company with its authorization. Appendix C says only the calibrated simulator parameters are released, not the raw records. Table 1 marks AutoVend as open source "upon publication", and `hf papers info` lists no repository, so nothing was available to check at reading time. The full system prompt, daily prompt, and tool specifications are in Appendices A and B.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported.**
  * *Most agents lose to a simple rule.* Mean terminal net assets in thousand CNY: GPT-6.1 Sol 309.35, rule-based 210.97, GLM-5.3-Flash 148.43, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 133.92, Claude Sonnet 5.5 103.04, Kimi K3 91.52. All three GPT runs exceed the baseline and none of the other 12 runs does; all 15 runs finish above the 40,000 CNY starting capital.
  * *Profit and cost-efficiency rank models differently.* DeepSeek earns 31,010 CNY of net-asset growth per USD of API cost against 1,117 for GPT; the paper says GPT's API spend is about 80 times DeepSeek's for about 2.9 times the growth.
  * *No model leads every dimension.* Claude has the lowest waste (0.16%) and pricing regret (1.32 CNY per machine per day) but empty shelves on 25.21% of site-days and the lowest assortment score (0.500); GPT has the highest profit and the largest pricing regret (30.18); every model's assortment score is below the baseline's 1.034.
  * *Demand model fits held-out sales better than simple references.* Total volume within 0.7%; machine-week WAPE 0.473 against 0.606 (naive) and 0.561 (persistence); subcategory TVD 0.153 against 0.206; median within-machine Spearman 0.577.
* **Weaker or narrower than the headline.**
  * *One world, one seed, three runs.* The paper states this limit itself. Kimi K3's standard deviation is 45.6% of its mean and DeepSeek's about 20% (reader calculation from Table 2), so the order of the four models below the baseline is not settled: their means span 91.52 to 148.43 with standard deviations of 11 to 42.
  * *The parts agents act on most are assumed.* Validation replays real shelves at reference prices; the paper says price and environmental responses "remain unvalidated by design". Any agent strategy that works through pricing is therefore scored against assumed elasticities (reader observation). A machine-week WAPE of 0.473 is also a large error in absolute terms, even though it beats both references.
  * *The reference metrics are heuristic.* Assortment and pricing regret are measured against a heuristic reference mix and a price search under fixed conditions, not against an optimum. GPT's large pricing regret alongside the highest profit shows that the metric and the outcome can disagree, as the paper notes.
  * *"Does not improve from feedback" is not identified.* Daily asset growth falls from the early to the late window for all five model means and 13 of 15 runs, but the paper says changing conditions and inventory states prevent a causal reading. The rule-based baseline's own change is shown only in Figure 6.
  * *Failure modes are single cases.* The four cases (49 rejected allocations in one Kimi run, a repeated 21-product purchase bundle, a 35 CNY visit for 5.14 CNY of gross profit, the same four infeasible allocations on days 163 and 168) are stated to illustrate behavior without establishing prevalence.
  * *One harness.* All models run under Pi with its compaction and the notebook tool; the paper does not test whether the weak models' results come from the model or from how this harness manages 180 days of history (reader observation).
  * *API cost is estimated.* API efficiency uses standard reference rates on recorded token usage, not billed amounts.

# Q9 Contributions

* A multi-site, shared-inventory, paid-execution business benchmark with a unified task, tool interface, and evaluation protocol.
* A demand simulator calibrated on real vending records, with a held-out check of volume, mix, and product ranking.
* An evaluation showing four of five current agents below a classical inventory rule, with operational diagnostics (reassignment gap, visit-cycle returns, early against late growth) that locate losses in coordination, execution cost, and policy revision.
* A cost-efficiency view (net-asset growth per API dollar) that reorders the models.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Vary the world.** The authors call for varied operating conditions and controlled interventions such as explicit state verification and persistent policy updates. Several world seeds and demand regimes would show whether GPT-6.1 Sol's lead and the others' deficit hold.
* **Validate price response (reader suggestion).** Price experiments in the source data, or sensitivity runs over the assumed elasticities, would show how much of each agent's result depends on those settings.
* **Hybrid agents (reader suggestion).** Give the agent the newsvendor rule as a tool or a starting policy. If agents then match or beat the rule, the gap is missing operations-research knowledge; if not, it is long-horizon execution.
* **Harness ablations (reader suggestion).** Run the same models with a different memory design, since two of the four failure cases are about not carrying a correction forward.
* **Reader connection.** FDE-Gym (arXiv:2610.22350 in this wiki) cites Vending-Bench as a business simulation that replaces human judges; AutoVend adds the cost-per-outcome view that such delivery benchmarks need.
