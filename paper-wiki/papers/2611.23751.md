# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** an agent following a procedural skill over a multi-step episode, with each workflow step typed as D (deterministic, runs as code), G (generative, needs the large model), or E (enumerable: read unstructured state and pick one option from a finite set). An E step is a decision point template (question, options with one-sentence definitions, plus "none of the above"), instantiated with the runtime state.
* **Output (Y):** for each E step, a probability distribution over the options from a small model (System-1) in one forward pass, and a gate that either adopts its top choice or defers to the large model (System-2).
* **Constraints:** success must stay non-inferior to running every E and G step on System-2; the System-1 must work on skills and benchmarks it never saw; thresholds are fixed before test runs. Savings are bounded by the share of cost spent at E steps minus the System-1 overhead.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Dual-system agents with a typed decision model as System-1 already exist for search and specific tool harnesses, and the paper cites several (SearchJev, AgentDecision, the commercial Jev). In those, engineers hand-pick the decision points of one fixed workflow and train on them. The new step is the source of supervision: procedural skills already say where a choice arises and among which options, so decision points can be mined from a public skill corpus once and one model trained for all of them. Compiling skills or SOPs into programs is also prior work; the paper combines it with a learned, calibrated scorer at the semantic branches.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If decision points are mined from many public skills and paired with states from real executions, one compact model trained on them will make the enumerable decisions of unseen workflows accurately enough, and with confidence calibrated enough, that a single threshold decides when to trust it, cutting cost and latency without lowering task success.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Agent skills.** SKILL.md skills loaded on demand; studies of how agents retrieve, follow, and internalize skills (SkillRL among them); synthesis of tasks and trajectories from skill corpora.
* **Workflow compilation.** Natural-language procedures into explicit programs, SOPs into pseudo-code, skills into state machines or runtime interfaces, recurring paths into rules.
* **Typed decision models.** Commercial Jev (TypeSafe AI); open-weight pointer or decision heads (Kev, SearchJev, AgentDecision); debiased probabilities through cyclic permutations (AnyJev).
* **Selective deferral and cascades.** Selective classification, LLM cascades and routing, earlier fast-slow agents with a small generator.
* **Calibration.** Temperature and label-smoothing studies.
* **Key people.** Rui Li, Liyang He, Zhenya Huang, Qi Liu (University of Science and Technology of China), Zheng Zhang (Nanyang Technological University). Likely critics: authors of delegation-boundary studies, who would point at the ALFWorld collapse without the gate and ask how the method behaves on workflows that cannot be compiled.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Mine decision points.** An LLM analyzer reads each skill and its bundled documents three times and returns typed candidate decision points, each with a verbatim supporting quote checked by exact string match. Equivalent points are merged; E points found in at least two of three passes and answerable from state alone are kept.
2. **Get real states.** For each skill an LLM writes self-contained multi-stage tasks with input files, a reference solution, a hidden verifier, and two distractor skills; a task is kept only if, in a network-isolated container, the verifier rejects the initial files and accepts the reference solution. An OpenHands agent executes each task; an LLM locator aligns the trajectory to the skill's decision points and copies the preceding observations verbatim.
3. **Label by order-invariant consensus.** Two teacher passes per state, the second with reversed option order; only states where both agree are kept.
4. **Train one pointer model.** Qwen3.5-4B-Base with an option-interaction head: the hidden states at a decision token and at each option's closing token pass through a position-free bidirectional attention layer, then each option is matched against the decision state. Loss is cross-entropy with label smoothing ε = 0.1.
5. **Gate.** Adopt System-1's choice when its top probability is at least τ, the smallest threshold with 95% agreement on calibration decisions. AnyS1-cold uses one global τ = 0.918 fitted on 153 examples from held-out development skills. AnyS1-warm fits τ per benchmark from 50 unlabeled development episodes in which System-2 answers every E step and System-1's scores are recorded alongside.

All offline LLM steps use DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Benchmarks (no skill overlap with training).** SOPBench: 268 held-out customer-service tasks, compiled deterministically from machine-readable SOPs. ALFWorld: 134 unseen tasks, compiled from SkillRL skill banks into six task-family programs. WebShop: 500 instructions, compiled from SkillRL skills into seven category programs.
* **System-2.** DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (primary) and GPT-6 Sol; System-1 weights are unchanged across the two.
* **Configurations.** Uncompiled System-2 agent reading skills (the SOPBench reference agent; ReAct with SkillRL skills elsewhere), compiled workflow with System-2 at every E and G step (All-S2), commercial Jev as System-1, AnyS1-warm, AnyS1-cold, and ungated All-S1.
* **Metrics.** Pass, success rate, WebShop score; per-episode cost saving against the uncompiled agent (API list prices for System-2 and Jev; the self-hosted 4B model priced at $0.0045 per 1K decisions from L4 GPU throughput); mean critical-path model-call time. All configurations share an exact System-2 request cache with All-S2, so runs diverge only where System-1 answers.
* **Ablations.** Gate; executed states against LLM-written state summaries; label smoothing against hard-label CE and CE + Brier; operating curves against equal-rate random delegation; number of calibration episodes.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

Any-DP is built from 1,072 public skills (16 categories) of the SkillMD-138K corpus: 17,865 typed decision points (21.3% D, 64.5% E, 14.2% G), 2,846 container-verified tasks, 25,986 consensus-labeled states, 89.2% teacher agreement. The contributions paragraph says Any-DP is released, and a project page is given under the author list (https://middle-chunjie.github.io/AnyS1/); this note did not open it or verify what is available.

**Ablations.**

* Without the gate (All-S1), SOPBench is unchanged (84.3) and WebShop slips to 66.3, but ALFWorld falls from 96.3% to 40.3%.
* Executed states against LLM-written summaries, three seeds: 83.8 against 65.0 on the held-out container development set (185 states), 93.5 against 91.7 on SOPBench E decisions, 68.9 against 63.6 on ALFWorld E decisions.
* Label smoothing against hard-label CE, one seed: accuracy 82.4 against 82.3, ECE 0.094 against 0.146, gated agreement 97.7% against 92.4%; without smoothing the fitted threshold inflates toward 0.998.
* At the warm threshold ALFWorld delegates 50% of E decisions at 92.4% agreement with System-2 (68.2% for a random subset of the same size), WebShop 41% at 84.3% (69.8%), SOPBench 98% (91% overall agreement). The cold threshold delegates 40% on SOPBench at 100% agreement and 55% on WebShop at 80.0%. Twenty development episodes match the full 50-episode fit within 1 point of delegation.
* System-1 scores a decision in 21.8 to 31.7 ms median against 1.51 to 1.78 s for a System-2 E call.

**Limits on the evidence.**

* Most of the headline saving is compilation, not System-1. All-S2 already saves 76% to 86% (Flash) and 56% to 86% (Sol); AnyS1-warm adds 5 to 10 points on Flash (reader arithmetic from Table 2) and up to 19 on Sol (ALFWorld, 56 to 75). The abstract's "75–91%" is relative to the uncompiled agent. The paper's own Eq. 1 analysis says the same: after compilation, E steps hold 17% to 22% of uncompiled cost on ALFWorld and WebShop and 4% on SOPBench.
* The decision points that System-1 answers at test time come from compiled programs of SOPs and SkillRL skill banks. Automatic compilation of open-ended repository skills is listed as future work, so "any agent workflow" currently means any workflow someone has compiled into D/E/G steps.
* Labels and the gate target are agreement with System-2 (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash teachers; τ set for 95% agreement), not ground-truth correctness; task success is the only ground-truth check. Delegated WebShop decisions agree with System-2 84.3% of the time, yet the score does not fall, which suggests many disagreements are harmless or System-2 is not always right there (reader inference).
* Effectiveness differences among compiled configurations are within about one point on 134 to 500 tasks, with no intervals or repeated runs reported for the online results; one ALFWorld task is 0.75 points (reader arithmetic). "Preserves reliability" is supported; the +0.5 average-point edge is not distinguishable from noise.
* The gain of compilation over the uncompiled agent (about 17 points on average) is not this paper's contribution to explain, and the uncompiled baselines differ by benchmark (reference agent or ReAct).
* The warm setting costs 50 development episodes with System-2 at every E step per workflow (stated).

# Q9 Contributions

1. The idea of mining System-1 supervision from procedural skills, and Any-DP, a dataset of typed decision points with executed states and consensus labels.
2. One label-smoothed 4B pointer model with a confidence gate that transfers to three unseen benchmarks and a second System-2 family without retraining.
3. Analyses showing that executed states beat LLM-written ones, that calibration matters more for the gate than for raw accuracy, that an ungated System-1 breaks long-horizon search, and that the E-step share sets the savings ceiling.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Stated by the paper.** Adapt deferral thresholds online from deferred System-2 calls; automatically compile open-ended repository skills.
* **Uncompiled use.** Test System-1 at decision points inside an ordinary skill-reading agent loop, where the saving per decision is larger because nothing has been compiled away, and where locating the decision point at run time is the hard part (reader suggestion).
* **Ground-truth labels.** Where a hidden verifier exists (the 2,846 synthesized tasks), label decisions by which branch leads to verified success, to see how often teacher consensus is wrong (reader suggestion).
* **For this repository.** The D/E/G typing is a practical lens for auditing the OS's own skills: steps that are fixed commands could be scripts, and steps that pick among listed options are candidates for a cheap model with a fallback (reader inference).
