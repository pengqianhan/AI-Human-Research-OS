# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** telemetry from two production autonomous-LLM-trading-agent systems built by the same lab on one design lineage — DX Terminal Pro (3,505 user-funded vaults, one frozen Qwen3-235B agent per vault, trading real ETH across 12 Base memecoin pools, 21 days, Feb-Mar 2026, 7.5M invocations / ~300K onchain actions) and the DXAP live alpha fleet (500-599 user-created agents, mostly OpenRouter-mix models with qwen3.7-plus dominant, mostly paper-traded but including 5,035 real-money fills, Hyperliquid perpetuals, Jun-Aug 2026, 231,638 finalized turns / 14,596 fills) — joined at turn id, attempt id, and the prompt-template/config revision resolved at that turn.
* **Output (Y):** a population-scale empirical record answering what determines agent trading behavior (configuration surfaces vs. model reasoning vs. strategy text), whether sizing responds to risk, how much favorable price movement agents actually capture, whether either fleet or any tested frontier model shows a measurable edge, and a 17-rule methodology canon distilled from the program's own three prior retractions.
* **Core problem:** by mid-2026 autonomous LLM agents routinely hold and move real capital in public markets, and a growing evaluation literature scores them mostly by leaderboard P&L over hour-to-day horizons; what is missing is a sustained, population-scale account of what thousands of such agents actually do once given configuration surfaces, strategy text, and capital, and which parts of the surrounding system — not the model — determine that behavior.
* **Scope:** two systems from one lab, ~6 months of continuous telemetry (with a 2-month gap between deployments), one memecoin venue (DX Terminal Pro) and one perpetuals venue (DXAP), predominantly one dominant model family (Qwen) in each fleet; a separate paired-replay league adds a narrower cross-model decision-quality comparison (3 frontier models, 416 captured scenarios).

# Q2 Problem Novelty

The paper does not claim to originate LLM trading-agent evaluation — it positions itself against leaderboard-style live-capital tournaments (nof1's Alpha Arena), finer task-decomposed evaluation (KTD-Fin), shared-simulated-market multi-agent studies (Agent Market Arena), the reference multi-agent role architecture many systems build on ([TradingAgents](2412.20138.md)), and a survey of the broader LLM-financial-trading-agent literature (Xia et al.). Its stated novelty is access: none of those has the user-to-agent-to-execution trace at population scale that this paper's two production systems provide, letting it treat "the operating layer" — sliders, prompt templates, rendered market surfaces, order-path mechanics — as the experimental variable rather than a black box behind aggregate P&L. Relative to the authors' own prior work (which introduced DX Terminal Pro's architecture and five failure modes but explicitly deferred "deeper analysis of the live user-to-agent-to-execution traces"), this paper is stated as answering that deferral and extending it to the successor DXAP system, adding herd-return/cascade economics, a production memory-fabrication trajectory, the full DXAP volatility/selection/liquidation/capture record, a cross-system text-vs-constraint contrast, and the methodology canon.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

The paper is a measurement program rather than a single confirmatory hypothesis, but its four headline claims are each falsifiable empirical propositions tested against day-clustered inference, permutation nulls, and a common-fee restatement: (1) that operating-layer mechanics (a risk slider, agent identity, a leaderboard render boundary) explain agent behavior better than strategy text or market state; (2) that position sizing does not respond to realized market volatility even though it should if agents were managing risk adaptively; (3) that agents systematically fail to retain favorable price excursion they reach, i.e. capture is close to chance even though the market state that produces the excursion is itself fairly predictable; and (4) that no tested population or frontier model shows a directional trading edge, and that frontier-model choices are statistically indistinguishable in decision quality at a short replay horizon even though they differ sharply in how repeatable those choices are across identical repeats.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Two systems, one design lineage, metric-level pooling.** Both systems share the same five-slider configuration (activity, risk tolerance, size, holding style, diversification, each 1-5) plus free-text strategy, a one-action-per-turn loop with typed tool calls, and a continuous Go-template prompt lineage — but differ in schema, so the paper pools at the metric level (slider-to-behavior mappings, trade rate, hold time, win rate, fee burden) rather than at the row level, with historical joins keyed on turn id + attempt id + the template/config revision resolved *at that turn*, never the current roster template.
2. **Evidence-class discipline.** Every claim is tagged firm (registered analysis, day-clustered uncertainty, at least one ablation/permutation check), provisional (positive but narrow or qualified), or weak/retracted (kept only to document a failure mode already caught and reversed) — three of the program's own prior results are retracted and appear only as retractions, including a timestamp-artifact edge and two "only net-positive posture/edition" claims killed by calendar-footprint checks.
3. **Regression-discontinuity isolation of a render-boundary cause.** Rather than only correlating rendered-symbol visibility with selection (46.5% of entries land in the 9 rendered "movers" symbols against an 8.9% random-availability baseline, a 5.2× over-selection), a regression discontinuity across the rank-3/rank-4 render cutoff isolates the render boundary itself as causal (1.75× selection jump exactly at the cut, symbols just below being statistically identical in market state) — the paper's self-described "one clean causal result."
4. **Volatility-sextile and posture×frequency stratification for risk analysis.** Splitting 6,400 closed DXAP positions into volatility sextiles (Spearman(volatility, leverage) ≈ 0 despite a 5.7× volatility spread) and stratifying liquidations by strategy posture × trade-frequency slider (a single momentum×frequency-5 cell, ~11% of the book, holds 62% of liquidations at day-stratified Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio 22.37) localizes two distinct risk defects to specific, actionable configuration surfaces rather than to "the model."
5. **Excursion-vs-realized-return decomposition and a mechanical-bracket counterfactual.** Comparing each position's maximum favorable excursion within 24h against its realized return isolates a capture gap (43.2% of positions reach ≥+300 bps MFE, 49.3% of those still close negative) as distinct from a prediction problem (upside is nearly as predictable pre-trade as liquidation risk, ROC 0.751 vs 0.783, but capture itself is near-chance, ROC 0.541); a paired, day-clustered comparison then shows a fixed 2%/4% stop/target bracket recovers +39.0 bps/position, with ~60% of the gain from blow-up prevention and the remainder from improved giveback on ordinary positions.
6. **A 17-rule methodology canon and an H2 preregistered probe.** Each canon rule (Section 9) is tied to a specific retraction or failed claim the program made (e.g. "the market day is the inferential unit, never the trade," "compare at a common fee rate," "a rolling p-value read many times is not a p-value"), offered as the paper's most transferable output; a separate preregistered probe (H2) tests whether a small neural encoder reading the rendered pre-action turn card carries liquidation information beyond 25 concrete features, finding a real but order-line-gated increment (no signal before the order is written, then a measurable jump) that the paper explicitly restricts to "a neural encoder carries information," never "the agent knew."

# Q6 Experiment Design

DX Terminal Pro: 3,505 funded vaults (3,454 active in the measured window), each running one frozen Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 agent (SGLang, temp 0.6) at ~5-minute cadence emitting exactly one tool call (buy/sell/observe), trading real ETH across 12 memecoin tokens on Uniswap V4 pools on Base under a 2.3%/swap fee, for 21 days (Feb 26-Mar 18, 2026); telemetry lives in Athena, reconciled against onchain vault state, with a public Dune dashboard. DXAP live alpha: 500-599 agents all-history (91-117 concurrently active), an OpenRouter model mix dominated by qwen3.7-plus (86/91 active agents on Jul 22), trading ~99-100 Hyperliquid perpetuals (including HIP-3 synthetics) on 15m/30m/1h/4h schedules plus agent-created triggers through a 10-tool manifest with mandatory finalize_turn, mostly paper-traded at a $10,000 starting balance with live prices plus 5,035 real-money fills (6 of 19 real-money users, $14-$2,000 notionals) that are flagged throughout and never pooled silently with paper fills; telemetry lives in a ClickHouse analytics store, and cross-era P&L is restated at a common 5.5 bps/side fee because the builder fee moved across eras (4.5→14.5→5.5 bps/side).

**Population and benchmark comparison (Sec. 3).** DX Terminal Pro was bursty and social: 1,544 of 3,454 active vaults bought the same token (FEET) within one hour on Mar 1 (peak 441 buys/min), and of 908 launch-day buyers the 82 still holding at measurement were 89% underwater (mean P&L −20.1%); 3,878 sell cascades (≥10 vaults/10min) were recorded, 92.9% of trades fell in two-sided 5-minute windows, portfolio Jaccard similarity rose 0.304→0.473 over the run, median true return was 0.492×, 16.2% of vaults finished profitable, and 92% of deposited ETH was withdrawn. Against a matched Hyperliquid retail benchmark (1,961 leaderboard traders, $1K-$100K equity band), the DXAP fleet's trailing 4-day roundtrip win rate is 41% vs. retail's 50% at nearly identical hold times (2.05h vs 2.18h); only 15% of week-active agents are net-positive vs. 53% of retail; the fleet leans structurally long (~78% of entries vs. ~60%) at 4-5× the effective leverage (median chosen leverage 5.0×); cumulative realized P&L is −$217K at the common 5.5 bps fee (−$148K at zero fee, so fees are not the explanation for losses); the fleet's one relative edge is horizon (24h+ positions earn +0.23% median account return vs. −0.15% for sub-1h positions).

**Operating layer determines behavior (Sec. 4).** The riskTolerance slider maps to leverage at +0.425×/level (p=3.8×10⁻²⁷⁹); agent fixed effects absorb 60% of leverage variance; user-named leverage in strategy text tracks realized leverage at Spearman ρ=+0.836. The render-boundary RDD gives a 1.75× [1.49, 2.06] selection jump at the rank-3/4 cut. The two systems resolve slider-vs-text conflicts oppositely — DX Terminal Pro's sliders bound even insistent strategy text (3.95% vs 1.51% invocation trade rate at the lowest activity setting, elevated but bounded), while a separate DXAP user-behavior study found strategy text routinely overriding the frequency slider — and DX Terminal Pro owners who wrote concrete numeric instructions were profitable 4.2× as often as the median, with the 87 UI-only owners who never used chat the highest-profit cohort (41% in profit).

**Risk behavior (Sec. 5).** Across 6 volatility sextiles (1,067 positions each) spanning a 5.7× volatility range, median leverage is 5.0× in every sextile (Spearman(vol, leverage)=−0.001, p=0.92) while notional moves the wrong way (Spearman(vol, notional)=+0.165, p=2.7×10⁻⁴⁰); median realized return degrades from −10.6 to −98.2 bps and liquidation rate rises 0.7%→4.3% across sextiles, holding even excluding all 205 liquidations (−8.5 to −53.6 bps, ρ=−0.104, p=1.8×10⁻¹⁶). Liquidations concentrate 62% (128/205) in one posture×frequency-slider cell (~11% of the book, day-stratified Mantel-Haenszel OR 22.37 [12.59, 37.45]), with zero liquidations across 411 momentum-posture positions at frequency <5. A forced-numeric-restatement intervention (requiring the model to state liquidation distance before entry) fails outright: agents state it 45.0% of the time and size identically regardless, and staters liquidate more often (5.8% vs 1.2%). The preregistered H2 probe finds a small +0.0150 PR-AUC increment (90% day-clustered CI [+0.0013, +0.0312]) over a 25-feature concrete baseline (PR-AUC 0.1145) that appears only after the order line is read in the turn card (pre-order anchors all span zero), so the deployable form is a post-order screening check (catches 71/205 liquidations vs. 57/205 for concrete features at a fixed 10% flagging budget), not a pre-decision warning.

**Capture and exits (Sec. 6).** 43.2% of 6,400 closed positions reach ≥+300 bps max favorable excursion (MFE) within 24h; 49.3% of those close negative; only 13.3% keep at least half the excursion; book-wide median MFE is +247.9 bps against median realized −33.3 bps. Upside is predictable from pre-trade state (ROC 0.751) nearly as well as liquidation risk (ROC 0.783), but capture itself is near-chance (ROC 0.541); selecting for predicted upside currently worsens P&L (top predicted-run quintile: median MFE +410 bps, median realized −71.0 bps). A fixed 2%/4% stop/target bracket earns +39.0 bps/position [+21.3, +56.5] paired and day-clustered (+16.6 [+2.1, +30.8] excluding all liquidations), ~60% of the gain from blow-up prevention (+717 bps across the 205 liquidations); its current operational blocker is a non-retryable trigger-quota error that left 24 of 35 successful opens unprotected in one 48-hour window.

**Null results (Sec. 7) and model league (Sec. 8.4).** No information source tested shows a directional edge (a 77K-candidate signal ledger sorts at chance; a market_research sub-agent's recommendations are null at n=120; agent selection across horizons is at chance, ρ=+0.018, p=0.93); no group among 8 postures × 6 templates × 9 cohorts is day-clustered positive; chase-state entries (>+0.75%/1h) occur at 2.46× availability but show negative forward-4h return, corroborated as a cross-system null on 4,096 DX Terminal Pro entries; memory-write frequency correlates negatively with P&L (ρ=−0.200) and includes documented "ghost strategy" and funding-fabrication failure modes. A June 2026 paired-replay league of 3 frontier models on 416 captured production scenarios (day→8h-block hierarchical bootstrap) finds all three span 263.27-264.37 bps of regret with every interval overlapping and Holm-adjusted tests against the leader all non-significant (min p=0.46); a forced-action arm (280 cells) finds claude-fable-5 changes its chosen action across temperature-0.6 repeats in 35% of cells vs. ~90-95% for the qwen3.7 pair, while selection quality across repeats is at chance (rank percentile 0.486-0.508) for all three — consistency and correctness are reported as separate axes, only the first separating the models — and qwen3.7-plus completed the league for $8.25 in inference spend vs. $203.61 for claude-fable-5.

# Q9 Contributions

1. A continuous, population-scale (~7.5M invocations, ~300K onchain actions, 231,638 multi-tool turns, 14,596 fills) six-month measurement record of two production LLM trading-agent fleets sharing one design lineage, joined at turn-level template/config revision, with explicit firm/provisional/retracted evidence tagging.
2. Causal and near-causal evidence that operating-layer mechanics dominate behavior over model reasoning or strategy text: a risk slider fixing leverage (+0.425×/level) with agent identity absorbing 60% of variance, and a regression-discontinuity-isolated leaderboard render boundary routing selection 1.75× at the rank-3/4 cut — plus a cross-system finding that which surface (slider vs. text) wins a conflict is itself an undocumented, system-specific design choice.
3. Two localized, actionable risk defects: volatility-blind sizing (median leverage flat at 5.0× across a 5.7× volatility spread) and liquidation concentration in one posture×frequency-slider cell (62% of all liquidations, OR 22.37), each traced to a specific configuration surface rather than "bad decisions."
4. A capture-gap decomposition separating a solved prediction problem (favorable excursion is fairly predictable, ROC 0.751) from an unsolved capture problem (near-chance realized retention, ROC 0.541), with a mechanical stop/target bracket (+39.0 bps/position) as the single best-measured behavioral lever in the record.
5. Population-scale null results (no directional edge from any tested information source; no day-clustered-positive group across 8 postures × 6 templates × 9 cohorts) paired with a paired-replay finding that three frontier models are statistically indistinguishable in decision quality at this horizon (min Holm-adjusted p=0.46) while differing sharply in choice-repeatability and ~25× in inference cost — reframing "which model to use" as a solved-by-cost-not-quality question at this horizon.
6. A 17-rule methodology canon for agent-trading research, each rule tied to a specific retraction or failed claim in the program's own history, offered as the paper's most transferable output independent of either fleet's specific results.

# Q10 Next Steps

The paper's own Sec. 8 (Toward an Edge) is itself an explicit next-steps program rather than a result: it argues, from the evidence above, that harness/order-path engineering should be invested in first (atomic open-with-protection order path, tool-level volatility-scaled leverage, deliberate render A/B — all cheap, none requiring a better model); that tool surfaces (including sub-agents) should be kept only if they demonstrably change behavior against a null arm, citing the market_research sub-agent's own null result as grounds for demoting it from an informational to a behavioral surface; that a post-order representation check and a decision-provenance ledger should be deployed as the concrete, deployable form of the H2 probe; and that model differentiation should shift from model shopping (shown to buy nothing measurable at this horizon) to training on the 231,638-turn production record itself, previewing an early, explicitly "internal, not externally validated" SFT signal (DX-SFT-0.3) and a stated next step of live-branch GRPO reinforcement on branched production-turn continuations, with the canon serving as the reward-audit layer.

Beyond the paper's own framing, three points are worth flagging for a reader assessing this note's evidence. First, the model-decision league (Sec. 8.4) is explicitly labeled replay evidence under a frozen context, and the paper is careful to state it "says nothing about live P&L" — a reader should not extrapolate its null cross-model finding to a claim that model choice never matters in live deployment, only that it did not separate decision quality at this specific 416-scenario, 7-day, temperature-0.6 replay horizon. Second, because DXAP's real-money book (5,035 fills, 6 of 19 real-money users) is deliberately pooled with paper trades under flags rather than analyzed separately throughout, a reader who wants a real-capital-only version of the volatility-blindness or capture-gap findings would need to request that decomposition; it is not reported as a standalone table in this paper. Third, the "restraint beats added information" template finding (Sec. 7, explicitly provisional) is flagged by the authors themselves as exactly the shape of claim ("only net-positive X") their own canon has previously forced them to retract — it is presented candidly as awaiting replication on a fresh window rather than as an established result, a distinction worth preserving in any downstream citation of this paper.
