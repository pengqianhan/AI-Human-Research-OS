# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a natural-language instruction `x` and a computer-use environment `E` (an OS with installed applications and files) that the agent operates directly via screenshots and GUI actions; optionally, a multimodal demonstration `d` of the same or a related task recorded from a human or a stronger agent.
* **Output (Y):** a trajectory `τ = (x, o_1, y_1, ..., o_T, y_T)` of observation/response pairs, scored by an executable verifier `R(τ) ∈ {0,1}` that inspects the final state of `E`; when a demonstration is supplied, the agent additionally emits a `subtask_complete` control action that advances a pointer through the demonstration's segmented subtask structure.
* **Core problem:** foundation GUI agents face a training bottleneck (data scarcity/bias — trajectories are only useful when their initial state is instantiable, actions executable, and outcomes verifiable, and cheap-to-generate short single-app tasks dominate the corpus) and an interaction bottleneck (routine workflows depend on user-specific tools and tacit conventions that instructions leave implicit, so an agent that succeeds once may resolve the same ambiguity differently and fail next time). How can a GUI agent be trained at scale on genuinely diverse, verifiable long-horizon data, and how can it use an optional demonstration to resolve procedural ambiguity without being reduced to rigid trajectory replay?
* **Scope:** desktop/office computer-use agents (Linux/macOS sandboxes plus native app control); the demonstration mechanism is explicitly a *prior*, not a target — the live screenshot always retains veto power over the demonstrated steps, and the policy must remain competent in the no-demonstration regime (`g_t = ∅`).

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Foundation CUA systems (UI-TARS, Qwen-UI-Agent) and human-taught GUI agents that expose reusable procedural knowledge from screen recordings (ShowUI-Aloha) already exist, as do environment-grounded data-scaling efforts (AgentNet, ScaleCUA, InfiniteWeb, CUA-Gym). UI-Mate's novelty is treating a demonstration as a segmented, subtask-level *workflow prior* rather than either a full instruction-conditioning signal or a literal action sequence to replay: it converts a recording offline into subtasks with natural-language goals, verifiable completion criteria, and advisory (non-pixel-coordinate) action descriptions, then lets the live observation override any workflow step that is stale, missing, or misaligned. The paper also introduces OSWorkerBench specifically to isolate this contribution — a controlled paired protocol (same instruction/environment/budget/verifier, only demonstration availability changes) that existing instruction-only benchmarks (OSWorld, WindowsAgentArena) cannot measure.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If a closed-loop, environment-grounded data pipeline (task generation, environment construction, rollout filtering, capability-tree-driven rebalancing) feeds SFT and online agentic RL, and the resulting policy is additionally trained to condition on a workflow view `g_t = (d, n_t)` — a progress checklist showing all subtask goals with completed/current/upcoming markers, but only the active subtask's detailed action steps — rather than replaying a demonstration verbatim, then: (1) the base training stack alone yields open-weight state-of-the-art general computer-use results; and (2) adding one relevant demonstration substantially and consistently raises both strict task success and partial progress on long-horizon tasks, more than average completion — it improves the *consistency* with which the agent resolves underspecified intent. The paper tests both claims on OSWorld-Verified, WindowsAgentArena, and the new OSWorkerBench (self-demo setting: demonstration and target are the *same* task), but explicitly does not yet establish the harder variant-demo claim — that demonstrations transfer procedural structure across *non-identical* related tasks — reporting only a preliminary, not-yet-benchmark-stable pilot on 10 of 45 variant-demo targets.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Foundation CUA / GUI agents:** UI-TARS, UI-TARS-2, Qwen-UI-Agent — vision-language models that perceive interfaces, ground actions, and execute extended on-screen workflows. UI-Mate builds its base policy in this lineage (on a Qwen3.6-27B base) but adds the demonstration-conditioning mechanism and the environment-grounded training stack.
* **Scalable GUI training data:** AgentNet, ScaleCUA (cross-platform trajectory scaling), InfiniteWeb (synthetic web environments), CUA-Gym (verifiable training environments). UI-Mate's data pipeline draws on these as raw instruction sources but adds a hierarchical capability tree specifically to diagnose and rebalance coverage gaps (long-horizon, cross-application, error-recovery tasks are systematically underrepresented in cheaply-generated corpora).
* **Human-taught / demonstration-guided agents:** ShowUI-Aloha shows screen recordings expose reusable procedural knowledge. UI-Mate's DemoCUA differs by converting a recording into a flexible subtask-level workflow (with an explicit `subtask_complete` control action and observation-driven pointer advance) rather than a step-by-step script.
* **Benchmarks:** OSWorld, WindowsAgentArena (general instruction-only computer use, which UI-Mate also evaluates on for its non-demonstration claim); OSWorkerBench is the paper's own new benchmark.
* **Organization:** Tencent Hy (Hunyuan) Frontier Team; core/co-lead contributors include Longxu Dou, Zihang Jiang, Lei Ke, Weixian Lei, Yingchen Yu (marked project co-leads/lead/supervisor in the author-contributions section).

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Environment-grounded data pipeline (closed loop).** Task instructions are drawn from four complementary sources: open-source CUA datasets (AgentNet, ScaleCUA), atomic subtasks decomposed from failed/stalled rollouts (targeting operations agents demonstrably struggle with), instructions grounded in real documents/spreadsheets/presentations/websites (concrete entities, long-horizon multi-app workflows), and capability-tree-driven generation (surfacing fine-grained operations everyday workflows would otherwise skip). A hierarchical capability tree extracted from rollout diagnostics identifies underrepresented applications/operations/workflows and feeds back into later task/environment construction — a data flywheel rather than a one-shot corpus.
2. **Training stack.** SFT teaches the interaction protocol, visual grounding, application workflows, and multi-step execution; online agentic RL (with trajectory-to-token credit assignment, asynchronous group-relative optimization, and adaptive curriculum sampling) then trains the policy directly against executable task-verifier bundles across massively parallel environments.
3. **DemoCUA formalization.** A demonstration `d = (s_1,...,s_N)` is segmented offline into subtasks `s_n = (φ_n, v_n, u_n)`: a natural-language goal `φ_n`, a verifiable completion criterion `v_n`, and an ordered sequence of advisory (non-executable, no pixel coordinates) action descriptions `u_n`. At each live step the agent conditions on a workflow *view* `g_t = (d, n_t)` — the full list of subtask goals annotated completed/current/upcoming, but detailed action steps only for the currently active subtask — never the whole raw recording at once. The action space is extended with `subtask_complete`; emitting it (once the live observation `o_t` satisfies `v_{n_t}`) advances the pointer `n_t`. The live screenshot retains veto power throughout: the correct action is whatever the current observation demands, and `g_t = ∅` (no demonstration) is treated as the base case the policy must remain competent in.
4. **Training for graceful demonstration use.** Training examples deliberately cover cases where the demonstration agrees with, diverges from, or is entirely unrelated to the current interface state, so the model learns to treat the workflow as guidance to follow, skip, or override — not a fixed script to execute blind.
5. **OSWorkerBench + unified harness.** 100 long-horizon office tasks across 41 normalized applications and 10 job families (67 Long-Memory tasks requiring delayed reuse of dynamic information; 49 Multi-App tasks requiring information transfer across ≥3 applications), supporting instruction-only and demonstration-guided evaluation with two demonstration regimes (33-task self-demo from successful strong-agent rollouts of the same target; 45-task variant-demo from human recordings of related-but-different source tasks). A shared harness (observation-reasoning-action loop, history management, platform adapters) connects the same trained policy to both benchmark environments and UI-Mate App's live desktop deployment, where a user can record their own demonstration and immediately run the agent against it.

# Q6 Experiment Design

**General computer use** (instruction-only, no demonstration): UI-Mate-27B evaluated against closed-weight models, other open-weight models, and general CUA baselines on OSWorld-Verified, WindowsAgentArena, and OSWorkerBench (instruction-only mode). **DemoCUA / demonstration-guided evaluation:** paired protocol on OSWorkerBench's 33-task self-demo subset and a 30-task OSWorld-Verified subset — same instruction, initialized environment, interaction budget, and executable verifier evaluated with and without the paired demonstration, isolating the demonstration's marginal value from instruction-only capability. A decision-turn analysis further examines how demonstration availability changes agent behavior at the level of individual decision turns (Section 7.2.4). The 45-task variant-demo setting is evaluated only as a preliminary 10-task pilot, not a main benchmark claim. Baselines compared against include Qwen3.6-27B (the base model), Kimi K2.6, EvoCUA-32B, GPT-5.5, Claude Sonnet 5, and Qwen3.7-Plus.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

* Releases a new benchmark, OSWorkerBench (100 long-horizon office tasks, 41 applications, with paired self-demo and variant-demo resources) — this is a genuine new-dataset contribution, distinct from the training corpus.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

The general-capability claim is well supported by paired comparisons on two independent, pre-existing, non-author-controlled benchmarks (OSWorld-Verified 77.0%, WindowsAgentArena 66.2%, both reported as new open-weight state of the art against multiple named open- and closed-weight baselines), and the OSWorkerBench instruction-only gain over the same-architecture base model (Qwen3.6-27B) isolates the training-pipeline contribution specifically (+17.7 strict success, +24.5 progress points). The demonstration-guidance claim is the paper's strongest and most carefully isolated result: the self-demo paired protocol holds instruction, environment, budget, and verifier fixed and varies only demonstration availability, and the effect size is large and consistent across three different task pools (OSWorkerBench self-demo: strict success 17.2%→35.4%, progress 67.9%→81.1%; a 30-task OSWorld-Verified subset: 40.3%→65.8%; GameDev: 76.8→81.2). The paper is explicitly honest about the limits of this evidence: it states plainly that all quantitative DemoCUA results use the *self-demo* setting (demonstration and target are literally the same task) and "should not be interpreted as procedural transfer across task variants" — the harder variant-demo claim is reported only as an unstable 10-task pilot, not a benchmark result, which is a credible self-imposed boundary rather than an overclaim. A separate, self-disclosed engineering limitation (Section on workflow context placement) notes the workflow currently sits at the start of context, invalidating KV-cache reuse on every subtask update — a known efficiency cost, not a correctness one.

# Q9 Contributions

1. A closed-loop, environment-grounded data pipeline that jointly constructs tasks and executable environments, filters rollouts, and uses a hierarchical capability tree to diagnose and rebalance training-data coverage gaps, feeding both SFT and online agentic RL.
2. DemoCUA: an in-context-learning formulation that converts multimodal human or agent demonstrations into adaptive, subtask-level workflows (goal + verifiable completion criterion + advisory action descriptions per subtask) rather than rigid action-sequence replay, with the live observation retaining veto power at every step.
3. OSWorkerBench: a new 100-task, 41-application, 10-job-family long-horizon office benchmark with a controlled paired protocol (self-demo vs. variant-demo) that isolates the marginal value of demonstration guidance from instruction-only agent capability — a measurement contribution existing benchmarks (OSWorld, WindowsAgentArena) do not support.
4. UI-Mate-27B sets open-weight state of the art on OSWorld-Verified (77.0%) and WindowsAgentArena (66.2%), and demonstration guidance raises OSWorkerBench self-demo strict success from 17.2% to 35.4% and progress from 67.9% to 81.1%.

# Q10 Next Steps

* The paper's own stated open problem (Section 10, Discussion and Future Work): the variant-demo setting (procedural transfer across non-identical related tasks) is unresolved — three directions are named: training agents to extract transferable structure from a partially matching demonstration rather than replaying it; offline acquisition of demonstrations at scale from books/documentation/instructional video instead of per-task recording; and retrieval of demonstrations against live state so coverage scales with a corpus rather than annotation effort.
* A separately named future direction is verifier-grounded progress credit: using PCM's (Section 9's progress-credit mechanism's) milestone structure to reward milestone completion/recovery and penalize regression directly, with deterministic checks for verifiable milestones and learned judges reserved for uncertain states, keeping the environment's terminal residual authoritative.
* Named engineering fix: moving the workflow representation to the end of context (append-only) to restore KV-cache reuse across an episode, currently invalidated because subtask updates sit at the context prefix.
* **Research-OS fit:** the self-demo vs. variant-demo distinction is a useful frame for any Research-OS skill or agent workflow that wants to condition on a prior successful run — the paper's evidence says same-task demonstration guidance works well today, but transfer across related-but-different tasks is still an open research question, which bounds how much confidence to place in "show the agent a past example" as a general reliability strategy.
