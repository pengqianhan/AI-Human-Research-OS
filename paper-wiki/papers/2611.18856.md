# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a harness H_S already evolved by recursive self-improvement (RSI) in a source setting (policy model M_S, benchmark X_S), plus whatever record of that optimization is carried forward; a target setting that changes either the benchmark (cross-dataset, same policy) or the policy model (cross-model, same benchmark and split) while the harness interface stays executable.
* **Output (Y):** a target harness after N further rounds of proposer-selector optimization, scored on the target's held-out split.
* **Constraints:** only evolve-set feedback may reach the proposer, selector, or memory; policy weights stay frozen; matched round counts (N = 10) and candidate budgets (k = 5 per round) across conditions.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Harness-RSI methods (Meta-Harness, Agentic Harness Engineering, RRSI) optimize inside one fixed model and benchmark, and transfer studies ask whether a finished harness still helps elsewhere. The paper frames the case in between: optimization that *continues* after a change, where an inherited edit may need to be kept, revised, or removed. Prior systems keep candidate archives, edit histories, improvement libraries (Harness²), or problem-grouped harness banks (HarnessBank); SIM's specific combination is a per-problem history of attempts with rationale, pre-registered predictions of which tasks an edit will fix, observed outcomes including regressions, and provenance, read in full by both proposer and selector.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

An evolved harness alone does not say why its edits were made or how they performed. Carrying that evidence into target optimization lets the optimizer judge which inherited mechanisms still apply, so continuation from the same harness ends higher with the full record than with no record or with diagnoses only.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Harness RSI.** Meta-Harness (the problem formulation the paper follows), Agentic Harness Engineering (its minimal coding seed), RRSI (regularized search; the paper adopts its workspace and engineering environments, seeds, and scoring interfaces), self-modifying agents, SIA (harness plus weight updates).
* **Transfer and continuation.** Harness² (an improvement library reused across benchmarks), HarnessBank, Meta-Skill, Meta2, Continual Harness, Adaptive Auto-Harness, EvoHarnessBench; retention and interference concerns for self-evolving agents.
* **Benchmarks.** Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 4, the Meta-Harness online text-classification environment, Harvey LAB, JobBench, GDPval, APEX-Agents, EngDesign, Frontier-Eng.
* **Key people.** Xin Yu (Penn State, Google intern), Fei Xia, Ting Yu, and colleagues at Google Cloud AI and Google DeepMind; Lingzhou Xue (Penn State). Likely critics: authors of RRSI and Harness², who also store optimization history and could argue the gain comes from more context rather than its structure.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Entries keyed by harness problem.** For a root-cause hypothesis c_i (for example, premature completion) an entry holds an ordered list of attempts, each with the modification and its rationale, predicted and observed task outcomes, and provenance (domain, benchmark, policy, round). Predictions are written before evaluation and checked afterward.
2. **Read by proposer and selector.** The complete memory goes into the proposer's system prompt and is read by the selector, with no retrieval or truncation. Each proposal cites an existing root cause or names a new one.
3. **Append-only update.** After selection only the chosen candidate is recorded, including outcomes when it regresses or misses a predicted fix; entries with identical root-cause text merge without dropping history.
4. **Four conditions.** Cold Start (seed harness, no memory), Harness Transfer (H_S, no memory), Problem-Only Memory (H_S, root causes only), Full SIM (H_S, full record). The three transfer conditions share H_S, so Harness Transfer is the direct control for the memory.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Domains (source to target).** Coding: Terminal-Bench 2.1 to Terminal-Bench 4 (seeds: minimal AHE and Terminus-2). Text classification: three source datasets to eight held-out datasets in the Meta-Harness environment (seeds NoMemory, FewShotAll). Agentic workspace: Harvey LAB to the equal-weight mean of JobBench, GDPval, and APEX-Agents (Gemini 3.8 Flash judge). Engineering: EngDesign to Frontier-Eng. ReAct over an MCP gateway seeds the last two.
* **Splits.** Uniform random evolve and held-out halves; for example TB4 has 33 held-out tasks (165 rollouts) and Frontier-Eng 23 (115 rollouts). Five rollouts per task.
* **Models.** Cross-dataset: Gemini 3.8 Flash as policy in both stages, and as proposer and selector. Cross-model: source RSI with Claude Opus 5.5 as policy, then Gemini 3.8 Flash on the same benchmark and split.
* **Extra studies.** A generation chain on TB2.1 (Gemini 2.5 Flash, 3.5 Flash, 3.8 Flash, 4 Argon); one harness for Grok 4.20 Reasoning, Claude Opus 5.5, and Gemini 3.8 Flash on text classification; ten independent Terminus-2 source runs each continued with and without SIM on TB4.
* **Reporting.** Except for the paired coding study, each condition is one complete RSI run; the five rollouts estimate execution noise, not optimization variance (stated).

# Q8 Evidence Quality

**Cross-dataset (Table 5, held-out target, before and after target RSI).**

| Coding / AHE | 16.36 → 19.39 | 18.18 → 20.61 | 18.18 → 18.79 | 18.18 → 24.85 |
| Coding / Terminus-2 | 18.18 → 19.39 | 19.39 → 19.39 | 19.39 → 20.61 | 19.39 → 21.82 |
| Text cls. / NoMemory | 68.60 → 72.00 | 71.40 → 71.00 | 71.40 → 73.40 | 71.40 → 73.60 |
| Text cls. / FewShotAll | 75.72 → 75.92 | 72.92 → 74.12 | 72.92 → 75.52 | 72.92 → 77.92 |
| Workspace / ReAct | 73.89 → 82.98 | 74.93 → 85.07 | 74.93 → 85.54 | 74.93 → 87.89 |
| Engineering / ReAct | 39.13 → 40.87 | 39.13 → 42.61 | 39.13 → 42.61 | 39.13 → 46.96 |

Full SIM finishes highest in all six, 2.43 to 4.35 points above Harness Transfer and 0.20 to 6.06 above Problem-Only Memory; Problem-Only ranges from 1.82 below to 2.40 above Harness Transfer. FewShotAll's evolved harness starts below the seed (72.92 vs 75.72), and harness-only continuation stays below Cold Start.

**Cross-model, Opus 5.5 to Gemini 3.8 Flash (Table 6).** The Opus-evolved harness starts below Gemini's seed in all four domains (deficits 0.60 to 5.48 points). Full SIM finishes highest everywhere, 3.60 to 11.33 points above Harness Transfer: coding 21.82 vs 18.18, text classification 83.80 vs 80.20, workspace 84.75 vs 73.42 (both from 68.41), engineering 50.43 vs 42.61. Harness Transfer still ends below Cold Start in three domains.

**Paired coding study (Table 3).** Ten Terminus-2 source runs, each continued for ten TB4 rounds with and without SIM: SIM higher in 9 of 10, mean 21.70 vs 19.33 from a shared 18.36 start, mean difference 2.36 (95% CI 1.05 to 3.68; paired t(9) = 4.06, p = 0.003; signed-rank p = 0.006; d_z = 1.28).

**Other studies.** Generation chain (Table 1): CRSI beats fresh RSI on Gemini 3.5 Flash (76.8 vs 73.3) and 3.8 Flash (90.5 vs 88.9) but not on 4 Argon (94.8 vs 95.6). Shared harness (Table 2): unified synthesis reaches 75.24 held-out against 75.20 for per-model harnesses and 73.28 for the shared seed; sequential CRSI 74.20. Source retention (AHE): Full SIM loses 3 of 225 successful TB2.1 rollouts, Problem-Only 4, Harness Transfer 6. Case studies follow an inherited completion gate that SIM keeps and extends with target checks (feasibility versus optimality), while the no-memory run tightens it and repairs two deadlocks.

**Limits on the evidence.**

* Outside coding, every cell is one RSI run (stated); the six-plus-four "Full SIM wins" pattern is consistent, but per-setting margins have no optimization-variance estimate.
* Coding margins are small in rollout units: TB4 held-out is 165 rollouts, so one rollout is 0.61 points and the paired mean of 2.36 is about four rollouts; every Table 3 score is a whole number of successes out of 165. Frontier-Eng's 4.35-point cross-dataset margin is 5 of 115 rollouts (reader arithmetic).
* Full SIM also gives the optimizer more context than Harness Transfer; Problem-Only Memory partly controls for this, but a length-matched unstructured log (as in Harness² or free-text notes) is not among the conditions (reader observation).
* Gemini 3.8 Flash is policy, proposer, selector, and the workspace judge in the main comparisons, so workspace scores share a model with the agent being judged (reader observation; judge bias is stated in the ethics section).
* The generation chain reports one decimal and no variance; the 4 Argon reversal shows the benefit is not monotone across generations.

# Q9 Contributions

1. A CRSI setting with cross-dataset and cross-model variants that existing harness-RSI methods can use without changing their proposal, evaluation, or selection procedures.
2. Self-Improvement Memory: a problem-keyed record of edits, rationale, pre-registered predictions, observed outcomes, and provenance that conditions both proposal and selection.
3. Matched-start evidence across four domains, a ten-pair coding study with significance tests, and case studies of how inherited edits are reused or revised.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Stated by the paper.** More repeated runs outside coding; longer transition chains; adapting planning, token allocation, and tool interfaces; settings without a compatible harness interface.
* **Length-matched control.** Compare SIM with an unstructured log of the same size to separate the effect of structure from the effect of more history (reader suggestion).
* **Memory growth.** All entries are read without retrieval; over many transitions the record will outgrow a prompt, and how to compress it without losing the predicted-versus-observed evidence is open.
* **Cross-family judges.** Re-score workspace results with a judge outside the Gemini family.
