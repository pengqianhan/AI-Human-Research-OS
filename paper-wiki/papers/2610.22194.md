# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a fixed base checkpoint M_0, an initial candidate data pool D_0, a fixed SFT training script (three epochs), a token budget per recipe, and target benchmarks {T_k} under one evaluation protocol.
* **Output (Y):** a sequence of data recipes. Recipe R_t gives every row in the round's pool D_t a sampling weight, possibly zero; rows written during round t join the pool, so D_{t+1} extends D_t. Each round trains C_t = train(M_0, R_t) from the base, never from the previous checkpoint, so the pool and the recipe are the only things that carry research history.
* **The research policy:** π maps the round's pool, recipe, feedback F_t (benchmark scores, per-example failures, training dynamics, a comparison with earlier rounds), and history H_{<t} to a data action a_t, and R_{t+1} = a_t(R_t). AutoSFT is one instance of π.
* **What is measured:** benchmark scores per round (IFEval and IFBench as avg@10, MBPP+ as avg@3), and which of six data-action families a run reaches, labelled after the run from the diff between consecutive recipes rather than from the agent's own account. Agents are never shown the action space; they write recipes in free form.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

The paper builds on an observation from PostTrainBench's audit of agent post-training runs: given the whole training stack, agents settle on SFT and spend most of their effort revising data. Recent systems therefore treat data as the search object, but each gives the agent one kind of control: selecting rows from a fixed pool (TREX, AutoSelection, DataChef, Luo et al.) or calling a generator (Autodata, ANDES, BigBang). A human data engineer instead adjusts category proportions, prunes and repairs rows, replaces overlong responses, writes examples for missing capabilities, and orders the data. The question is whether an automated system can make that whole range of changes, and what structure it needs to do so. The second novelty is methodological: retraining from the base every round under a fixed token budget makes each round's change individually attributable, which a loop that continues training from the last checkpoint cannot offer.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

The pilot motivates two requirements. If execution (job submission, monitoring, conversion, evaluation) leaves the deciding agent's context, the agent keeps its research history instead of losing it to compaction. If proposals come from several lines of reasoning over the same round, the search escapes the single action family a lone agent collapses into. A third claim follows from a separate limitation: if a subagent can write verified data for capabilities the pool barely covers, the recipe can reach capabilities that selection and editing cannot. The overall claim is that automating SFT data research depends more on covering diverse data actions than on a stronger single action.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Data selection and synthesis for post-training.** LIMA and s1 (a small, well-chosen set can match a much larger one) and the authors' earlier work on data arrangement; selection by information gain or diversity (DELIFT, MIG); synthesis from seed sets, by evolving complexity, or by extraction from aligned models (Self-Instruct, WizardLM, Magpie, AgentInstruct, SEIF); and preference-data curation. All produce a fixed dataset without closing the loop on downstream training.
* **Automated data optimization.** PostTrainBench (agents converge on SFT with data as the search object). On selection: TREX (MCTS over recipes), AutoSelection (operator programs over a fixed pool), DataChef (a policy trained with a proxy reward), and Luo et al. (iterating on data alone, with gains that plateau after rounds 8-15). On synthesis: Autodata, ANDES, and BigBang. On research organization: [Arbor](2606.11926.md) (hypothesis trees), AutoSOTA (multi-agent decomposition), and the Anthropic automated researcher (Wen et al., *Automated weak-to-strong researcher*: diverse initial directions), whose finding that distinct starting directions keep parallel agents diverse motivates the four analysis angles.
* **Benchmarks for AI research.** MLE-bench and other ML-research and AI-infrastructure benchmarks; self-improvement benchmarks such as Agent²RL-Bench; data-centric ones closest to this setting: DataEnvGym, Curation-Bench, RSIBench-Data, and DataPrep-Bench. The paper differs by requiring from-base retraining, repeated evaluation, and a verification gate on synthesis.
* **Key people.** A Tsinghua-led team (Tsinghua NLP Group on Hugging Face), with authors also listed at UIUC, ModelBest, CUHK, and BUPT; the HTML's author-affiliation mapping is garbled, so individual affiliations are not stated here. Corresponding emails belong to Bingxiang He, Xiaozhi Wang, and Chaojun Xiao; senior authors include Zhiyuan Liu and Ning Ding. Likely critics: the PostTrainBench authors, whose open-ended setting the pilot alters in six ways; and authors of single-action systems (DataChef, Autodata), who would ask for head-to-head runs under the same budget.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

**A data action space used only as a yardstick.** Six families of change, each fixed by what happens to rows and at what level the change is decided (category, row, capability, or whole recipe). The text names mixture (proportions), pruning, repair (editing instructions and answers), and resupervision (replacing answers); the other two, writing new examples and changing order, are named here from the introduction and Section 5.2, since Figure 3 was read only through its caption. Actions that leave the recipe unchanged, such as holding out a validation set or re-evaluating a checkpoint, are research actions and fall outside the space.

**The pilot that motivates the design.** A single Codex agent running GPT-5.5 at medium effort researches data for Qwen3-1.7B-Base. The PostTrainBench setting (one H100, internet, ten wall-clock hours) is corrected in six ways: SFT fixed, an initial pool (argilla/ifeval-like-data) instead of web search, IFBench added, retraining from base, benchmark targets (IFEval 80, IFBench 50) instead of a wall-clock budget, and richer feedback. The agent submits 33 rounds in 11.5 hours, reaching neither target. Three failure modes appear:

* *Collapse onto one family.* Every round changes proportions; round 9 is best on both benchmarks (IFEval 67.76, IFBench 39.96), and the 23 rounds after it never improve. The agent uses 22 different words for the move but never repairs a row, replaces a response, or writes an example.
* *Wall-clock budgets shrink rounds.* In the open-ended run, recipes grow to 264k rows by round 4 and then shrink every round to 69k (26% of the peak): once a checkpoint is worth keeping, more data buys nothing under that objective, but time buys another attempt. Under score targets, recipes stay near the token budget.
* *Context lost to execution output.* The session fills to 94.7% of a 258,400-token window and is compacted ten times. Polling job status is the most frequent call (371) but only 1.7% of the 9.9M bytes; training output (13.4%), rereading the run record (11.8%), applying the data action (9.7%), and evaluation output (8.9%) take most of the space, along with the agent's reasoning (16.3%) and compaction summaries (8.3%).

**AutoSFT: a meta agent with three kinds of subagent.**

* *Executor.* Runs the whole lifecycle of submitting, monitoring, converting, and evaluating in its own session, then writes feedback artifacts and a structured report to files and returns only their paths. Its evaluation skill forbids it from choosing the model, changing the benchmark set, or reading result values.
* *Four analysis subagents.* Each reads the round from one evidence source: training data (quality, difficulty, coverage, redundancy, repairable rows), failure cases (passing against failing outputs), learning dynamics (loss and monitoring signals against the data), and history (failed interventions against ideas never properly tried). Each returns one executable proposal with evidence. Their skills share the proposal section word for word and differ only in scope, so diversity comes from the starting evidence rather than from different action menus. They cannot edit the pool, recipe, or memory.
* *Synthesis subagent.* Runs every round, picks capability targets that the pool undersupplies (far fewer than the 1,000 rows it treats as sufficient), and writes rows through model API calls rather than scripts. Each batch must pass a programmatic verifier for the target constraint, one model review for semantic fit, spot checks by read-only subagents, and an n-gram overlap check against the evaluation sets. Whether a round trains on the new rows stays the meta agent's call.
* *Memory with pre-registration.* Each round records its hypothesis before training (the submission path refuses rounds without one): the planned change, why it should move the metric, the comparison round, the expected direction, and a finer observation that would show it working. The verdict answers two questions separately (did the mean move beyond repeat-round noise; did the finer effect appear), and the lesson is appended without editing earlier entries. The meta agent also builds a capability taxonomy whose leaves carry programs that decide them, which lets it select rows by capability and gives synthesis stable targets.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Shared setup.** Codex harness with GPT-5.5 at medium effort as the research agent; DeepSeek V4 Flash as the generation backend; Qwen3-1.7B-Base trained with Megatron-LM on 8 H800s for three epochs per round (global batch 16, learning rate 1e-4 to 1e-7 cosine); evaluation with OpenCompass and vLLM at temperature 0.7, zero-shot.
* **Executor ablation.** The pilot's single agent against the same agent with an executor, both snapshotted at 11.5 hours, comparing compactions and the composition of the meta agent's events.
* **Analysis ablation.** One agent reading each round against a meta agent with four analysis subagents; IFEval only, 3M-token cap, sequence length 32,768; the base run gets 50 rounds and the analysis run 30 in the same time budget. Action families are labelled from recipe diffs, with each family's new-best rate (Table 4).
* **Synthesis ablation.** Selection and editing against the same plus the synthesis subagent; 100M-token cap that counts generated rows; IFEval and IFBench. The budget is larger because new data only becomes valuable once selection can no longer improve the recipe.
* **Code transfer.** MBPP+ with target 70; pool of 742,842 Python instruction-code pairs (OPC-SFT-Stage2, KodCode-v1-SFT-R1, Magicoder-OSS-Instruct-75K); sequence length 8,192; 100M tokens; three configurations (base, + analysis, + analysis + synthesis) over 64 hours. There is no ready-made capability taxonomy, so the system builds its own from the benchmark and the pool.
* **Not included.** Repeated searches per configuration; a base model other than Qwen3-1.7B-Base; head-to-head runs against TREX, DataChef, Autodata, or other cited systems.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

No new dataset. The instruction-following pool is argilla/ifeval-like-data and the code pool combines public corpora (OPC-SFT-Stage2, KodCode-v1-SFT-R1, Magicoder-OSS-Instruct-75K). The paper says it records the initial pool, every per-round recipe, and the raw evaluation outputs in a run ledger. Its reproducibility statement promises the implementation (training and evaluation script and agent scaffolding) "as anonymized supplementary material", while the author block and Hugging Face list [Thinking-Space/AutoSFT](https://github.com/Thinking-Space/AutoSFT) (10 stars at fetch time); the repository was not opened. Appendix D estimates cost: about 0.8 GPU-hours of training and 0.15 of evaluation per round, about 130 GPU-hours and USD 1,900 of API usage for a 20-round synthesis run (2.6 × 10^8 tokens and about USD 95 per round), and on the order of 10^3 GPU-hours and USD 10^4 for the whole paper.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported.**
  * *The single agent collapses and loses context.* All 33 pilot rounds are proportion changes (32 reweights and one replay that reassembles a recipe from the pool), round 9 is never beaten, and the context is compacted ten times (Appendix A). The trace breakdown is careful about its own limits: it shows that a few large payloads dominate, not that context pressure caused the plateau.
  * *The executor removes execution clutter.* Compactions fall from ten to five over comparable time, shell commands fall from 58% to 29% of the meta agent's events (a third of what remains is delegation), and the base agent's 268 status checks (23.0% of its commands) leave its context.
  * *Analysis widens the actions and raises the score.* The analysis run reaches 63.33 IFEval at round 6, above the base run's best over 50 rounds, and 67.10 at round 30. The base run's best is 60.60 in its first 30 rounds and 62.35 over all 50, a lead of 6.50 at equal rounds and 4.75 against 20 extra base rounds. Repair and resupervision make up 55% of the analysis run's actions; the base run never leaves the mixture family. Selecting data by validation results sets a new best in 10 of 24 uses, and editing instructions and answers in 6 of 14 (Table 4).
  * *A worked case shows the mechanism.* The failure reading traces repetition errors to 792 examples sharing one template; replacing them with 2,752 examples that vary cue wording and order raises strict repetition accuracy from 39.02 to 73.78. The base run instead adds more of the same template, which moves it from 23.78 to 25.00 (Figure 14).
  * *Synthesis reaches undersupplied capabilities.* Best IFEval rises from 78.15 to 81.58 and best IFBench from 49.02 to 51.95, and every IFEval checkpoint from round 9 on beats the base run's best over 31 rounds. The `repeat_prompt` capability jumps from about 40% to 75.6% in round 9, when its batch arrives; the round-9 targets each matched only 4 to 161 staged rows before synthesis.
* **Weaker or narrower than the headline.**
  * *One search per configuration.* Each comparison is a single run, and the authors note that repeated evaluation bounds checkpoint noise but not the variation between independent searches. The pilot's own accidental repeat (round 15 reproduced round 9's recipe exactly and scored 1.53 IFEval and 2.46 IFBench lower) shows that round-to-round noise is close to the size of some reported gains (reader inference).
  * *Reported scores are the loop's own targets.* Every reported number is on a benchmark the loop optimizes, and the feedback includes per-example failures on that benchmark, which the failure-case subagent reads. The ethics statement says off-target and held-out benchmarks are reported, but the results sections report only IFEval, IFBench, and MBPP+, each a target of the run it appears in (reader observation). The n-gram decontamination applies to synthesized rows, not to selection or editing guided by benchmark failures.
  * *Different budgets per comparison.* The analysis ablation uses a 3M-token cap and IFEval only, the synthesis ablation 100M tokens and two benchmarks. The components are never shown stacked under one budget on instruction following, so the full system's instruction-following result is not reported as one number.
  * *Small model, one scale.* All results use Qwen3-1.7B-Base. Synthesis does not move capabilities the model cannot yet follow: `odd_even_syllables` stays at zero despite batches in three rounds.
  * *The code result is short.* Within 64 hours the synthesis configuration completes only 6 rounds (65.8% at round 6) against 15 for the others (64.8% at round 13 with analysis), and neither reaches the target of 70. The abstract's "+3.7 MBPP+ over a single agent" comes from a figure read here only through its caption.

# Q9 Contributions

* A framing of automated SFT data research as a fixed-model, from-base, fixed-budget loop whose research policy is the object of study, with six data-action families labelled from recipe diffs after the fact.
* A diagnosed pilot showing that one agent owning the loop collapses onto one action family, shrinks its rounds under a wall-clock objective, and loses its context to execution output, with a byte-level breakdown of what fills the window.
* AutoSFT's structure (an execution-isolating executor, four analysis subagents whose skills differ only in starting evidence, and a verifier-gated synthesis subagent) together with append-only memory that requires a hypothesis before each round.
* One-component-at-a-time ablations that tie each component to a measurable change in context loss, action coverage, and score, plus a first transfer to code generation without a ready-made capability taxonomy.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **From the authors.** Larger base models and other scales; repeated searches per configuration; broader domains than one code benchmark with short runs.
* **Held-out evaluation (reader suggestion).** Report benchmarks the loop never sees, such as other instruction-following suites or general capability tests, beside IFEval and IFBench, so search success on the target can be told apart from better models. The paper's ethics statement already commits to this.
* **Stack the components under one budget (reader suggestion).** Run executor, analysis, and synthesis together with the same token cap and benchmarks as each ablation, and repeat searches to separate the component effect from search variance.
* **Compare against single-action systems (reader suggestion).** Put DataChef-style selection or Autodata-style synthesis under the same from-base protocol, to test the claim that coverage of actions matters more than a stronger single action.
* **Reuse the pre-registration pattern elsewhere (reader connection).** Registering a hypothesis, a comparison round, and a finer expected observation before each experiment is a cheap guard for the long research loops in [Arbor](2606.11926.md) and the improvement campaigns that [AREX-2](2609.38288.md) trains on.
