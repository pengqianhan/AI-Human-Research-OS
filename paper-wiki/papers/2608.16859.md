# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a generated observation/rollout from a world model (a text-to-video generator, a native action model, or a camera-pose model) produced in response to a probe case — a scene plus a concrete action or camera instruction drawn from one of six probe families.
* **Output (Y):** a per-case verdict across up to eight evaluation settings grouped into three axes (Observation Quality, Transition Correctness, World Persistence), each scored 0-1 (rescaled to 1-100) and backed by a structured, inspectable "evidence tree" showing the reasoning that produced the score, rather than a bare number.
* **Core problem:** existing world-model benchmarks compute metrics brute-force against a fixed rubric, so a low score cannot be diagnosed — there is no reasoning chain a reader can examine or verify to determine which invariant (physics, causality, world-state persistence) a rollout actually violated.
* **Scope:** contributes an evaluation harness plus the benchmark data it generates (330 cases over 18 world models); it does not modify or train the world models under test.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

World-model benchmarking and LLM-as-a-judge-style evaluation are both established. The paper's stated novelty is bringing the "harness paradigm" already used to scaffold LLM *agents* to the evaluator itself: rather than one fixed rubric or a single end-to-end judge call, HarnessEval-W interprets the context of each evaluation case, decomposes the evaluation question into measurable subproblems, and spawns specialized sub-agents — each with tailored context and diagnostic tools — to reason over its own subproblem, with a parent agent validating and aggregating that evidence into the final verdict.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If a world-model evaluation question is decomposed into measurable subproblems that specialized sub-agents each reason over with tailored context and diagnostic tools, and a parent agent validates and aggregates that evidence into a final verdict, then the resulting judgments will align more closely with human preference and be more consistent across repeated evaluations than a monolithic fixed-rubric scorer, while also yielding an auditable per-case reasoning chain.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* Extends the "harness paradigm" from the LLM-agent ecosystem (prompt/tool/state scaffolding around a foundation model) into the evaluation role rather than the task-execution role this repository's wiki otherwise tracks it in (see [Agent harness engineering](../topics/agent-harness-engineering.md)).
* Compares directly against WBench, an existing fixed-rubric world-model benchmark, as its primary baseline for human-alignment and cross-run consistency.
* Broadly related to LLM-as-a-judge literature (see [LLM-as-a-Judge](../concepts/llm-as-a-judge.md)), but specifically targets the judge's opaqueness rather than its calibration.
* Authors: a 43-author team credited to MirroS-Lab, submitted 2026-08-17.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

**Benchmark construction pipeline:** an Image Generator produces the initial observation for a case; an Image-grounded Planner specifies a concrete probe action grounded in visible evidence, drawn from one of six probe families matched to the scene's affordances (e.g., exploratory viewpoint changes need an exploration route, intentional transitions need a visible target); a Case Validator then audits feasibility and evidence adequacy before the case is accepted. Scenes are sampled across a six-axis taxonomy (environment type, foreground entities, midground spatial structure, scene density, visual style from photorealistic to anime, and first/third-person perspective).

**Evaluation pipeline:** for each accepted case, HarnessEval-W interprets the case's context and decomposes the governing evaluation question — spanning Observation Quality (render quality, physical plausibility of a single frame), Transition Correctness (did an exploratory/intentional/physical change execute correctly), and World Persistence (do invariants survive drift, revisits stay consistent, do offscreen processes keep evolving) — into measurable subproblems. For example, judging an "Intentional Change" spawns sub-agents that separately check target visibility, whether the visible transition actually occurred, resulting state validity, extraneous events, and overall judgeability. Each sub-agent reasons with its own tailored diagnostic tools; the parent agent then validates the gathered sub-agent evidence and summarizes it into a final verdict, so every score traces back to an explicit evidence tree.

# Q6 Experiment Design

Applied to 330 evaluation cases across 18 world models spanning three families: 10 text-to-video generators (Seedance 2.0, Wan 2.7, Kling 3.0, MiniMax H3, Grok Imagine 1.5, FLUX 3, Cosmos3-Super, HunyuanVideo 1.5, Wan 2.2, LTX-2.3), 3 native action models (SANA-WM, ABot-World, DreamX-World), and 5 camera-pose models (LingBot World v2, Lyra 2, Fantasy-World, HY-WorldPlay 1.5, InSpatio-World).

Human alignment is measured as correlation with human preference: Intentional Transition judgments reach Spearman ρ=0.93 (Kendall τ=0.82); Physical Transition judgments reach ρ=0.87 (τ=0.74). Against WBench (the fixed-rubric baseline), HarnessEval-W reports higher pairwise judgment accuracy on Physical transitions (71.7% vs. 31.9%), a much lower draw rate (1.8% vs. 52.2%), and a claimed 4.9× narrower disagreement envelope across repeated evaluations of the same case. A cross-axis correlation analysis finds Render Quality and Physical Observation are nearly independent (r=-0.04), Intentional and Physical Transition scores are strongly coupled (r=0.98), and Exploratory transitions are largely unrelated to either (r≈-0.15/-0.18). A fine-tuning case study shows that adapting a text-to-video model into an action model improves Revisit Consistency (+8.4 points) while degrading Intentional (-24.2) and Physical (-11.2) transition handling, which the authors read as evidence that current action-model fine-tuning data emphasizes free exploration over commanded interventions.

Overall leaderboard (top of 18): Seedance 2.0 (75.5), Wan 2.7 (75.0), Kling 3.0 (74.4), MiniMax H3 (74.3).

# Q8 Evidence Quality

The human-alignment correlations (Spearman ρ up to 0.93) and the WBench comparison (pairwise accuracy, draw rate, disagreement-envelope width) are the paper's strongest quantitative evidence that harness-based decomposition improves both alignment and reliability over a fixed-rubric baseline. Several methodological details were not confirmed in the retrieved material and are flagged rather than assumed: the size, protocol, and inter-rater agreement of the human-preference reference set behind the Spearman/Kendall numbers; the reference standard underlying the WBench pairwise-accuracy/draw-rate comparison; and the extent of independent human audit of the 330 generated cases beyond the pipeline's own Case Validator agent (the benchmark's cases are self-generated by the paper's own agentic construction pipeline, so coverage and difficulty are self-selected rather than externally curated). These gaps stem from an incomplete retrieval of the paper's appendix/human-study methodology, not from a stated weakness in the paper itself.

# Q9 Contributions

1. Reframes world-model evaluation as an agentic decomposition problem: a parent agent plus specialized sub-agents producing an auditable evidence tree per case, instead of a fixed-rubric scalar score.
2. A benchmark-construction pipeline (Image Generator, Image-grounded Planner, Case Validator) producing 330 cases spanning a six-axis scene taxonomy and six probe families.
3. An empirical demonstration across 18 world models (text-to-video, native action, and camera-pose families) that the approach aligns closely with human preference and is materially more consistent than a fixed-rubric baseline, plus a cross-axis correlation analysis and a fine-tuning-transfer case study.

# Q10 Next Steps

* The paper's own stated directions: test-time scaling for agentic benchmarks (deeper decomposition, multi-step verification); scaling the skill library to handle increasingly complex physics and scenarios; and self-improving evaluation loops where a detected skill gap triggers acquisition of a new diagnostic skill and library update.
* Open questions the retrieved material leaves unresolved: how sensitive HarnessEval-W's verdicts are to the sub-agent/parent LLM backbone choice; whether the harness-based decomposition generalizes from world/video models to evaluating language or coding agents; how the pipeline's self-generated case set compares in coverage and difficulty to a human-curated benchmark.
* **Research-OS fit:** a concrete instance of the concern this wiki already tracks under [LLM-as-a-Judge](../concepts/llm-as-a-judge.md) — that a judge's *interface* (what it exposes beyond a scalar score) matters as much as its calibration. HarnessEval-W's evidence tree is a structural answer to that concern, worth comparing against how this repository verifies delegated or multi-step agent work.
