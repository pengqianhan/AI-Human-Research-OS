# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** at each decision step, an observation `o_t = f(p, s, {π_i}, i)` built entirely from the agent's own tool calls: current asset prices `p`, current holdings `s`, and whatever additional per-asset data `π_i` (fundamentals, technicals) or general information `i` (news, macro signals) the agent chooses to retrieve. No pre-packaged information, human-curated digest, or fixed workflow is provided; everything beyond price and position must be actively acquired.
* **Output (Y):** a discrete per-asset trading action `a_t ∈ {Buy, Sell, Hold}` computed as `a_t = f(o_t, r_t)` from the observation and the agent's own ReAct-style natural-language reasoning trace `r_t`, executed against live or paper-traded real-time market prices with genuine economic consequences.
* **Core problem:** existing dynamic agent-evaluation frameworks (SWE-Bench-style static-bank-plus-dynamic-execution designs, GAIA, BrowseComp, XBench, Tau2Bench; fully-dynamic ones like LiveCodeBench and FutureX) and existing financial benchmarks (static ones like FinSearchComp and FinBench; "dynamic" simulation-style ones like InvestorBench, Finance Agent Benchmark, LiveTradeBench, and StockBench) still lack genuine autonomy and real-time constraints — they remain heavily dependent on fixed prompts and predefined workflows, and the absence of real time pressure, real market volatility, and real economic consequences creates a disconnect between benchmark performance and real-world capability.
* **Scope:** three live markets — U.S. equities (all Nasdaq-100 constituents plus a zero-return cash asset), China A-shares (all SSE-50 constituents), and cryptocurrency (10 major USDT pairs tracked by the Bitwise Index) — evaluated live from October 1 to November 7, 2025 (US hourly, A-share daily) and November 1–14, 2025 (crypto, daily), using six LLM backbones wrapped in identically structured agents with an identical five-tool MCP toolchain.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Prior "live" agent-evaluation designs split into two families the paper explicitly places itself against: benchmarks that pair a **static task bank with dynamic execution** (SWE-Bench, GAIA, BrowseComp, XBench, Tau2Bench) and benchmarks with **fully dynamic task sets and environments** (LiveCodeBench, FutureX). Financial-agent benchmarks split similarly into static-knowledge tests (FinSearchComp, FinBench) and dynamic-but-still-constrained simulations (InvestorBench, Finance Agent Benchmark, LiveTradeBench, StockBench) that the paper argues still fall short of genuine autonomy and real-time constraint. AI-Trader's stated novelty is threefold: (1) a **fully autonomous minimal-information paradigm** — the agent receives only price, holdings, and tools, with zero human-curated pre-packaged context, forcing it to self-direct all information acquisition rather than reason over an already-assembled analyst brief (a sharp contrast with TradingAgents' analyst-team-prepares-structured-reports design and FinAgent's tool-computed-diagnostics design, both already tracked in this wiki, which pre-structure much more of the agent's input); (2) genuinely **live, continuously evolving markets** across three structurally different regulatory and liquidity regimes rather than historical backtest or simulated data; (3) **data-uncontamination by construction**, since live and future market states cannot have leaked into any model's training data the way historical backtest windows can.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If LLM agents are given only minimal context (current prices, holdings, and a small fixed tool set) and must autonomously search, verify, and synthesize live market information with zero human-curated input or intervention, then measured trading performance in genuinely dynamic, live markets will diverge sharply from performance on static reasoning and knowledge benchmarks — general intelligence will not reliably predict trading competence — and the decisive factor for cross-market robustness will be risk-control discipline rather than raw predictive or reasoning strength, with the magnitude of achievable excess return depending on market liquidity and regulatory structure (easier in liquid, less policy-driven markets) rather than being uniform across markets.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **LLM agents for autonomous decision-making.** Traces the shift from text-completion systems to agents capable of sophisticated reasoning, planning, and environmental interaction (Anthropic's Claude releases, Qwen, AutoAgent, OSWorld, GPT-series work), framing long-horizon decision-making under uncertainty as the core capability AI-Trader isolates in a financial setting.
* **Tool use and environmental interaction.** Positions the Model Context Protocol (MCP) as the standardized interface AI-Trader's own toolchain is built on, situating the benchmark within the broader tool-use literature on sustaining a perception–decision–action loop.
* **From static to live agent evaluation.** Groups prior live-environment benchmarks into static-bank-plus-dynamic-execution (SWE-Bench, GAIA, BrowseComp, XBench, Tau2Bench) versus fully-dynamic task-and-environment designs (LiveCodeBench, FutureX), and prior financial benchmarks into static-knowledge (FinSearchComp, FinBench) versus dynamic-but-constrained (InvestorBench, Finance Agent Benchmark, LiveTradeBench, StockBench) — the explicit gap AI-Trader targets is genuine autonomy plus genuine real-time constraint, present in neither family.
* **Authors and affiliation.** Tianyu Fan, Yuhao Yang, Yangqin Jiang, Yifei Zhang, Yuxuan Chen, and corresponding author Chao Huang, all at the University of Hong Kong's HKUDS lab; Fan and Huang are also co-authors of the cited AutoAgent framework, a zero-code LLM-agent system from the same group.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Fully autonomous minimal-information paradigm.** The agent's only guaranteed inputs are current prices and current holdings; every other piece of information (fundamentals, technicals, news, macro context) must be actively retrieved through tool calls. The paper states this as three explicit design principles: all information must be acquired through tools, all decisions must be based on autonomous reasoning, and all actions must be executable under real-world constraints.
2. **Three structurally distinct live markets.** U.S. equities (Nasdaq-100 constituents — high liquidity, transparent disclosure, Fed-policy and earnings driven), China A-shares (SSE-50 constituents — distinct regulatory framework, policy- and sentiment-driven, episodic volatility), and cryptocurrency (10 major pairs via the Bitwise Index, spanning store-of-value, smart-contract, payment, and oracle assets — continuous 24/7 trading, extreme volatility) are deliberately chosen for contrasting regulatory, liquidity, and investor-composition regimes to stress-test generalization.
3. **Observe-Reason-Act closed loop.** Following the ReAct paradigm ("think first, act later"), each step either invokes another tool for more observation or commits to a discrete per-asset action (Buy/Sell/Hold) — a deliberately simple, interpretable action space rather than continuous position sizing. All intermediate natural-language reasoning is fully recorded, making the decision process observable, auditable, and reproducible. A self-correction mechanism rejects any proposed action that would exceed available liquidity or violate compliance constraints and forces the agent to re-invoke tools and re-reason.
4. **A minimal, uniform five-tool MCP toolchain shared identically across all six backbones**, isolating model-level differences from tool or architecture confounds: **Check Price** (time-aware historical/intraday OHLCV query, market-rule-aware ticker recognition), **Search** (keyword-driven live web search strictly filtered to information available at the current simulated time, preventing future-data leakage), **News** (structured financial news with sentiment signals, time-stamped and security-tagged), **Math** (numerical calculation support), and **Trade** (executes orders under market-specific rules, e.g., A-share 100-share lot sizing, and updates holdings/cash in real time with full auditability).
5. **Extensible, plug-and-play platform design.** A registration mechanism lets third-party developers inject custom agents or MCP tools into the same evaluation harness, positioning AI-Trader as a growing community platform rather than a closed, fixed evaluation set.

# Q6 Experiment Design

Six mainstream LLM backbones (DeepSeek-v3.1, MiniMax-M2, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, GPT-5, Qwen3-Max, Gemini-2.5-Flash) are each wrapped in an identically structured agent — same objectives, same tools, same architecture — to attribute performance differences to the backbone model's intrinsic reasoning and decision-making rather than agent design. Live evaluation windows: U.S. equities and A-shares from October 1 to November 7, 2025 (hourly for US, daily for A-shares); cryptocurrency from November 1 to November 14, 2025 (daily; crypto trades 365 days/year versus ~252 trading days for equities). Metrics: Cumulative Return (CR), Sortino Ratio (downside-deviation-adjusted, target return zero), Volatility (annualized sample standard deviation), and Maximum Drawdown (MDD). Baselines: QQQ Invesco ETF (US), the SSE-50 index (A-shares), and a CD5 crypto index (crypto). The paper additionally reports trade-execution statistics (fraction of periods with no trade, average trades per period) and two narrative case studies of individual trading days illustrating specific reasoning behaviors.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

AI-Trader is a live, continuously updating evaluation platform rather than a static dataset — the paper releases the platform code, the five-tool MCP toolchain, and evaluation logs at [github.com/HKUDS/AI-Trader](https://github.com/HKUDS/AI-Trader) (21,634 GitHub stars per HF metadata at the time of this note, reflecting strong community trending activity), with a companion website at ai4trade.ai.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

This note is built from the complete arXiv paper text (Abstract, §1 Introduction, §2 Related Works, §3 Framework of AI-Trader including 3.1 Live Trading Environment and 3.2 Trading Agents, §4 Experimental Evaluation including 4.1–4.4 Case Study, §5 Conclusion, References, and Appendices A–B) via `hf papers read`, which returned a full, unTruncated conversion for this paper. Headline claims are consistent between the abstract and the body's numbered findings. Three evidence-quality points are worth flagging beyond what the paper itself states: (1) each market's results are a **single live run per model over a short window** (roughly 5.5 weeks for US/A-shares, 2 weeks for crypto), with no repeated trials, confidence intervals, or seed variance reported — a materially weaker statistical protocol than, e.g., ASI-Bench's mean±std over three runs elsewhere in this wiki, and the paper does not caveat this itself. (2) In the crypto market, every agent *and* the CD5 Index baseline lose money over the window (returns from −12.18% to −18.63%), so "the sole agent outperforming the baseline" (DeepSeek-v3.1 at −12.18% vs. the baseline's −14.30%) is a genuine but relative finding during a market-wide downturn, not an absolute-return success — the paper states this plainly in the text rather than obscuring it, but a header-level read of "outperforms baseline" claims could miss the sign. (3) The two case studies are explicitly framed as illustrative single-day narratives ("through case study, we observed...") rather than a systematic, quantified error taxonomy across full trajectory logs, so they should be read as qualitative existence proofs of specific behaviors (successful defensive reasoning; unverified-news-driven misjudgment) rather than measured failure rates.

# Q9 Contributions

1. AI-Trader, the first fully-automated, live, data-uncontaminated evaluation benchmark for LLM agents in financial decision-making, spanning three structurally different market regimes (U.S. equities, China A-shares, cryptocurrencies) at multiple trading frequencies.
2. A fully autonomous minimal-information paradigm that gives agents only price, holdings, and tools — zero human-curated pre-packaged information — isolating genuine autonomous information-acquisition and synthesis capability from benchmark-specific prompt scaffolding.
3. A uniform, minimal five-tool MCP toolchain (Check Price, Search, News, Math, Trade) applied identically across six backbone LLMs, isolating model-level reasoning differences from confounds in agent architecture or tool design.
4. Empirical evidence that general LLM intelligence does not reliably predict trading capability — strong performers on reasoning and knowledge benchmarks (GPT-5, Qwen3-Max) post some of the weakest live trading results — and that risk-control discipline, not aggressive return-seeking, is the strongest predictor of cross-market robustness, demonstrated by MiniMax-M2's consistent relative outperformance across both the U.S. and A-share markets.
5. Direct evidence against cross-market strategy generalization: the same model performs very differently across markets (DeepSeek-v3.1 strong in the U.S. and crypto markets, negative in A-shares), showing current agents have not achieved genuine cross-market transfer.
6. A finding that excess returns are markedly easier to generate in liquid, less policy-driven markets (multiple agents beat QQQ in the U.S.) than in policy-driven ones (no agent beat the SSE-50 index in A-shares) over the same evaluation period.
7. Two qualitative case studies exposing both a strength (autonomously anticipating and defensively repositioning ahead of a tariff-driven equity selloff via multi-source search and reasoning) and a concrete, systemic failure mode (adopting a single unverified news narrative without cross-verification, leading to a missed market rally) in current agents' live decision pipelines.

# Q10 Next Steps

The paper frames AI-Trader as a growing, deliberately harder-over-time evaluation platform, with a registration mechanism inviting third-party agents and tools so the benchmark keeps pace with improving agent capability; it explicitly names the absence of a systematic information-verification module (exposed concretely by Case 2) as a clear, actionable direction for future agent design rather than a hypothetical gap. This note adds one methodological question the paper's own results raise but the read text does not address: because each market's findings rest on a single live run over one short window per model, with no repeated trials or variance estimate, a natural next step — not yet reported — would be repeating the live evaluation across multiple non-overlapping windows per market before treating any one model's relative ranking (for instance, MiniMax-M2's cross-market robustness advantage) as a stable property of that model rather than a feature specific to the particular ~5-week sample studied here.

* [LLM agents](../topics/llm-agents.md) - a ReAct-grounded, fully autonomous information-acquisition design (zero pre-packaged context, all observation built from the agent's own tool calls) that pushes this topic's tool-use and verification themes into a domain where "correct" is measured by real, live economic consequence rather than a static task-completion check.
* [TradingAgents](2412.20138.md) - both are single- or few-agent LLM trading systems evaluated against passive benchmarks, but TradingAgents backtests a bespoke five-role, structured-report pipeline over a fixed ~3-month historical window on three large-cap tickers, while AI-Trader strips the agent down to a minimal ReAct loop with five generic tools and evaluates it live across three structurally different markets — a design choice that isolates backbone-model capability rather than architecture design, at the cost of not testing whether TradingAgents-style role specialization would improve on any of AI-Trader's six backbones.
