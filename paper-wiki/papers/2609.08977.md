# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** continuous streaming multimodal input during an ongoing human-AI interaction — video, speech, and text arriving concurrently and asynchronously, including spontaneous interruptions, background noise, multi-party speech, and backchannel acknowledgments (e.g., "uh-huh").
* **Output (Y):** Gander, an end-to-end model that continuously decides, at fixed one-second intervals, whether to keep listening, speak, or interrupt its own ongoing response, while also determining when a request exceeds what it can resolve locally and must be delegated to a separate long-horizon task-execution agent — released together with its model weights, code, and (per the abstract) training data.
* **Core problem:** conventional human-AI interaction is turn-based and text-centric, unlike the continuous, overlapping, interruptible nature of human-human communication. The paper frames two open questions this must resolve: can true interactivity be composed from external modules (a voice-activity detector plus ASR plus a dialogue model), or must interaction timing be intrinsic to the model itself; and can one model simultaneously provide the low latency realtime interaction requires and the long-horizon reasoning complex agentic tasks require, or does this create an inherent responsiveness-vs-intelligence trade-off.
* **Scope:** an industrial technical report (Tencent Hunyuan Speech Team, with Zhejiang University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, CUHK, and NTU co-authors) evaluated across four dimensions — conversational ability, omni understanding, interactive capability, and agentic intelligence — using established benchmarks and project-page demos, since the paper states no dedicated benchmark for "Omni Interaction Agents" yet exists.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

The report does not claim to originate full-duplex speech interaction (citing BayLing-Duplex, Qwen-Audio-3.0-Realtime, Audio Interaction Model, Seeduplex, and GPT-Live/Moshi), omni multimodal modeling (Qwen Omni, MiniCPM-o 4.5, JoyAI-VL-Interaction, SeedRealtime), or voice-driven task delegation (Qwen Audio Agent, GPT-Live with Codex, Claude Voice Mode) — all positioned as prior work it builds on or against. Its stated novelty is architectural unification plus an intrinsicness claim: rather than composing perception, interaction timing, and task execution as separately built or externally orchestrated modules, Gander (1) predicts interaction control (listen/speak/interrupt) as part of the model's own autoregressive token stream, once per fixed time window, so that no external VAD triggers responses; and (2) resolves the responsiveness-vs-intelligence trade-off with a two-tier Cerebellum-Brain split in which the Brain is explicitly training-free and plug-and-play — a stronger reasoning model can be substituted without retraining the realtime interaction model — coordinated through a structured tool-call protocol and a dedicated agent orchestration runtime (a persistent Gateway managing task lifecycle, permissioning, and delivery) rather than an ad hoc prompt-based handoff.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If interaction-state decisions are predicted intrinsically, once per fixed window, as part of a single flattened multimodal token stream that interleaves perceptual input and generated output on a shared temporal axis — rather than triggered by an externally composed VAD/ASR pipeline — and if a lightweight, low-latency front cerebellum handles realtime perception and conversational control while delegating complex, long-horizon work to a separate, training-free back brain through a structured tool-call interface mediated by a persistent orchestration runtime, then the resulting system should (i) match or exceed dedicated full-duplex systems on turn-taking and interruption timing without sacrificing task delegation accuracy achievable by the back brain alone; (ii) retain spoken-dialogue and omni-understanding competence close to its base model despite training focused on interaction rather than static perception; and (iii) support long-horizon agentic task execution without requiring the interaction-facing model itself to be retrained as backend reasoning models improve.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Speech language models and full-duplex interaction.** BayLing-Duplex embeds listen/speak/terminate decisions as dedicated state tokens inside one autoregressive model; Qwen-Audio-3.0-Realtime processes audio in incremental streaming chunks; Audio Interaction Model extends realtime interaction from conversational speech to general proactive audio response; Seeduplex adds attentive listening and interference suppression in noisy, multi-speaker conditions; GPT-Live adopts a Moshi-style full-duplex architecture. The paper frames all of these as still largely audio-centric, evaluated in relatively constrained conversational settings.
* **Omni modalities and continuous multimodal interaction.** Qwen Omni integrates multiple input modalities with streaming speech generation; MiniCPM-o 4.5's Omni Flow places multimodal input/output on a unified temporal axis (Gander's front cerebellum explicitly builds on MiniCPM-o 4.5's Thinker-Talker design and uses it as the reported baseline for omni-understanding regression, Q6); JoyAI-VL-Interaction treats continuous visual perception itself as part of interaction; SeedRealtime adds native audio-visual full-duplex interaction with proactive response to observed environmental change.
* **From omni conversation to voice agents.** Qwen Audio Agent provides a realtime voice runtime that delegates complex tasks (code generation, file operations) to a backend workflow; GPT-Live with Codex and Claude Voice Mode are cited as product-oriented precedents for pairing voice interaction with general assistant capability. The paper argues these systems still treat conversational interaction and task execution as only partially coupled capabilities, which Gander's Cerebellum-Brain design aims to integrate into one continuous process.
* **Key people and affiliations.** Orantqing, Shengpeng Ji, Junlong Tong, and Jialong Zuo are marked as equal first contributors; primary affiliation is the Hunyuan Speech Team at Tencent, with co-authors from Zhejiang University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, and Nanyang Technological University. Co-author Zhifei Xie is also the lead author of this wiki's [VoiceMem](2608.26005.md) — a separate, closely adjacent real-time voice-agent-memory paper this report does not cite, but which targets the same streaming-interaction regime.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Three-component architecture.** A *front cerebellum* (a realtime, full-duplex omni model built on a Thinker-Talker design) handles continuous multimodal perception and interactive communication; a *back brain* (a training-free general-purpose task-execution agent — the default implementation uses the Codex app server, with Claude Code named as an alternative) handles long-horizon reasoning, file/code manipulation, retrieval, and other tool-mediated work; an *agent orchestration runtime* sits between them as the coordination substrate for both realtime multimodal inference and asynchronous background task orchestration.
2. **Structured delegation protocol.** The cerebellum emits three tool operations bound to the transport-layer-confirmed user turn (not to tool parameters the cerebellum generates on its own, to keep delegation grounded in authenticated user input): `task_start` (creates and names a new background task), `task_send` (routes further input to an existing task, in `main` mode to steer the live task or `fork` mode for a read-only side query that does not alter primary-task state), and `task_resolve` (deterministic lifecycle control — `cancel`, `allow_once`, `allow_session`, `deny`).
3. **Lean vs. coordinator orchestration modes.** In *lean* mode the runtime executes the cerebellum's classified task action directly with the original user turn as instruction — shorter control path, lower latency, more determinism. In *coordinator* mode, a separate control-plane model conditioned on trusted user requests and bounded system state produces declarative execution directives (reasoning intensity, questioning policy, permission policy, delivery strategy) that the Gateway then structurally validates against task state, provider capabilities, permission boundaries, and deployment configuration; the coordinator itself never executes tasks or touches files, shell, or network.
4. **Gateway: five persistent entities behind a unified Worker Provider interface.** Project, Task, Run, WorkerEvent, and Delivery organize backend execution, state transitions, worker scheduling, workspace isolation, concurrency control, permission handling, and result delivery; each backend (Provider) declares its own capabilities (native steering, read-only side queries, user interaction, cross-process recovery, supported input modalities, context acquisition, parallel execution). The default Codex-backed implementation keeps one persistent thread per task lineage — follow-ups are injected into the live thread, while read-only side queries fork an isolated thread to preserve primary-thread state.
5. **Training-free back brain with three runtime-specific tools.** Beyond native file/command-line/retrieval tools, the back brain can call `context_fetch` (retrieve realtime task context/events/artifacts by reference, role, event type, time range, and character budget), `memory_search` (persistent multimodal memory across sessions), and `share` (push verified findings, intermediate progress, or corrections back to the realtime front cerebellum).
6. **Omni perception via two concurrent, incremental encoders.** Vision: an any-resolution slicing scheme, each slice independently encoded by a SigLIP vision transformer and condensed by a query-based resampler into a small fixed token count per slice (~16× reduction versus the raw patch grid, run at up to 448×448 to bound per-frame cost under realtime latency constraints). Audio: a streaming, chunk-wise speech encoder emitting ~50 frames/second, downsampled 5× by a lightweight MLP projector to ~10 tokens/second before entering the backbone — both pathways refresh continuously rather than only at turn boundaries.
7. **Streaming chunk flattening.** The interaction is partitioned into fixed one-second windows; each chunk flattens that window's encoded audio/visual tokens, a predicted control token (listen / speak / interrupt), and up to *N* model-chosen text tokens into one causal sequence, so the model attends to the newest perceptual evidence before generating, once per window — deciding the interaction action before deciding content is what the paper credits for more stable full-duplex behavior than an externally triggered response. Context is managed with a fixed, sliding 128-chunk budget (~2 minutes), bounding per-step inference cost as sessions grow arbitrarily long.
8. **Decoupled speech generation.** Rather than the LLM backbone autoregressing directly over high-frame-rate acoustic units, the backbone's final-layer hidden state (plus the concurrent text token) feeds a small autoregressive speech-token decoder producing discrete semantic speech units interleaved with text along the shared timeline, followed by a streaming, chunk-wise causal flow-matching decoder that renders incremental waveform chunks conditioned on a reference-audio prompt (enabling zero-shot voice control) rather than waiting for a full utterance.
9. **Four-family, 2.7M-example training corpus.** Speech interaction (~37%, including a purpose-built full-duplex "InteractionSpeech" subset with explicitly annotated, independently timed interruption and backchannel events, filtered by a rule-based gate plus a four-dimension LLM-judge rating); audio-visual interaction (~41%, streaming video QA/narration/proactive response, realigned and rewritten to an ~8-token/second response rate); agentic interaction (~13%, seed-driven audio-agentic and GUI/video-grounded omni-agentic trajectory synthesis spanning the full task lifecycle — initiation, follow-up, constraint update, clarification, progress inquiry, cancellation, delivery); and robustness/negative data (~8%, irrelevant-video, no-command, anti-interference, and multi-party-tracking examples) to reduce spurious responses.

# Q6 Experiment Design

All results come from a single Gander checkpoint with a training-identical system prompt (no benchmark-specific tuning); where the back brain participates it is the training-free Codex worker provider driven by GPT-5.6, restricted to the tools each benchmark exposes. Three evaluation axes exercise different amounts of the system and use incommensurable scoring protocols, so results are reported separately rather than combined:

**Full-duplex interaction (Full-Duplex-Bench v3, 100 tool-augmented service scenarios with natural disfluencies).** Metrics: tool-selection F1 (ToolSel), argument correctness (ArgAcc), response-quality (RespQual, via a GPT-4o judge), a strict binary Pass@1 (exact tool multiset plus correct arguments), and three timing metrics — Take-turn (fraction of appropriately timed turns), Interrupt (fraction begun before the user finished, lower is better), and Filler (fraction prefaced by a conversational placeholder, lower is better, defined only over scenarios the suite's own LLM-based latency analysis returns a verdict for). The released scoring scripts run unmodified end-to-end — audio in, Talker-plus-speech-decoder audio out, scoring against an ASR transcript of that output — so results include real synthesis and recognition error. Because the benchmark's system prompt instructs baselines to execute immediately rather than ask clarifying questions but the externally instantiated back brain never sees that prompt, the authors placed an equivalent instruction in the back brain's working directory to keep both sides under matched constraints. A **back-brain-only** condition (bypassing the cerebellum and audio path, driven directly from the user-turn transcript) is reported separately as an execution-tier upper bound; it supports none of the three interaction metrics, which are left blank rather than scored as zero.

**Spoken conversation (SpokenQA: Llama Questions, Web Questions; VoiceBench: AlpacaEval, SD-QA — 2,052 utterances total).** Speech enters the Thinker directly and text output is scored; open-ended responses are GPT-4o-judged. Baseline numbers are taken from the authors' own prior Audio-Interaction paper's comparison table, with systems grouped by interaction regime (turn-based vs. full-duplex streaming) because a turn-based model receives the full utterance and unconstrained compute before responding, while a streaming model must additionally decide, at every step, whether the utterance is complete.

**Omni understanding (WorldSense, n=3,172; Daily-Omni, n=1,197 — 4,369 multiple-choice questions).** Follows each benchmark's official harness and two-stage answer extraction; exact-letter-match grading (no LLM judge), greedy decoding (deterministic). This setting is fully turn-based (the whole question is available before decoding, no speech is synthesized, the back brain is not invoked), so it isolates the front cerebellum's perceptual/reasoning ability from its interaction behavior. A modality ablation additionally presents audio-visual, video-only, and audio-only conditions per question (13,107 inferences total) to quantify cross-modal fusion.

**Results.** On Full-Duplex-Bench v3, Gander posts the best turn-taking behavior in the table — 100.0% appropriately timed turns (tied only with the cascaded Whisper→GPT-4o→TTS baseline) and the lowest premature-interruption rate (8.0%, vs. 13.5% for GPT-Realtime and 47.9% for the weakest baseline, Ultravox v0.7) — while trailing on all four task-accuracy metrics, narrowly against the weakest baseline (Pass@1 0.400 vs. 0.410) and more widely against the strongest (GPT-Realtime, Pass@1 0.600). Its 51.6% filler rate is its one non-leading interaction metric, which the paper attributes to holding the floor while a delegated task runs rather than a timing defect. The back-brain-only condition reaches ToolSel 0.934 — higher than every full end-to-end system in the table, GPT-Realtime included — alongside Pass@1 0.520 and RespQual 0.740, indicating the execution tier itself is not the limiting factor for accuracy; the paper attributes the gap between this row and the full system to two effects it does not separately ablate: the composite system must decide for itself when to delegate, and its output is scored via an ASR transcript of synthesized speech rather than clean text.

On SpokenQA/VoiceBench, Gander (9B) leads the full-duplex-streaming group on both SpokenQA subsets (75.60, 59.30 — ahead of Audio-Interaction by +8.29/+4.96 and Moshi by more than 13/33 points) and places second in that group on both VoiceBench subsets (0.32 below Audio-Interaction on AlpacaEval, 5.30 below on SD-QA). Against the full field including turn-based systems, Gander's SpokenQA scores still place second overall (2.90 behind Baichuan-Omni-1.5 on Llama Questions, 3.50 behind Qwen2.5-Omni-7B on Web Questions), while AlpacaEval and SD-QA place sixth and fifth across the full nine-system field. The back brain was never invoked on any of the 2,052 samples, so every Table 4 score reflects the front cerebellum acting alone — the paper frames this as intended selective escalation rather than under-delegation, since the benchmark is self-contained question answering rather than long-horizon tool work.

On omni understanding, Gander reaches 49.62 (WorldSense) and 78.53 (Daily-Omni), staying within 1.67 points of its MiniCPM-o 4.5 base on Daily-Omni (and still ahead of Qwen3-Omni's 70.70) while dropping 6.08 points below base on WorldSense; the vision tower is frozen and bit-for-bit unchanged throughout training, so the paper attributes the WorldSense regression to post-training answering behavior and an interaction corpus whose video content is largely irrelevant-context distractors, not degraded visual encoding. The modality ablation shows a genuine fusion effect rather than single-stream reliance: audio-visual input beats the stronger single modality by +5.01 points on WorldSense and +19.13 on Daily-Omni, tracking what each benchmark actually requires (Daily-Omni needs temporal audio-visual alignment; WorldSense is often answerable from one modality alone).

# Q9 Contributions

1. Gander: a single end-to-end model unifying omni multimodal perception, intrinsic full-duplex realtime interaction, and delegated long-horizon agentic execution, rather than composing these as three separately trained or externally orchestrated components.
2. A Cerebellum-Brain architecture in which interaction control (listen/speak/interrupt) is predicted as an intrinsic, once-per-window decision inside one flattened multimodal token stream rather than triggered by an external VAD/orchestration layer — evidenced by Gander simultaneously avoiding both the "waits too long and misses the turn" and "commits too early and interrupts" failure modes that appear separately across the compared baselines in Table 3.
3. A structured Cerebellum-to-Brain delegation protocol (`task_start`/`task_send`/`task_resolve`, with main/fork routing and cancel/allow_once/allow_session/deny resolution) plus an agent orchestration runtime — a persistent five-entity Gateway with lean and coordinator control modes — that keeps the Brain training-free and swappable, so a stronger backend reasoning agent (Codex, Claude Code) can be substituted without retraining the interaction-facing model.
4. A 2.7M-example, four-family training corpus with an explicitly annotated, independently timed full-duplex interaction subset (competitive interruption vs. supportive backchannel, each with strict operational acceptance criteria) and dedicated robustness/negative data for irrelevant context, silence, noise, and multi-party settings.
5. Reported evaluation evidence, disclosed with its trade-offs: best-in-table turn-taking and interruption timing on Full-Duplex-Bench v3 against six commercial and open systems while trailing on four task-accuracy metrics; leading full-duplex-regime spoken-QA accuracy competitive with turn-based systems operating under materially easier interaction constraints; and a measured, non-hidden cross-modal fusion gain on omni-understanding benchmarks alongside a real regression from its own base model on one of the two benchmarks tested.

# Q10 Next Steps

The paper's own Section 6 names five open challenges: **data and model scaling** (agent invocation and conversational behavior remain sensitive to training-data distribution in complex omni scenarios; further scaling is planned via the separate industrial Hy-Realtime model, not yet described in this report); **stable post-training** (no extensive on-policy distillation or reinforcement learning has yet been applied for long-horizon Omni Interaction Agent scenarios; effective reward design, credit assignment, and optimization stability under joint multimodal-interaction-plus-agentic-execution are named as open, with the Cerebellum-Brain split introducing its own coordination questions between local interaction quality and global task objectives); **Brain-Cerebellum architectural exploration** (current inter-component communication relies on ASR-derived signals; richer, more structured bidirectional communication — in both directions — is future work); **memory and long-context management** (long multimodal histories, tool calls, and evolving task states strain context and memory — the front cerebellum's own fixed 128-chunk (~2-minute) sliding window is a concrete present limit this note read directly, Q5); and **evaluation** (existing benchmarks evaluate omni understanding, duplex interaction, and agentic execution in isolation and do not adequately capture Brain-Cerebellum collaboration — this note's fetch was truncated partway through this final bullet, see Q8, so its full proposed remedy was not read).

This note adds one observation the paper does not itself isolate: Table 3's back-brain-only row (ToolSel 0.934, the highest in the table, versus the full system's 0.759) is the clearest evidence in the paper that the accuracy gap is not an execution-tier limitation, but the paper offers two candidate causes — the cerebellum's own delegation-timing decision, and ASR/TTS-introduced degradation from scoring synthesized speech — without an ablation isolating which dominates. Separately, Table 4 shows the back brain was never invoked across 2,052 spoken-conversation samples (read by the paper as correct selective routing), while Table 3 is the one benchmark specifically built to require delegation; together the two tables demonstrate correct non-delegation on an easy benchmark and a partially-explained accuracy gap on a hard one, without a benchmark condition that isolates whether the delegation decision itself is well-calibrated independent of the downstream speech pipeline.
