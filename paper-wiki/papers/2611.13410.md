# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a full-precision (BF16) agent checkpoint that serves as frozen teacher and as the starting point of a quantized student; a fixed quantizer (4-bit or 2-bit weights, 16-bit or 8-bit activations); training environments whose state can be saved, restored, and compared.
* **Output (Y):** a quantized student that completes multi-turn tool-use tasks at close to the teacher's success rate, running alone at inference with no teacher and no extra prompt.
* **Constraints:** corrections must come from contexts the student itself reaches; each failed student rollout needs a successful teacher rollout on the same task and initial state; a correction is kept only if executing it reproduces the reference environment transition.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Low-bit quantization of LLMs is an established problem, and training-based recovery (quantization-aware training, distillation, RL with verifiable rewards) already exists. The paper's angle is the agent setting. It reports that quantized agents fail by overlooking a task requirement or an environment condition, for example reading a file before entering its directory, and calls the resulting mismatch with a successful full-precision run a *behavioral divergence*. It argues that the available training data lacks corrections exactly there: offline data does not visit the student's failure contexts, and the student's own retries rarely contain a correct action. In its BFCL V4 multi-turn diagnostic, unguided retries from the same pre-turn state give a correct action in four attempts at only 9.6% of divergent turns.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If the teacher explains, at the first divergent turn, what the student overlooked, and the student produces the corrected response itself with the correction checked against the environment, then distilling those corrected turns (without the explanation in context) recovers more agent capability than on-policy distillation or RL on the student's unguided rollouts, at lower training cost. A secondary hypothesis is about the loss: forward KL toward the steered student at corrected turns and reverse KL toward the teacher elsewhere is better than either direction used throughout.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Capability recovery under low-bit quantization.** Weight reconstruction, scaling, and rotation (GPTQ, QuaRot, and others); training with hard token targets (EfficientQAT); soft teacher distributions (BitDistiller); verifiable rewards (QeRL, NVIDIA's quantization-aware RL).
* **Teacher guidance for agents.** Teacher-generated responses and rollouts conditioned on privileged context (EDGE, EDGE-OPD); FutureBridge-OPD, which judges a teacher intervention by the distillation signal in later student rollouts; OVCSD, which verifies privileged continuations from student failure states by task success. SteerDistill instead verifies at the divergent turn, against the reference transition.
* **Context distillation.** [On-policy distillation](../concepts/on-policy-distillation.md) (Agarwal et al.) and on-policy context distillation (Ye et al.), where the benefit of text in the teacher's context is moved into the weights of a learner that does not see it.
* **Key people.** Genming Cui and Xiangyu Li (equal contribution; Li is project lead), Zixu Hao, Xin Ding, Yunxin Liu, and Ting Cao (corresponding author), at the Institute for AI Industry Research (AIR), Tsinghua University, with Cui and Ding also at USTC. A "T. Cao" is among the authors of the cited BitDistiller, whose quantizer implementation this work reuses. Likely critics: the QeRL and NeMo-RL authors, whose methods were ported from NVFP4 on newer GPUs to integer quantization on A100s for the comparison.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

One training cycle has two stages, repeated with the updated student.

1. **Teacher-steered trajectory revision.** The frozen quantized student rolls out. For each failed rollout that has a successful teacher reference, the two trajectories are aligned by environment state transitions, so different actions with the same effect count as equal, and the first divergent student turn is selected. History and environment state before that turn are restored. The teacher writes a structured steering prompt (the discrepancy, when the guidance applies, a corrective rule); the student generates a revised response with the prompt in context and executes it. *Local verification* compares the resulting state and returned information with the reference transition. On failure the teacher refines the prompt from the attempt's feedback, up to R = 4 attempts. An accepted response replaces the failed turn, the student continues without guidance, and if the task still fails the process repeats at the next divergence. Verified corrections are kept even when the task ends unsolved.
2. **Internalization.** The learner never sees steering prompts. At corrected turns it minimizes forward KL from the frozen student snapshot conditioned on the prompt; at all other turns it minimizes reverse KL to the full-precision teacher on the student-generated responses. The learner uses fake quantization with a straight-through estimator onto BF16 master weights.

The difference from prior teacher-guided distillation is that the correction is the student's own output under guidance, checked locally by execution, and that the supervision source switches per turn. In the BFCL V4 diagnostic, steering raises turn-level Pass@4 at divergent turns from 9.6% to 82.3%.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Benchmarks.** [BFCL](../concepts/bfcl.md) V4 (Overall), [τ²-Bench](../concepts/tau2-bench.md) (Pass@1), [WebShop](../concepts/webshop.md) and [ALFWorld](../concepts/alfworld.md) (success rate), and Search QA (mean exact match over seven QA datasets, following [Search-R1](../concepts/search-r1.md)).
* **Models and precisions.** Qwen3-8B, Qwen3.5-4B, and Gemma 4 12B-it at W4A16; Qwen3-8B also at W4A8 and W2A16. Each BF16 checkpoint is first post-trained with [GRPO](../concepts/grpo.md) per task family and then used as teacher and as initialization for every quantized method.
* **Training data.** Executable tool-use tasks from [Agent World Model](../concepts/agent-world-model.md) for BFCL V4 and τ²-Bench (one checkpoint evaluated on both); the WebShop and ALFWorld training environments; the NQ and HotpotQA training sets for Search QA.
* **Baselines.** GPTQ (post-training quantization), EfficientQAT, BitDistiller (offline distillation), On-Policy QAD (NVIDIA NeMo-RL workflow), and QeRL (RL with verifiable rewards). All training-based methods share the learning rate (8e-6), batch size (16 trajectories), and two epochs.
* **Efficiency.** τ²-Bench Pass@1 against training GPU hours (8 A100 GPUs; steering and verification time included), and output tokens per successful task.
* **Ablations (BFCL V4, Qwen3-8B, W4A16).** Where to intervene, who produces the correction, who writes the prompt and in what format, whether to verify and whether to require final success, and the KL routing.

The design covers each claimed component with an ablation, but only on one benchmark and one model.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

No new dataset. Evaluation uses public benchmarks, and training uses Agent World Model tasks and the benchmarks' own training environments. Quantization follows BitDistiller's asymmetric group-wise integer implementation with weight group size 64. The τ²-Bench user simulator is GPT-5.6 at temperature 0. Code is linked at https://github.com/air-embodied-brain/SteerDistill with a project page at https://air-embodied-brain.github.io/SteerDistill; this note did not inspect either.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

**Main table (average of five benchmark scores, percent).**

| Model, precision | BF16 | Strongest baseline | SteerDistill | Gap to BF16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B, W4A16 | 53.54 | 51.00 (QeRL) | 53.18 | 0.36 |
| Qwen3.5-4B, W4A16 | 61.68 | 59.24 (QeRL) | 60.95 | 0.73 |
| Gemma 4 12B-it, W4A16 | 65.13 | 62.64 (QeRL) | 64.31 | 0.82 |
| Qwen3-8B, W4A8 | 53.54 | 49.69 (QeRL) | 51.62 | 1.92 |
| Qwen3-8B, W2A16 | 53.54 | 39.58 (On-Policy QAD) | 42.97 | 10.57 |

Gaps to BF16 are reader subtractions. SteerDistill is best on every benchmark in every block except Search QA at W2A16 (34.05 against BitDistiller's 34.22). "Within one point of BF16" holds for the three W4A16 rows only; at 2-bit weights the method leads the baselines by 3.39 points but is still more than ten points below full precision.

**Training cost.** On τ²-Bench with Qwen3-8B, the 32-GPU-hour checkpoint reaches 31.68% Pass@1, against QeRL's 29.84% at 96 GPU hours and On-Policy QAD's 28.77% at about 40. The "67% fewer GPU hours" is 32 against 96. Curves are means over four evaluation runs per checkpoint.

**Inference cost.** Successful τ²-Bench tasks average 4.0K output tokens (BF16 3.9K; quantized baselines 4.1K to 5.2K), 11.6 interaction steps (BF16 11.4; baselines 12.1 to 13.8), and 4.24 tool calls. Each average is over that method's own successful tasks, so the task sets differ, as the appendix says.

**Ablations (BFCL V4 Overall; full method 46.84, On-Policy QAD 44.43).**

| Change | Score |
|---|---|
| Intervene at a random eligible turn | 43.52 |
| Intervene at first action mismatch | 45.01 |
| Unguided student retry | 44.46 |
| Teacher takes over the turn | 45.06 |
| Free-form teacher prompt | 44.78 |
| Student writes the structured prompt | 41.74 |
| GPT-5.6-Sol writes the structured prompt | 45.27 |
| No local verification | 42.18 |
| Keep corrections only from finally successful tasks | 46.30 |
| Reverse KL throughout | 45.29 |
| Forward KL throughout | 44.58 |
| Routing reversed | 44.28 |

Three variants fall below plain on-policy distillation: random intervention, student-written prompts, and unverified corrections. Bad corrections cost more than no corrections. A stronger external model writing the prompt (45.27) does worse than the same-family full-precision teacher.

**Limits on the evidence (reader observations).**

* Table 1 has no intervals and the number of training runs per cell is not stated; only the τ²-Bench efficiency curves show variation (four evaluation runs). The margins over QeRL on single benchmarks are one to three points.
* Without the initial GRPO post-training (Qwen3-8B, W4A16), the lead over QeRL shrinks to 0.82 points on BFCL V4 (39.67 against 38.85) and 0.13 points on τ²-Bench (29.05 against 28.92). The paper reads this table as the ordering being unchanged; the τ²-Bench gap is small enough that this is not established without intervals.
* All 30 entries in the W4A8 block are multiples of 0.05 and 21 are multiples of 0.1, while the W4A16 and W2A16 blocks have 4 of 55 and 1 of 30 entries on the 0.05 grid (script check on the HTML table). The Qwen3.5-4B BF16 row is also given to one decimal. The paper does not say whether these blocks were produced or rounded differently.
* QeRL and On-Policy QAD were ported from their reference setups (NVFP4 on newer GPUs) to integer group-wise quantization on A100s, so their numbers are this paper's adaptations.
* In the ablation table the full method's Non-Live score (88.29) is above BF16 (87.14), so the student is not bounded by the teacher on every subset.
* The diagnostic numbers 9.6% and 82.3% measure whether a correct action appears under steering during training, not what the distilled student does unaided.

The central claim, that locally verified, student-generated corrections are a better distillation target than unguided rollouts, is supported by the ablations on one benchmark and by consistent margins across fifteen benchmark-model cells at W4A16. The size of the advantage over RL is less certain than the tables suggest.

# Q9 Contributions

1. A description of how quantized agents fail (overlooked requirements and conditions that show up as state-transition divergences from a successful full-precision run), with a measurement that the student's own retries almost never fix them.
2. Closed-loop teacher steering: divergence localization by environment state, structured guidance refined from execution feedback, and local verification against the reference transition.
3. A turn-routed distillation objective that internalizes guided behavior without the guidance.
4. Results on five benchmarks, three models, and three precisions, with training-cost and trajectory-length comparisons.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Limits the paper states.** The method needs successful teacher references, restorable environment state, and reliable local verification; none of these is tested when absent. A failed rollout with no successful reference gets no correction, so tasks the teacher cannot solve are untouched. Local verification checks one transition, not the reasoning or the final outcome.
* **Report variation.** Repeat the W4A16 comparison over training seeds, at least for the no-GRPO setting where the lead is smallest (reader suggestion).
* **Beyond quantization.** The loop needs only a stronger and a weaker policy sharing an environment with restorable state. It could be tried for distilling a large agent into a small one, where the two models do not share a checkpoint (reader suggestion; the paper's GPT-5.6-Sol prompt-writer result hints that a mismatched teacher steers less well).
* **Environments without restore.** Web and desktop tasks rarely allow state restoration; a replay-based or approximate version of local verification would be needed.
* **Emergent recovery.** The appendix shows the distilled student recovering from execution errors mid-rollout, which was not a training target. Measuring how often this happens would say whether the method teaches specific fixes or a more general habit of checking preconditions.
