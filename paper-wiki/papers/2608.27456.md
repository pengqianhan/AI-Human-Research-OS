# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** an MLLM agent embodied as a first-person character inside a real-scale, georegistered 3D reconstruction of Hong Kong, receiving egocentric visual observations oₜ, an optional overhead interactive map it may pan/zoom/orbit (but which computes no route for it), and a task instruction; the agent's only privileged channel is its own emitted structured actions — it has no access to remaining distance, shortest path, or any route API.
* **Output (Y):** UrbanGround, a closed-loop evaluation sandbox and a five-level task ladder testing whether local perceptual grounding (recognizing what is visible now) composes into sustained, goal-directed spatial agency (navigating, adapting, and completing multi-step tasks) as the agent moves through a large, dynamic, physically simulated city.
* **Core problem:** MLLM agents can interpret an individual street view, but "urban agency depends on whether such local evidence remains useful after the agent starts to move" — once the agent turns a corner, a landmark that supported its last decision may leave view even though its location still matters. The paper investigates, in a physically constrained real-scale replica rather than a synthetic or viewpoint-sampled proxy, how far current MLLM agents can turn local perception into reliable, sustained action.
* **Scope:** evaluation only — the paper introduces no training method or new model; it builds a testbed and evaluates ten existing frontier and near-frontier MLLMs as agents within it, using Hong Kong as the sole evaluated city.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Prior spatial-agent testbeds split into three lines that each fall short of what UrbanGround targets. Game environments (MineDojo, GROOT, and several Minecraft-focused extended-trajectory systems) offer long-horizon interaction but their spatial structure is game-defined rather than grounded in a real place. Physical-world indoor embodied QA/navigation benchmarks (OpenEQA, NavBench, and related systems) evaluate real embodied reasoning but at a spatial scope small enough to need only local reference frames, not sustained large-scale orientation. Urban-scale prior work is closest but still falls into two limited families: street-view-based systems (VELMA, CityNavAgent, CitySeeker, CityWalker) are mediated by discretely sampled viewpoints rather than continuous physics, and interactive urban simulators (MetaUrban, UrbanWorld, SimWorld) use generated scenes that lack the geographic and structural complexity of a real city. UrbanGround's stated novelty is being the first real-scale, georegistered urban sandbox — built from official territory-wide 3D geospatial data rather than sampled imagery or procedural generation — that supports continuous first-person physics and closed-loop interaction, letting the same three research questions (grounding, distance-scaling, and robustness to change) be tested under conditions a synthetic or viewpoint-based testbed cannot reproduce.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If a testbed grounds an MLLM agent in a continuous-physics, georegistered reconstruction of a real city and separates the evaluation into (a) whether the agent can answer spatial questions from active local observation, (b) whether that local grounding still supports navigation as destinations become farther and less explicitly specified, and (c) whether resulting behavior survives changes in route availability and pedestrian motion — then contemporary MLLM agents will show reliable atomic visual-recognition ability but comparatively unreliable orientation and pedestrian-aware movement, and the central failure will not be any single missing atomic skill but the *composition* gap: local abilities failing to aggregate into sustained goal-directed behavior as interaction horizon grows, with errors accumulating rather than being corrected through replanning.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Game environments.** MineDojo (Fan et al., 2022) and GROOT (Cai et al., 2024) establish long-horizon agent interaction in Minecraft; several more recent systems extend Minecraft-focused trajectories further, but the paper's stated limitation is that success in these settings is shaped by game mechanics rather than grounded in real-world spatial structure.
* **Physical-world indoor embodied QA/navigation.** OpenEQA (Majumdar et al., 2024) and NavBench (Qiao et al., 2025), plus related systems, test real embodied spatial reasoning but at a bounded spatial extent that the paper argues requires only local reference frames, not sustained cross-district orientation.
* **Urban-scale prior work — closest to this paper.** Street-view-based agents (VELMA, CityNavAgent, CitySeeker, CityWalker) operate over discretely sampled viewpoints without continuous physics; interactive urban simulators (MetaUrban, UrbanWorld, SimWorld) provide continuous interaction but in generated scenes that the paper says lack real geographic complexity. UrbanGround is positioned as combining what neither family has alone: real geographic complexity (Hong Kong Lands Department's official 3D Visualisation Map and 3D Pedestrian Network) with continuous, closed-loop first-person physics.
* **Key people and affiliations.** Lead author Tianjie Ju and corresponding authors Zhuosheng Zhang, Hao Fei, Qi Gu, and Gongshen Liu, with a full author list spanning Shanghai Jiao Tong University, the National University of Singapore, Meituan, the Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shanghai University, and the University of Oxford; submitted 2026-08-27.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Real-scale, georegistered sandbox built from official geospatial data.** UrbanGround streams a Unity-based reconstruction of Hong Kong built from the Hong Kong Lands Department's 3D Visualisation Map (a textured mesh from oblique imagery, WGS84-referenced) and 3D Pedestrian Network (a georeferenced walkway connectivity graph), with physical collision, so agent motion is constrained the way a real pedestrian's would be. All agent positions are logged in real geographic coordinates.
2. **Configurable environmental dynamics layered on top of the static city.** A continuous time system drives sky, illumination, and shadows (fixed or advancing); a weather system (rain, fog) affects visibility and surface rendering; a pedestrian population, built from Microsoft's open-source Rocketbox avatar library, spawns and animates on the pedestrian network with collision tracking — giving the sandbox the same city under varying, controllable conditions rather than one static snapshot.
3. **Closed-loop first-person and map action space, with no privileged shortcuts.** The agent acts through first-person controls (move, sprint, look, jump, open_map, each parameterized by direction/duration) and map controls (map_select, map_pan, map_zoom, map_orbit, close_map) plus a terminate action, and can freely inspect an overhead interactive map — but that map computes no route, and the agent is never given remaining distance, shortest path, or any route API; every spatial estimate the agent uses must come from its own accumulated observation and reasoning.
4. **A five-level evaluation ladder isolating what composes into what.** Level 1 (Visual Recognition, Orientation, Active Exploration QA) tests local grounding alone (RQ1: can the agent ground a scene well enough to answer spatial questions after active observation?). Level 2 (Short/Long/Instructional/Constrained Navigation) and Level 3 (Place-Type Search, Implicit Intent Inference) test whether that grounding supports navigation as destinations grow farther and less explicit (RQ2). Level 4 (Time-Window Scheduling, Multi-Stop Planning) extends RQ2 to sustained multi-task maintenance. Level 5 (Dynamic Road Closure, Navigation Among Pedestrians) tests RQ3: whether behavior survives route-availability and pedestrian-motion changes introduced mid-episode. All 810 task instances are manually verified across multiple Hong Kong districts, with a human tester completing each within a 100-step limit to confirm feasibility before it enters the benchmark.
5. **Metrics matched to what each level tests.** Question-answering tasks are scored by answer accuracy against an annotated ground truth; navigation success requires the final position to land within 15 m of the destination while satisfying any task constraints; Pedestrian Network Adherence (PNA) measures the fraction of action time spent on the registered walkway network (a *local compliance* signal, distinct from task success); and the dynamic tasks add a Safe Progress Rate (SPR, fraction of episodes that avoid an invalidated route segment) and a Pedestrian Collision Rate (PCR).

# Q6 Experiment Design

Ten MLLMs are evaluated as agents with no fine-tuning: GPT-5.5, GPT-5.4, and GPT-5.2 (OpenAI); Claude-Opus-5 and Claude-Opus-4.6 (Anthropic); Gemini-3.6-Flash and Gemini-3.1-Pro (Google); and Doubao-Seed-2.0-Pro, GLM-5V-Turbo, and Kimi-K3. On RQ1 (local grounding, Table 1), visual recognition accuracy is uniformly strong (82.5–93.8% across the four models reported in the note's source table) but orientation accuracy is dramatically weaker and close to the random baseline for a 4-option question (23–58%, e.g. Claude-Opus-5 58.3%, GPT-5.5 40.0%); overall accuracy tracks this gap (63.6–79.1%), and PNA sits systematically below overall accuracy for every model, read as agents sacrificing pedestrian-network compliance for faster task completion.

On RQ2 (navigation scaling, Table 2), short-range navigation is achievable (23.8–75.0% across models) but long-range navigation collapses to near-zero for every model tested (0.0–2.5%), and multi-task planning (time-window scheduling, multi-stop planning) is likewise near-zero (0–3.3%); overall navigation success ranges from 8.1% (Gemini-3.6-Flash) to 22.9% (Claude-Opus-4.6). A distance-stratified breakdown (Figure 7) shows performance declining continuously by distance quartile even *within* the nominally "short-range" task category — there is no discrete distance threshold at which success falls off a cliff. A partial-credit analysis (Figure 8) finds that over half of long-range episodes still reduce goal distance despite under 2% reaching full success, and multi-stop agents reach only roughly 10–20% of required destinations on average — evidence the failure is one of sustained composition and correction, not of the agent moving randomly.

On RQ3 (robustness, Tables 3–4), local QA accuracy degrades moderately but unevenly across lighting/weather conditions (e.g. Claude-Opus-5 falls from 79.1% clear to 66.4% dusk; Gemini-3.1-Pro falls from 53.2% clear to 37.3% night), while navigation accuracy under the same conditions is more model-dependent (GPT-5.5 and Kimi-K3 stay comparatively stable; GPT-5.4 and Gemini-3.1-Pro degrade more sharply). Under dynamic conditions, road-closure Safe Progress Rate is low (10–46% across models) despite Pedestrian Network Adherence remaining high (roughly 93–96%) in the same episodes — agents keep locally compliant movement on the registered network even after the planned route is globally invalidated by a closure. Pedestrian collision rates are uniformly high (76–90% across models) regardless of PNA, indicating network-geometry compliance does not imply obstacle-aware movement around simulated pedestrians.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

Introduces UrbanGround: 810 manually verified task instances across multiple Hong Kong districts, built on Hong Kong Lands Department 3D Visualisation Map and 3D Pedestrian Network data (public-domain geospatial sources) and Microsoft's open-source Rocketbox pedestrian-avatar library. # Q9 Contributions

1. The first real-scale, georegistered urban sandbox for MLLM agents built from official territory-wide 3D geospatial data (Hong Kong) with continuous first-person physics and closed-loop interaction, rather than sampled street-view images or a procedurally generated city.
2. A five-level spatial-agency evaluation ladder that decomposes the problem from atomic local grounding through sustained multi-task navigation to adaptation under dynamic route and pedestrian changes, isolating which layer of the composition breaks down.
3. An evaluation of ten contemporary MLLM agents showing a consistent pattern across models: strong visual recognition, weak (near-chance) orientation, navigation success that collapses with distance well before any single hard failure threshold, and route-adaptation failure (low Safe Progress Rate) that persists even when local pedestrian-network compliance stays high — evidence for a composition gap rather than a missing single atomic skill.
4. Public release of the sandbox (native and web builds), task suite, and evaluation framework for further study of sustained spatial agency in real-world-grounded environments.

# Q10 Next Steps

The paper's implicit and stated limitations (per this note's extraction): evaluation covers Hong Kong only, so generalization to other cities' street layouts and pedestrian norms is untested; models are evaluated only through their API, so internal state or memory dynamics cannot be inspected to explain *why* orientation or long-range navigation fails; the interaction budget is capped at 100 steps, which may understate real-world long-horizon demands; the action space is a simplified discrete first-person control scheme rather than continuous embodied motion; and pedestrian behavior is scripted animation rather than modeled human motion, which likely affects the reported collision rates in ways the paper does not decompose. Implied future directions include architectures for persistent spatial-state maintenance as landmarks leave view, explicit replanning/recovery mechanisms triggered on route invalidation rather than repeated forward motion, fusion of first-person and map-based evidence, comparison against specialized navigation models rather than only general-purpose MLLMs, and extension to additional cities' geospatial data.

This note adds one connection beyond the paper's own framing: UrbanGround's central finding — that agents maintain locally compliant behavior (high Pedestrian Network Adherence) while failing the global condition that behavior was supposed to serve (low road-closure Safe Progress Rate, near-zero long-range success despite continuous local progress) — is the navigation-domain instance of the same local-versus-global gap this wiki's [Agent environments](../topics/agent-environments.md) topic already surfaces through evaluator design (e.g. VibeWorlding's physical-feasibility check passing while intent-fulfillment still fails): a locally-scored proxy signal can stay high exactly while the property it was meant to proxy for collapses.
