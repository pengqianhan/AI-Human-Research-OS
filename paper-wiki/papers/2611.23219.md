# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a frozen LLM in a minimal sandboxed harness (Claude Code or Pi, default system prompt, memory, web, and subagents disabled) acting as teacher, with a goal prompt (improve a player's performance on unseen single-player games and keep the artifacts general), a default player prompt, a toolset drawn from {Read/Write, Bash, Gameplay}, and a budget of 20 context updates and 150 observed gameplays.
* **Output (Y):** the player context c_final (prompt plus any text files the teacher writes), evaluated by fresh player instances of the same model on held-out game benchmarks.
* **Constraints:** no human-written games and no validation scores; the teacher writes its own games (PuzzleScript, or Python in ablations), which the Gameplay tool rejects if they do not compile or a BFS solver proves them unwinnable; the player plays blind, with binary reward, fixed turn horizons, and output-token budgets; reasoning effort low.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Asymmetric self-play, GEPA, ACE, MCE, Meta-Harness, the Darwin Gödel Machine, and Hyperagents all let models generate data, rewrite context, or edit their own code, but the task distribution, objective, and outer loop are specified externally. Open-Ended Optimization and the concurrent ASPIRE leave the procedure to the model but keep access to external data or hidden evaluation items from the same setting. Here the model gets neither tasks nor scores, must invent its own practice and progress checks, and is judged by transfer to different environments.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

Sufficiently capable models no longer need a prescribed self-improvement algorithm: given primitive tools for generating experience and editing text, they compose a learning procedure that improves held-out performance, and this meta-capability increases across model generations.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Asymmetric self-play and unsupervised environment design.** Teacher-student task generation, learnability rewards, and collapse-prevention mechanisms (judges, real-data goalposts).
* **Context and harness optimization.** GEPA, ACE, MCE, Meta-Harness; self-modifying agents (Darwin Gödel Machine, Hyperagents).
* **Self-constructed learning procedures.** Open-Ended Optimization; ASPIRE (concurrent).
* **Cognitive frameworks.** Zimmerman's cyclical model of self-regulated learning; the Zone of Proximal Development.
* **Benchmarks.** PuzzleScript community games, ARC-AGI-3, MazeBench.
* **Key people.** Shobhita Sundaram and Phillip Isola (MIT CSAIL), Cansu Sancaktar (Tübingen). Likely critics: self-play and UED researchers who would ask how much of the effect is the strong game priors the paper itself flags.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **Teacher-player loop with no loop prescribed.** Algorithm 1 only samples teacher actions until the budget runs out; every update is one edit tool-call to the player directory. The Gameplay tool runs a fresh player with teacher-chosen artifacts and returns score, end reason, remaining budget, and a transcript path.
2. **Measure by transfer.** Self-improvement is J(c_final) − J(c_0) on held-out benchmarks: PuzzleScript-t20 (20 human-authored levels, solution length ≤ 12, 20 turns), PuzzleScript-t30 (20 levels, length 13 to 22, 30 turns), ARC-AGI-3 Level 1 (17 games), MazeBench (16 rooms of a 3D ASCII world, 100 turns, SPL). Benchmarks drop levels that Claude Opus-5 solves in 8 of 8 rollouts, and the baseline is rerun afterwards.
3. **Behavior analysis.** Algorithmic counts (A/B tests, trivial games, update sizes, updates that raise an observed win rate); an adapted rubric of 13 self-regulated-learning subprocesses scored 0 to 3 by GPT 6-Astra; replay rates against observed win rate.
4. **Information ablations.** What the teacher is told (summaries of a nine-game Sokoban-style set at 0 to 6,551 tokens) and what its tools impose (free versus t20-matched turn and token budgets).

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Models.** Claude Opus-4.6 and Opus-5, GPT 5.6-Sol and 6-Astra, GLM-4.7 and 5.3; teacher and player are the same model; three teacher seeds each.
* **Evaluation.** Four rollouts per PuzzleScript and ARC level, two per MazeBench room.
* **Comparisons.** GEPA with Opus-5 as player and reflection model on 10 training and 10 validation real PuzzleScript levels, halted at the matched 150-gameplay budget. Toolset ablations (Read-Write, +Bash, +Gameplay at 20 or 30 turns, Python Gameplay). Cross-model transfer of c_final between Opus-5 and GPT 6-Astra. Budget scaling, saturated-game checks, and a check of teacher-observed against measured gains (8 rollouts).

# Q8 Evidence Quality

**Who self-improves (Figure 4, PuzzleScript-t20).** The newer model of each family beats its default-context baseline: +20% Opus-5, +10% GPT 6-Astra, +6% GLM 5.3; Opus-4.6, GPT 5.6-Sol, and GLM-4.7 show little or no gain. Opus-5's self-organized learning on invented games roughly matches GEPA optimizing on 20 real games at the same rollout budget (Figure 3).

**Transfer (Figure 9, Opus-5).** Context learned with 20-turn PuzzleScript gameplay improves PuzzleScript-t20 by 20%, t30 by 9%, ARC-AGI-L1 by 21%, and MazeBench SPL by 0.15. Gameplay at 30 turns does best on all benchmarks, including t20. Read-Write alone beats the baseline but recovers under half the Gameplay gain except on MazeBench; adding Bash helps further, and in one Bash-only session the teacher wrote and ran its own game simulator. Python gameplay recovers nearly all of the PuzzleScript gain in three of four cases.

**Cross-model (Table 1, PuzzleScript-t20 win rate).**

| Player | Artifact source | Win rate (%) |
|---|---|---|
| Opus 5 | none | 25.4 ± 3.6 |
| Opus 5 | GPT-6 Astra | 47.5 ± 4.5 |
| GPT-6 Astra | none | 67.1 ± 3.8 |
| GPT-6 Astra | Opus 5 | 72.1 ± 4.0 |

**Behaviors.** Self-improvers A/B-test more, make fewer trivial games (solution length < 5 with a 100% observed win rate), score higher on most SRL subprocesses, and replay low-to-medium win-rate games most; for Opus-5 and GLM 5.3 most replays fall on games with a 20% to 60% observed win rate, which the paper likens to the inverted U of learnability objectives; some Opus-5 and GPT 6-Astra sessions wrote procedural generators with BFS difficulty filters. Richer descriptions of the Sokoban-style set first help both that set and ARC-AGI-3, then help only the set; imposing t20 budgets raises t20 and lowers t30. On ten saturated levels, learned artifacts keep 98.8% against 100%.

**Limits on the evidence.**

* Gains are read from figures as "%" with ±1 SD over three teacher seeds; Table 1's numbers suggest percentage points of win rate (reader inference). PuzzleScript-t20 has 80 rollouts per evaluation, so one rollout is 1.25 points (reader arithmetic).
* All held-out sets exclude levels Opus-5 solved 8 of 8 times. Rerunning the baseline removes the direct selection effect, but levels kept partly because Opus-5 failed by chance can regress upward for any context, which would favor measured gains for Opus-5 specifically (reader inference). GPT 6-Astra already wins 67% on this Opus-filtered set.
* The SRL judge is GPT 6-Astra, one of the six models being judged (reader observation), and the paper states the SRL alignment is correlational, not causal.
* Only Opus-5 is used for the transfer, GEPA, and information studies, so "newer models generalize" rests on one model beyond PuzzleScript-t20.
* Games are a domain with rich curriculum literature in pretraining data, and the evaluation is short-horizon with 20 updates; performance plateaus with larger budgets (stated).

# Q9 Contributions

1. A controlled, data-free setting in which a frozen LLM must design its own context-optimization procedure, with success measured by transfer to held-out game benchmarks.
2. Evidence that newer generations in three families self-improve there while older ones do not, and descriptions of their strategies in self-regulated-learning and learnability terms.
3. Transfer and ablation results showing self-written text generalizing across horizons, engines, 3D representations, and models, and showing evaluation-setting information trading generalization for specialization.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Stated by the paper.** Characterize less human-interpretable behaviors; test domains where models have weaker priors; find what sustains improvement past 20 updates, toward 100 or 1,000; study the safety implications of unprompted self-improvement.
* **Cross-family judging and selection.** Re-annotate SRL behavior with a judge outside the evaluated families, and build held-out sets filtered by a model not being studied (reader suggestion).
* **Weight-space version.** The same open procedure with fine-tuning as the update primitive would test whether the finding is specific to text optimization.
