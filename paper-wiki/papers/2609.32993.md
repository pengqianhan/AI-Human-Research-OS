# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a pool D of multi-step agent trajectories for a target domain, each a sequence of actions with an episode-level success label (WebArena browser actions, ScienceWorld and WebShop text commands), plus a base policy to fine-tune and, in two of three settings, an environment with a verifier.
* **Output (Y):** a reusable eXperience tree, a deterministic hierarchy whose leaves are canonicalized actions and whose internal nodes are recurring, success-bearing compositions of sub-skills with a depth d_v, and a policy whose weights have absorbed that structure through one of three training integrations.
* **Core problem:** SFT and RLVR consume a trajectory as a flat token stream and weight every token equally, so the sub-procedures that recur across tasks (browse-and-select, pick-up-and-move) are never credited as units, and each scarce trajectory teaches less than it contains. Harnesses that do exploit the structure keep it as LLM-written skills in context, which costs LLM calls, is not reproducible from the data, and never enters the weights.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

The pieces have long histories: option discovery and trajectory segmentation in hierarchical RL (Sutton, Konidaris, CompILE), compression-as-abstraction (Sequitur, DreamCoder, LILO), and BPE applied to quantized continuous-control actions (PRISE, Subwords-as-Skills). LLM-agent work mostly abstracts experience with another LLM (Agent Workflow Memory, Voyager, SkillWeaver, WALT) and leaves it in context. What is new is the combination for LLM agents: a typed-action alphabet from a canonicalizer, a merge criterion that weighs success and length rather than raw recurrence, and the use of the mined vocabulary not as a fixed macro-action space but as a prior fed into training as data, reward, and context. The problem matters because verified agent trajectories are expensive to make, so extracting more from a fixed pool is a direct lever on data efficiency.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If reusable, success-bearing action compositions are mined deterministically from a trajectory pool and used to (a) define RL training instances with a completion bonus that grows with composition depth, (b) add a reward bonus for executed compositions that is active only while the verifier cannot separate a rollout group, or (c) serve as the privileged context of a same-weights teacher, then the resulting policy will outperform the standard recipe trained on identical data and budget, and the gain will depend on the mined structure itself (not on chunking, random trees, or extra compute).

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

* **Canonicalization.** Each raw action (thought excluded) becomes verb<role>: the verb from the function or command, the role from the accessibility-tree role (WebArena), DOM element (MiniWoB++), or a nine-class object lexicon (ScienceWorld); stripped values are kept in slots, so the mapping is deterministic, complete, and lossless.
* **X-Score merging.** For adjacent symbols u, v: X(u, v) = f_uv · (ℓ_u + ℓ_v)^{p_ℓ} · (succ(uv) + ε)^{p_s}, with recurrence f_uv, expanded length ℓ, and success fraction succ; the highest-scoring pair is merged recursively, subject to a compression constraint f_uv − 1 > η(ℓ_u + ℓ_v) and a skill cap. Nodes have depth one more than the deeper child. Applying the tree to a trajectory yields a tiling into maximal skills.
* **Offline RL (no environment).** Each node at a position in a gold trajectory is one GRPO instance: the policy sees the gold prefix, emits up to ℓ_v actions, and continues only while each action reproduces the gold action, with recorded observations appended. Reward r = (1 − α)·(fraction of steps matched) + α·(1 + γ d_v)·[all steps matched], α = 0.3, γ = 0.5.
* **Online RLVR (adaptive skill bonus).** r_i = R_out(τ_i) + λ_g Σ_{v ∈ matched(τ_i)} b_v, with λ_g = λ_0 · clip(1 − w_g / w_ref, 0, 1), where w_g is the group's verifier success fraction. The bonus is full when no rollout succeeds and zero once the group's success rate reaches w_ref. Node values b_v are 1 + 0.5 d_v on ScienceWorld (nested matches credited once) and s_v k_v / T on WebShop (corpus success rate times goal-relevant steps over episode length).
* **OPSD (privileged context).** The same weights act as teacher with the retrieved X-Tree rendering (one line per node with success rate and count) prepended; a per-token distillation term gated by σ(β δ_t) pulls the student toward the teacher only where the context raises the token's probability, with coefficient c = 0.01.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Offline RL on WebArena:** 256 skills mined from 7,974 Go-Browse trajectories; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct SFT on half the pool, then GRPO on nodes from the other half (verl, 805 steps, 6 nodes per step, n = 2); evaluation on the 694 deterministic tasks with a 30-step cap and a normalized SR (official evaluator returns 1, or the gold string appears verbatim for exact-match tasks). Baselines: Go-Browse SFT-Full at 2 and 4 epochs (compute matched), offline RL with outcome-only reward, with random spans, plain mixing of nodes, a depth curriculum, and a random tree. Transfer to Qwen2.5-14B and GLM-4-9B.
* **Online RLVR:** ScienceWorld (80 skills from 1,673 AgentTraj-L trajectories; folds G0 seen, G1 unseen variations, G2 unseen tasks with 19 training and 10 held-out types) and WebShop (48 skills from 1,824 trajectories; 512 held-out episodes, success and graded score). SFT warm starts from 200 / 500 trajectories at 1.5B / 3B / 7B, then GRPO (n = 8) with and without the bonus; λ_0 = 0.75, w_ref = 0.4 / 0.85. Analyses: rollout count n ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}; resource-matched runs mining from the SFT data only; mining-corpus size from 50 to 1,824 trajectories; random-span and random-tree banks; re-mining with held-out task types removed.
* **OPSD:** same warm starts and evaluation; only the teacher's skill bank changes (gpt-oss-120b-written on ScienceWorld, GPT-o3-written on WebShop, or the X-Tree rendering); ablation with an empty bank and a word-scrambled X-Tree (WebShop, 3B).
* **Also:** X-Tree as in-context advice on MiniWoB++ against AWM, SkillWeaver, WALT, and WebXSkill (Appendix C); one X-Tree node per SFT row on ScienceWorld (Appendix D).
* **Reporting:** means over three seeds; Appendix H gives standard deviations and paired t statistics.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

* **Supported:**
  * *Offline RL headline.* WebArena SR 22.9 for the full recipe against 18.4 for Go-Browse SFT-Full, 18.8 with two extra SFT epochs (compute matched), 18.2 with a binary outcome reward, 19.5 with random spans, 19.9 with whole trajectories, and 17.9 with a random tree (below SFT-only). Plain mixing (20.7) and the depth curriculum (21.2) show the completion bonus adds beyond the data unit. Gains concentrate on procedure-heavy sites (admin +6.4, gitlab +5.2, shopping +4.9) and shrink on reddit, map, and wiki.
  * *Transfer of the offline recipe.* Qwen2.5-14B 24.1 → 25.7; GLM-4-9B 14.9 → 16.9.
  * *Online bonus.* Ahead of outcome-only GRPO in 11 of 12 cells of the rollout sweep, with the largest margins at small n (n = 4: 24.2 / 23.9 / 12.1 → 28.0 / 27.5 / 16.5 on G0 / G1 / G2) and a narrowing lead at n = 16, consistent with the designed fade-out. WebShop success improves by up to 3.6 and graded score by up to 4.6, growing with scale.
  * *Not the extra mining data.* Resource-matched WebShop runs mining only from the SFT data still gain (+1.1 / +3.2 / +1.0 across three warm-start sizes); a 100-trajectory X-Tree (11 skills) already beats outcome-only (75.2 vs 73.8); re-mining ScienceWorld without the held-out task types keeps a +2.0 G2 lead.
  * *OPSD.* The rendered X-Tree matches or beats LLM-written banks at every scale (by up to +3.7 SR on ScienceWorld and +1.7 on WebShop) with no LLM calls, and at 7B OPSD reaches +5.8 SR (ScienceWorld) and +4.4 graded score (WebShop) over outcome-only RLVR. An empty privileged context stays at the outcome-only level (74.2 vs 74.0), so the gain is not the distillation mechanism alone.
  * *Side results.* As in-context advice on MiniWoB++, X-Tree leads four LLM-written banks at three backbones (49.8 / 65.1 / 72.6 vs 44.9 / 60.4 / 68.1 no-skill); one node per SFT row cuts tokens per supervised action 4.0x (1,766 → 446) while adding +4.9 / +2.4 / +9.1 SR.
* **Weaker or bounded:**
  * *Margins against spread.* Most online gains are 1-5 points while reported standard deviations are around 2.5 (e.g., 38.6 ± 2.5 for 1.5B outcome-only G0); the paper supplies paired t statistics in Appendix H, which this note did not read in full (reader note).
  * *Structure ablation is mixed on held-out tasks.* On ScienceWorld G2 a random tree (26.7) outscores X-Tree (25.6) and random spans recover +2.2 of X-Tree's +3.3 on G1; the authors concede the three banks are comparable on the held-out fold and rest the attribution on G0 and G1.
  * *WebArena protocol.* The main SR is a normalized metric that also credits exact-match tasks whose gold string appears in the output; the official-evaluator numbers are in Table 17, which this note did not read (reader note). All methods share the metric.
  * *Scrambled X-Tree keeps most of the OPSD gain.* On WebShop 3B, scrambling the words of the rendering drops only 0.6 (76.5 vs 77.1), so part of the privileged-context effect comes from vocabulary rather than the mined chains (reader note).
  * *Scale and models.* Qwen2.5 at 1.5B-7B for online settings, a single 14B and GLM-4-9B transfer check offline; canonicalizers are hand-written per environment, so applying X-Tree to a new action space needs a new template.
  * *Integrations are never combined,* and the tree is mined once from a fixed pool rather than from the improving policy; both are stated limitations.

# Q9 Contributions

* A deterministic, LLM-free miner that turns a trajectory pool into an auditable hierarchy of reusable skills, with a merge criterion (X-Score) that separates verifier-rewarded compositions from frequent but failure-prone ones.
* Three ways to train the hierarchy into weights: node-as-instance offline RL with a depth-scaled completion reward, an adaptive skill bonus for RLVR that is active only when the outcome signal is silent, and X-Tree as privileged context for on-policy self-distillation.
* Evidence across three environments and three scales that each integration beats its standard recipe at matched data and budget, with controls (random spans, random trees, compute-matched SFT, resource-matched mining) that attribute most of the gain to the mined structure.
* A demonstration that an LLM-written skill bank can be replaced by a counted one at zero LLM cost in OPSD and as in-context advice.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Stated by the authors:** mine X-Tree from the policy's own trajectories so structure and policy improve together; combine the three integrations in one model; use the tree's thin regions to decide which trajectories are worth synthesizing.
* **Held-out attribution (reader note):** the G2 ablation leaves open whether X-Score beats random merging for unseen tasks; more seeds or a larger held-out fold would settle it.
* **Alphabet without hand-written templates (reader note):** the canonicalizer is the one environment-specific component; learning the role lexicon, or deriving it from tool schemas, would make the miner portable to new harnesses.
* **Relevance to this repository (reader note):** the same counting could run over this repository's agent transcripts to find recurring command sequences worth packaging as skills, with the success-weighted X-Score as a cheap check on whether a candidate skill actually co-occurs with successful runs.
