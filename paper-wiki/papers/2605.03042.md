# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a research intuition or idea, handed to the harness at the start of a long-horizon, multi-session ML research workflow (literature review, hypothesis generation, experimentation, manuscript writing, rebuttal).
* **Output (Y):** research artifacts (experiment logs, a manuscript, a claim ledger) produced by an executor model and iteratively checked by a reviewer model from a different model family, plus per-claim audit verdicts (supported / partially supported / invalidated) that trace each reported claim back to raw evidence.
* **Core problem:** a single agent performing long-horizon research does not fail visibly; it produces *plausible unsupported success* — results that are misreported, incomplete, or that silently inherit the executor's own framing, with no independent check on the chain from evidence to claim.
* **Scope:** contributes a system (harness architecture, assurance mechanisms) plus early deployment/adoption evidence; it explicitly does not include a controlled, compute-matched benchmark isolating the causal effect of cross-model review (deferred to future work, Appendix E).

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Autonomous research agents (The AI Scientist, AI Scientist-v2, Agent Laboratory, AI co-scientist, data-to-paper) are an active area, but the paper identifies three recurring gaps it targets directly: (1) many systems use the same or a closely related model family for both execution and review, a same-model self-refinement pattern (à la Self-Refine, Reflexion) that leaves correlated blind spots uncaught; (2) workflows are tightly coupled end-to-end, making it hard to replace one stage or resume from a checkpoint; (3) few provide explicit, system-level checks on experimental integrity and manuscript quality beyond a single review pass. ARIS's stated novelty is treating a stringent working assumption — "any long-term task performed by a single agent is unreliable" — as the organizing constraint, and building persistent state, modular skills, and independent cross-family assurance as its three direct system-level responses, rather than optional add-ons.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If a research workflow is (i) decomposed into modular, replaceable skills exchanging state through versioned plain-text artifacts, (ii) backed by a persistent per-project research wiki that survives across sessions, and (iii) checked at each stage by a reviewer model from a different model family under a formal evidence-to-claim audit cascade, then the resulting system will catch more plausible-but-unsupported claims and produce more evidence-grounded research artifacts than a single-agent or same-family self-refinement loop — with the two-role executor/reviewer pattern justified as the minimum needed to break self-play blind spots (framed by analogy to adversarial vs. stochastic bandits and two-player game convergence, explicitly presented as a design analogy rather than a formal result).

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* Directly contrasts with The AI Scientist / AI Scientist-v2 (end-to-end idea-to-paper automation), Agent Laboratory and its AgentRxiv extension (human-in-the-loop checkpoints, shared research repository), AI co-scientist (hypothesis generation focus), and data-to-paper (annotated-data-to-paper with human-verifiable traceability) — the paper's stated differentiator is that few of these expose a per-project structured memory jointly recording literature notes, ideas, experiments, negative outcomes, and claim status for reuse across sessions.
* Builds on self-refinement and multi-agent debate literature (Self-Refine, Reflexion, multi-agent debate, divergent-thinking debate) as motivation for cross-model rather than same-model critique.
* Cites Luo et al.'s critical analysis of autonomous-research-system pitfalls (inappropriate benchmark selection, data leakage, metric misuse, post-hoc selection bias) as direct motivation for the assurance stack in §3.
* Distinguishes itself from general-purpose or software-engineering agent frameworks (AutoGen, CAMEL, OpenHands, SWE-agent, MetaGPT, ChatDev) by being research-specific with domain-aware skills and enforced reviewer/executor family separation; positions itself relative to Lee et al.'s Meta-Harness (outer-loop harness optimization) as a hand-engineered harness with only a prototype version of that outer loop.
* Authors: a 3-author team (Ruofeng Yang, Yongcan Li, Shuai Li) from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and the Shanghai Innovation Institute; submitted 2026-05-03.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

**Cross-model adversarial collaboration.** The executor produces an artifact (code, manuscript section, experiment design); a reviewer — recommended to be from a different model family (e.g., Claude executor with GPT-family reviewer via Codex/Oracle MCP, or vice versa) — reads the artifact directly (not the executor's summary of it, to avoid inheriting its framing) and returns a structured score plus action items under a predefined rubric. The loop repeats until the score clears a threshold (default 6/10) with critical items resolved, or a round cap (default 4) is hit. Reviewers are configured along two axes: access scope (document-only / artifact-augmented / repository-level) and context policy (fresh, to avoid confirmation bias, vs. cross-round, to verify prior issues were addressed).

**Three-layer architecture.** The *execution layer* is a library of 65+ single-file SKILL.md specifications (YAML frontmatter + natural-language workflow spec) exchanging state through versioned plain-text artifacts (e.g., IDEA_REPORT.md → EXPERIMENT_LOG.md → NARRATIVE_REPORT.md), plus a per-project research wiki storing four entity types (papers, ideas, experiments, claims) connected by eight typed relationships (extends, contradicts, addresses_gap, inspired_by, tested_by, supports, invalidates, supersedes) — critically, retaining *rejected* ideas so future sessions don't re-propose dead ends. The *orchestration layer* chains skills into five workflows (idea discovery, experiment bridge, auto-review, paper writing, rebuttal) across four phases, with four effort presets (lite ≈0.4×, balanced 1×, max ≈2.5×, beast ≈5–8×) that scale search/review breadth without changing the reviewer's own reasoning budget. The *assurance layer* runs a three-stage evidence-to-claim audit cascade: Stage 1 (experiment-integrity audit) checks for model-derived reference labels, self-normalized scores, phantom results, dead-code metric inflation, and scope inflation; Stage 2 (result-to-claim mapping) assigns each claim a supported/partial/invalidated verdict into a claim ledger, propagating Stage 1 integrity failures; Stage 3 (paper-claim audit) uses a fresh zero-context reviewer thread to cross-check every quantitative manuscript claim against the ledger and raw files. Manuscript assurance separately adds a five-pass scientific-editing pipeline, a 20-category proof-verification taxonomy for theory-heavy sections, dual source+PDF visual review, and a three-axis citation audit (existence, metadata correctness, context appropriateness). A prototype meta-optimization outer loop passively logs usage events, analyzes patterns (override frequency, repeated tool failures, review-score plateaus) via /meta-optimize, and proposes SKILL.md patches that a GPT-5.4-xhigh reviewer must score ≥7/10 before being surfaced to a human, who makes the final call — the system never auto-applies harness changes.

# Q6 Experiment Design

The paper reports deployment/adoption evidence rather than a controlled benchmark, and explicitly labels this a limitation. The skill library grew from 21 core skills at initial release to 65+ spanning robotics, hardware design, communications, mathematical proof, grant writing, and presentation generation; three additional executor platforms are documented via community-maintained adaptation guides beyond the three natively tested (Claude Code, Codex CLI, Cursor). One documented overnight run (~8 hours) on a single paper is reported end-to-end: four review-revise rounds, an internal reviewer score rising from 5.0 to 7.5/10, more than 20 GPU experiments launched, and claims removed that lacked evidential support. The paper is explicit that this is one trajectory on one paper, not causal evidence that cross-family review beats same-family review or that two reviewers is an optimal committee size; isolating that effect is deferred to a controlled, compute-matched benchmark protocol described as future work (Appendix E).

# Q7 Datasets and Code

No benchmark dataset is introduced; the "evaluation" is observational deployment evidence (adoption footprint, one documented overnight run) rather than a held-out test set. Project page / code: [github.com/wanshuiyin/Auto-claude-code-research-in-sleep](https://github.com/wanshuiyin/Auto-claude-code-research-in-sleep). The system also ships as a standalone Rust CLI (ARIS-Code, built on the "claw-code" harness) bundling all skills as slash commands with a native cross-model review tool.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

The paper's own framing is candid about its evidentiary limits: "the main limitations are the absence of controlled evaluation and the reliance on observational deployment evidence" (§7). The single overnight run (5.0→7.5/10 reviewer score, 20+ GPU experiments, claims pruned) demonstrates that the assurance/revision loop operates end-to-end on at least one real trajectory, but with a sample size of one it cannot establish that cross-family review outperforms same-family review, nor bound the audit cascade's false-negative rate (how many unsupported claims it fails to catch). The bandit/game-theoretic framing motivating the two-role minimum is explicitly labeled a design analogy rather than a formal regret or equilibrium result — the paper does not claim mathematical guarantees from it. Adoption metrics (65+ skills, three tested plus three community-adapted platforms) evidence engineering maturity and uptake but not research-output quality. The paper also discloses a self-referential limitation: ARIS itself assisted in drafting and reviewing this report, with authors retaining final editorial responsibility — a transparency point rather than an independent validation.

# Q9 Contributions

1. A cross-model assurance stack — a three-stage evidence-to-claim audit cascade (experiment-integrity audit, result-to-claim mapping, paper-claim audit against a claim ledger) plus manuscript-level checks (five-pass editing, proof verification, visual PDF review, three-axis citation audit) — operationalizing the "plausible unsupported success" risk into concrete, checkable failure modes.
2. A modular three-layer system (execution/orchestration/assurance) with 65+ reusable Markdown skills, a persistent cross-session research wiki with typed paper/idea/experiment/claim relationships, adjustable effort presets, configurable reviewer routing, and a prototype reviewer-gated meta-optimization loop over the harness's own skill files.
3. Early deployment evidence across multiple executor platforms (three natively tested, three community-adapted) with a documented end-to-end overnight run, reported candidly as observational rather than causal.

# Q10 Next Steps

* The paper's own stated directions: a compute-matched controlled benchmark to isolate the causal contribution of cross-model heterogeneity (protocol sketched in Appendix E); local-only reviewer routing for confidentiality-sensitive repositories (not yet implemented); and user studies of researcher productivity.
* A more speculative direction the authors flag explicitly as untested: generalizing the cross-model accountability primitives (reviewer independence, evidence-to-claim audit, provenance-aware claim ledgers) beyond manuscripts, inserting them between any model output and a downstream training-data or reward signal — framed as a hypothesis about mitigating LLM-judge bias and model-collapse-under-recursive-training risk, not a claim made in this report.
* Open questions the retrieved material leaves unresolved: how the three-stage audit cascade's false-negative rate compares to human peer review; whether reviewer-bias amplification (the loop overfitting to one reviewer model's preferences) is measurable and correctable; and how the system would need to change for domains without deterministic evaluators (a question this wiki already tracks under [Research verifiability](../topics/research-verifiability.md)).
* **Research-OS fit:** ARIS's file-system-as-state design (all session state in versionable text, checkpoint-based resumption) and its rejected-idea-preserving research wiki are close structural analogues to this repository's own memory-layer contract (`PROJECT_MEMORY.md`, `paper_skeleton.md`) — worth comparing its typed paper/idea/experiment/claim relationships against how this repo currently links claims to evidence.
