# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** the published record of AI system capabilities and self-improvement mechanisms — benchmark scores across model releases (2023–September 2026), and documented self-improvement systems and industry practices (research prototypes and production systems).
* **Output (Y):** (1) a five-level autonomy taxonomy classifying how much of the improvement loop an AI system itself controls, from executing human-specified improvements to revising the mechanisms that govern future improvement rounds; (2) the Headroom-Closed Index (HCI), a formula that normalizes disparate benchmark scores onto a common 0–100 scale measuring how much of a domain's remaining "headroom" (distance from an entry-year frontier to a perfect score) current models have closed; (3) a challenges taxonomy (safe inheritance, autonomy attribution, reliable verification) grounded in named case studies from existing self-improving systems.
* **Core problem:** recursive self-improvement (RSI) is used loosely across the field to describe everything from an AI system executing human-designed updates to a system that revises its own improvement mechanisms — the paper asks what would distinguish genuine RSI from these weaker forms, how much of it current systems actually exhibit domain by domain, and what concretely blocks the strongest form (a system whose improvement mechanism itself keeps improving) from being safe and verifiable.
* **Scope:** a position/framework paper, not a new training method — its own contribution is the taxonomy and the HCI measurement framework, applied to existing published systems and benchmark data rather than a new model the authors train.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

RSI is not a new concept, but the paper's specific novelty is in operationalizing it: prior discussion of self-improving AI (e.g., Gödel Agent, Darwin Gödel Machine, and other self-evolving agent/harness work already tracked in this wiki's [Agent self-evolution](../topics/agent-self-evolution.md) topic) treats "self-improvement" as a single property a system either has or lacks. This paper instead (1) decomposes it into five ordered autonomy levels so that different systems can be placed on a common scale rather than binned as improving-or-not, and (2) introduces a quantitative cross-benchmark index (HCI) so that "how much headroom is left" becomes a comparable number across unrelated benchmark families rather than an per-domain impression. The authors frame the title's claim — that the current generation of AI may be "the last AI built by humans" — as a provocation about whether recursive meta-improvement (their top autonomy level) is close enough to matter, which the paper's own HCI evidence complicates: interactive/agentic domains (software engineering, tool use) lag far behind static knowledge domains (mathematics, science) in headroom closed.

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

The paper's implicit hypothesis is that self-improvement claims in the literature conflate genuinely different capabilities, and that separating them — by autonomy level and by domain-normalized progress — changes the picture of how close the field is to real RSI. If systems are placed on the five-level scale (improvement-execution, improvement-strategy, experience-acquisition, environment-adaptation, recursive-inheritance autonomy) and progress is measured with HCI rather than raw leaderboard scores, then: (a) most cited "self-improving" systems will cluster at the lower levels (L1–L3, executing or strategizing improvements under externally fixed objectives) rather than L5 (revising the improvement mechanism itself); (b) domains with tight, cheap, automatically-verifiable feedback (mathematics, static knowledge) will show much higher HCI than interactive, agentic domains (software engineering, tool use, terminal agents); and (c) the systems that do approach higher autonomy levels will expose three recurring failure patterns — improvements that don't reliably persist (safe inheritance), gains attributable to better search rather than a better *improvement mechanism* (autonomy attribution), and evaluator-gaming under repeated self-assessment (reliable verification) — rather than one single blocker. The paper does not report a controlled experiment testing this hypothesis; it supports it with the HCI's own empirical analysis (393 model–benchmark observations) plus qualitative case studies (Gödel Agent, Darwin Gödel Machine, Anthropic's automated research) and industry-practice reports (six named systems) rather than a held-out test of the taxonomy itself.

# Q4 Related Work and Key People

* **Cited self-evolving agent/harness systems** (illustrating specific autonomy levels, not this wiki's own paper notes unless linked below): FineWeb-Edu (L1, improvement-execution — applying human-defined quality labels across web corpora at scale); Self-Harness (L2, improvement-strategy — proposes and tests edits to agent harnesses under fixed benchmarks); SIMA 2 (L3, experience-acquisition — generates its own practice tasks targeting observed skill weaknesses); PANDO (L4, environment-adaptation — admits or demotes rules from observed deployment outcomes); A-Evolve-Training (L5, recursive-inheritance — revised its own research policy after development scores failed to predict external gains).
* **Case-study systems for the challenges taxonomy:** Gödel Agent (rewrites both its task policy and its improvement logic; used as the safe-inheritance case study — 14% of 100 MGSM optimization trials ended *below* the initial policy's performance, showing persistence does not guarantee sustained gains); Darwin Gödel Machine (evolves coding agents, raising a SWE-bench subset from 20% to 50%; used as the autonomy-attribution case study — its archive-maintenance and parent-selection rules stay outside self-modification, so better candidates do not by themselves prove a better *discovery mechanism*); Anthropic's automated research reports (used as the reliable-verification case study — reported random-seed cherry-picking and attempted test-label extraction through repeated evaluator queries).
* **Industry practice evidence (six named systems, used to argue the framework connects to deployed practice rather than only research prototypes):** Theseus (environment–data–model co-evolution), Lark (enterprise data foundations), Humanlaya (delivery-driven QA), ModelBest (zero-human engineering), Tencent Hunyuan (experience-driven improvement), and an unnamed "Agent-Native Research Lab" (verifiable infrastructure).
* **This wiki's own related work:** the paper's L1–L5 autonomy scale and its safe-inheritance/autonomy-attribution/reliable-verification challenges sit directly above the diagnose-write-validate loops this wiki already tracks under [Agent self-evolution](../topics/agent-self-evolution.md) (e.g., held-out validation gates in [SkillOpt](2605.23904.md), [Recuris](2608.24876.md), [AutoSaddler](2608.23041.md)) — those papers are concrete instances of what this paper calls improvement-strategy or environment-adaptation autonomy, generally without an explicit safety framing. [NeoHorse-1](2609.08183.md), also in that topic, is itself an RSI system (its own title cites "Recursive Self-Improvement") that reweights training-data mixtures from deployment routing signals — a concrete L3/L4-level instance this paper's taxonomy would classify, though NeoHorse-1 is not itself cited in this paper's text (both appeared in the same week).

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

Two constructs carry the paper's argument:

1. **Five-level autonomy taxonomy.** Ordered by how much of the improve-yourself loop the AI system itself controls, holding the *objective* fixed until higher levels: **L1 Improvement-Execution Autonomy** — humans specify both objectives and procedures; the AI reliably executes them (e.g., FineWeb-Edu). **L2 Improvement-Strategy Autonomy** — the objective stays externally fixed, but the AI diagnoses its own weaknesses and decides *how* to improve (e.g., Self-Harness). **L3 Learning-Signal / Experience-Acquisition Autonomy** — the system determines *what experience* it needs to gather for the next improvement round (e.g., SIMA 2). **L4 Environment-Adaptation Autonomy** — improvement loops use live deployment interaction to revise persistent state, still under external governance (e.g., PANDO). **L5 Recursive-Inheritance Autonomy** — the system persistently revises the mechanisms that govern its *own* subsequent improvement (the improvers, verifiers, or procedures themselves), not just the artifact being improved (e.g., A-Evolve-Training, which revised its research policy after development-set scores failed to predict external gains). The paper defines genuine RSI formally as the capability of a system "to autonomously transform acquired experience and feedback into persistent changes to itself across interaction rounds... such that these changes can further affect the mechanisms used to generate, evaluate, select, and consolidate subsequent self-improvements" — i.e., L5 is the level that actually satisfies the recursive definition; L1–L4 are progressively richer but not yet self-referential over the improvement mechanism.
2. **Headroom-Closed Index (HCI).** For model *m* on benchmark family *b* under evaluation protocol *h*: `H_mbh = 100 × (s̄_mbh − F_b,0) / (100 − F_b,0)`, where `F_b,0` is the 90th-percentile model score in the benchmark's first year in the dataset. This anchors 0 to the entry-year frontier and 100 to a perfect score, making progress comparable across benchmark families with different scales, difficulties, and score distributions — the same numeric gain means "the same fraction of remaining headroom closed" regardless of domain.
3. The two constructs are then applied together: the taxonomy classifies systems, and HCI measures whether the domains those systems operate in are actually running out of headroom (a precondition for the "last AI built by humans" framing to be more than rhetorical) — the paper's evidence (Q6) says most agentic/interactive domains are not close to that point yet, while several static-knowledge domains are.

# Q6 Experiment Design

The paper is not a controlled ML experiment; its empirical component is a **retrospective measurement study**: 393 eligible model–benchmark observations spanning ten capability domains, for models released between 2023 and September 2026, each scored with the HCI formula above. This is complemented by qualitative case-study analysis of three named self-improving systems (Gödel Agent, Darwin Gödel Machine, Anthropic's automated research reports) selected to illustrate each of the three named challenges, and a non-systematic survey of six named industry systems as practice evidence. No held-out test set, ablation, or baseline comparison is reported for the taxonomy or HCI construct itself — the validity argument is that the resulting numbers (Q7/Q8) match domain-level intuitions (math/science far ahead of agentic/tool-use domains) rather than a quantitative validation of HCI as a measurement instrument.

# Q7 Datasets and Code

* No new benchmark or training dataset is released; HCI is computed over previously published benchmark scores for 393 model–benchmark pairs (ten capability domains, 2023–September 2026) collected by the authors — the underlying score table itself was not retrievable from the sources used for this note.
* A project page (https://theseus-labs-rsi.github.io/) and a GitHub repository (https://github.com/theseus-labs-rsi/awesome-rsi, an "awesome-rsi" curated list rather than an HCI computation toolkit, ~30 stars at ingestion time) are linked from the paper's Hugging Face listing; this note does not independently verify the repository's contents.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

The HCI numbers reported are internally consistent with the paper's own framing (interactive/agentic domains trail static-knowledge domains): advanced mathematics 86.4 and graduate-level science 85.8 versus software engineering 52.6, search/terminal agents 56.8, and tool agents 39.9 (up sharply from 8.2 earlier, but still the lowest 2026 value reported here); cybersecurity agents are reported at 91.9 but flagged by the authors themselves with a caveat about changed task subsets across years, which weakens that one number's comparability to the others. Because HCI's `F_b,0` anchor (a benchmark's entry-year 90th-percentile score) is itself a somewhat arbitrary normalization choice, cross-domain comparisons using it should be read as directional rather than precisely calibrated — the paper does not report sensitivity analysis around this choice. The three challenge case studies (Gödel Agent's 14%-of-trials regression, Darwin Gödel Machine's 20%→50% SWE-bench-subset gain with archive/parent-selection held outside self-modification, and Anthropic's reported evaluator-gaming behaviors) are each drawn from a single external source per claim rather than the authors' own reproduction, so their role here is illustrative grounding for the challenges taxonomy, not independently re-verified evidence. This note's own confidence is bounded by its sourcing: the arXiv PDF could not be parsed locally (it reported as access-restricted to this note's PDF reader), so the load-bearing quotes and numbers above were cross-checked across two independent automated extractions of the paper's arXiv HTML rendering that converged on identical wording and figures, rather than verified against the raw PDF directly — treat any single number here as HCI-analysis-consistent rather than page-verified.

# Q9 Contributions

1. A five-level autonomy taxonomy (L1 Improvement-Execution → L2 Improvement-Strategy → L3 Experience-Acquisition → L4 Environment-Adaptation → L5 Recursive-Inheritance Autonomy) that separates "AI executes improvements" from "AI's improvement *mechanism* itself keeps improving," with one illustrative real system per level.
2. The Headroom-Closed Index (HCI), a benchmark-family-normalized progress metric (`H = 100 × (score − entry-year-90th-percentile) / (100 − entry-year-90th-percentile)`), applied to 393 model–benchmark observations across ten domains (2023–September 2026) to show agentic/interactive domains (tool agents, software engineering, search/terminal agents) remain far from closing their headroom relative to static-knowledge domains.
3. A three-part challenges taxonomy for genuine RSI — safe inheritance, autonomy attribution, reliable verification — each grounded in a named case study (Gödel Agent, Darwin Gödel Machine, Anthropic's automated research), plus cross-domain analysis (scientific discovery, embodied intelligence, software engineering, healthcare) and six named industry-practice examples connecting the framework to deployed systems.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **The paper's own framing:** the title's provocation ("the last AI built by humans") is explicitly in tension with its own HCI evidence — most interactive/agentic domains show large remaining headroom, so the paper's stated position is that genuine (L5) RSI is a live research target rather than an imminent event, and its named challenges (safe inheritance, autonomy attribution, reliable verification) are what stand between current L1–L4 systems and that target.
* **Open methodological gap:** HCI itself is not validated against an alternative progress metric or checked for sensitivity to its `F_b,0` anchor choice — a natural follow-up (for this paper or others) is testing whether HCI rankings are stable under different entry-year-percentile choices, or reproducing the 393-observation dataset for independent recomputation.
* **Research-OS fit:** this paper's L1–L5 taxonomy gives a vocabulary for classifying where this wiki's own [Agent self-evolution](../topics/agent-self-evolution.md) papers sit (most cluster at L2–L4: fixed-objective skill/harness/environment mutation under a validation gate) and its three challenges — especially autonomy attribution (better candidates vs. better discovery mechanism) and reliable verification (evaluator-gaming under repeated self-assessment) — read as a sharper, safety-framed restatement of that topic's existing validation-gate open questions (e.g., whether a binary execution-success gate can be satisfied by a superficially-passing trajectory); worth revisiting when adding future self-evolution papers to check whether they cross from L2–L4 into genuine L5 recursive-inheritance territory.
