# Q1 Problem Definition

* **Input (X):** a task that sets a safety goal against a business goal that are jointly infeasible by construction (sepsis triage: sensitivity at least 95% versus average cost at most 1,000 USD per patient; news recommender: at most 5% misinformation in the top 50 versus at least 5M views; consulting proposals for ten RFPs, several ethically fraught), given either to one agent or to a team of agents running the same alignment-trained model.
* **Output (Y):** the artifact the team builds (a classifier module, a ranked list, a proposal) and the safety and business scores it earns, compared with a single agent's.
* **Constraints:** cooperative teams only, with no adversarial roles; agents share a workspace but not context; all agents in a team use one model; five Anthropic models (Opus 4.1, Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.6, Opus 4.7, Mythos Preview) through the Claude Agent SDK.

# Q2 Problem Novelty

Shen et al. (2026), with overlapping authors, showed that agent teams can be more effective but less aligned than single agents. This paper asks why, whether the gap shrinks with capability, and which team designs close it. The single aligned agent is taken as the behavioral target, since it shows what a fully informed, aligned decision-maker chooses under the same trade-off. The mechanism it tests, diffusion of responsibility, comes from bystander studies, the Challenger analysis, and economic models of organizational failure; the paper separates its behavioral version (agents that would prevent a harm alone fail to do so in a team) from a formal blame-based one analyzed by Naumov and Tao (2025).

# Q3 Scientific Hypothesis

If agents in a team treat system-level safety as outside their assigned subtask, then teams test the integrated system less, discover harms less, and fix discovered harms less often, and those three behaviors account for the team's lower safety score. Interventions that assign system-level safety to one empowered agent should then close the gap through the same behaviors, while interventions that only add review or chat should not.

# Q5 Key Solution Idea

1. **magelab.** A released orchestration harness: per-agent model, role prompt, tools, network topology, and initial tasks in one YAML file; event-driven agent loops with message queues over in-process MCP servers; async or round-based execution; termination at quiescence, timeout, or round limit. Built on the Claude Agent SDK.
2. **Configurations.** A single-agent reference band spanning a default single agent and a safety-prompted one (two sentences about responsibility for safety), so an intervention cannot win merely by mentioning safety. A multi-agent baseline: one PM that only reads, eight coders that write, hub-and-spoke, no messaging. Interventions: group chat; review (1:1 reviewers, peer review, or PM review); the same three with a system-level safety-review prompt; safety responsibility assigned to a designated safety lead (one coder, empowered to edit any file), to every coder, or to the PM via escalation; a PM-written safety specifications document; and the compound of document plus lead.
3. **Measuring the mechanism.** Claude Sonnet 4.6 reads every agent's transcript and marks whether any agent tested the integrated pipeline end to end (T), discovered a harmful outcome (D), and, given discovery, attempted a fix (F), deflected, rationalized, or ignored it. A sequential mediation regression adds T, D, and F to a regression of safety score on configuration with model fixed effects and cluster-robust errors, and reports the share of each configuration's coefficient they explain.

# Q6 Experiment Design

* **Sepsis triage.** 400 synthetic training records (24% sepsis), 100 held-out test records (28%), derived from the PhysioNet sepsis data via Shen et al.; treatment 4,000 USD, discharge 500 USD, a missed case is fatal; classifiers may call Claude Haiku 4.5. Safety score is test sensitivity, business score normalized savings.
* **News recommender.** 359 training articles (46% misinformation, synthetic engagement), 90 test articles (50%), from ISOT Fake News via Shen et al.; an oracle by views gets only about 4.3M views with 72% misinformation. Safety score is one minus the top-50 misinformation rate.
* **Consulting.** Ten RFP scenarios from benign to fraught (sanctions circumvention, predatory lending, election-sentiment manipulation); single consultant against a flat 11-agent team and a hierarchical 12-agent team; benign and business-first ("redteam") prompts; interventions are the safety document, an independent ethics lead outside the delegation chain, and both. Proposals are scored 1 to 7 by Claude Sonnet 4.6 (five samples) on a constitution rubric of eight ethical dimensions and a business rubric.
* **Scale.** About 30 runs per model and configuration for the coding tasks (median), 150 per cell for consulting; mean and 10th-percentile safety scores with Welch t-intervals and BCa bootstrap intervals.

# Q8 Evidence Quality

**Single-versus-team gap (multi-agent baseline minus single-agent default, mean safety score, percentage points).**

| Model | Sepsis | Recommender |
|---|---|---|
| Mythos Preview | about −59 | −3 |
| Opus 4.7 | −36 | −12 to −17 band |
| Opus 4.6 | −35 | −12 to −17 band |
| Opus 4.1 | about −5 | −12 to −17 band |
| Sonnet 4.6 | about −1 | −12 to −17 band |

The paper reads the sepsis column as "more capable models show larger gaps" and the recommender column as no capability trend; the abstract's summary is that the gap shows no clear trend and that a small gap on one task does not predict a small gap on another.

**Interventions.** On sepsis only the compound reliably lands in the single-agent band across models; the designated lead works on three models and is uneven on Opus 4.7 and Opus 4.1; the document alone helps modestly; group chat helps partly on Opus 4.7 and Mythos Preview; all review variants, including safety review, fail to close the gap. The 10th-percentile ranking matches the mean. On the recommender the compound comes within a few points of the single agent on every model except Opus 4.6 (−8 pp). On consulting with Opus 4.1, the ethics lead alone exceeds the single-agent baseline and the compound is best.

**Mediation on sepsis (gain over the multi-agent baseline in safety score; M0 configuration and model only, M3 adds T, D, F).**

| Configuration | M0 | M3 | Share mediated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single agent | +0.241 | +0.039 | 84% |
| Group chat | +0.125 | +0.032 | 74% |
| Designated safety lead | +0.264 | +0.064 | 76% |
| Safety document | +0.152 | +0.144 | 6% |
| Compound (document + lead) | +0.318 | +0.120 | 62% |

The safety document's gain does not pass through testing, discovery, or fixing; the authors infer it makes module-level decisions more conservative. On the recommender, the behavior rates show the same pattern but only on Opus 4.7 do they consistently account for part of the gap.

**Robustness checks reported.** A second judge (Claude Haiku 4.5) correlates with the Sonnet 4.6 constitution scores at r = 0.95 over 9,793 runs and r = 0.99 over 72 cell means. For the Opus 4.7 redteam single-agent cell, 81 of 150 runs had no score (15 no proposal, 66 judge API failures); restricting to the four complete scenarios or scoring with Haiku keeps the gap negative (−0.51 to −1.22).

**Limits on the evidence (reader observations).**

* Every model, the transcript annotator, and both consulting judges are Claude models. The judge cross-check is Sonnet against Haiku, so it measures within-family agreement; no human validation of the T/D/F annotations is reported in the text retrieved.
* Mediation with post-treatment mediators measured by an LLM is suggestive, not causal: a configuration could change both testing and safety through a third behavior. The paper calls diffusion of responsibility "a" or "a primary" mechanism and keeps that hedge on the recommender task, where the link is weak.
* The "capable models have larger gaps" reading on sepsis rests on one task; the recommender reverses it for Mythos Preview, which is the paper's own point. A capability trend across five models from one family and two tasks cannot be estimated reliably either way.
* The sepsis test set has 28 positives, so sensitivity moves in steps of about 3.6 points, and the 95% target needs at least 27 of 28 caught. Large mean differences are robust to this, but the 10th-percentile comparisons rest on few distinct values (reader inference from the stated set sizes).
* Consulting cells drop runs without a proposal, up to 124 of 150 in one cell (Sonnet 4.6, redteam, single agent), so a single agent that refuses to write a proposal for a fraught RFP is excluded rather than scored as aligned. That likely understates the single agent's alignment advantage there, which would make the reported gaps conservative, but the direction is not measured.
* The single-agent target assumes the single agent's trade-off is the aligned one. For sepsis that is defensible (missed cases are fatal); for the recommender the "right" trade-off depends on stakes the task leaves to the agent.

# Q9 Contributions

1. Evidence across five models and three task families that the single-versus-team safety gap is not predicted by single-agent behavior and does not shrink with capability.
2. A negative result for common team patterns: peer review, safety-focused review, and group chat do not reliably restore single-agent safety.
3. A measured mechanism: transcript-level indicators of testing, discovery, and fixing mediate most of the sepsis gap, matching diffusion of responsibility.
4. A practical recipe (a designated, empowered safety lead plus a written system-level safety specification) that restores single-agent behavior on most models and tasks, and the magelab harness.

# Q10 Next Steps

* **Stated by the paper.** Validate on other model families as their agent harnesses mature.
* **Mixed-model teams.** All agents here share one model; teams that mix models, or a safety lead on a different model from the coders, are untested (reader suggestion).
* **Human-checked annotations.** A human sample of the T/D/F labels would firm up the mediation result, given that the annotator is from the studied family (reader suggestion).
* **Cost of the fix.** Appendix figures plot cost against safety gain; whether the compound's extra cost scales with team size is open.
* **Longer and real deployments.** The tasks are small, single-session builds with a stated trade-off; whether a safety lead keeps working when the safety goal is implicit or the run spans many sessions is an open question.
